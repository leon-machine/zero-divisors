# Quantum fields in one algebra

**Abstract.** Quantum field theory describes particles that can be created and destroyed, and this trail builds it inside one real geometric algebra. The tower's fermionic modes do not by themselves sit anywhere in spacetime. With the spacetime volume element as the field's imaginary unit, second quantisation gives a local field with the standard observables.

The walk adds mass, the photon and gravity's source, then adopts one premise, an indefinite inner product for gauge fields. Under it the first and second quantum corrections of electrodynamics and the running of the force strengths come out with the textbook values. The triangle anomaly cancels within each generation. The trail ends with the gauge structure of the gluons and weak bosons, the tie between spin and statistics, and what is still open.

The walk first attaches the modes to spacetime, sorts out the imaginary units and quantises the free fields. It then adopts one premise and follows the loops of electrodynamics, the anomaly and the gauge fields of the strong and weak forces. It ends with spin and statistics, and with the questions the field theory leaves open.

## What we assume

**Postulate 1 ((KF), the Krein-form premise for gauge fields: on the palatial storey the gauge fields, their ghosts and their Pauli-Villars regulator fields carry one indefinite (Krein) inner product, the Gupta-Bleuler form $[a(u), a(w)^{\dagger}] = -u\cdot w$ on the polarisations, and the physical space is positive through the quartet mechanism).** To describe light and the other force fields in a way that treats space and time alike, physicists use bookkeeping in which some unphysical states have negative or zero length, and remove them again before anything is measured. The same device is used to tame the infinities of quantum corrections, with auxiliary particles that count negatively. The series' quantum spaces all have positive lengths, so it cannot use this bookkeeping as it stands. This premise adopts it: the force fields of the series carry the standard indefinite measure built from the spacetime metric of their polarisations, and only states of positive length remain once the unphysical parts cancel in groups of four. Several results use it, among them the series' calculations of the first and second quantum corrections of electrodynamics, its calculation of how the force strengths change with energy, and its construction of the ghost bookkeeping. One of the two uses, the auxiliary negative particles, turns out not to be needed for the screening of charge by the vacuum, which can be computed without them. It is taken as given, not derived; it would be refuted if the physical states it leaves were found to include ones of negative length.

## Quantum fields in one algebra

Each step below adds one piece to a quantum field theory written entirely in one real algebra.

The series' tower stacks extra planes on top of the algebra of spacetime, and each plane carries one fermionic quantum mode. But the spacetime symmetries, the translations, rotations and boosts, commute with every mode operator, so a field built from the modes $a_j$, \[ \psi(x) = \sum_j u_j(x)\,a_j , \] on which translations act covariantly can only have constant mode functions $u_j$.

The obstruction is that each floor of the tower is finite, and a group like the boosts, which never closes up on itself, has no nontrivial quantum action on a finite floor.

**Theorem 2 (The ground does not attach the modes).** The tower adds quantum modes on top of the spacetime algebra, but the spacetime symmetries, translations, rotations and boosts, do not act on those modes at all. So the tower alone cannot place its modes in spacetime; something else must supply the link.

Along the trail the tower's modes are attached to spacetime through the conformal spinor space and quantised with the spacetime volume element as the field's unit, and the field theory built on them reproduces standard electrodynamics through the second correction, a scattering rate and the cancellation of the anomaly, each in a stated scheme.

Most of the loop results hold under one adopted premise, the indefinite Krein form for gauge fields, whose one-particle form the algebra restates as the trace form of Clifford conjugation without deriving it.

Four matters remain open.

Renormalisation in a regulator-free scheme stops at the vacuum polarisation: that Ward-preserving extensions exist for the other one-loop kernels is imported, not proved. The second-order result still uses dimensional regularisation, a borrowed method.

The interacting gauge fields are not yet built: quartet positivity holds for free fields, and the interacting case rests on Kugo and Ojima's hypotheses. Confinement of pure colour, the mass-gap question, is not addressed.

Spin-statistics for interacting fields beyond perturbation theory waits on a modular action that the store does not supply.
