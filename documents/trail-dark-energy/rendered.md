# Dark energy and the frozen point

**Abstract.** The conformal completion contains one distinguished fixed direction, the frozen point, and its length is set by the cosmological constant. This trail follows what that identification buys.

The frozen point supplies the extra vector that a known polynomial form of gravity needs, and its wobbles turn out to carry no physics. Where space is empty apart from the cosmological constant, its length cannot change, so dark energy behaves exactly like a cosmological constant.

On one stated condition this becomes a sharp prediction that dark energy never evolves, which a confirmed measurement of evolution would refute. The trail ends with what the framework does not do: it leaves the value of the cosmological constant, and even its sign, as an input.

The walk first builds the geometry of the frozen point, then reads off its prediction for dark energy, and finally asks what kind of number the cosmological constant is. Results a step rests on but that are not on the trail are listed at the end.

## What we assume

**Postulate 1 ((FS), the field-space premise of the gravitational action: the varied gravitational connection, a $\Cl(2,4)$ gauge field with nondegenerate soldering, admits a parallel tractor $I$ with $\ip{X}{I} = 1$ (the physical scale), a holonomy reduction to the stabiliser of $I$).** The series borrows a rule, an action, from which the equations of gravity follow, and that rule gives Einstein's equations only if one decides in advance which shapes of the gravitational field are allowed to vary: those that keep one special fixed direction, the frozen point, unchanged everywhere, measured in the actual scale of the spacetime. Without that decision the same rule gives a different theory, conformal gravity. Several attempts to derive the decision from the series' own structures failed; each turned out to be the same assumption in another form. So it is recorded as an assumption of its own. It explains why the cosmological constant is a fixed number, but for the same reason a measurement that dark energy never changes would not count as separate evidence for it. It would be refuted if dark energy were found to change, or if gravity were found to deviate from Einstein's equations where the rule applies.

## Dark energy and the frozen point

These results identify dark energy with the length of one fixed direction in the completed algebra.

We start from a fact carried over from earlier in the series. For a gravitational field on flat spacetime, the vacuum equations amount to one tractor, the frozen point $I$, being parallel (covariantly constant), and with a cosmological constant $\Lambda$ its square length is \[ Q(I) = \Lambda/3 \]

The rotors that leave it fixed form the de Sitter group $SO(1,4)$, or the anti-de Sitter group when $\Lambda < 0$.

**Theorem 2 (Imported: the frozen point and its geometry).** This fact collects earlier results about the frozen point, a fixed direction in the six-dimensional conformal model attached to flat spacetime. Einstein's vacuum equations amount to this direction staying constant as the gravitational gauge fields carry it from place to place, and its length is set by the cosmological constant. The symmetries that leave it alone form the de Sitter group, or the anti-de Sitter group when the cosmological constant is negative. The fact also writes down the gauge field that says what constant means here.

Parallelism of $I$ reduces the conformal $SO(2,4)$ structure to the de Sitter group, and the action becomes a square of the curvature of the $\Cl(2,4)$ gauge field, with no inverse metric anywhere.

**Theorem 4 (The frozen point makes the Stelle-West vector).** The frozen point is a fixed direction in the larger conformal space whose length encodes the cosmological constant. At the black hole's wall, where the connection is singular, its value diverges but its field equations still reach the wall; and the curvature-squared form itself exists only while the cosmological constant is nonzero.

An action built from two fixed-length vectors raises a worry: their wobbles might be extra fields that matter could feel. In the action the two vectors are held to the constraints \[ Q(I) = \Lambda/3 , \qquad \ip{V}{I} = 0 , \qquad Q(V) = -3/\Lambda \] and counting shows nine possible wobbles and nine broken symmetries, matched one to one.

So every wobble is pure gauge, and the graviton gains no extra polarisation.

**Theorem 5 (The point at infinity has no physical fluctuation: the $(I,V)$-sector is pure gauge).** Gravity's action here, written for gauge fields on flat spacetime, contains two fixed-length vectors tied to the point at infinity. One might worry that their wobbles are extra physical fields to which matter could couple. This result counts the possible wobbles and the broken symmetries, finds nine of each, and shows they match one to one, so every wobble can be undone by a symmetry. The graviton therefore carries no extra polarisation from this sector. This holds within the action's own variational principle, with both vectors treated as fields held to their fixed lengths; that the action so fixed is the known MacDowell-Mansouri one rests on the earlier theorem's assembly-level step.

If the frozen point cannot wobble, its length cannot drift, and that is what dark energy would need to evolve. On the Einstein region the frozen point is parallel and the connection preserves lengths, so \[ \partial_a Q(I) = 2\ip{\nabla_a I}{I} = 0 \] and dark energy has pressure equal to minus its energy density, $w = -1$.

Going further needs a stated condition: that the frozen point can be defined in regions full of matter so that it stays parallel there, which the obvious extension does not do.

**Theorem 6 ($w = -1$ on the vacuum region; the registration, and its condition).** Where space is empty apart from the cosmological constant, the frozen point's length cannot change as the gravitational gauge fields carry it across flat spacetime, so dark energy behaves exactly like a cosmological constant, with pressure equal to minus its energy density. The paper goes further, on a stated condition: if dark energy is this length, and if the gravitational field is of the kind that keeps the frozen point fixed in regions full of matter, measured in the spacetime's own scale (an assumption the series now adopts; its own transport rule does not do this), then dark energy never evolves. With that assumption the cosmological constant is simply a fixed number, the standard cosmological model's assumption, so finding that dark energy never changes would not be separate evidence for it. That is a rigid, testable stance, and a confirmed measurement of evolving dark energy would refute it.

Granting that condition turns the result into a registered prediction. Dark energy cannot evolve: $w = -1$ exactly, at every epoch.

Any confirmed measurement of a different value at any point in cosmic history would rule the idea out.

**Prediction 7 (Dark energy cannot evolve: w = -1).** The framework says dark energy is not a substance that can change over time but a fixed geometric quantity. That means its equation of state, the ratio of its pressure to its energy density, must be exactly minus one at all times. This rests on one stated assumption about how the underlying geometry extends into regions filled with matter, an assumption the series now adopts for its law of gravity. Under that assumption dark energy behaves exactly as in the standard cosmological model, so a measurement that it never changes would agree with the series without being separate evidence for the assumption. Any confirmed measurement of a different value at any point in cosmic history would rule the idea out.

The prediction says the length is fixed; the remaining steps ask what fixes it. A conserved number in a physical theory is exactly one of three things: dynamical (D), a parameter of the laws (P), or initial data (I) that varies between solutions; being kinematic (K) is a separate question.

So ruling out the dynamical and kinematic options does not leave initial data as the only possibility.

**Theorem 8 (The four alternatives are exhaustive; the three are not).** A number in a physical theory can be fixed by the dynamics, can be a setting of the laws themselves, or can vary from one solution to another as initial data. These three options exclude one another and cover every case, while being kinematic is a separate question. So ruling out kinematic and dynamical does not leave initial data as the only possibility: the number might be a parameter of the laws.

Applying that sorting to the frozen point's length gives a definite answer. Under the action exactly as written, $\Lambda$ is fixed before variation, and every solution has \[ H^2 = Q(I) = \Lambda/3 \] on its de Sitter region, so the length cannot change within a solution yet differs between theories.

It is a parameter of the laws, not initial data. Making it initial data would need an extra equation of motion that the action does not provide.

**Theorem 9 (Under the action as written, the norm is a parameter of the laws).** The cosmological constant fixes the length of the frozen point. Under the action exactly as written, this length cannot fluctuate within any one solution, yet it differs between theories with different cosmological constants. That makes it a setting of the laws rather than initial data chosen for each universe. Making it initial data would need an extra equation of motion that the action does not provide.

One might still hope the series' own machinery, the transport that repairs places where division fails, could pick the value. When the cosmological constant is zero the frozen point is null, and the transport completes that degenerate space to signature $(2,4)$, supplying a canonical null partner $\varphi$ of $I$.

Every vector orthogonal to the Lorentz frame is then $aI + b\varphi$ with norm $2ab$, and a frozen point of cosmological constant $\Lambda$ is one with \[ 2ab = \Lambda/3 \] So the transport fixes everything about the frozen point except its norm.

**Theorem 10 (The transport constrains the frozen point only through its norm).** When the cosmological constant is zero the frozen point becomes a vector of zero length, a degenerate spot that the series' transport completes into a larger, well-behaved space. That completion comes with a canonical plane and a preferred pair of directions in it. Every possible value of the cosmological constant corresponds to a point in this plane, and the available symmetries can move that point anywhere except to a point of different length. So the transport pins down everything about the frozen point except its length, which is the cosmological constant, and gives no reason to prefer a positive value over a negative one.

**Fact (imported) 13 (Imported: the MacDowell-Mansouri theorem, Stelle-West form).** MacDowell and Mansouri, and later Stelle and West, showed that gravity with a cosmological constant can be written as a theory of a larger symmetry group, the de Sitter group, broken by a vector of fixed length. On flat spacetime this means adding one extra direction to the algebra in which the gravitational fields take their values. The action is then the square of a field strength, and it splits into a topological piece, the usual Einstein term and the cosmological term. Its equations are the Einstein equations with that cosmological constant.

**Fact (imported) 14 (Imported: walls, and the sphere theorem).** Where geometry degenerates in the mildest, tame way, along a wall where one direction stops being measurable, the algebraic description of geometry does not break but carries on through the wall. The metric blows up there in a controlled way, and the completion adds exactly one partner to the dying direction. A further imported result, which rests on an assumed principle (the Indifference principle) rather than a proof, says that the sphere of directions around the wall is built from pairings of the wall's boundary modes.

**Fact (imported) 15 (Covariant form of the vacuum equations).** In the interior region, Einstein's equations for empty space can be recast as a single condition on the gravitational fields: a particular marker in the six-dimensional conformal extension of spacetime, attached to the physical scale, must stay constant as it is carried from point to point by a gauge field built from gravity. This result proves that the two formulations are exactly equivalent, and that the marker is a null (lightlike) object. It also notes that merely having some constant marker of this kind is a weaker condition that does not by itself give the field equations.

**Fact (imported) 20 (The stabiliser rotates from parabolic to reductive).** The symmetries that keep the frozen point fixed change character with the cosmological constant. With no cosmological constant they are the symmetries of flat spacetime, translations included; with a positive or negative one they become the symmetries of de Sitter or anti-de Sitter space, with no translations. **Fact (imported) 21 (Imported: the minimal completion of a degenerate algebra, and Rule 1).** When a geometric algebra has degenerate directions, the smallest way to remove them adds one partner for each, and the result is unique. For the basic kind of division failure in an ordinary algebra, the repair must add one positive and one negative direction. For another kind, adding a single direction of either sign gives the same algebra, so the algebra alone cannot tell which sign was chosen.

Both orientations of the spacetime algebra sit inside it, which is why the copy theorem cannot tell this world from its mirror. The odd-dimensional algebras one step up do tell the two orientations apart, because their centres differ.
