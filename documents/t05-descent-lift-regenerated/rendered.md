# The Descent, Lifted: What the pinching discards, a pairing stores: Companion note to T1 and T5: the reversible lift of the descent costs one rung per mode and needs exactly the generator the singularity paper excludes (regenerated from the store)

**Abstract.** The series' descent, the pinching onto the number-diagonal algebra, is one-way: the root paper proves no homomorphism returns, and the singularity paper shows it sends an entangled pair to a classical shared bit. This note treats the descent as a channel and asks whether it lifts as the transport principle lifts an element without an inverse: reversibly, by adjoining what it discards. It does, at an exact price. On the even (physical) states of $\nu$ modes the descent has Kraus rank $2^{\nu - 1}$, and an even isometric lift needs that many orthogonal even record states; $k$ rungs of the tower supply $2^{k - 1}$, so the minimal record is $\nu$ rungs, one per mode. One lift is explicit: a coupling in the singularity paper's supplied class applied to a record prepared in the Thouless state with all pairing amplitudes one, by a rotation of the family whose member at amplitude $4/3$ is that paper's necessity witness. The pairing may sit on the record or on the pair; no number-conserving dynamics replaces it. The discarded coherence reappears as entanglement across the pair-record cut with the pair's Schmidt coefficients; the classical shared bit is the reduced view of a pure four-mode state. Seen from the two sides of the descent, the one-way door and the exclusion of pairing terms are one fact within the supplied dynamics. The lift does not touch the Hawking angle, and extending the principle from elements to maps is a reading, not a theorem.

## What we assume

**Postulate 1 ((L$_E$): boundary-mode locality, as descent covariance on finite windows).** \emph{Premise (L$_{E}$), boundary-mode locality.} On every finite window of the boundary modes, $v_{1}, \dots, v_{n}$ satisfying the CAR, the internal dynamics $\alpha_{t}$ of the boundary modes commutes with the descent at all times:
\[
\alpha_{t}\circ\mathcal{E} \;=\; \mathcal{E}\circ\alpha_{t} \quad \text{for all } t, \qquad \mathcal{E} = E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{n}, \quad E_{k}(x) = N_{k}xN_{k} + (1 - N_{k})x(1 - N_{k}),
\]
$\mathcal{E}$ the descent of the window (Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases}).

\emph{The version 1 wording, equivalent.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boundary-mode-locality-is-descent-covariance}, on every finite window this holds if and only if the dynamics is generated by elements of the abelian algebra $\mathcal{D}$ that the $\sigma_{k} = 2N_{k} - 1$ generate, diagonal in the joint number basis: the internal dynamics is generated by the geometric data the framework supplies on $E$, the boost bivector field $x \mapsto \sigma_{r}(x)$ and its products. Cross-point products such as $\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}$ correlate phases without moving occupation; hopping $v_{j}^{*}v_{k} + v_{k}^{*}v_{j}$, pairing $v_{j}^{*}v_{k}^{*} + v_{k}v_{j}$ and every other generator with a component outside $\mathcal{D}$ are excluded by the one clause. Within the class of generators the framework supplies, the exclusion of hopping is a theorem (Theorem \claimref{singularity.class-relative-no-go-no-supplied}); what the premise adds is the exclusion of unsupplied generators, pairing terms and the possible differential dynamics, which Proposition \claimref{singularity.l-stabiliser-tangent} places in the tangent of the vacuum orbit and in its stabiliser.

\emph{What it forbids.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.number-diagonal-dynamics-spreads-nothing}, every dynamics the premise admits carries an evolved mode operator only to the modes directly coupled to it, and every local commutator and out-of-time-order correlator is almost periodic and bounded: the premise forbids exponential real-time commutator growth. The real-time spreading law $t_{*} = \kappa^{-1}\ln N$ of Proposition \claimref{scrambling.spreading-law-chaos-bound-saturated} is therefore not realised under it, and the scrambling time is a modular-time statement: the growth $e^{\kappa t}$ is a rate of the rotor flow, the KMS-strip continuation of the phase flow that is the windows' time evolution (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.phase-flow-is-time-rotor-flow-kms-continuation}). The premise is kept over fast real-time scrambling; the two cannot both hold.

**Postulate 2 ((P): univalence).** Physical states of the boundary modes are even: $\omega \circ \alpha = \omega$, equivalently $\omega$ annihilates the odd sector $\Aod$.

## Setting, and what is imported



**setting  (Setting, and what is imported).** The transport principle is a rule for elements: where an element has no inverse, adjoin the minimum that gives it one, and keep the descent that forgets the adjunction \cite[Thm.~4.13, Prop.~7.5, Thm.~7.6]{ButlerT1}. The descent is a conditional expectation, the pinching onto the algebra that commutes with the ladder's occupation projector, and it is one-way: no homomorphism returns from the ascent to the base. The singularity paper reads the same pinching, mode by mode, as the parity average $\EN(x) = \tfrac12(x + \G x\G)$ and, on several modes, as the pinching onto the joint-number-diagonal algebra \cite[Thm.~5.7, Prop.~9.6]{ButlerT5}; applied to an entangled pair $c\ket{00} + s\ket{11}$ it returns the classical shared bit $\operatorname{diag}(c^{2}, s^{2})$.

This note asks the inverted question. A map without an inverse can still be lifted: enlarged to a reversible map on a bigger system, followed by forgetting the enlargement. That is Stinespring's dilation, and it is the exact analogue for maps of what the transport principle does for elements. The question is whether the descent's lift can be built inside the tower, from rungs, by operations the framework's own premises allow, and what it costs. The answer is computed exactly, in the singularity paper's conventions, and the kill conditions of the section ``The claim, and the kill conditions fixed before computation'' were fixed before the computation.

Three things are fixed on this page. First, the states. The singularity paper's premise (P) says physical states of the modes are even, and its imported univalence rule says the observable algebra is the even subalgebra \cite[Prem.~7.8, Fact~7.9]{ButlerT5}. Everything below is stated on even states, and a single mode then has nothing to lift: its only even pure state is the vacuum, on which the descent is the identity. The descent's first physical action is on the pair. Second, the reduced state. For fermions the reduced state of a subsystem is the restriction of the joint state to the subsystem's algebra; the tensor-product partial trace agrees with it when the complementary factor is even, which is the only case the lift uses, and the script computes the restriction throughout. Third, the vocabulary, for a reader of quantum information who has not read the series: a \emph{rung} is one $(1,1)$ ascent of the tower, which adjoins one fermionic mode \cite[Thm.~4.2]{ButlerT3}; the \emph{descent} is the pinching above; the \emph{supplied class} is the set of generators the singularity paper's completion provides, polynomials in the parity sections $\sigma_{k} = 2N_{k} - 1$, all joint-number-diagonal \cite[Prem.~7.30, Thm.~C.8]{ButlerT5}; a \emph{pairing} generator is $a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}$ or its adjoint, the part of $\mathfrak{so}(2\nu)$ that moves the vacuum \cite[Prop.~13.4]{ButlerT5}; and the two-ledger words \emph{transport-essential}, \emph{convenient} and \emph{absent} grade a result by whether the $(1,1)$ increment does irreplaceable work in it, some work, or none \cite{ButlerT17}.

## The claim, and the kill conditions fixed before computation



**setting  (The claim, and the kill conditions fixed before computation).** The kill conditions and predictions below are as registered, in the deposited pre-registration \cite{ButlerPreregT05dilation}; every departure between that wording and what the note does is collected in Remark \claimref{note.descent-lift.departures-registered-wording}.

**setting  (The claim, and the kill conditions fixed before computation).** \textbf{Claim D (the descent lifts, at a price).} The descent has a lift within the tower: an even isometric dilation whose record is a set of rungs, realised by an even unitary that factorises as a coupling in the singularity paper's supplied class composed with a pairing rotation on the record. The coherence the descent discards is stored as system-record entanglement with Schmidt coefficients equal to the system's amplitudes. The only step outside the supplied class is the pairing rotation, so within the dynamics the framework supplies the descent has no lift: the root paper's one-way door and the singularity paper's exclusion of pairing terms are one fact.

\begin{itemize}
\item[K1] (one rung suffices) If an even isometric dilation of the single-mode parity average exists with a one-rung record, the count ``two rungs for one mode'' is dead. Test: the Kraus operators span $\{1, \G\}$, both even and number-diagonal, so an even isometry has the form $P_{0} \otimes r_{0} + P_{1} \otimes r_{1}$ with $r_{0}, r_{1}$ even record states that must be orthogonal ($\EN$ kills the coherence); the even sector of one rung is one-dimensional.
\item[K2] (no pairing needed) If an even unitary generated by number-conserving elements only (hops within the record, hops between system and record, Cartan elements, joint-number-diagonal couplings) realises a lift from the record vacuum, the clause ``the only step outside the supplied class is a pairing rotation'' is dead. Test: such a unitary conserves the total number, the joint state stays in total number $\leq \nu$, and the record's even states there are too few to be orthogonal.
\item[K3] (coupling outside the supplied class) If the controlled coupling $C$ of the factorisation is not joint-number-diagonal (not a polynomial in the parity sections), the factorisation clause is dead.
\item[K4] (coherence not stored) If the lifted state of $c\ket{0} + s\ket{1}$ does not have reduced spectrum $(c^{2}, s^{2})$ on the system and on the record, with the joint state pure, the reading ``the discarded coherence is the system-record entanglement'' is dead.
\item[K5] (the principle) The root paper's transport principle concerns elements without inverses. Applying it to a map without an inverse is an extension. If nothing in the root paper forces a non-invertible map to be lifted, the lift is a reading and the result is transport-convenient at most. Expected to trigger; recorded as such, not as a kill of the mathematics.
\end{itemize}
Predictions, as registered: the minimal even record for one mode is two rungs, in their even sector, and for $\nu$ modes $\nu + 1$ rungs, the per-mode product lift using $2\nu$ and not being minimal (P1); the record must leave its vacuum by two quanta, a pairing creation, and the stabiliser of the record vacuum fixes its ray (P2); $C = 1 - 2P^{R}_{11}P^{S}_{1}$ for one mode (P3); at $(c, s) = (\tfrac45, \tfrac35)$ the lifted state is $c\ket{0}\ket{+} + s\ket{1}\ket{-}$ with reduced spectra $(\tfrac{16}{25}, \tfrac{9}{25})$ on both sides, the system marginal being the thermal marginal at $e^{-\omega/T} = \tfrac{9}{16}$ (P4); the record preparation is the necessity witness rotation at amplitude $1$, and with amplitude $A$ the lift is a partial descent with coherence factor $(1 - A^{2})/(1 + A^{2})$ (P5); a one-rung stochastic version exists and records nothing recoverable (P6); K5 triggers, with ledger entry transport-convenient (P7).

## The descent as a channel on physical states





**Theorem 10 (Kraus form of the descent on even states).** Let $\G_{k} = (-1)^{N_{k}}$ be the parity of mode $k$ and $\G^{\kappa} = \prod_{k}\G_{k}^{\kappa_{k}}$ the parity monomials, $\kappa \in \{0,1\}^{\nu}$. On the full Fock space of $\nu$ modes the descent is $\EN(\rho) = 2^{-\nu}\sum_{\kappa}\G^{\kappa}\rho\,\G^{\kappa}$, of Kraus rank $2^{\nu}$. On the even sector, where $\G_{\mathrm{tot}} = \prod_{k}\G_{k} = 1$, the monomials pair up and the descent is $\EN(\rho) = 2^{1-\nu}\sum_{\kappa_{\nu} = 0}\G^{\kappa}\rho\,\G^{\kappa}$, of Kraus rank $2^{\nu - 1}$, every Kraus operator even and number-diagonal. For $\nu = 1$ it is the identity. For $\nu = 2$ it is $\EN(\rho) = \tfrac12(\rho + \G_{0}\rho\,\G_{0})$ on $\operatorname{span}\{\ket{00}, \ket{11}\}$, which kills exactly the pairing coherence: $c\ket{00} + s\ket{11} \mapsto \operatorname{diag}(c^{2}, s^{2})$.

*Proof.* The mode-wise parity average is $\tfrac12(\rho + \G_{k}\rho\G_{k})$ and the averages commute, so their composite is the stated sum (Fact \claimref{descent-lift.imported-descent-modes-univalence-supplied-class}); it kills every matrix unit $\ket{A}\bra{B}$ with $A \neq B$, since some $\G_{k}$ has opposite signs on $A$ and $B$, and fixes the diagonal ones: the pinching. The Kraus rank is the Choi rank (\claimref{descent-lift.imported-stinespring-kraus-choi}), which the script computes as $4$ on the full two-mode space and $2$ on its even sector, and $4$ on the even sector of three modes. On the even sector $\G_{\nu} = \prod_{k < \nu}\G_{k}$, so the $2^{\nu}$ monomials collapse to $2^{\nu-1}$ distinct operators, each appearing twice. On one mode the even sector is the vacuum line. On the pair, $\G_{0}$ and $\G_{1}$ agree on the even sector ($+1$ on $\ket{00}$, $-1$ on $\ket{11}$), and the script checks the descent of $(\tfrac45)\ket{00} + (\tfrac35)\ket{11}$ is $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac{16}{25}, \tfrac{9}{25})$ with the coherence $\tfrac{12}{25}$ removed.


**Remark  (Where the descent first acts).** On a single mode the descent acts only on the odd-even coherence $\ket{0}\bra{1}$, which (P) declares unphysical: the single-mode descent is the identity on every physical state. The descent's first physical action is on the pair, and what it removes there is the pairing coherence between $\ket{00}$ and $\ket{11}$, the coherence that makes the pair entangled. That is the entangled-pair-to-classical-bit computation of the singularity paper's descent, and it is the object lifted below.

## The lift, and its cost





**Definition 11 (Lift).** A \emph{lift} of the descent on $\nu$ modes is an isometry $W : H_{S} \to H_{S} \otimes H_{R}$, $H_{R}$ the Fock space of $k$ rungs, that is even, $W\G_{S} = (\G_{S}\otimes\G_{R})W$, and induces the descent on the even sector: the restriction of $W\rho W^{\dagger}$ to the system's algebra equals $\EN(\rho)$ for every even $\rho$. A lift is \emph{realised} by an even unitary $U$ on $H_{S}\otimes H_{R}$ with $W = U(\,\cdot \otimes \ket{0_{R}})$.



**Theorem 12 (The lift costs one rung per mode, and needs a pairing).** Let $\nu \geq 2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] A lift has the form $W\ket{A} = \ket{A} \otimes r_{A}$ on the even basis states $\ket{A}$, with $r_{A} = \sum_{\kappa}(K_{\kappa})_{AA}\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ for orthonormal record states $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$, one per Kraus operator, and every $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ even. Hence the record must contain $2^{\nu-1}$ orthonormal even states.
\item[(ii)] The even sector of $k$ rungs has dimension $2^{k-1}$. Hence a lift needs $k \geq \nu$ rungs, one per mode, and one rung never suffices.
\item[(iii)] With $\nu$ rungs a lift exists: $W = \sum_{\kappa_{\nu} = 0}\G^{\kappa} \otimes \ket{r_{\kappa}}$, the $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ any orthonormal basis of the record's even sector; for the pair, $W = 1 \otimes \ket{00} + \G_{0} \otimes \ket{11}$, up to the normalisation $W^{\dagger}W = 2$.
\item[(iv)] The lift factorises as $W = C\,(\,\cdot \otimes \varphi)$ with $\varphi = \sum_{\kappa}\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ and $C = \sum_{\kappa}\G^{\kappa} \otimes \ket{r_{\kappa}}\bra{r_{\kappa}}$. For the pair, $C = 1 - 2P^{R}_{11}N_{0}$, a polynomial in the parity sections, joint-number-diagonal, even, an involution: an element of the supplied class. And $\varphi = \ket{00} + \ket{11}$ is the Thouless state of the record pair with amplitude $1$, a Witt vacuum of a rotated decomposition.
\item[(v)] Any even unitary $U$ realising a lift from the record vacuum has a generator that does not conserve the total number; among the quadratic generators, those of the Bogoliubov algebra $\mathfrak{so}(2(\nu + k))$, that is a generator with a nonzero pairing component. A unitary generated by number-conserving elements (hops within the record, hops between system and record, Cartan elements, joint-number-diagonal couplings) realises no lift. The pairing may sit on the record, as in (iv), or on the system and be carried to the record by hops.
\end{enumerate}

*Proof.* (i) By Fact \claimref{descent-lift.imported-stinespring-kraus-choi} every dilation is $W = \sum_{\kappa}K_{\kappa} \otimes \ket{r_{\kappa}}$ with $K_{\kappa}$ a Kraus family and the $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ orthonormal, and by Proposition \claimref{descent-lift.kraus-form-descent-even-states} the Kraus operators are diagonal in the number basis, so $W\ket{A} = \ket{A} \otimes r_{A}$. Evenness of $W$ and of $\ket{A}$ force $r_{A}$ even for every $A$; since the matrix $((K_{\kappa})_{AA})$ is invertible (the Kraus operators are linearly independent), each $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ is a combination of the $r_{A}$ and is even. The record must therefore contain $2^{\nu-1}$ orthonormal even states. (ii) The even basis states of $k$ rungs are the subsets of even size, $2^{k-1}$ of them; the script counts them for $k \leq 4$. (iii) $W$ is even because each $\G^{\kappa}$ and each $\ket{r_{\kappa}}$ is; $W^{\dagger}W = \sum_{\kappa}(\G^{\kappa})^{2} = 2^{\nu-1}$; and the restriction of $W\rho W^{\dagger}$ to the system's algebra is $\sum_{\kappa}\G^{\kappa}\rho\G^{\kappa}$ because the record states are orthonormal and even, which is $2^{\nu-1}\EN(\rho)$ on even $\rho$. The script verifies evenness, the norm and the induced channel on every even matrix unit for $\nu = 2$ (two rungs) and $\nu = 3$ (three rungs), computing the restriction to the system's algebra directly and, for the pair, checking that it agrees with the tensor-product partial trace. (iv) $C(\ket{A} \otimes \varphi) = \sum_{\kappa}\G^{\kappa}\ket{A} \otimes \ket{r_{\kappa}} = W\ket{A}$. For the pair, $\G_{0} = 1 - 2N_{0}$ gives $C = 1 - 2P^{R}_{11}N_{0} = 1 - \tfrac14(1 - \G_{0})(1 - \G_{2})(1 - \G_{3})$; the script checks the polynomial identity, diagonality, commutation with every $N_{k}$ and with the total parity, and $C^{2} = 1$. **Theorem 17 (Within the supplied dynamics the descent has no lift).** Let the admissible generators on the system and any number of rungs be the Hermitian elements of the singularity paper's supplied class together with the Hermitian quadratic elements, in $\mathfrak{so}(2(\nu + k))$, that stabilise the joint vacuum ray: all hops and Cartan elements, on the system, on the rungs and between them. Then no unitary generated by admissible elements realises a lift of the descent from the rungs' vacuum. A lift exists in the ascent by $\nu$ rungs when a pairing rotation is added to the admissible generators, and never without a generator that fails to conserve the total number; the lift of Theorem \claimref{descent-lift.lift-costs-one-rung-per-mode}(iv) places the pairing on the rungs.

*Proof.* A Hermitian quadratic element is $X = \sum_{i,j} h_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{j} + \sum_{i<j} (p_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j} + \bar{p}_{ij}a_{j}a_{i}) + \lambda$, and $X\ket{0} = \sum_{i<j} p_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}\ket{0} + \lambda\ket{0}$ with the states $a^{\dagger}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{j}\ket{0}$ independent; if $X$ stabilises the vacuum ray then $p = 0$, and $X$ is a combination of hops and Cartan elements, which conserve the total number. under which the ascent embedding $A \hookrightarrow B$ becomes the twisted diagonal $a \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(a, \hat{a})$. In particular Rule 1 preserves the Morita class: the Wedderburn type, and with it the simplicity or splitness and the type-C diagnosis of the section ``A taxonomy of obstructions'', are the same for $B$ as for $A$, and the clock residue $d = p - q \pmod 8$ is fixed. (The \emph{presence} of types N and P is not preserved and could not be: Proposition \claimref{transport.no-transport-terminates} below shows every completion carries fresh obstructions of its own.) The adjoined ladder pair is, in the matrix description, exactly the off-diagonal matrix units, and the descent projectors $f^*f,\, ff^*$ are the diagonal ones.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.rule-1-morita-trivial, version 5 (T01 Thm 7.1).

**Fact (imported) 34 (The transport algorithm).** The Principle says what is true; practice needs to know what to do. The theorems distil into a six-step procedure, and every step names the theorem, proved above, that licenses it. the table accompanying \claimref{transport.transport-algorithm} is the one-page version: the theorems in operational form. That the steps form a decision procedure with no gaps is itself a theorem, Corollary \claimref{transport.no-fourth-rule}.




\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{Step 1.}] \textbf{Locate the failure.} Identify the element at which invertibility fails, or, if the construction is a family, the locus at which it fails. Nothing later depends on how this is done; it is a computation in the case at hand. What the algebra guarantees is that the failure cannot be argued away: a null vector has no inverse in \emph{any} unital algebra containing the base (Lemma \claimref{transport.noinverse}), and neither has an idempotent.

\item[\textbf{Step 2.}] \textbf{Diagnose the type.} Ask, in order:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Is the failing element a vector $v$ with $Q(v) = 0$? Then the obstruction is \textbf{type N}. These exist exactly in mixed signature, $pq \neq 0$ (Proposition \claimref{transport.existence-locus-type-n}).
  \item Is it a \emph{central} idempotent? Then \textbf{type C}. These exist exactly when $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, and are then exactly the two projectors $\tfrac12(1 \pm \omega)$ (Theorem \claimref{transport.existence-classification-type-c}); equivalently, exactly when the algebra is not simple (Theorem \claimref{transport.classification-real-clifford-algebras}).
  \item Otherwise \textbf{type P}: a non-central idempotent, or an element annihilated by one.
  \end{itemize}
  The trichotomy is exhaustive: every zero divisor of a nondegenerate real Clifford algebra is annihilated by a nontrivial idempotent, possibly central in the split signatures, and then exactly when the element lies in one ideal (Theorem \claimref{transport.exhaustiveness-every-zero-divisor-annihilated-idempotent}). Two cautions. The nilpotency locus is strictly larger than the type-N locus, and nilpotence alone does not diagnose N (Example \claimref{note.transport.nilpotents-without-null-vectors-two-loci}); and a single algebra may carry all three types at once (Example \claimref{note.transport.cl-1-0-cl-2-1}).

\item[\textbf{Step 3.}] \textbf{Apply the rule.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Type N, Rule 1.} Adjoin one hyperbolic plane: $\Cl(p,q) \to \Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$. Do not expect the element to be repaired: no equivariant assignment of a partner to $v$ exists, in this ascent or any other (Theorem \claimref{transport.no-go-level-elements}), and the obstruction is a cohomology class that every enlargement pulls back unchanged (Theorem \claimref{transport.cohomological-form-no-go}). What is repaired is the space of points (Step 4). The increment is forced: $(+1,+1)$, never $(+2,0)$ or $(0,+2)$, among null-lift completions (Theorem \claimref{transport.minimality}), and no algebra with a smaller or differently balanced quadric can host the resolution however the base is embedded in it (Theorem \claimref{transport.representation-independent-minimality-completion}).
  \item \textbf{Type C, Rule 2.} Adjoin one generator $\nu$ orthogonal to $\mathbb{V}$, of either nonzero square. The grade involution becomes inner, the two ideals fuse, and the completion is $\Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$, simple (Theorem \claimref{transport.rule-2-ideal-fusion-sharp-form}). The null-generator ascent is not an option: it produces a square-zero ideal and never fuses (Theorem \claimref{transport.one-generator-minimal-minimal-completions-classified}(2)). The sign of $\nu^2$ is free and the algebra forgets it (Remark \claimref{note.transport.bit-algebra-forgets}); if it matters for the application, it must be fixed by structure outside the algebra.
  \item \textbf{Type P.} Do nothing. The identity assignment $\sigma(e) = e$ is a total, fully equivariant von Neumann repair, at signature increment $(0,0)$ (Proposition \claimref{transport.type-p-self-repairing}).
  \end{itemize}

\item[\textbf{Step 4.}] \textbf{Compute in the completion.} For type N this is the substantive step, and it is where the classical formulas apply. Represent points, not elements: lift $x \in \mathbb{V}$ to the null ray of
  \[
  X(x) \;=\; \nO + x + \tfrac12 Q(x)\,\nInf ,
  \]
  which satisfies the incidence condition $Q(x) = 2st$ in the homogeneous coordinates $(s,x,t)$ of Proposition \claimref{transport.swap-rescale-normal-form}. The reciprocal is then the linear involution
  \[
  \iota(s,\,x,\,t) \;=\; (2t,\ x,\ \tfrac12 s),
  \]
  total on the quadric and never failing (Theorem \claimref{transport.totality}). All nonlinearity and all division have been deferred to a single read-out. This is the linearise-then-normalise pattern of homogeneous coordinates and M\"obius matrices, encountered here as forced rather than chosen (Remark \claimref{note.transport.classical}).

\item[\textbf{Step 5.}] \textbf{Descend, and state what descent costs.} Return the answer to $\mathbb{V}$ with the read-out $\dsc[\,s\nO + x + t\nInf\,] = x/s$. On the invertible locus the composite gives exactly the classical answer (Lemma \claimref{transport.descent-read-out-map}(2)); where the first slot vanishes the answer is an ideal point and corresponds to no point of $\mathbb{V}$ (Lemma \claimref{transport.descent-read-out-map}(3)). \emph{The refusal of the read-out is information, not failure}: it says the answer exists and lies outside the original space. At the level of elements the corresponding statement is stronger and worth knowing before relying on any descent: the pinching onto the base is a conditional expectation whose kernel is exactly the adjoined ladder sector (Proposition \claimref{transport.pinching-kernel}), and there is no unital algebra homomorphism from either completion back to its base at all (Theorem \claimref{transport.door-one-way}). Descent is invertible on the transported elements and impossible on the elements the ascent adjoined.

\item[\textbf{Step 6.}] \textbf{Iterate, or stop.} Ask whether the completion carries an obstruction of its own. It does: no transport terminates in a division algebra (Proposition \claimref{transport.no-transport-terminates}). But the bookkeeping is finite in the following sense: Rule 1 fixes the mod-8 residue, so the taxonomy of the completion is that of the base and iterating changes nothing structural (Theorem \claimref{transport.rule-1-morita-trivial}), while Rule 2 moves the residue off the split classes and can therefore occur at most once in any transport history (Proposition \claimref{transport.fusion-occurs-most-once}). A transport history takes at most one step on the clock, and then remains fixed.
\end{enumerate}

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.transport-algorithm, version 3 (T01 Sec 8.1).

**Fact (imported) 35 (Type P is self-repairing).** Let $A$ be any unital ring, $\mathcal{E}(A)$ its set of idempotents, and $A' \supseteq A$ any unital extension. The identity assignment
\[
\sigma : \mathcal{E}(A) \to A' , \qquad \sigma(e) = e,
\]
satisfies both von Neumann identities, $e\,\sigma(e)\,e = e$ and $\sigma(e)\,e\,\sigma(e) = \sigma(e)$, and is equivariant under \emph{every} ring automorphism of every extension: for any automorphism $\theta$ of $A'$ preserving $A$, $\sigma(\theta(e)) = \theta(\sigma(e))$ trivially. In the terminology of the taxonomy: the transport rule for type P is the identity, at signature increment $(0,0)$, and the repair is already present in the base algebra.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.type-p-self-repairing, version 2 (T01 Prop 6.1).
