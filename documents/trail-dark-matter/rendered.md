# Dark matter and the dark towers

**Abstract.** Astronomers find about 5.4 times as much dark matter as ordinary matter. In this framework the dark matter lives in separate towers that touch ours only through gravity.

This trail meets the towers, the mirror tower that copies ours, and the reason their matter stays dark and never decays. Under its premises the mirror accounts for one unit of the dark matter, a copy of ordinary matter to within half a percent.

A counting rule then matches the full observed ratio, but it is recorded as a fit, not a prediction. The walk ends by setting the dark sector, as the series builds it, against the data, where no arrangement yet passes every test.

The walk first sets out which dark towers exist and why they stay dark, then turns to how much matter they hold and what it is made of.

It closes with tests against observations.

## What we assume

**Postulate 1 (P1: the single frozen point).** This assumption says the origin of the universe happened once, in one way, for everything. Every hidden sector feels the same gravity, and every sector that can have a handedness received the same one. It is assumed, not derived.

**Postulate 2 (P2: indifference, scoped to sector labels).** This assumption says the birth of the universe treats alike only things that are genuinely alike. The only hidden sectors that count as alike are pairs related by a reflection, so only those pairs are forced to match. Which member of the matching pair we live in is simply a matter of where we stand. What must match are the sector's fixed labels, such as its stored charge, not how hot it is: the assumption leaves the temperature of the mirror sector open, and it takes the number of modes the birth deposits to be finite.

**Postulate 3 (P3: nearest-dimension representatives).** Each hidden sector could in principle be realised with several different numbers of dimensions. This assumption picks, for each one, the number closest to our four, with a tie-break for the cases exactly in between. It is a choice the paper makes, not something it derives.

**Postulate 4 (P4: democracy of the asymmetry over the oriented towers).** The universe's slight excess of matter over antimatter is assumed to be imprinted equally, particle type by particle type, in every sector able to carry such an excess. For the mirror copy of our world this is proved separately, so the assumption does real work in only one remaining case.

**Postulate 5 (R1: equal mass per quantum).** This assumption says the basic particles in every hidden sector weigh the same. The paper needs it for its formula for the amount of dark matter and does not derive it.

**Postulate 6 (R2: one quantum per unit ball).** This assumption says that in every hidden sector the particles are packed at the same typical spacing: one per ball of a fixed size, measured in that sector's own number of dimensions. It also takes for granted that every sector contains matter at all.

**Postulate 7 (R3: one cell per unshared direction).** When a hidden sector has a direction our world lacks, only a thin slice of it counts towards the gravity we feel, and this assumption fixes that slice at one particle spacing. A mechanism is offered that gives this value, but it explains the choice rather than proving it.

**Postulate 8 (Premise H1: a constituent's momentum).** This is an assumption, not a result: a constituent's motion is read from a spinor in the tower's own algebra, linked to the constituent in the larger conformal algebra in a way that respects the tower's rotations. In our four-dimensional world this is already established; in three and five dimensions it is assumed.

**Postulate 9 (Premise H2: gauge algebras as hierarchy automorphisms).** This is an assumption, not a result: the forces acting on a tower's matter are taken to be the symmetries of its smallest composite that can have mass. Our own tower's forces arise this way in the Standard-Model paper, at the level of matching observation, and the other towers are assumed to follow the same pattern.

**Postulate 10 (Premise U: the transposition rule).** This is an assumption, not a result: it fixes how the steps that give our own particle physics are repeated in the other towers. It says what a constituent is, which mixings of constituents and of their internal pairs count as symmetries, and that any square root of minus one must come from the tower's own algebra rather than being added from outside.

## Dark matter and the dark towers

Each step below either says what the dark towers are or checks what they should do.

Real Clifford algebras repeat with period eight, and the series reads each repeating family as a tower. Along a tower the residue \[ p - q \pmod 8 \] is invariant, with $p$ and $q$ the numbers of positive and negative directions.

Taking each tower at the dimension nearest four (premise P3), exactly the residues $0$ and $4$ lack a single time direction, the two-time case excluded by importing the standard instability. That leaves six towers that admit matter: ours and five dark ones.

**Theorem 11 (Towers are residues, and the single-clock filter).** Clifford algebras repeat with period eight, and the paper reads the eight repeating families as eight parallel towers, each labelled by a number from 0 to 7. If the dimension may vary freely, every label can host a world with exactly one direction of time, so a premise is needed to pick one representative per label. Taking the dimension nearest four (premise P3), two labels are left without a single time direction, one with none and one with two, the latter excluded by importing the standard instability of two-time physics; the other six can hold matter: our world and five dark ones. Which member of a mirror pair is called our world depends on a sign convention for time that premise P2 declares to carry no physics.

The closest dark tower is the mirror, reached by reversing the signature, so that time and space directions swap roles. Every input of the Standard-Model chain is invariant under that reversal, so the mirror carries its own copy of the derived Standard Model.

With a common ceiling and under the single frozen point (P1) and indifference (P2), \[ \Omega_{\mathrm{mirror}} = \Omega_{b} \] to half a percent, where $\Omega_{b}$ is the ordinary (baryonic) matter density. A cold mirror is, however, an exception to that same indifference, which would otherwise make the mirror as hot as ours.

**Theorem 12 (The mirror tower is a copy of this one).** Swapping which directions count as time and which as space turns our world into a mirror world, and this result shows that every ingredient used to derive our particle physics survives the swap. So the mirror world has its own copy of the Standard Model as the series derives it (under Hypothesis (S)), with mirror photons and mirror neutrinos. If the two worlds share one cutoff scale (a common ceiling), then under the framework's premises of a single frozen point (P1) and indifference (P2), which fix a common coupling there and an equal matter excess, it also has the same particle masses and the same matter excess, so its matter equals our ordinary matter to within half a percent, the gap coming from the two worlds' nuclear histories. A later note (T14c) shows that the same indifference principle, applied as widely, would also make the mirror world as hot as ours, which observations rule out, so the cold mirror world the series relies on is an unstated exception to that principle.

A copy of our world raises the question of why we cannot see it.

Under P1, the sector decomposition and the wall-sequence theorem's hypotheses (a compact base and good couplings), the tower charge is exactly superselected, so dark-tower matter is exactly stable. That it only gravitates, at first order, comes from the series' acoustic no-go, conditional on the Indifference premise and premise (Q).

**Theorem 13 (Superselected, hence dark and exactly stable).** Assuming all the sectors share one spacetime metric set by a single frozen point (Premise P1), matter in the hidden sectors can affect us only through its weight, which is why it is dark; this part is carried over from earlier work, holds in the framework's spherically averaged description of the modes near horizons, and inherits two of that work's assumptions (the Indifference premise and premise (Q)). Given how the sectors are split, and provided the sheaf paper's conditions for its walls hold (a compact base and well-behaved couplings), this result argues that no process can change a particle's tower, so dark matter of this kind never decays; energy can still pass between the towers through gravity. The sectors met only once, at the universe's birth; a black hole can pass energy between them, but never a particle's sector label.

This gives the trail its first prediction.

Direct-detection searches should stay null permanently, because dark matter couples to ordinary matter through gravity alone at first order. The expectation is conditional on the single-frozen-point premise and on the acoustic no-go.

**Prediction 14 (Direct detection stays null).** Experiments that try to catch dark-matter particles bumping into ordinary atoms should never see anything. In this framework dark matter lives in separate towers that touch ours only through gravity, which is far too weak to register in such detectors. This expectation is conditional: it rests on the framework's premise of a single frozen point, and on a result carried over from earlier work that holds in a spherically averaged description and under two further assumptions of that work.

The next question is how much dark matter the towers hold. Under P1 to P4, with every tower's matter diluting as ours, the mirror supplies an asymmetric dark share of one $\Omega_{b}$ to half a percent.

That leaves a symmetric remainder of \[ 4.364\,\Omega_{b} \] which is not derived.

**Theorem 15 (The population count: one share to half a percent, and a symmetric remainder).** Astronomers find about 5.4 times as much dark matter as ordinary matter. Assuming the four premises P1 to P4 (a single frozen point, indifference, nearest-dimension representatives and a matter excess shared evenly across the oriented towers), and that every hidden sector's matter thins out with cosmic expansion as ours does, this result accounts for one of those units: a mirror copy of ordinary matter holding the same amount as ours to within half a percent. The other 4.4 units are not explained here; they would have to be a different, symmetric kind of matter in the other hidden sectors, felt by us only through gravity. The account is held at observation level on those premises.

A separate counting rule attempts the whole ratio. Under premises R1 to R3, each populated tower holds one quantum of a common mass per unit ball of its own space, so a tower with $D$-dimensional space has share $V_{3}/V_{D}$, with $V_{D}$ the volume of the unit ball.

Summed over the towers, the dark-to-baryon ratio is \[ \frac{11\pi + 16}{3\pi} \approx 5.3643, \] against the observed $5.364 \pm 0.065$.

**Theorem 16 (The unit-ball rule).** This rule assigns every dark tower one particle of a common mass per unit-sized ball of its own space, and compares that density with ours. Towers with fewer or more spatial dimensions then contribute different amounts, fixed by the volumes of balls in two, three and four dimensions. Under its three premises the rule gives a dark-to-ordinary matter ratio of about 5.364, the observed value; but the relation was found by solving for that value, and many equally simple formulas land inside the measurement's error, so the agreement is a fit rather than a prediction.

A close match calls for caution rather than celebration.

The relation was found by fitting, and it is one of fourteen equally simple values within the error bar of one Planck column. It rests on premises R1 to R3 and on the costed clause that empties residues $0$ and $4$, so it is recorded as a fit at observation level, not a prediction.

**Conjecture 17 (The status of the rule).** The paper's formula for the amount of dark matter matches the measured value closely, and this assessment explains why that match should not be over-read. The formula was found by working backwards from the measurement, and many equally simple formulas fit just as well. It rests on three unproved premises (R1 to R3), and on a further choice, made knowing the answer it needed, that two of the hidden families hold no matter; the match is to one Planck data set; another survey combination gives a lower value that no version of the formula can reach. So it is recorded as a fitted relation at observation level, not a prediction.

The counting rule says how much matter the dark towers hold, but not what that matter is made of. Repeating our own construction, mass begins at two constituents in dimensions three and four and at one constituent in dimension five.

Under premise H2 and Premise U the gauge algebra is \[ \so(2) \oplus \so(2) \] in the three-dimensional towers and $\uu(3) = \su(3) \oplus \uu(1)$ in the five-dimensional towers. Reading a constituent's momentum from its spinor is established in our tower but a premise in three and five dimensions (premise H1).

**Theorem 18 (The gauge algebras of the odd towers).** Assuming that a tower's forces are the symmetries of its smallest composite that can have mass, as in the Standard-Model paper, and a rule for carrying that construction to other towers, this theorem finds the forces of the odd towers. The three-dimensional towers get two independent forces like electromagnetism. The five-dimensional towers get a colour force like our strong force together with one force like electromagnetism, nine force carriers in all.

Abelian dark forces raise the question of dark radiation, which the early universe limits. The three-dimensional towers' radiation channels close, while the mirror and five-dimensional channels stay open.

The framework predicts \[ \Delta\Neff \approx 0 \] conditionally on those open channels closing, which holds if the descent's deposit is no hotter than estimated, and on a cold mirror, an exception to P2.

**Prediction 19 (No dark radiation).** The afterglow of the early universe records how many kinds of light, fast-moving particles existed at the time. The framework predicts that the dark towers add none, provided the remaining ways of heating them stay closed, which holds if the descent left them no hotter than the paper estimates. It also needs the mirror world to have started cold, which a later note (T14c) shows is an unstated exception to the framework's own indifference principle; applied evenly, that principle would make the mirror as hot as our world and the prediction would fail.

The last step puts the whole dark sector, as the series builds it, against the data.

At observation level no configuration of the five dark towers as built passes every test. The least exposed needs four choices the series does not make, and it stays $1.24$ above Roux and Cline's limit.

**Conjecture 20 (The dark towers as built, against the data).** At the level of observation, this table checks the five dark towers, as the series builds them, against radiation limits, colliding galaxy clusters, the mirror's astrophysics and the equal-mass rule. No arrangement passes every test as it stands. The best one needs four extra choices that the series does not make, and even then remains in mild tension with one published limit.

## Results cited, not shown

**Fact (imported) 21 (Imported: the eight towers and the mod-8 clock).** Climbing the ladder of algebras by adding one plus-square direction and one minus-square direction never changes the difference between the two counts. Each step replaces the algebra by two-by-two matrices with entries in the old one, so nothing essential changes. The possible starting points therefore sort into eight separate towers, according to that difference counted modulo eight.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.imported-eight-towers-mod-8-clock, version 4 (T14 Fact 2.5).

**Fact (imported) 22 (Gravity-only coupling at first order, restated from the acoustic no-go).** In the framework's spherically averaged (s-wave) description of fields near horizons, with an assembled four-dimensional lift, and under two assumptions of earlier papers in the series (one about how the horizon's spherical structure is built, one about the joint state of the near-horizon modes), the series showed that the structure from which spacetime's geometry is made can be gripped, at first order, only by probes that carry no charge and do not change sign under charge conjugation, which the series reads as energy and momentum. So matter that feels gravity but shares none of our other forces can affect us through that structure only by its weight, to first order. If, as the dark-matter paper's single-frozen-point premise says, that structure is its only contact with us, it has no other effect we could observe at that order.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.imported-gravity-only-coupling, version 3 (T14 Fact 2.6).

**Fact (imported) 23 (Imported: the inputs of the Standard-Model chain).** The Standard-Model results of the series come from a chain of derivations. This entry lists every ingredient that chain uses, from the six-dimensional conformal geometry and its spinors to the mass formula, a counting rule for hypercharge, one structural hypothesis about the slots of the composite's spinor states (Hypothesis (S)) and a few textbook results. The point is that nothing else goes in, so later work can ask which ingredients survive in other settings. That the list is complete is a reading, carried as an assumption.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.imported-inputs-standard-model-chain, version 6 (T14 Fact 4.2).

**Fact (imported) 24 (The mirror share is one, and how fragile that is).** The mirror world's matter weighs exactly as much as ours, because the mirror is an exact copy, with the same particles and the same force strengths at the start, and the copy theorem gives it the same matter excess, so the premise that the flag imprints one asymmetry on every tower (P4) is not needed for it. How exact that is depends on what the mirror's matter becomes; a later note (T14c) finds it mostly neutrons rather than helium at its coldest, a case this estimate does not cover. A rough one-loop estimate, over the ceiling range that rests on Hypothesis (U), shows that the equality is fragile: one extra kind of light quark or a slightly different starting strength of the strong force would change the mirror's share by large factors. For the odd-dimensional hidden worlds nothing of this kind can be said without a new rule.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.mirror-share-one-how-fragile, version 6 (T14 Prop 4.7).

**Fact (imported) 25 (No coupling rule for the odd towers follows from the series).** Saying how strong the forces are in an odd-dimensional hidden world, compared with ours, would need an extra rule. The series supplies none: the strength there carries units, so comparing it needs a length the theory does not fix; the series does not predict our own force strengths either; and its indifference premise (P2), the assumption that treats worlds alike, only links worlds that are mirror images. Any such rule would be a new assumption. The argument is at structural level, not a proof in the series' strict sense.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.no-coupling-rule-odd-towers-follows, version 4 (T14 Prop A.4).

**Fact (imported) 26 (Projection: every tower's content dilutes as this one's).** Hidden sectors with more or fewer dimensions than ours might thin out at a different rate as the universe expands, which would make them fade away or come to dominate. This result shows that, under the paper's rule for how such sectors feed gravity (its reading of Premise P1, one shared metric), provided any extra directions either grow with ours or stay fixed in size, and with a sector that lacks one of our directions counted as a family of evenly spaced sheets, their matter and radiation thin out exactly as ours do. Their share relative to ordinary matter therefore stays fixed over cosmic time.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones, version 3 (T14 Lem 6.1).

**Fact (imported) 27 (The radiation audit: what the type-by-residue theorem closes, and what stays open).** Each hidden sector could carry its own kind of light, and observations rule out much extra light in the early universe. This audit checks every sector: the three-dimensional ones lose their light either through a known confinement effect or, as a later note (T14c) shows for one of their two possible phases, because the force carrier gains a mass, while the mirror and five-dimensional ones could keep theirs. Any leftover massless particles in the confining sectors pick up small masses from those sectors' higher rungs, at a scale set under Hypothesis (U). The open sectors would still be harmless if they started out no hotter than the framework's estimate and their light is removed by one of the listed routes, for instance by their being cold enough; so the stance of no detectable extra radiation holds only under those conditions. The same later note points out that a cold mirror is an unstated exception to the paper's indifference principle.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what, version 7 (T14 Prop 6.2).

**Fact (imported) 28 (The residue is a soldered invariant, not an algebraic one).** Each tower of possible matter is labelled by a number, its residue, which comes from how many directions are time-like and how many space-like. This result shows that the label only has meaning once the algebra is tied to the geometry of spacetime. As bare algebras, some different towers are exactly the same at the same dimension, for example our world's algebra and that of one dark tower. What tells them apart is which elements count as directions, and that is fixed by the metric.

**Fact (imported) 29 (Type by residue, and what each tower can carry intrinsically).** Each tower's basic algebra decides what kind of numbers its matter states naturally use: real numbers, complex numbers or quaternions. That choice depends only on the tower's label, and it fixes which continuous symmetries a tower carries before any further structure is added. For the representatives the paper uses, and in its labelling convention (the opposite convention swaps the two members of each mirror pair), the mirror tower comes out with real spinors and no built-in continuous charge, two of the odd towers with exactly one built-in charge each, and our own world with quaternionic spinors.

**Fact (imported) 30 (Which towers can carry the arrow).** The excess of matter over antimatter needs an orientation, a choice the theory says can only be made in the towers with even labels. Among those, taken at the dimension nearest four (premise P3), only our world and the mirror tower have a single direction of time, so only they can carry the excess. A tower with two time directions could in principle host a copy of the Standard Model, carrying the same two conditions as the Standard-Model derivation, but the paper keeps it closed by importing the standard result that physics with two times is unstable.

**Fact (imported) 31 (The constituent's field in each tower).** Each of the eight towers has its own kind of number for its basic constituents: real numbers, complex numbers or quaternions. This theorem reads off which from the classification of real Clifford algebras: complex for our tower and its mirror, real for the three-dimensional towers and one sterile tower, quaternionic for the five-dimensional towers and the other sterile tower. The answer is the same in the larger conformal algebra and in the tower itself.

**Fact (imported) 32 (The momentum-preserving mixings of constituents).** Several constituents can be mixed into one another using the tower's own kind of number. This theorem shows that the mixings that keep their total momentum are exactly the unitary ones for that kind of number: rotations for real numbers, unitary matrices for complex numbers, and their quaternionic analogue for quaternions.

**Fact (imported) 33 (The ladder pairs of the causal towers).** Each constituent carries internal ladder pairs, pairs of creation and annihilation operations built from the directions of its conformal algebra. In the three-dimensional towers there are two, built from real numbers alone. In the four- and five-dimensional towers there are three, and the third can only be built with a square root of minus one; in our tower and the mirror that root is fixed up to sign.

In one of them the algebra itself provides exactly one such root, and every other candidate spoils the pair structure. In the other the algebra provides none, so any of a whole sphere of quaternionic roots will do, all equivalent to one another.

**Fact (imported) 35 (Where mass begins in each causal tower).** A single constituent in a three- or four-dimensional tower always moves like light; mass appears only when two combine, and then only if they are not aligned in a particular way. In five dimensions a single constituent can already be massive. In every tower with a time direction, collections of constituents never move faster than light.

**Fact (imported) 36 (The infinite wall sequence).** Provided the wall is compact and each repair is done gently enough (a good coupling at every stage), repairing a degenerate wall on one side creates a new wall a little further away, and that wall can be repaired in the same way. Repeating this forever gives an infinite chain of walls, each carrying an algebra built on two more dimensions than the last. The degeneracy is never destroyed, only pushed further from the physical region at each step.

**Prediction 37 (No indirect dark-sector signals).** Some searches look for the leftovers of dark-matter particles destroying each other or breaking down, such as unexpected gamma rays. This framework says dark matter can turn into ordinary particles only through gravity, far too weakly to be seen, and that there is almost no anti-dark matter for it to meet, so those searches should find nothing. This expectation is conditional: it rests on the framework's premise of a single frozen point, from which exact stability follows, and on a result carried over from earlier work for the gravity-only coupling, which holds in a spherically averaged description and under two further assumptions of that work.

Along the trail, the dark matter is the content of hidden towers felt only through gravity, with the mirror supplying one unit of it and the symmetric remainder left underived. As built, no arrangement of the dark towers yet passes every test against the data.

The trail rests on stated premises: a single frozen point (P1), indifference (P2), nearest-dimension representatives (P3), an evenly shared asymmetry (P4), Hypothesis (S) behind the mirror's copy, the counting premises R1 to R3, premises H1, H2 and U behind the odd towers' forces, and Hypothesis (U) behind the radiation audit. The predictions of null searches and no extra radiation are conditional on those premises, and a confirmed signal would count against them.
