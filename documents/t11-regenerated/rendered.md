**Theorem 7 (Invariant forms on a simple Lie algebra).** On a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over $\C$, any two nonzero invariant symmetric bilinear forms are proportional.

*Proof.* Let $B_{1}$ be the Killing form, nondegenerate by Cartan's criterion \cite[\S5.1]{Humphreys1972}, and $B_{2}$ invariant. Then $B_{2}(A, \cdot) = B_{1}(\phi A, \cdot)$ defines a linear $\phi$, and invariance of both forms makes $\phi$ commute with the adjoint action; the adjoint representation of a simple algebra is irreducible, so $\phi$ is a scalar by Schur's lemma \cite[\S6.1]{Humphreys1972}. ## The mass-weight dictionary





**Theorem 8 (The mass-weight dictionary).** Let $\sigma$ be a density of conformal weight $w$ on a vacuum Einstein background with frozen point $I$, and impose the coupling
\[
I \cdot D\sigma = 0.
\]
Then, in the scale of $I$, $\sigma$ obeys the Klein-Gordon equation $(\Box + m^{2})\sigma = 0$ with
\begin{equation}
m^{2} = -\,w(w+n-1)\,Q(I), \qquad\text{in } n = 4:\quad m^{2} = -\,w(w+3)\,\frac{\Lambda}{3} = -\,w(w+3)\,H^{2}.
\end{equation}
The mass of a field is its conformal weight, read in the units the frozen point provides, and every mass in the theory is $\sqrt{\Lambda}$ times a factor fixed by the weight.

*Proof.* Pair $I = (1, 0, \rho_{I})$ with $D\sigma$ (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-thomas-operator}) in the metric $\ip{(\sigma,\mu,\rho)}{(\sigma',\mu',\rho')} = \sigma\rho' + \rho\sigma' + g(\mu,\mu')$. The middle slots contribute nothing since $I$ has $\mu = 0$, leaving the two hyperbolic cross terms:
\[
I \cdot D\sigma = 1 \cdot \bigl(-(\Box\sigma - wJ\sigma)\bigr) + \rho_{I} \cdot w(n+2w-2)\,\sigma .
\]
Substituting $\rho_{I} = J/n$ from Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection} and collecting the $\sigma$-terms:
\[
I \cdot D\sigma = -\Box\sigma + wJ\sigma + \frac{J}{n}\,w(n+2w-2)\,\sigma
= -\Box\sigma + \frac{wJ}{n}\bigl[n + (n+2w-2)\bigr]\sigma
= -\Box\sigma + \frac{2wJ}{n}(n+w-1)\,\sigma .
\]
Setting this to zero gives $\Box\sigma = \tfrac{2wJ}{n}(n+w-1)\,\sigma$, that is $(\Box + m^{2})\sigma = 0$ with
\[
m^{2} = -\frac{2J}{n}\,w(n+w-1) = -\,w(w+n-1)\,Q(I),
\]
the last step by $Q(I) = 2J/n$ (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}). In $n = 4$ with $J = 2\Lambda/3$: $m^{2} = -w(w+3)\Lambda/3$. The script forms the pairing slot by slot from the two Facts, with $\sigma$ and $\Box\sigma$ as formal coordinates, and reads $m^{2}$ off the resulting Klein-Gordon equation, on a rational grid in $(w, n, J)$ and as an identity of rational functions in a formal $\Lambda$; as a consequence, the opposite sign fails the same pairing wherever $m^{2} \neq 0$, which the script also records.




**Theorem 9 (The weight polynomial and the two series).** In $n = 4$, with $\Delta := -w$ and $H^{2} = \Lambda/3 > 0$, the dictionary reads $m^{2} = \Delta(3-\Delta)H^{2}$, the de Sitter mass formula of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group}, whose two series are named below by that Fact; what is proved here is the algebra of $-w(w+3)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $m = 0$ exactly for $w \in \{0, -3\}$, the shadow pair $w \leftrightarrow -3-w$ preserving $m^{2}$;
\item the conformally coupled scalar $w = 1 - n/2 = -1$ has $m^{2} = 2H^{2}$; in general dimension $w = 1 - n/2$ gives $m^{2} = \tfrac{n(n-2)}{4}Q(I)$;
\item for real $w$, $m^{2} = \bigl[\tfrac94 - (w + \tfrac32)^{2}\bigr]H^{2} \le \tfrac94 H^{2}$, with equality at $w = -\tfrac32$; $m^{2} \ge 0$ exactly for $w \in [-3, 0]$, where $m$ runs over $[0, 3H/2]$, the complementary series $0 < m < 3H/2$ together with its two endpoints (the massless pair and the $\nu = 0$ member of the principal series); a real weight outside $[-3,0]$ gives $m^{2} < 0$, outside both series of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group} that this paper uses;
\item for $m > 3H/2$ the weight is complex,
\begin{equation}
w = -\frac32 \pm i\nu, \qquad \nu = \sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}} - \frac94}, \qquad \nu = \frac{m}{H} - \frac98\,\frac{H}{m} + O\!\Bigl(\frac{H^{3}}{m^{3}}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the principal series: the real part is universal and the mass lies in the imaginary part.
\end{enumerate}

*Proof.* (1) $w(w+3) = 0$ iff $w \in \{0,-3\}$, and $w(w+3)$ is invariant under $w \mapsto -3-w$. (2) At $w = 1 - n/2$, $w + n - 1 = n/2$ and $-w(w+n-1) = (n/2)(n/2 - 1) = n(n-2)/4$; for $n = 4$ this is $2$. Directly from the operator: at this weight the top two slots of $D\sigma$ vanish, $I \cdot D\sigma = -(\Box\sigma - wJ\sigma) = -(\Box\sigma + J\sigma)$, and $J = 2\Lambda/3$ gives $m^{2} = 2\Lambda/3$, which is the classical value of the conformal coupling $\tfrac16 R$ on de Sitter space in these conventions; this is the convention-independent reference for the sign of \claimref{mass.mass-weight-dictionary}. (3) Complete the square: $-w(w+3) = \tfrac94 - (w+\tfrac32)^{2}$, which is nonnegative iff $|w + \tfrac32| \le \tfrac32$ iff $w \in [-3,0]$; for instance $w = 1$ gives $m^{2} = -4H^{2}$, outside both series of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group}, which also names the series in (3) and (4). (4) Solve the quadratic $w^{2} + 3w + m^{2}/H^{2} = 0$; its discriminant $9 - 4m^{2}/H^{2}$ is negative exactly when $m > 3H/2$. The expansion of $\nu = (m/H)\sqrt{1 - \tfrac94 H^{2}/m^{2}}$ is checked by the script as an exact truncated power series in $\epsilon := H/m$: $\sqrt{1 - \tfrac94\epsilon^{2}} = 1 - \tfrac98\epsilon^{2} - \tfrac{81}{128}\epsilon^{4} + \cdots$. The script checks the two massless roots and the shadow symmetry $m^{2}(w) = m^{2}(-3-w)$ on instances, the closed form $\tfrac{n(n-2)}{4}Q(I)$ at $w = 1 - n/2$ for four values of $n$, the completed square as a polynomial identity together with the sign of $m^{2}$ at real weights inside and outside $[-3,0]$, and item (4) in Gaussian rationals.


**Remark  (Conventions and the sign).** The dictionary is written in the conventions of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}, where $Q(I) = +\Lambda/3$, and there it carries a minus sign in the invariant form. The physical content is convention-independent: $m^{2} = \Delta(3-\Delta)H^{2}$ with $\Delta = -w$ is the de Sitter mass formula of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group}, and the conformally coupled scalar of Corollary \claimref{mass.weight-polynomial-two-series}(2) fixes it. The sign in the invariant form and the sign of the norm are tied together by the computation of Theorem \claimref{mass.mass-weight-dictionary}, and neither carries content on its own. \cite[Rem.~4.9]{ButlerT6} records that the norm reads $+\Lambda$ in the present conventions, and why the opposite sign could not carry the de Sitter stabiliser; this paper's displays are all in the present conventions. What no convention changes is the statement that a positive mass above $3H/2$ requires a complex weight.

**Remark  (What the dictionary does and does not yield).** The natural conjecture, that coupling to the frozen point explains the observed masses, fails quantitatively, and the failure is the finding. The unit is $H \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-33}\,\mathrm{eV}$, so the fields with real weight in $[-3,0]$, the complementary series with its endpoints, end at $m = 3H/2$, below every known massive particle. Every known massive field is in the principal series, with $\mathrm{Re}\,w = -\tfrac32$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,w = \nu \approx m/H$, about $3 \times 10^{38}$ for the electron. The dictionary does not remove the hierarchy problem; it restates it: the hierarchy is the question of why the imaginary parts of the conformal weights of matter are so large in Hubble units. What the dictionary does yield: a single origin for mass and dark energy, both couplings to $I$; the de Sitter representation theory of matter as its $w$-classification, with the $-\tfrac32$ of the principal series read as the universal real part of every weight; and, together with the area formula of the section ``Mass as symplectic area'', a two-scale structure for the matter programme: the frozen point supplies the unit, the spinor bracket supplies the compositeness, and the internal symmetry of the multi-spinor system (many conformal spinors) is where the forces arise, taken up by the standard-model paper \cite{ButlerT12}.

**Remark  (Point of contact: the dictionary in plain words).** The universe, in this framework, has exactly one built-in scale: the length of the point at infinity, which is the cosmological constant under another name. The theorem says that mass is how strongly a field is coupled to that one scale: each field carries a number, its weight, and its mass is that number read against that single scale. A massless field is one whose weight gives zero mass against that scale. Against the data the natural conjecture fails: the scale is the Hubble scale, whose length is cosmological, so a field whose weight is an ordinary real number is lighter than anything ever observed, and everyday particles must carry weights whose imaginary part is extremely large. The mechanism does not explain why matter is so heavy compared to dark energy; it converts that question into a new one, which is the old hierarchy problem restated. The next section adds the other half of the account from the conformal spinor side, and the section ``The exact join'' joins the two halves.

## Mass as symplectic area



**setting  (Mass as symplectic area).** The dictionary reads mass off the frozen point. **Theorem 10 (Mass as symplectic area: the two-spinor formula).** On the real slice, where $\varepsilon^{A'B'}$ is the conjugate of $\varepsilon^{AB}$ and $\bar\pi$ the conjugate of $\pi$ (the conjugation reverses the complex unit $I_{6}$): a single conformal spinor (twistor) carries null momentum $p_{AA'} = \pi_{A}\bar\pi_{A'}$ and is massless, and a system of two conformal spinors with momentum
\[
p_{AA'} = \pi^{1}_{A}\bar\pi^{1}_{A'} + \pi^{2}_{A}\bar\pi^{2}_{A'}
\]
has
\begin{equation}
m^{2} = p^{2} = 2\,\bigl|\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}\bigr|^{2},
\end{equation}
the squared symplectic area between the two spinor parts. Aligned spinors (vanishing bracket) give a null composite, and mass grows with the misalignment.

*Proof.* Contract with the spinor metric: $p^{2} = \varepsilon^{AB}\varepsilon^{A'B'} p_{AA'}\, p_{BB'}$. Expanding the product gives four terms. The two diagonal terms vanish by antisymmetry, $\varepsilon^{AB}\pi^{i}_{A}\pi^{i}_{B} = 0$; for a single conformal spinor this is already the whole computation, $p^{2} = 0$. The two cross terms are equal (exchange the roles of the two spinors, which relabels $A \leftrightarrow B$ and $A' \leftrightarrow B'$, two transpositions, and leaves both epsilons unchanged), and each is
\[
\varepsilon^{AB}\pi^{1}_{A}\pi^{2}_{B}\;\cdot\;\varepsilon^{A'B'}\bar\pi^{1}_{A'}\bar\pi^{2}_{B'}
= \br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}\cdot\overline{\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}}
= \bigl|\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}\bigr|^{2}
\]
on the real slice, where $\varepsilon^{A'B'}$ is the conjugate of $\varepsilon^{AB}$ under the conjugation reversing $I_{6}$ and the primed bracket is therefore the conjugate of the unprimed one. Hence $p^{2} = 2|\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}|^{2}$. The result is classical \cite{Hughston1979}; it is reproved here because the join needs every sign. The contraction identities are checked by the script on exact Gaussian-rational spinor instances.


**Remark  (Point of contact: the angle between two vacua).** The correspondence paper proves that a conformal spinor (a twistor) is a state of the completion's three ladder pairs, a pure spinor, and that each conformal spinor determines a Fock vacuum \cite[Thm.~5.3]{ButlerT9}. Under that identification the bracket $\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}$ is the overlap datum of the two constituents' vacua, and Proposition \claimref{mass.mass-symplectic-area-two-twistor-formula} acquires its structural reading: a composite is massless exactly when its constituents agree on a vacuum, and mass is the area of their disagreement. Mass, in this reading, is not a quantity intrinsic to one constituent but a relation: a misalignment within the composite, on the conformal spinor side; a grip on infinity, on the tractor side. That these are the same number is the join of the next section. The two-twistor description of massive particles is classical twistor theory \cite{PenroseRindler1986, Hughston1979}, read here on conformal spinors; what the series adds is the identification of the ambient objects on both sides of the pairing, which is what lets the join be computed rather than postulated.

**Theorem 12 (The chart form of the spin isomorphism).** For $x = x^{aa'}e_{a}\wedge e_{a'}$ in the $x$-slot:

**Definition 13 (The grip, and the mass of a composite).** Let $I$ be a nonzero ambient vector and $Z^{1}, \dots, Z^{N} \in \CS$ a composite of conformal spinors (twistors). The \emph{grip} of the composite on $I$ is the ambient vector $P = M(I)$ determined by
\[
\ip{P}{U} = M_{U\wedge I} \qquad\text{for all ambient } U,
\]
which exists and is unique because the pairing is nondegenerate. The \emph{mass-squared} of the composite relative to $I$ is $m^{2} := Q(P)$.

**setting  (The grip of a composite on the frozen point).** Three properties follow at once. First, $\ip{P}{I} = M_{I\wedge I} = 0$: the grip lies in $I^{\perp}$. Second, $m^{2}$ is invariant under the stabiliser of $I$: for $\gamma$ with $\gamma I = I$, $\ip{P(\gamma Z)}{U} = M_{U\wedge I}(\gamma Z) = M_{\gamma^{-1}(U\wedge I)}(Z) = M_{\gamma^{-1}U\wedge I}(Z) = \ip{P(Z)}{\gamma^{-1}U} = \ip{\gamma P(Z)}{U}$, so $P(\gamma Z) = \gamma P(Z)$ and $Q(P)$ is unchanged; for the frozen point of Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point} the stabiliser acts on the ambient space through $SO(1,4)$ (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}; the Lie-algebra form of the surjection is Lemma \claimref{mass.five-broken-generators}), and $P$ is a de Sitter vector. Third, at $\Lambda = 0$, $I = n_{\infty}$ and $\ip{P}{x} = M_{x\wedge n_{\infty}}$ is, by Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(2), the value of the translation generator on the composite, that is, its total momentum in the direction $x$; the $x$-slot part of $P$ is therefore the momentum, the remaining part is $-d\,n_{\infty}$ with $d$ the dilation charge, and since $n_{\infty}$ is null and orthogonal to the $x$-slot, $m^{2}$ is the Poincar\'e Casimir $p^{2}$. Definition \claimref{obj.mass.grip-mass-composite} is the de Sitter extension of the mass of Proposition \claimref{mass.mass-symplectic-area-two-twistor-formula}: the composite's momentum is its grip on the point at infinity, and its mass is the length of that grip. Lemma \claimref{mass.five-broken-generators} below identifies the generators $A(e_{a}\wedge I)$, $e_{a} \in I^{\perp}$, whose charges are the components of $P$, with the five generators of $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)$ broken by $I$, the de Sitter translations, which is the representation-theoretic form of the same statement. That $P$ is a real vector, so that $Q(P)$ is a value of the real $(1,4)$ form on $I^{\perp}$, is the next lemma.



**Theorem 14 (The grip is real).** Let $V_{\R}$ be the real span of $n_{\infty}$, $n_{0}$ and the real slice \claimref{note.mass.what-nullity-fixes} of the $x$-slot. For $U, V \in V_{\R}$ the generator $A(U\wedge V)$ lies in $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)$, the real form of $\Sigma$, the reversion form of $\CS$ (the twistor form); hence $M_{U\wedge V}$ is real, and for $I \in V_{\R}$ the grip $P = M(I)$ lies in $V_{\R}$. For the frozen point $I_{\Lambda}$, $P$ is a real vector of $I_{\Lambda}^{\perp}$ and $m^{2} = Q(P)$ is a value of the $(1,4)$ form of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}.

*Proof.* Conventions: the chart's complex unit, written $i$ below, is $I_{6}$, the complex unit of the conformal spinor space $\CS$, and the bar is the conjugation that reverses it.

The first conclusion is the chart form of \cite[Prop.~4.5(2)]{ButlerT9} (\claimref{correspondence.reality-conjugation-twistor-form-factor}), which states that the real bivectors act $\Sigma$-anti-Hermitianly; here it is the chart computation, in four steps. The second conclusion is this paper's.

\emph{Step 1, the criterion.} In the block form of Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}, $\Sigma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so for $A = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \\ A_{21} & A_{22}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ the matrix $\Sigma A = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} A_{21} & A_{22} \\ A_{11} & A_{12}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is anti-Hermitian iff $A_{12}^{\dagger} = -A_{12}$, $A_{21}^{\dagger} = -A_{21}$ and $A_{22} = -A_{11}^{\dagger}$.

\emph{Step 2, the grading and the translations.} The grading element has $A_{11} = -\tfrac12$, $A_{22} = \tfrac12$, $A_{12} = A_{21} = 0$, and satisfies the criterion. For $x$ in the slice \claimref{note.mass.what-nullity-fixes}, $x = \tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}K\varepsilon$ with $K$ Hermitian, the translation block of Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(2) is $b_{x} = x\varepsilon = \tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}K\varepsilon^{2} = -\tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}K$, anti-Hermitian.

\emph{Step 3, the special conformal generators.} The block of Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(3) is $c_{x} = -x^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon = \tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}\,\varepsilon K^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon$ (using $\varepsilon^{\mathsf T} = -\varepsilon$), whose adjoint is $-\tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}\,\varepsilon^{\dagger}\bar{K}\varepsilon^{\dagger} = -\tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}\,\varepsilon K^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon = -c_{x}$, since $\bar K = K^{\mathsf T}$ and $\varepsilon^{\dagger} = -\varepsilon$: anti-Hermitian.

\emph{Step 4, the Lorentz generators.} For two rotations $R_{U\wedge V}(Z) = \ip{U}{Z}V - \ip{V}{Z}U$, expanding both sides on a vector $Z$ gives
\[
[R_{U\wedge V}, R_{W\wedge Y}] = \ip{U}{W}R_{V\wedge Y} - \ip{U}{Y}R_{V\wedge W} - \ip{V}{W}R_{U\wedge Y} + \ip{V}{Y}R_{U\wedge W}
\]
(the four terms are $R_{U\wedge V}R_{W\wedge Y}Z = \ip{W}{Z}\,R_{U\wedge V}Y - \ip{Y}{Z}\,R_{U\wedge V}W$ minus the same with the pairs exchanged, regrouped by which of $U, V, W, Y$ carries $Z$). For $U = x$, $V = n_{\infty}$, $W = x'$, $Y = n_{0}$ with $x, x'$ in the slice, $\ip{n_{\infty}}{x'} = \ip{x}{n_{0}} = Q(n_{\infty}) = 0$ and $\ip{n_{\infty}}{n_{0}} = 1$ leave $[R_{x\wedge n_{\infty}}, R_{x'\wedge n_{0}}] = \ip{x}{x'}R_{n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0}} + R_{x\wedge x'}$. Hence $A(x\wedge x') = [A(x\wedge n_{\infty}), A(x'\wedge n_{0})] - \ip{x}{x'}A(n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0})$ lies in $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)$, a Lie algebra, because $\ip{x}{x'}$ is real.

\emph{The span, and the grip.} The pairs $n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0}$ (one), $x\wedge n_{\infty}$ (four), $x\wedge n_{0}$ (four) and $x\wedge x'$ (six) span $\Lambda^{2}V_{\R}$, of dimension $15$, so every $A(U\wedge V)$ with $U, V \in V_{\R}$ is in $\mathfrak{su}(2,2)$, and $M_{A}$ is real there because $\Sigma A$ anti-Hermitian makes $\bar{Z}\cdot AZ$ imaginary. Then $\ip{P}{U} = M_{U\wedge I}$ is real for every $U \in V_{\R}$, and since the pairing restricted to $V_{\R}$ is a real nondegenerate form (the form of signature $(2,4)$ of Fact \claimref{mass.imported-flat-model-twistor-chart-infinity}; in the basis $n_{\infty}, n_{0}$ and the slice \claimref{note.mass.what-nullity-fixes} its Gram matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\right) \oplus \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$), $P \in V_{\R}$. The last clause is $P \perp I_{\Lambda}$ with Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}. The script checks the commutator identity on the real points, the anti-Hermiticity of the generators for real pairs on four real points, and the reality of the pairings of $P$ with a real basis of $V_{\R}$.


**setting  (The grip of a composite on the frozen point).** For two twistors, write $t_{il} := \hp{\pi^{i}}{\omega^{l}}$ ($i, l \in \{1,2\}$) for the $2 \times 2$ matrix of mixed pairings, and

**Theorem 15 (The exact join).** Let $I_{\Lambda} = n_{\infty} + \lambda n_{0}$ with $\lambda = \Lambda/6$ be the frozen point of Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point}, and $Z^{1}, Z^{2} \in \CS$ two conformal spinors (twistors).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(The deformed bracket.)} The pairing of the composite against the frozen point is
\begin{equation}
B_{\Lambda} := \ip{I_{\Lambda}}{Z^{1}\wedge Z^{2}} = \br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}} + \frac{\Lambda}{6}\,[\omega^{1}\omega^{2}],
\end{equation}
and at $\Lambda = 0$ it is the classical contraction of the composite with the point at infinity $n_{\infty}$, read as a bivector of $\CS$ (Penrose's infinity twistor).
\item \emph{(The mass.)} The grip $P = M(I_{\Lambda})$ has squared length
\begin{equation}
m^{2} = Q(P) = 2\,|B_{\Lambda}|^{2} + Q(I_{\Lambda})\, C = 2\,|B_{\Lambda}|^{2} + \frac{\Lambda}{3}\, C .
\end{equation}
\item \emph{(The flat shadow.)} At $\Lambda = 0$, $m^{2} = 2|\ip{I}{Z^{1}\wedge Z^{2}}|^{2} = 2|\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}|^{2}$: Proposition \claimref{mass.mass-symplectic-area-two-twistor-formula}, read as the squared pairing of the composite against the frozen point.
\item \emph{(The point-supported composite.)} If both spinors lie on the line $L_{x}$ of the point $x$, the composite bivector is $\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}X(x)$ and $B_{\Lambda} = \sigma(x)\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}} = \bigl(1 - \tfrac{\Lambda Q(x)}{12}\bigr)\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}$: the flat bracket read in the de Sitter scale.
\item \emph{(The massless locus.)} For $\Lambda > 0$, $m^{2} \ge 0$, and $m^{2} = 0$ if and only if $B_{\Lambda} = 0$ and $h$ is a multiple of the identity; that multiple is zero, so the condition is $B_{\Lambda} = 0$ and $h = 0$ (\claimref{synthesis.many-twistor-join}(v)). The doubled composite $Z^{2} = Z^{1}$ has $B_{\Lambda} = 0$ and $m^{2} = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\,(\bar{Z}\cdot Z)^{2}$, so it is massless only when its constituent is null; and there are massless composites that are not doubled, for instance $Z^{1} = e_{1} + e_{4}$, $Z^{2} = \alpha e_{1} + \beta e_{4}$ with $\alpha \neq \beta$.
\end{enumerate}

*Proof.* Conventions: the chart's complex unit, written $i$ below, is $I_{6}$, the complex unit of the conformal spinor space $\CS$, and the bar is the conjugation that reverses it; $\Som$ and $\Spi$ are the halves of $\CS$ spanned by $e_{1}, e_{2}$ and by $e_{3}, e_{4}$, the $E = -1$ and $E = +1$ eigenspaces.

Linearity in $I_{\Lambda}$ gives \claimref{mass.exact-join}; at $\Lambda = 0$ the frozen point is $n_{\infty}$ (the infinity twistor) (Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point}(3)).

(2) \emph{Slots of the grip.} $M_{U\wedge I}$ is linear in $U \wedge I$, so with $I_{\Lambda} = n_{\infty} + \lambda n_{0}$ the grip is $P = P_{\infty} + \lambda P_{0}$, where $P_{\infty} := M(n_{\infty})$ and $P_{0} := M(n_{0})$. Put $d := M_{n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0}}$, and for $x$ in the $x$-slot $p(x) := M_{x\wedge n_{\infty}}$, $k(x) := M_{x\wedge n_{0}}$, three linear forms in $x$. Then $\ip{P}{n_{\infty}} = M_{n_{\infty}\wedge I_{\Lambda}} = \lambda M_{n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0}} = \lambda d$, $\ip{P}{n_{0}} = M_{n_{0}\wedge n_{\infty}} = -d$, and $\ip{P}{x} = p(x) + \lambda k(x)$. Writing $P = \alpha n_{\infty} + \beta n_{0} + v$ with $v$ in the $x$-slot, $\alpha = \ip{P}{n_{0}} = -d$, $\beta = \ip{P}{n_{\infty}} = \lambda d$, and $\ip{v}{x} = p(x) + \lambda k(x)$ for all $x$. Hence
\[
Q(P) = 2\alpha\beta + Q(v) = -2\lambda d^{2} + Q(p) + 2\lambda\, p\cdot k + \lambda^{2}\, Q(k),
\]
where $f \cdot g$ denotes the dual metric on linear forms on the $x$-slot. By Remark \claimref{note.mass.what-nullity-fixes} the metric matrix on the $x$-slot, in the components $x^{aa'}$, is $G = -\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon$; since $\varepsilon^{2} = -1$, $G^{2} = (\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon)^{2} = \varepsilon^{2}\otimes\varepsilon^{2} = 1$, so $G^{-1} = G$ and $f\cdot g = -\varepsilon_{ab}\varepsilon_{a'b'}f_{aa'}g_{bb'}$ with the same numerical symbols. \emph{The three forms in components.} By Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(2), $A(x\wedge n_{\infty})Z = b_{x}\pi \in \Som$, so by the relation displayed with \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism} and $M_{A}(Z^{1}, \dots, Z^{N}) := i\sum_{i=1}^{N} \bar{Z}^{i}\cdot(A Z^{i}), \qquad A \in \mathfrak{sl}(\T)$, $p(x) = i\sum_{i}\hp{\pi^{i}}{b_{x}\pi^{i}} = i\sum_{i} x^{aa'}\,\overline{\pi^{i}_{a}}\,(\varepsilon\pi^{i})_{a'}$, where $(\varepsilon\pi)_{a'} := \varepsilon_{a'b'}\pi^{b'}$ and $\overline{\pi^{i}_{a}}$ is the conjugate of the component of $\pi^{i}$ paired with $e_{a}$ by $\Sigma$. By Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(3), $A(x\wedge n_{0})Z = c_{x}\omega \in \Spi$, so $k(x) = i\sum_{i}\overline{\hp{c_{x}\omega^{i}}{\omega^{i}}} = i\sum_{i}\sum_{a'}(c_{x}\omega^{i})^{a'}\,\overline{\omega^{i}_{a'}} = -i\sum_{i}x^{ba'}\varepsilon_{ba}\omega^{i\,a}\,\overline{\omega^{i}_{a'}}$; thus $k = \sum_{i}k^{i}$ with $k^{i}_{aa'} = -i\,(\varepsilon\omega^{i})_{a}\,\overline{\omega^{i}_{a'}}$, where $(\varepsilon\omega)_{a} := \varepsilon_{ab}\omega^{b}$. By Lemma \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}(1), $A(n_{\infty}\wedge n_{0})Z = (-\tfrac12\omega, \tfrac12\pi)$, so $\bar{Z}\cdot AZ = -\tfrac12\hp{\pi}{\omega} + \tfrac12\overline{\hp{\pi}{\omega}} = -i\,\mathrm{Im}\hp{\pi}{\omega}$ and $d = \sum_{i}\mathrm{Im}\,t_{ii} = \mathrm{Im}\tr t$.

\emph{The three pairings.} Using $\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon = 1$, $\varepsilon^{2} = -1$ and $\det\varepsilon = 1$ for the $2\times 2$ symbol:
\begin{align*}
p^{i}\cdot p^{l} &= -\varepsilon_{ab}\varepsilon_{a'b'}\,(i)^{2}\,\overline{\pi^{i}_{a}}\,\overline{\pi^{l}_{b}}\,(\varepsilon\pi^{i})_{a'}(\varepsilon\pi^{l})_{b'}
= \overline{\br{\pi^{i}\pi^{l}}}\;\br{\varepsilon\pi^{i},\varepsilon\pi^{l}} = |\br{\pi^{i}\pi^{l}}|^{2},\\

**Theorem 16 (The Casimir form of the join).** Fix a composite $Z^{1}, \dots, Z^{N}$ of conformal spinors with Hermitian Gram matrix $h_{il} = \bar{Z}^{i}\cdot Z^{l}$. \begin{enumerate}
\item $Q(P) = Q(I)\,\bigl(C_{2}(\Lambda^{2}\CS) - C_{2}(I^{\perp})\bigr)$;
\item $C_{2}(\Lambda^{2}\CS) = \tr(h^{2}) - \tfrac14(\tr h)^{2}$, for any number of constituents;
\item hence, for two conformal spinors, $I = I_{\Lambda}$ and $\Lambda \neq 0$, the de Sitter Casimir of the composite is
\[
C_{2}(I_{\Lambda}^{\perp}) = \tfrac14(\tr h)^{2} + C - \frac{6\,|B_{\Lambda}|^{2}}{\Lambda}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Consequently (1) says that the grip $Q(P)$ equals the de Sitter mass $-Q(I)\,C_{2}(I^{\perp})$ plus $Q(I)\,C_{2}(\Lambda^{2}\CS)$, and the two coincide only where the conformal Casimir vanishes.

*Proof.* Conventions: the chart's complex unit, written $i$ below, is $I_{6}$, the complex unit of the conformal spinor space $\CS$, and the bar is the conjugation that reverses it.

(1) Take a basis $e_{1}, \dots, e_{5}$ of $I^{\perp}$ and $e_{6} = I$; then $g^{a6} = 0$ for $a \le 5$ and $g^{66} = 1/Q(I)$. **Theorem 17 (The five broken generators).** Let $\Omega_{I}(u,v) := \ip{I}{u\wedge v}$ be the $2$-form on the conformal spinor space $\CS$ (twistor space) of an ambient vector $I$; it is nondegenerate iff $I$ is non-simple iff $Q(I) \neq 0$. On the real form, for the frozen point with $\Lambda > 0$, the stabiliser is $\mathfrak{so}(1,4)$ and the five are the de Sitter translations, the $I$-half of the nine Goldstone modes of the action paper \cite[Prop.~5.4]{ButlerT10}.

*Proof.* The components of $\Omega_{I}$ are $\varepsilon(I\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{l})$, the Hodge dual of $I$, and its Pfaffian is a nonzero multiple of $\varepsilon(I\wedge I) = Q(I)$; so $\Omega_{I}$ is nondegenerate iff $Q(I) \neq 0$, which is the non-simplicity of $I$ (Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point}(3)). For $A \in \mathfrak{gl}(\CS)$ put $\beta_{A}(u,v) := \Omega_{I}(u, Av) = u^{\mathsf T}\Omega_{I}Av$; since $\Omega_{I}^{\mathsf T} = -\Omega_{I}$, $\Omega_{I}(Au,v) = -\beta_{A}(v,u)$, so $\Omega_{I}(Au,v) + \Omega_{I}(u,Av) = \beta_{A}(u,v) - \beta_{A}(v,u)$, and $A$ preserves $\Omega_{I}$ iff $\beta_{A}$, that is the matrix $\Omega_{I}A$, is symmetric. By invariance of the pairing under the action of $\mathfrak{sl}(\CS)$ (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-flat-model-twistor-chart-infinity}), $\ip{I}{A\cdot(u\wedge v)} = -\ip{A\cdot I}{u\wedge v}$, so $\Omega_{I}(Au,v) + \Omega_{I}(u,Av) = -\Omega_{A\cdot I}(u,v)$: preserving $\Omega_{I}$ is the same as stabilising $I$, and the antisymmetric part of $\beta_{A}$ is $-\tfrac12\Omega_{A\cdot I}$. Symmetry of a $4\times 4$ matrix is six conditions, so the stabiliser is $10$-dimensional; it is $\mathfrak{sp}(\Omega_{I}) \cong \mathfrak{so}(I^{\perp})$, whose elements are traceless. Antisymmetry is ten conditions, leaving the $6$-dimensional space $\{A : \Omega_{I}A \text{ antisymmetric}\}$, on which $A \mapsto A\cdot I$ is injective (an antisymmetric $\beta_{A}$ equal to $-\tfrac12\Omega_{A\cdot I}$ vanishes iff $A\cdot I = 0$, and then $A = 0$ by nondegeneracy), hence a bijection onto $\Lambda^{2}\CS$; $\mathfrak{gl}(\CS)$ is the direct sum of the two parts. The identity lies in the antisymmetric part ($\beta_{1} = \Omega_{I}$) and has trace $4$, so the trace cuts that part to a $5$-dimensional complement of the stabiliser inside $\mathfrak{sl}(\CS)$.

For $U \in I^{\perp}$, $A(U\wedge I)$ is not in the stabiliser, since $R_{U\wedge I}(I) = \ip{U}{I}I - Q(I)U = -Q(I)U \neq 0$, and $U \mapsto A(U\wedge I)$ is linear and injective on $I^{\perp}$. Its image lies in the antisymmetric part by equivariance: under the stabiliser $\mathfrak{so}(I^{\perp})$, $I^{\perp}$ is the $5$-dimensional vector representation and the stabiliser itself is the $10$-dimensional adjoint representation, irreducible and non-isomorphic, while $\mathfrak{gl}(\CS) = \mathfrak{sp}(\Omega_{I}) \oplus \{\text{antisymmetric part}\}$ is a decomposition into $\mathfrak{so}(I^{\perp})$-submodules (both conditions are invariant under conjugation by the stabiliser); the map $U \mapsto A(U\wedge I)$ is $\mathfrak{so}(I^{\perp})$-equivariant, so by Schur's lemma its component in $\mathfrak{sp}(\Omega_{I})$ vanishes. Since the five images are traceless (all of $A(\cdot)$ is), they span the $5$-dimensional traceless antisymmetric part. The real-form statement is Fact \claimref{mass.imported-frozen-point-tractor-connection}'s stabiliser together with the count $15 - 10 = 5$; the action paper's Proposition 5.4 counts nine broken generators for the pair $(I, V)$, of which the five directions of $I^{\perp}$ are the $I$-half.
