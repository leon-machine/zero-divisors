# Rule 2 Exercised Physically: Kaluza-Klein directions, planar fermions, and the commuting square: Companion note to T17: closing Direction 7 of the forward ledger (regenerated from the store)

**Abstract.** The capstone's forward ledger leaves one direction unowned: a physical system whose natural algebra carries a central splitting, so that Rule 2 of the transport principle, ideal fusion by one adjoined generator, is exercised by nature rather than by the classification table. This note supplies two, and the theorem behind both. Of the two one-dimensional extensions of an even-dimensional nondegenerate Clifford algebra exactly one carries the type-C obstruction, spacelike when $p - q \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and timelike when $p - q \equiv 0$, the other having complex type; each ideal of the obstructed extension is a copy of the base; and the Rule 2 repair by a generator of the opposite sign is exactly the Rule 1 ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$: the two adjoined directions span its hyperbolic plane and carry its ladder pair, and the even half that Rule 2 amplifies is the base algebra itself. Applied to the physical lineage: the five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein algebra $\Cl(1,4)$ splits, its two ideals are the two inequivalent five-dimensional Dirac representations, the fifth-direction reflection is the outer exchange, and the fusion is the conformal algebra $\Cl(2,4)$ or its spatial twin $\Cl(1,5)$, the bit the algebra forgets being whether the parity becomes a boost or a rotation. Applied to the plane: $\Cl(2,1)$ splits into the two two-component representations, the four-component fermion of planar electrodynamics and of graphene is the fused algebra, and the parity anomaly is the obstruction's physical face. Twenty-four exact checks verify every identity.

## Setting, and what is imported



**setting  (Setting, and what is imported).** The transport principle sorts the zero divisors of a real Clifford algebra into three types and repairs each by a minimal ascent \cite{ButlerT1}. Type C is the deepest: not a direction without a partner but a splitting of the algebra itself into two ideals, along the central idempotents $\varepsilon_\pm = \tfrac12(1 \pm \omega)$ built from the volume element, which no inner automorphism can exchange. Its repair, Rule 2, adjoins one generator, fuses the ideals into a simple algebra, and renders inner the very symmetry whose absence defined the obstruction. The capstone \cite{ButlerT17} records that every other direction of the programme has an owner among the series' papers, and that this one does not: no paper of the series exhibits a physical system whose natural algebra is split, with Kaluza-Klein extensions the named candidates. This note takes the row.

The claim is modest and the standard is the series'. What is proved here is algebra: where the obstruction sits among the one-dimensional extensions of an even-dimensional algebra, what its ideals are, and what its repair is. What is physical is imported as Facts with provenance: that odd-dimensional gamma matrices come in two inequivalent representations, that a single planar fermion breaks parity through an induced Chern-Simons term while a doubled one does not, that graphene's low-energy theory is that doubled fermion, that a five-dimensional fermion shifts a Chern-Simons level by half the sign of its mass. The note's contribution is the identification: those two representations are the two ideals, the parity that relates them is the outer exchange, the doubling is the fusion, and the fused algebra of the physical lineage is the conformal algebra the series has used since its root paper. the section ``What has been exercised, and what has not'' says exactly what has been exercised and what has not.

Notation follows the root paper: $\Cl(p,q)$ has $p$ generators of square $+1$ and $q$ of square $-1$, $n = p + q$, $d = p - q \bmod 8$ is the clock residue, $\omega = e_1 \cdots e_n$ is the volume element, $\Cl^0$ is the even subalgebra, and $\hat{a}$ is the grade involution. The series labels this world's algebra $\Cl(1,3)$ (time of square $+1$), residue $6$; the dark-matter paper explains why the labelling is a convention and what changes hands under its reversal \cite{ButlerT14}, and Corollary \claimref{rule2.odd-lorentzian-dimension-exactly-one-real} below is where that reversal bites. Below, ``spacelike'' and ``timelike'' name generators of square $-1$ and $+1$ respectively, the series' convention; in the mirror's convention the names swap and the mathematics does not.

## One-dimensional extensions of an even-dimensional algebra



**setting  (One-dimensional extensions of an even-dimensional algebra).** The physical algebras of the series are even-dimensional and simple. A Kaluza-Klein extension adjoins one direction, and the algebra becomes odd-dimensional, where the volume element is central and the type-C question is live. The first result places the obstruction exactly.



**Theorem 5 (The even subalgebra of a one-dimensional extension).** Let $\nu$ be a generator adjoined orthogonally to $\Cl(p,q)$. If $\nu^2 = -1$ then $e_i \mapsto e_i\nu$ extends to an isomorphism $\Cl(p,q) \cong \Cl^0(p,q{+}1)$; if $\nu^2 = +1$ then the same map gives $\Cl(q,p) \cong \Cl^0(p{+}1,q)$.

*Proof.* The images are even. Their squares are $(e_i\nu)^2 = e_i\nu e_i\nu = -e_i^2\nu^2$, which is $e_i^2$ for $\nu^2 = -1$ and $-e_i^2$ for $\nu^2 = +1$; for $i \neq j$, $(e_i\nu)(e_j\nu) = -e_ie_j\nu^2$ and $(e_j\nu)(e_i\nu) = -e_je_i\nu^2 = e_ie_j\nu^2$, so distinct images anticommute. By the universal property (\claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) the assignment extends to a unital homomorphism from $\Cl(p,q)$ (respectively $\Cl(q,p)$) into the even subalgebra. It is surjective: for $i \neq j$, $(e_i\nu)(e_j\nu) = -\nu^2 e_ie_j = \mp e_ie_j$, so every even blade of the extension not involving $\nu$ is, up to sign, a product of images, and every even blade involving $\nu$ is $e_I\nu$ with $|I|$ odd, which is $\pm(e_{i_1}\nu)(e_{i_2}\nu)(e_{i_3}\nu)\cdots$ after pairing all but the last factor. Both sides have dimension $2^n$, so the surjection is an isomorphism. The script confirms the relations, the evenness, and the dimension on $(1,3)$ and $(2,0)$ with both signs of $\nu^2$.




**Theorem 6 (Where the obstruction sits, and the commuting square).** Let $A = \Cl(p,q)$ with $n = p + q$ even, and let $A_s = \Cl(p,q{+}1)$ and $A_t = \Cl(p{+}1,q)$ be its spacelike and timelike one-dimensional extensions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Exactly one of $A_s$, $A_t$ carries the type-C obstruction: $A_s$ if $p - q \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, $A_t$ if $p - q \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. The other has complex type: its volume element is central with square $-1$, its centre is $\C$, and it is simple.
\item[(b)] Each of the two ideals of the obstructed extension is a copy of $A$: $\varepsilon_\pm A_s \cong \Cl^0(p,q{+}1) \cong A$ in the first case, $\varepsilon_\pm A_t \cong \Cl^0(p{+}1,q) \cong \Cl(q,p) \cong A$ in the second (the last isomorphism because the residues $0$ and $4$ are fixed by negation mod $8$).
\item[(c)] The Rule 2 repair of the obstructed extension by a generator of the opposite sign is $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$, the Rule 1 ascent of $A$: the two adjoined generators $e_{n+1}$ (the extension) and $\nu$ (the repair) span a hyperbolic plane, the null vectors $f = \tfrac12(\nu \pm e_{n+1})$, $f^* = \tfrac12(\nu \mp e_{n+1})$ (signs chosen so that each is null) are the ladder pair of Fact \claimref{transport.rule-1-morita-trivial}, and the two amplifications agree: $\Mat_2(\Cl^0(\text{extension})) = \Mat_2(A)$. The residue returns to $d$.
\item[(d)] The Rule 2 repair by a generator of the same sign is $\Cl(p,q{+}2)$ or $\Cl(p{+}2,q)$, isomorphic to $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ as an algebra, at residue $d \mp 2$; the adjoined plane is definite, carries no null vector, and admits no Rule 1 reading.
\end{enumerate}

*Proof.* (a) By Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-classification-centre-type-c}, an odd-dimensional algebra is type C exactly when $p' - q' \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and complex type exactly when $p' - q' \equiv 3 \pmod 4$; these exhaust the odd residues. For $A_s$, $p' - q' = p - q - 1$, which is $\equiv 1$ iff $p - q \equiv 2$ and $\equiv 3$ iff $p - q \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. For $A_t$, $p' - q' = p - q + 1$, which is $\equiv 1$ iff $p - q \equiv 0$ and $\equiv 3$ iff $p - q \equiv 2$. Since $n$ is even, $p - q$ is even, so exactly one case obtains for each extension and the two extensions fall on opposite sides. Simplicity of the complex-type algebra: its centre $\C$ is a field, and a semisimple algebra with a field for centre has one Wedderburn block.

(b) The corner isomorphism $\varepsilon_+ A' \cong \Cl^0(A')$ is Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}, and $\varepsilon_- A' \cong \varepsilon_+ A'$ through the grade involution, which exchanges the ideals. Lemma \claimref{rule2.even-subalgebra-one-dimensional-extension} identifies $\Cl^0(p,q{+}1) \cong \Cl(p,q)$ and $\Cl^0(p{+}1,q) \cong \Cl(q,p)$. In the timelike case, $p - q \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ gives $q - p \equiv -(p-q) \pmod 8$, and the classification of Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-classification-centre-type-c} assigns $\Cl(q,p)$ and $\Cl(p,q)$ the same type and dimension when $p - q \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ (residues $0$ and $4$ are each fixed by negation mod 8), so $\Cl(q,p) \cong \Cl(p,q)$ there (the script exercises the timelike form of Lemma \claimref{rule2.even-subalgebra-one-dimensional-extension} on $(1,3)$ and $(2,0)$).

(c) The spacelike case: the repair generator $\nu$ has $\nu^2 = +1$ and the extension generator $e_{n+1}$ has square $-1$, so the Gram matrix of the pair is $\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1)$, of signature $(1,1)$: a hyperbolic plane. Then $f = \tfrac12(\nu + e_{n+1})$ satisfies $f^2 = \tfrac14(\nu^2 + e_{n+1}^2 + \nu e_{n+1} + e_{n+1}\nu) = 0$, likewise $f^* = \tfrac12(\nu - e_{n+1})$, and $ff^* + f^*f = \tfrac14\bigl((\nu + e_{n+1})(\nu - e_{n+1}) + (\nu - e_{n+1})(\nu + e_{n+1})\bigr) = \tfrac12(\nu^2 - e_{n+1}^2) = 1$. The quadratic space is $\R^{p,q} \perp \langle e_{n+1}\rangle \perp \langle \nu \rangle = \R^{p+1,q+1}$ whichever order the two directions are adjoined in, so the algebra is $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ on the nose, and Fact \claimref{transport.rule-1-morita-trivial} reads it as $\Mat_2(A)$ through the ladder pair, while Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} reads it as $\Mat_2(\Cl^0(A_s))$ through $\varepsilon_\pm$ and $\nu$; by (b) the two coefficient algebras are the same algebra. The residue of $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ is $p - q$. The timelike case is the same computation with the signs of $\nu^2$ and $e_{n+1}^2$ exchanged and $f = \tfrac12(e_{n+1} + \nu)$, $f^* = \tfrac12(e_{n+1} - \nu)$.

(d) Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} gives the isomorphism of the two repairs and the residues $d' \pm 1$ of the fused algebras with $d' = d \mp 1$ the residue of the extension; the same-sign repair has residue $d \mp 2$. The adjoined plane has Gram matrix $\pm\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1)$, definite, so $(a e_{n+1} + b\nu)^2 = \pm(a^2 + b^2) \neq 0$ for $(a,b) \neq 0$: no null vector, hence no ladder pair, hence no reading of the repair as a $(1,1)$ ascent of anything.

The script verifies (a) on every even-dimensional $(p,q)$ with $2 \leq n \leq 6$ by computing the volume element's centrality and square directly, and verifies (c) and (d) on the same grid: the obstructed extension is type C, the opposite-sign repair sits at residue $d$ with a hyperbolic adjoined plane carrying a ladder pair, and the same-sign repair sits two residues away with a definite plane.

**Theorem 8 (Odd Lorentzian dimension: exactly one real form is split).** Let $n$ be odd and consider the two real forms of the Lorentzian algebra in dimension $n$, $\Cl(1,n{-}1)$ (time of square $+1$) and $\Cl(n{-}1,1)$ (time of square $-1$). Then $\Cl(1,n{-}1)$ is type C if and only if $n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $\Cl(n{-}1,1)$ is type C if and only if $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and in each case the other real form has complex type. The two real forms have the same complexification, which is split for every odd $n$: over $\C$ the element $\omega' = \omega$ or $i\omega$, whichever has square $+1$, is central, and $\tfrac12(1 \pm \omega')$ are its central idempotents. Thus the obstruction is present in every odd Lorentzian dimension; the real form decides whether it is visible as a splitting or hidden as a central complex structure.

*Proof.* $\Cl(1,n{-}1)$ is the spacelike extension of $\Cl(1,n{-}2)$, an even-dimensional algebra with $p - q = 3 - n$, which is $\equiv 2 \pmod 4$ iff $n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $\equiv 0$ iff $n \equiv 3$; Theorem \claimref{rule2.where-obstruction-sits-commuting-square}(a) gives the first claim and the complex type of the other case. $\Cl(n{-}1,1)$ is the extension of $\Cl(n{-}2,1)$, $p - q = n - 3$, by a generator of square $+1$, obstructed iff $n - 3 \equiv 0$, i.e.\ $n \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, complex type otherwise. For the complexification: over $\C$ the substitution $e_i \mapsto ie_i$ on the generators of square $-1$ turns the relations of $\Cl(p,q)$ into those of $\Cl(n,0)$, so $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \C$ depends only on $n$ (\claimref{std-algebra.complexification}); the volume element remains central, and if $\omega^2 = -1$ then $(i\omega)^2 = +1$, so $\omega'$ as defined is a central involution and $\tfrac12(1 \pm \omega')$ are orthogonal central idempotents summing to $1$. The script checks the alternation, with its complex-type complement, in dimensions $3, 5, 7, 9, 11$.


**Remark  (The three dimensions that matter below).** $n = 5$: $\Cl(1,4)$ is split, $\Cl(4,1)$ complex type. $n = 3$: $\Cl(2,1)$ is split, $\Cl(1,2)$ complex type. $n = 11$: $\Cl(10,1)$ is split, $\Cl(1,10)$ complex type. In the series' own convention the five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein extension of this world is the split one; the planar and eleven-dimensional cases are split in the mirror's convention. Physics done with complex gamma matrices sees the splitting in every odd dimension, as the corollary says it must; what the real form governs is the reality of the spinors, Remark \claimref{note.rule2.eleven-dimensions-reality-spinors}.

## The five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein algebra



**setting  (The five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein algebra).** Take the physical algebra $\Cl(1,3)$ with generators $e_0$ (square $+1$) and $e_1, e_2, e_3$ (square $-1$), spacetime pseudoscalar $\gamma = e_0e_1e_2e_3$ with $\gamma^2 = -1$, and adjoin one spacelike direction $e_4$, $e_4^2 = -1$: the algebra $\Cl(1,4)$ of a five-dimensional spacetime with one extra spatial dimension, which is what a Kaluza-Klein reduction on a circle sees pointwise.



**Fact (imported) 9 (Imported: gamma matrices in odd dimension).** In odd spacetime dimension $n$ the complex Clifford algebra has exactly two inequivalent irreducible representations, each of dimension $2^{(n-1)/2}$, related by $\Gamma^M \mapsto -\Gamma^M$ for all $M$ and distinguished by the scalar value of the product $\Gamma^0\Gamma^1\cdots\Gamma^{n-1}$, which is $\pm$ a fixed phase; equivalently, the last gamma matrix is $\pm$ a fixed multiple of the product of the first $n - 1$. \emph{Provenance:} standard; \cite[App.~B]{Polchinski1998}, \cite[\S 3]{VanProeyen1999}.

(ii) The map $a \mapsto \varepsilon_+ a$ is a homomorphism because $\varepsilon_+$ is a central idempotent. Injectivity: $\varepsilon_+ a = 0$ means $a = \omega a$; but for $a \in \Cl(1,3)$ every blade of $a$ omits $e_4$ while every blade of $\omega a = \gamma e_4 a$ contains it, so $a = \omega a$ forces $a = 0$. Both $\Cl(1,3)$ and the ideal have dimension $16$ (the ideal is $\Cl^0(1,4)$ by Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}), so the map is onto. For the fifth generator, $e_4 = \gamma^{-1}\omega = -\gamma\omega$, so $\varepsilon_+ e_4 = -\gamma\varepsilon_+\omega = -\gamma\varepsilon_+$ since $\omega\varepsilon_+ = \varepsilon_+$, and $\varepsilon_- e_4 = -\gamma\varepsilon_-\omega = +\gamma\varepsilon_-$ since $\omega\varepsilon_- = -\varepsilon_-$. Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-gamma-matrices-odd-dimension} identifies the two assignments $\Gamma^4 = \mp\Gamma^0\Gamma^1\Gamma^2\Gamma^3$ as the two inequivalent representations, and $\Cl(1,3) \cong \Mat_2(\HH)$ (\claimref{std-algebra.sta-matrix}) has the one irreducible module $\HH^2$, the Dirac spinor. (iii) $\Ad_{e_4}$ negates $e_0, \ldots, e_3$ (each anticommutes with $e_4$, \claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}) and fixes $e_4$; the grade involution negates all five; the composite fixes $e_0, \ldots, e_3$ and negates $e_4$, which is $R$ on generators, hence on the algebra. It negates $\omega$ (one generator negated) and so exchanges $\varepsilon_\pm$; an inner automorphism fixes the centre pointwise, so $R$ is outer. (iv) is Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} with Lemma \claimref{rule2.even-subalgebra-one-dimensional-extension} for $\Cl^0(1,4) \cong \Cl(1,3)$, and $\Mat_2(\Mat_2(\HH)) = \Mat_4(\HH)$. (v) $\Ad_\nu = \hat{\cdot}$ on $A_5$ is Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}, and $\Ad_{\nu e_4} = \Ad_\nu \circ \Ad_{e_4} = \hat{\cdot} \circ \Ad_{e_4} = R$ on $A_5$ by (iii). $(\nu e_4)^2 = -\nu^2 e_4^2 = \nu^2$, which is $+1$ for $\Cl(2,4)$ and $-1$ for $\Cl(1,5)$; a bivector of square $+1$ generates a boost, of square $-1$ a rotation. (vi) is Theorem \claimref{rule2.where-obstruction-sits-commuting-square}(c), (d) at $(p,q) = (1,3)$, with Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-completion-spacetime-algebra-spinors} naming the algebra. **Fact (imported) 11 (Imported: the five-dimensional Chern-Simons shift).** A five-dimensional Dirac fermion of mass $m$ charged under a gauge field, integrated out, shifts the coefficient of the five-dimensional Chern-Simons term by $\tfrac12\sgn(m)$ (times the representation's cubic index for a non-abelian field); the sign is odd under the reflection of one spatial direction, so that the parity-violating term is present for a single species and absent for a pair whose contributions cancel. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{Witten1996, IMS1997}.

*Provenance:* E. Witten, Phase transitions in M-theory and F-theory, Nucl. Phys. B 471 (1996) 195--216.; K. Intriligator, D. R. Morrison, N. Seiberg, Five-dimensional supersymmetric gauge theories and degenerations of Calabi-Yau spaces, Nucl. Phys. B 497 (1997) 56--100..

**Remark  (What the Kaluza-Klein obstruction is, physically).** Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(ii) says that a five-dimensional fermion cannot avoid choosing an ideal: to write its Dirac operator one picks $\Gamma^4 = -\gamma$ or $+\gamma$, and no change of basis undoes the choice. Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iii) says that the five-dimensional parity exchanges the choices; Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-five-dimensional-chern-simons-shift} says that the choice, together with the sign of the mass, is physical, carried by the induced Chern-Simons level. The obstruction is therefore not a formality of the real classification table: it is the statement that a five-dimensional fermion with a mass is parity-violating in a way its four-dimensional restriction is not, and that the violation is labelled by the ideal. Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iv)-(v) says what repairs it: a sixth direction, in which the two species become the $\varepsilon_+$ and $\varepsilon_-$ halves of one $\Mat_4(\HH)$-module, exchanged by the inner $\Ad_{\nu e_4}$. That is the algebraic form of the familiar statement that a six-dimensional spinor reduces to a five-dimensional fermion in each of the two representations, and that the pair is parity-invariant. Which sixth direction is Remark \claimref{note.rule2.forgotten-bit-read}.

**Remark  (The forgotten bit, read).** Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} says the fused algebra forgets the sign of $\nu^2$. Here the bit has a name on each side. With $\nu^2 = +1$ the repair is $\Cl(2,4)$: the sixth direction is a second time, the pair $(\nu, e_4)$ is the hyperbolic plane of the conformal completion, the parity of the fifth direction is a boost in that plane, and the fused algebra is the one whose spinors are the conformal spinors $\CS$ (twistors) (Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-completion-spacetime-algebra-spinors}); the Kaluza-Klein direction and the conformal completion's null pair are the same two dimensions, differently sliced. With $\nu^2 = -1$ the repair is $\Cl(1,5)$: the sixth direction is a sixth space, the parity is a rotation into it, and the algebra sits at residue $4$. The two are isomorphic as algebras and the algebra cannot tell them apart; the geometry can, by whether the adjoined plane carries a null vector. The series' physical lineage has always chosen $\Cl(2,4)$, by the vector-level minimality of the root paper; the note observes that the same choice is, from the Kaluza-Klein side, the choice that returns the residue to $6$ and reads the fifth direction as half of a null pair.

**Remark  (The conformal spinor space as a five-dimensional spinor).** The Fock space of Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-completion-spacetime-algebra-spinors} is the complex spinor of $\Cl(2,4)$; as a module over $\Cl(1,4)_\C = \Mat_4(\C) \oplus \Mat_4(\C)$ it is $\varepsilon_+ \oplus \varepsilon_-$ halves, one copy of each inequivalent five-dimensional representation, since the odd element $\nu$ maps each half onto the other (Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}); the two halves restrict to the same module over $\Cl^0(1,4) \cong \Cl(1,3)$, the Dirac spinor. The chirality halves of the six-dimensional spinor are a different splitting, by the six-dimensional volume element, which the odd elements of $\Cl(1,4)$ do not preserve; each chirality half restricted to $\mathrm{Spin}(1,4) \subset \mathrm{Spin}(2,4)$ is the five-dimensional Dirac spinor, the fundamental $\HH^2 = \C^4$ of $Sp(1,1) \subset SU(2,2)$. So the conformal spinor space $\CS$ (twistor space), as a representation of the five-dimensional Lorentz group (which is also the four-dimensional de Sitter group), is a five-dimensional Dirac spinor. This is standard group theory and the script does not check it; it is recorded because it is the spinor-side form of the square of a figure of the original.

**Fact (imported) 12 (Imported: the parity anomaly).** A single two-component Dirac fermion of mass $m$ in $2{+}1$ dimensions, coupled to a $U(1)$ gauge field, has an effective action containing a Chern-Simons term at level $\tfrac12\sgn(m)$ up to a sign that flips between the two inequivalent two-component representations; the term is odd under the reflection of one spatial axis, so a single species breaks parity, while two species, one in each representation with equal mass (equivalently the four-component fermion with its parity-invariant mass term), induce no net Chern-Simons term. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{NiemiSemenoff1983, Redlich1984, AGW1984}.

**Fact (imported) 13 (Imported: graphene's two valleys, and the doubling).** The low-energy theory of electrons on the honeycomb lattice consists of two two-component Dirac fermions, one at each of the two inequivalent corners $K, K'$ of the Brillouin zone, whose gamma matrices differ by the sign of one spatial gamma matrix; a lattice regularisation of a single two-component species is excluded by the fermion-doubling theorem, so the pair is forced. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{Semenoff1984, NielsenNinomiya1981, GSC2007}.

**Theorem 14 (Rule 2 in the plane).** In $A_3 = \Cl(2,1)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\omega = e_xe_ye_t$ is central with $\omega^2 = +1$; $A_3 = \varepsilon_+ A_3 \oplus \varepsilon_- A_3$ with each ideal a copy of $\Cl^0(2,1) \cong \Cl(2,0) \cong \Mat_2(\R)$, and $\varepsilon_+ e_t = -\varepsilon_+ e_xe_y$, $\varepsilon_- e_t = +\varepsilon_- e_xe_y$: the two ideals are the two inequivalent two-component representations, which fix the time gamma matrix as $\mp$ the spatial bivector; the reflection of one spatial axis is $\hat{\cdot}\circ\Ad_{e_x}$, negates $\omega$, exchanges the ideals, and is outer;
\item[(ii)] the Rule 2 repairs are $\Cl(3,1)$ ($\nu^2 = +1$, a third spatial direction) and $\Cl(2,2)$ ($\nu^2 = -1$, a second time), both $\cong \Mat_2(\Mat_2(\R)) = \Mat_4(\R)$, in which $\Ad_\nu$ is the grade involution of $A_3$ and $\Ad_{\nu e_x}$ is the planar reflection: a rotation into the third direction, $(\nu e_x)^2 = -1$, in $\Cl(3,1)$, and a boost, $(\nu e_x)^2 = +1$, in $\Cl(2,2)$; $\Cl(3,1)$ is the Rule 1 ascent of $\Cl(2,0)$ and the residues run $2 \to 1 \to 2$ or $2 \to 1 \to 0$;
\item[(iii)] the time-positive form $\Cl(1,2)$ has central volume element of square $-1$: complex type, $\Cl(1,2) \cong \Mat_2(\C)$, no real splitting; the splitting appears on complexification.
\end{enumerate}

*Proof.* (i) $\omega^2 = (-1)^3(-1)^1 = +1$ and $p - q = 1$: type C by Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-classification-centre-type-c}; the corner is $\Cl^0(2,1)$ by Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} and $\Cl(2,0)$ by Lemma \claimref{rule2.even-subalgebra-one-dimensional-extension}, which is $\Mat_2(\R)$ by \claimref{std-algebra.cl11-matrix}. $e_t = (e_xe_y)^{-1}\omega = -e_xe_y\omega$ since $(e_xe_y)^2 = -1$, so $\varepsilon_\pm e_t = -e_xe_y\varepsilon_\pm\omega = \mp e_xe_y\varepsilon_\pm$. The reflection along $e_x$ is $\hat{\cdot}\circ\Ad_{e_x}$ by the argument of Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iii) with $e_x$ in place of $e_4$, and is outer for the same reason. (ii) Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}, Lemma \claimref{rule2.even-subalgebra-one-dimensional-extension}, and the computations of Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(v)-(vi) with $\nu^2 = +1$ now the spacelike case: $(\nu e_x)^2 = -\nu^2e_x^2 = -\nu^2$. Theorem \claimref{rule2.where-obstruction-sits-commuting-square}(c) at $(p,q) = (2,0)$, residue $2 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, whose obstructing extension is the one by a generator of square $-1$, here $e_t$, and whose opposite-sign repair is $\nu^2 = +1$, gives $\Cl(3,1)$ as the Rule 1 ascent of $\Cl(2,0)$. (iii) $\omega^2 = (-1)^3(-1)^2 = -1$ for $\Cl(1,2)$, $p - q = -1 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$; the isomorphism with $\Mat_2(\C)$ is the classification, \claimref{primer.classification-real-clifford-algebras}. **Remark  (What the planar obstruction is, physically).** Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-parity-anomaly} is Proposition \claimref{rule2.rule-2-plane}(i) with a coupling switched on: the ideal a two-component fermion lives in is the sign of its induced Chern-Simons level, the parity that exchanges the ideals is the reflection that flips the level, and a single species cannot be parity-invariant because no inner change of variables moves it to the other ideal. The four-component fermion of planar electrodynamics is Proposition \claimref{rule2.rule-2-plane}(ii): a module over $\Mat_4(\R)$, the fused algebra, on which the two species are the $\varepsilon_\pm$ halves and the matrix $\nu = \gamma^3$, which anticommutes with the three planar gamma matrices, is the inner automorphism that exchanges them; the parity-invariant mass is the one that respects the fusion. Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-graphenes-two-valleys-doubling} is the same proposition realised in a crystal: the two valleys are the two ideals, related by the sign of one spatial gamma matrix, which by (i) is exactly the reflection that exchanges them, and the doubling theorem is the statement that a lattice, having a third direction available to it in the form of a lattice regulator, cannot host the unfused algebra. In the series' language: nature has done the fusion, and the two-component fermion is what one sees when one looks at a single ideal of it.

**Remark  (Eleven dimensions, and the reality of spinors).** Corollary \claimref{rule2.odd-lorentzian-dimension-exactly-one-real} at $n = 11$ says $\Cl(10,1) \cong \Mat_{32}(\R) \oplus \Mat_{32}(\R)$ is split while $\Cl(1,10) \cong \Mat_{32}(\C)$ has complex type. The irreducible real module of the first is $\R^{32}$ and of the second is $\C^{32}$, which is the classical statement that Majorana spinors exist in eleven dimensions with time of square $-1$ and not with time of square $+1$, the reason eleven-dimensional supergravity is written in the former convention. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{KugoTownsend1983, VanProeyen1999}. The note records this as a third physical face of the same theorem, without a section of its own: the Rule 2 repair of $\Cl(10,1)$ is $\Cl(11,1)$ or $\Cl(10,2)$, both $\Mat_{64}(\R)$; the former is the Rule 1 ascent of $\Cl(10,0)$, and the latter is the algebra of the two-time proposals, which the note mentions and does not endorse.

In particular $\Cl(1,1) \cong \Mat_2(\R)$. Also $\Cl(2,0) \cong \Mat_2(\R)$, by $e_1 \mapsto \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$, $e_2 \mapsto \begin{psmallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{psmallmatrix}$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.cl11-matrix, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 19 (Complexification, and the odd-dimensional split).** \begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For nondegenerate $V$ of dimension $n$, $\Cl(V, Q) \otimes_\R \C \cong \Cl_n(\C)$, the Clifford algebra of $\C^{n}$ with $Q(z) = \sum z_i^{2}$; it depends only on $n$. $\Cl_n(\C) \cong \Mat_{2^{n/2}}(\C)$ for $n$ even and $\Cl_n(\C) \cong \Mat_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(\C) \oplus \Mat_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(\C)$ for $n$ odd.
\item[(ii)] Let $n$ be odd, $V$ nondegenerate, and $\omega = e_1 \cdots e_n$ for an orthonormal basis ($Q(e_i) = \pm 1$), so that $\omega^{2} = \pm 1$. Then $\omega$ is central and invertible, $\Cl^{-} = \Cl^{+}\omega$, and
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\omega^{2} = 1$, the central idempotents $P_{\pm} = \tfrac12(1 \pm \omega)$ split $\Cl(V) = \Cl(V) P_+ \oplus \Cl(V) P_-$, and $a \mapsto a P_{\pm}$ is an isomorphism $\Cl^{+} \to \Cl(V) P_{\pm}$, so $\Cl(V) \cong \Cl^{+} \oplus \Cl^{+}$;
\item if $\omega^{2} = -1$, $a \otimes (x + \mathrm{i} y) \mapsto a(x + y\omega)$ is an isomorphism $\Cl^{+} \otimes_\R \C \to \Cl(V)$, with $\omega$ as the imaginary unit.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.complexification, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 20 (The Clifford algebra exists and is unique).** For every real quadratic space $(V, Q)$ a Clifford algebra exists: $\Cl(V, Q) = T(V) / I_Q$, the tensor algebra modulo the two-sided ideal generated by $\{v \otimes v - Q(v)\,1 : v \in V\}$, with $\iota$ the inclusion of $V = T^{1}(V)$ followed by the quotient map. Any two Clifford algebras of $(V, Q)$ are isomorphic by a unique isomorphism compatible with the maps from $V$. Concretely, for any real symmetric Gram matrix $G$ on $\R^{n}$ (degenerate allowed), the operators $c(v) = v \wedge {} + \, \iota_{Gv}$ on the exterior algebra $\Lambda \R^{n}$ satisfy $c(v)^{2} = Q(v)$, and so give a representation $\Cl(V, Q) \to \operatorname{End} \Lambda V$. (That this representation is faithful is shown afterwards, in the blade basis result, by the triangularity of $x \mapsto x \cdot 1$.)

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.existence-uniqueness, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 21 (Orthogonal vectors anticommute).** For $u, v \in V$: $u v = -v u$ if and only if $B(u, v) = 0$. In an orthogonal basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ for $i \ne j$ and $e_i^{2} = Q(e_i)$, and the product of two basis blades is $e_I e_J = \pm \bigl(\prod_{i \in I \cap J} Q(e_i)\bigr)\, e_{I \triangle J}$, the sign being $(-1)$ to the number of pairs $(i, j) \in I \times J$ with $i > j$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 22 (The spacetime algebra as a matrix algebra, and the spacetime split).** (i) $\Cl(1,3) \cong \Mat_2(\HH)$: with quaternion units $\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \mathbf{q}_3$, the assignment $\gamma_0 \mapsto \begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{psmallmatrix}$, $\gamma_k \mapsto \begin{psmallmatrix} 0 & \mathbf{q}_k \\ \mathbf{q}_k & 0 \end{psmallmatrix}$ extends to an isomorphism. (ii) As a labelled reading, the Dirac matrices $\gamma^{D}_0 = \begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{psmallmatrix}$, $\gamma^{D}_k = \begin{psmallmatrix} 0 & \sigma_k \\ -\sigma_k & 0 \end{psmallmatrix}$ ($2 \times 2$ blocks) give a faithful representation $\Cl(1,3) \to \Mat_4(\C)$ whose complex span is all of $\Mat_4(\C)$: $\C \otimes \Cl(1,3) \cong \Mat_4(\C)$. (iii) The relative vectors $\sigma_k = \gamma_k \gamma_0$ satisfy $\sigma_j \sigma_k + \sigma_k \sigma_j = 2\delta_{jk}$ and $\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3 = I$, and generate the even subalgebra: $\Cl^{+}(1,3) \cong \Cl(3,0)$, so the spacetime pseudoscalar is the pseudoscalar of the observer's space.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.sta-matrix, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 23 (The ascent renders the grade involution inner).** Let $\gamma = \pm 1$. Conjugation by $\nu$ in $B_\gamma$ restricts on $A$ to the grade involution:
\[
\nu\, a\, \nu^{-1} \;=\; \hat{a} \qquad (a \in A).
\]
In particular $\nu \omega \nu^{-1} = -\omega$ and $\nu \varepsilon_\pm \nu^{-1} = \varepsilon_\mp$: the two formerly central projectors become an inner-conjugate pair. Consequently the two-sided ideal of $B_\gamma$ generated by either projector is all of $B_\gamma$, by the one-line computation
\[
\varepsilon_+ \;+\; \nu\,\varepsilon_+\,\nu^{-1} \;=\; \varepsilon_+ + \varepsilon_- \;=\; 1 .
\]
The obstruction of Remark \claimref{note.transport.typec-depth} is thereby resolved in the exact sense there identified: the symmetry exchanging the two ideals, outer on the split algebra, is inner in the ascent, and its availability is what fuses them.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.ascent-renders-grade-involution-inner, version 3 (T01 Prop 5.2).

**Fact (imported) 24 (The centre).** For $n \geq 1$, the centre of $\Cl(p,q)$ is $\R \cdot 1$ if $n$ is even, and $\R \cdot 1 \oplus \R \cdot \omega$ if $n$ is odd, where $\omega = e_1 e_2 \cdots e_n$ is the pseudoscalar.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.centre, version 4 (T01 Lem 2.6).

**Fact (imported) 25 (Classification of the real Clifford algebras).** Let $d \equiv p - q \pmod 8$. Then $\Cl(p,q)$ is isomorphic, as a real associative algebra, to
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
d = 0:\ \Mat_{k}(\R), &
d = 1:\ \Mat_{k}(\R) \oplus \Mat_{k}(\R), &
d = 2:\ \Mat_{k}(\R), &
d = 3:\ \Mat_{k}(\C), \\[2pt]
d = 4:\ \Mat_{k}(\HH), &
d = 5:\ \Mat_{k}(\HH) \oplus \Mat_{k}(\HH), &
d = 6:\ \Mat_{k}(\HH), &
d = 7:\ \Mat_{k}(\C),
\end{array}
\]
with $k$ in each case determined by $\dim_\R \Cl(p,q) = 2^n$. In particular $\Cl(p,q)$ is a simple algebra except when $d \in \{1, 5\}$, i.e.\ except when $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, where it is the direct sum of two isomorphic simple algebras.

*Provenance:* M. F. Atiyah, R. Bott and A. Shapiro, Clifford modules (1964); I. R. Porteous, Clifford Algebras and the Classical Groups (1995); P. Lounesto, Clifford Algebras and Spinors (2001).

**Fact (imported) 26 (The corner is the even subalgebra).** The map $\phi : \Cl^0(p,q) \to \varepsilon_+ A$, $\phi(a) = \varepsilon_+ a$, is an isomorphism of unital algebras (with unit $\varepsilon_+$ on the right-hand side). Moreover $\varepsilon_+ B_\gamma\, \varepsilon_+ = \varepsilon_+ A$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.corner-even-subalgebra, version 4 (T01 Lem 5.3).

**Fact (imported) 27 (Existence and classification of type C).** $\Cl(p,q)$ ($n \geq 1$) contains a nontrivial central idempotent if and only if
\[
p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4 ,
\]
and in that case the nontrivial central idempotents are exactly the two elements
\[
\varepsilon_\pm = \tfrac12\,(1 \pm \omega),
\]
which are exchanged by the grade involution $\hat{\cdot}$ (an \emph{outer} automorphism of the split algebra; no inner automorphism exchanges them, centrality being conjugation-invariant), and satisfy $\varepsilon_+ \varepsilon_- = 0$ and $\varepsilon_+ + \varepsilon_- = 1$, and effect the splitting $\Cl(p,q) = \varepsilon_+ A \,\oplus\, \varepsilon_- A$ into the two simple ideals of Theorem \claimref{transport.classification-real-clifford-algebras}.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.existence-classification-type-c, version 4 (T01 Thm 3.6).

**Fact (imported) 28 (Fusion occurs at most once).** Call a \emph{transport history} any finite sequence of algebras beginning at a nondegenerate $\Cl(p,q)$, each obtained from its predecessor by Rule 1 (the $(1,1)$ ascent) or, when the predecessor carries type C, by Rule 2 (ideal fusion). Then along any transport history:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the clock residue $d \bmod 8$ is constant under Rule 1 (Theorem \claimref{transport.rule-1-morita-trivial}) and moves by $\pm 1$ under Rule 2;
\item type C is present exactly at $d \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, i.e.\ at residues $1$ and $5$ (Theorem \claimref{transport.existence-classification-type-c}), and Rule 2 maps these to residues in $\{0, 2\}$ and $\{4, 6\}$ respectively, none of which is $\equiv 1 \pmod 4$;
\item hence Rule 2 can be applied at most once in any transport history, and after it (or from the start, if $d \not\equiv 1 \pmod 4$) every subsequent algebra in the history has the same Wedderburn type: the state machine takes at most one step on the mod-8 clock, and then remains fixed.
\end{enumerate}
In particular the physical lineage is stationary from the start: $\Cl(1,3)$ has $d \equiv 6$, is simple, never demands Rule 2, and every Rule-1 ascent $\Cl(1{+}k, 3{+}k) \cong \Mat_{2^k}(\Cl(1,3))$ remains at residue $6$, quaternionic type.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.fusion-occurs-most-once, version 5 (T01 Prop 7.4).

**Fact (imported) 29 (Matrix units, and the Peirce isomorphism).** Let $\gamma = \pm 1$ and set
\[
E_{11} = \varepsilon_+, \qquad E_{22} = \varepsilon_-, \qquad E_{12} = \varepsilon_+ \nu, \qquad E_{21} = \gamma\, \varepsilon_- \nu .
\]
Then $E_{11} + E_{22} = 1$ and $E_{ij} E_{kl} = \delta_{jk} E_{il}$: a full $2 \times 2$ system of matrix units. Consequently
\[
B_\gamma \;\cong\; \Mat_2\bigl(E_{11} B_\gamma E_{11}\bigr), \qquad b \;\longmapsto\; \bigl(E_{1i}\, b\, E_{j1}\bigr)_{ij},
\]
an isomorphism of unital algebras with inverse $(c_{ij}) \mapsto \sum_{i,j} E_{i1} c_{ij} E_{1j}$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.matrix-units-peirce-isomorphism, version 3 (T01 Lem 5.4).

**Fact (imported) 30 (Minimality).** Let $(\mathbb{W}, f)$ be any null-lift completion of $(\mathbb{V}, Q)$. Then $\mathbb{W}$ contains at least one direction of positive square and one of negative square; in particular $\dim \mathbb{W} \geq 2$, and enlargements of signature increment $(+k, 0)$ or $(0, +k)$ are excluded for every $k$. A single hyperbolic plane suffices, and any two-dimensional $\mathbb{W}$ admitted by the definition is a hyperbolic plane; hence the minimal completion is
\[
\Cl(p,q) \;\longrightarrow\; \Cl(p{+}1, q{+}1),
\]
with the adjoined space unique up to isometry, and the signature increment is $(+1,+1)$ in every signature.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.minimality, version 4 (T01 Thm 4.13).

**Fact (imported) 31 (No-go at the level of elements).** Let $(\mathbb{V}, Q)$ be nondegenerate of mixed signature $(p,q)$, $p, q \geq 1$, with $n = p + q \geq 3$, and let $\Ncone \subset \mathbb{V}$ be its null cone. Let $(\mathbb{V}', Q')$ be \emph{any} extension of $(\mathbb{V}, Q)$ in the sense of Definition \claimref{obj.transport.extension-quadratic-spaces}, possibly $\mathbb{V}$ itself. Then there is no map $\sigma : \Ncone \to \Cl(\mathbb{V}')$ satisfying $v\,\sigma(v)\,v = v$ for every null $v$ that is equivariant, in the sense of Definition \claimref{obj.transport.equivariance}, even under the identity component $\Spin^0(p,q)$; a fortiori none equivariant under $\Spin(p,q)$ or $\Pin(p,q)$. Equivariant division by zero is impossible at the level of elements, in the Clifford algebra of every ascent, however large.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.no-go-level-elements, version 4 (T01 Thm 4.2).

**Fact (imported) 32 (One generator is minimal; the minimal completions classified).** Let $A = \Cl(p,q)$, $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, and consider the Clifford algebras $\Cl(\mathbb{V}', Q')$ of arbitrary extensions $(\mathbb{V}', Q') \supseteq (\mathbb{V}, Q)$ of the quadratic space, $Q'$ possibly degenerate, each containing $A$ canonically and unitally. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\dim \mathbb{V}' = n$ (no ascent): $A$ itself is not simple (Theorem \claimref{transport.existence-classification-type-c}).
\item $\dim \mathbb{V}' = n + 1$, adjoined generator null ($\gamma = 0$): the fusion fails structurally. The subspace $J = A\nu$ is a nonzero proper two-sided ideal of $B_0$ with $J^2 = 0$, and $B_0 / J \cong A$: the degenerate ascent is an infinitesimal thickening of $A$, not a completion of it. (This square-zero ideal is the fibre-level germ of the degeneration sheaf of the series' sheaf-theoretic paper \cite{ButlerT4}.)
\item $\dim \mathbb{V}' = n + 1$, adjoined generator nondegenerate ($\gamma = \pm 1$ after rescaling): both ascents are simple and $\cong \Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$ (Theorem \claimref{transport.rule-2-ideal-fusion-sharp-form}).
\item $\dim \mathbb{V}' \geq n + 2$: $\dim \Cl(\mathbb{V}') \geq 2^{n+2}$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the minimal simple Clifford completions of $A$ are exactly the two one-generator ascents $\Cl(p{+}1,q)$ and $\Cl(p,q{+}1)$, of dimension $2^{n+1}$; they are inequivalent as quadratic data (the extensions $\langle +1 \rangle$ and $\langle -1 \rangle$ are non-isometric) but isomorphic as algebras; and so the minimal completion of a type-C obstruction is \emph{unique up to isomorphism}, with the residual geometric choice, the sign of the adjoined direction's square, not detected by the completed algebra.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.one-generator-minimal-minimal-completions-classified, version 5 (T01 Thm 5.7).

**Fact (imported) 33 (Rule 1 is Morita-trivial).** The elements
\[
E_{11} = ff^*, \qquad E_{22} = f^*f, \qquad E_{12} = f, \qquad E_{21} = f^*
\]
form a full $2 \times 2$ system of matrix units in $B$ ($E_{11} + E_{22} = 1$, $E_{ij}E_{kl} = \delta_{jk}E_{il}$); the corner is the base, $E_{11} B E_{11} = A\,ff^* \cong A$ via $a \mapsto a\,ff^*$; and therefore
\[
\Cl(p{+}1, q{+}1) \;\cong\; \Mat_2\bigl(\Cl(p,q)\bigr),
\]
under which the ascent embedding $A \hookrightarrow B$ becomes the twisted diagonal $a \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(a, \hat{a})$. In particular Rule 1 preserves the Morita class: the Wedderburn type, and with it the simplicity or splitness and the type-C diagnosis of the section ``A taxonomy of obstructions'', are the same for $B$ as for $A$, and the clock residue $d = p - q \pmod 8$ is fixed. (The \emph{presence} of types N and P is not preserved and could not be: Proposition \claimref{transport.no-transport-terminates} below shows every completion carries fresh obstructions of its own.) The adjoined ladder pair is, in the matrix description, exactly the off-diagonal matrix units, and the descent projectors $f^*f,\, ff^*$ are the diagonal ones.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.rule-1-morita-trivial, version 5 (T01 Thm 7.1).

**Fact (imported) 34 (Rule 2: ideal fusion, sharp form).** Let $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $\gamma = \pm 1$. Then
\[
B_\gamma \;=\; \Cl(p{+}1,q) \ \text{or}\ \Cl(p,q{+}1) \;\cong\; \Mat_2\bigl(\Cl^0(p,q)\bigr),
\]
by the matrix units of Lemma \claimref{transport.matrix-units-peirce-isomorphism} and the corner identification of Lemma \claimref{transport.corner-even-subalgebra}. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B_\gamma$ is simple, and its centre is $\R$: the fusion does not reintroduce a central splitting, and $B_\gamma$ carries no type-C obstruction.
\item The images of $\varepsilon_\pm$ in $B_\gamma$ are a conjugate pair of non-central idempotents, the diagonal matrix units of the fused algebra, exchanged by the inner automorphism $\mathrm{Ad}_\nu$, which restricts on $A$ to the grade involution (Proposition \claimref{transport.ascent-renders-grade-involution-inner}).
\item The two ascents are isomorphic as algebras: $\Cl(p{+}1,q) \cong \Cl(p,q{+}1)$, both canonically $\Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$, with an isomorphism through the matrix units that fixes the corner.
\end{enumerate}

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.rule-2-ideal-fusion-sharp-form, version 4 (T01 Thm 5.5).

**Fact (imported) 35 (The sign of $\omega^2$).** $\displaystyle \omega^2 = (-1)^{\tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}} (-1)^q = (-1)^{\tfrac{(p-q)(p-q-1)}{2}}$. In particular $\omega^2 = +1$ if and only if $p - q \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 4$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim transport.sign, version 3 (T01 Lem 2.7).

**Refutation 36 (The two one-generator ascents differ as algebras).** Refuted. The conjecture was that the two one-generator ascents of a type-C algebra, adjoining $\nu$ with $Q(\nu) = +1$ or with $Q(\nu) = -1$, are non-isomorphic algebras. The corrected statement is that the two ascents are always isomorphic as algebras; the sign of $Q(\nu)$ is a geometric bit that the algebra forgets.

**Refutation 37 (The original conjecture, refuted below).** Division by zero promotes the element. For every null vector $v$ of $\Cl(p,q)$ there is, in the minimal ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$, a canonically chosen partner $\sigma(v)$ satisfying $v\,\sigma(v)\,v = v$, the choice equivariant under the symmetries of the original algebra: the impossible division is repaired, element by element, one ascent up.
