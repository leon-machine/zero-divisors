## Introduction



**setting  (Introduction).** One relation carries both theories. The defining relation of a Clifford algebra, $\{e_{a}, e_{b}\} = 2g_{ab}\mathbf{1}$, reads on an orthogonal basis as a metric and on a Witt basis as the canonical anticommutation relations of a fermionic mode system. The metric \emph{is} the anticommutator and the anticommutator \emph{is} the metric: geometric versus quantum is a choice of chart on one object, not a choice of object. This paper develops that identification into a synthesis and then addresses its principal remaining task, the laboratory, deriving laboratory quantum mechanics as the slow shadow of the frozen phase (the series' name for the descended, condensed vacuum in which the laboratory is located) and carrying the account as far as the framework reaches, which is to the hard problem and no further.

The synthesis first. Witt index is quantum capacity, the tower paper's theorem \cite[Thm.~4.3]{ButlerT3}, and there are two of them: the real capacity $\min(p,q)$, the number of modes realisable by real null vectors, and the complex capacity $\lfloor (p+q)/2 \rfloor$ of the complexification. Lorentzian signature has real capacity one, so pre-ascent spacetime natively supports exactly one real quantum mode, the boost qubit; each ascent adjoins one hyperbolic plane, raising both capacities by one, so the transport principle \emph{is} capacity increment and the tower's one-qubit-per-rung ladder is its restatement, and the reading that Hawking thermality is single-mode because one rung was ascended is offered and labelled a reading; and the complexified ascended space has complex capacity three, the pair count under which the Standard-Model paper selects the strong force and four dimensions, on the conditionalities it declares. Every point of spacetime is null under the completion's embedding, so spacetime is a family of the algebra's non-invertible elements: division by zero is not confined to special loci but is the condition of every point. Each point's null vector acts on the twistor space as a nilpotent whose kernel is the point's incidence line, and every twistor on that line is the Fock vacuum of its own isotropic system: a point is not one vacuum but a projective line of them, and conformally compactified spacetime is the manifold of such lines, the Klein quadric, over which the two theories read as the dynamics \emph{of} the vacua (general relativity, how the vacuum family varies from point to point) and the dynamics \emph{above} a vacuum (quantum mechanics, excitations over a chosen vacuum). The series' internal cross-checks are then sorted by level: one count in two vocabularies, an identification, and a reading.

Then the laboratory, and there the paper is deliberately deflationary. Of the three postulated inputs of nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, one is sourced in structures derived before the question was asked, half of another is sourced under an open hypothesis and the other half is not, and the third was not a separate postulate: the mass is the condensate, at the level the families paper's reading holds; the time orientation that distinguishes particles from antiparticles is the planted flag's bit, under the Standard-Model paper's Hypothesis (S); the rest frame of the nonrelativistic limit is the laboratory's own and is not sourced by anything in the series; and the imaginary unit, Hestenes' spin plane, is the rest frame read through the law, one datum with the frame and not two, so it is not imported (Lemma \claimref{one-relation.one-datum-not-two-imaginary-unit}). Given that the law is first order, which is the one dynamical import, its form on even fields is forced by its square (Lemma \claimref{one-relation.form-law-forced-square}), and given the law the arrow of time and the frequency sign are one bit and not two, at $\Cl(1,3)$ level (Corollary \claimref{one-relation.arrow-frequency-are-one-bit-level}). **Theorem 25 (The vector derivative is the covariant first-order operator).** Let $D = A^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}$ be a first-order operator with constant coefficients $A^{\mu} \in \Cl(1,3)$ acting by left multiplication on $\Cl(1,3)$-valued fields on Minkowski space (hence on every minimal left ideal), and suppose $D$ is covariant under the spin group: $D(R\,\psi\circ\Lambda_{R}^{-1}) = R\,(D\psi)\circ\Lambda_{R}^{-1}$ for every rotor $R \in \mathrm{Spin}^{+}(1,3)$, $\Lambda_{R}$ its vector transformation. **Fact (imported) 67 (Duality).** Let $I$ be the unit pseudoscalar of $\Cl(p, q, r)$. For every signature, right multiplication by $I$ maps $\Cl^{k}$ into $\Cl^{n-k}$, and for $a \in V$

**Fact (imported) 68 (Which vectors and versors are invertible).** In $\Cl(V, Q)$, a vector $v$ is invertible if and only if $Q(v) \ne 0$, and then $v^{-1} = v / Q(v)$. \begin{equation}
g \tilde{g} = \tilde{g} g = Q(u_1) Q(u_2) \cdots Q(u_k) \ne 0,
\end{equation}
so $g$ is invertible with $g^{-1} = \tilde{g} / \bigl(Q(u_1) \cdots Q(u_k)\bigr)$. None of this needs the form to be nondegenerate.

**Fact (imported) 82 (The pinching, and its kernel).** Define the pinching $E : B \to B$, $E(x) = PxP + (1-P)x(1-P)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ is an idempotent linear map whose image is the commutant $C = \{x \in B : xP = Px\} = A \oplus A\,ff^*$, on which it restricts to the identity, and $E$ is a $C$-bimodule map: $E(cxc') = c\,E(x)\,c'$ for $c, c' \in C$, with $E(1) = 1$: a conditional expectation onto $C$.
\item Its kernel is exactly the adjoined ladder sector:
\[
\ker E \;=\; PB(1-P) \,\oplus\, (1-P)BP \;=\; A f^* \,\oplus\, A f .
\]
