# Where the Pairing Generator Is: The connection moves the vacuum by the optical scalars of its null direction: Companion note to T5 and T16: a dictionary, one claim closed for the pre-ascent fibre, one not closed (regenerated from the store)

**Abstract.** The singularity paper places the pairing terms, the generators that change a Witt decomposition and so create entanglement from a vacuum, in the tangent of the vacuum orbit, and records two things it does not do: it compares two Witt decompositions without deriving them, and supplies no generator in that tangent. This note states both as claims with kill conditions fixed before computation, on the laboratory paper's pre-ascent Dirac fibre, two modes at a point, not the boundary-mode line. For the vacuum given by a null direction $\ell$ and a chirality bit, the stabiliser in $\so(1,3)$ is the parabolic algebra of $\ell$, and the functional $\pv(B) = \ip{B\cdot\ell}{m}$ that cuts it out is, on the Fock module, the pair-creation amplitude $-\bra{12}B\ket{0}$. The connection therefore has a pairing part, the transverse derivative of the null direction: the Newman-Penrose coefficients $\kappa, \tau, \sigma, \rho$. What is proved is the dictionary and the exact Schwarzschild classes; the identifications are imports, and the whole stands without the transport principle. In Schwarzschild, with $\ell$ the outgoing radial direction, only $\rho$ survives: zero along the $t$- and $r$-lines, zero on the horizon in a regular frame, opposite in sign on the two sides. Holonomy moves the vacuum only for loops with an $n \wedge \mb$ component, $\ell$ being a repeated principal null direction. The mismatch angle is not reached: the two frames share a vacuum, the selected transport has zero class, and no holonomy carries a mode weight. Nothing here pairs the ladders at two points.

## The two claims, and the kill conditions fixed before computation



**setting  (The two claims, and the kill conditions fixed before computation).** The series' method is to state the conditions under which a claim dies before computing anything. **setting  (The two claims, and the kill conditions fixed before computation).** \textbf{Claim R2 (the pairing generator).} The framework's own connection, relative to the framework's own vacuum field, has a nonzero pairing class along some directions, and that class is fixed by data the framework supplies: the vacuum field selected by the curvature (the radial pair of Fact \claimref{pairing.imported-schwarzschild-connection-curvature}, its radial member by the sign of the square, and a null direction in it by the flag bit) and the connection. A pairing generator exists and is derived, not premised, up to the flag bit.

\begin{itemize}
\item[K2.1] (nothing pairs) If the pairing class vanishes for every direction at every radius, the connection preserves the vacuum field and the claim is dead.
\item[K2.2] (frame artefact) If the class is nonzero but is not the transverse derivative $\ip{\nabla_X \ell}{m}$ of the vacuum's null direction, it is a property of the frame and not of the vacuum field, and the result is a reading, not a derivation. Test: the identity must hold in $\Q(F)$, and the vanishing locus must be invariant under $\ell \mapsto \lambda\ell$.
\item[K2.3] (consistency with the singularity paper) If the class along $\partial_t$ or $\partial_r$ is nonzero, the diagonality of that paper's supplied class \cite[Prop.~7.31, Thm.~C.8]{ButlerT5} is contradicted at the pre-ascent level; this would kill that paper's statement, not the claim, and would be reported as such.
\item[K2.4] (intra-fibre only) If every class found acts within the fibre at one point, and no component of the connection pairs the ladder at $x$ with the ladder at $y \neq x$, the cross-point clause of (L$_E$) is untouched and the claim closes only in the intra-fibre sense. Parallel transport along a path identifies the fibre at $x$ with the fibre at $y$, so ``transport, then compare'' is the intra-fibre result along a path; the cross-point object is a generator in $\so(V_{x} \oplus V_{y})$ for the completion's independent ladders, which no connection supplies. This condition bounds the claim and was expected to trigger.
\end{itemize}
Predictions, as registered: the class vanishes along $\partial_t$ and $\partial_r$, is proportional to $\sqrt f/r$ in the transverse directions, vanishes at the horizon and changes sign across it (P2.1); it equals $\ip{\nabla_X\ell}{m}$ (P2.2); the mixed-plane curvature has ``nonzero pairing class in the $n$-$m$ planes and zero in the $\ell$-$m$ planes'', and the radial and angular planes have none (P2.3); in the flat conformal model the classes of a displacement $dx$ are $(\ip{dx}{\ell}, \ip{dx}{m}, 0)$ and vanish only along the ray (P2.4).

## Transport moves the vacuum by the transverse derivative of its null direction



**setting  (Transport moves the vacuum by the transverse derivative of its null direction).** Let a vacuum field be given on a region: a null direction field $\ell(x)$ with a transverse complex null vector $m(x)$, and let $\nabla$ be the Levi-Civita connection. In an orthonormal frame with $\ell = e_{0} + e_{1}$ and $m = e_{2} + ie_{3}$ of constant components, $\nabla_{X} e_{b} = \omega^{a}{}_{b}(X)\,e_{a}$, and the connection acts on the Clifford fibre by the bivector
\[
\Omega(X) \;=\; \sum_{a<b}\omega^{ab}(X)\,e_{a}e_{b}, \qquad \omega^{ab} = \omega^{a}{}_{c}\,\eta^{cb},
\]
in the sense that $\nabla_{X} v = \tfrac12[\Omega(X), v]$ for every vector $v$ of constant frame components. The spin transport of the vacuum along $X$ is the spin action of the same $\Omega(X)$.



**Definition 12 (The pairing class of the transport).** The pairing class of the transport along $X$, relative to the vacuum field, is $\pv(\Omega(X)) = \ip{\Omega(X)\cdot\ell}{m}$. The transport preserves the vacuum ray along $X$ if and only if $\pv(\Omega(X)) = 0$ (Proposition \claimref{pairing.stabiliser-parabolic-algebra-null-direction}), and by Proposition \claimref{pairing.pairing-functional-pair-creation-amplitude} the spin transport $\exp(s\,\Omega(X))$ carries the vacuum, to first order in $s$ and up to a scalar, to $\ket{0} - s\,\pv(\Omega(X))\,\ket{12}$: the class is the Thouless amplitude of the mismatch. (Comparing the vacuum field against the transported frame instead reverses the sign; nothing below depends on the convention.)



**Theorem 13 (The pairing class is the transverse derivative of the null direction).** For every direction $X$,
\[
\pv(\Omega(X)) \;=\; \ip{\nabla_{X}\ell}{m},
\]
and under a rescaling $\ell \mapsto \lambda\ell$ of the null direction by a function the class scales by $\lambda$, so its vanishing locus is a property of the null direction field and not of its normalisation. For a real direction $X$ the vacuum is parallel along $X$ exactly when the null direction is, $\nabla_{X}\ell \propto \ell$.

*Proof.* $\nabla_{X}\ell = \tfrac12[\Omega(X), \ell] = \Omega(X)\cdot\ell$ by the defining property of $\Omega$, so the identity is the definition of $\pv$ once $\Omega$ reproduces $\nabla$, which is what the script verifies for both Schwarzschild frames. For the rescaling, $\nabla_{X}(\lambda\ell) = \lambda\nabla_{X}\ell + (X\lambda)\ell$ and $\ip{\ell}{m} = 0$; the script models the derivative term, which is nonzero along $e_{1}$, and confirms the scaling in every direction with $\lambda = F/(1+F)$. **Remark  (Reading, and prior art).** Proposition \claimref{pairing.pairing-class-transverse-derivative-null-direction} is elementary once Proposition \claimref{pairing.stabiliser-parabolic-algebra-null-direction} is in hand, and its content is a dictionary: by Fact \claimref{pairing.imported-spin-coefficients-null-tetrad} the components of the pairing class on the null tetrad are, up to normalisation and sign convention, the spin coefficients $\kappa, \tau, \sigma, \rho$, so the pairing generator of the framework's vacuum is the transverse deformation of the vacuum's null congruence, the optical scalars of Sachs and Newman-Penrose: the vacuum is parallel along its own integral curve if $\ell$ is geodesic ($\kappa = 0$), along $n$ if $\tau = 0$, and along every transverse real direction if the congruence has neither expansion, twist nor shear ($\rho = \sigma = 0$). The direction the modular flow of the boundary modes selects is $B_1$, the $t$-direction, by (i).
\end{enumerate}

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim singularity.modular-flow-boundary-modes-holonomy-universal, version 2 (T05 Prop 13.2).

**Fact (imported) 33 (Bivectors form the Lie algebra of the orthogonal group).** For $A, C \in \Cl^{2}(V, Q)$ and $x \in V$, with the commutator product $A \times X = \tfrac12(AX - XA)$:
\begin{equation}
A \times C = \langle A C \rangle_2 \in \Cl^{2}, \qquad A \times x = \langle A x \rangle_1 \in V, \qquad B(A \times x, y) + B(x, A \times y) = 0 .
\end{equation}
So $\Cl^{2}$ is a Lie algebra under $\times$, and $A \mapsto (x \mapsto A \times x)$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism $\Cl^{2} \to \so(V, Q)$, the skew-adjoint maps of $(V, Q)$. When $(V, Q)$ is nondegenerate it is an isomorphism, so $\dim \so(V, Q) = \binom{n}{2}$. The scalar part is cyclic: $\langle X Y \rangle_0 = \langle Y X \rangle_0$.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.bivector-lie-algebra, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 34 (Contraction by a vector is a derivation).** For $a \in V$ and $X, Y \in \Cl(V, Q)$:
\begin{align}
a \rfloor (X Y) &= (a \rfloor X) Y + \hat{X} (a \rfloor Y), \\
a \rfloor (X \wedge Y) &= (a \rfloor X) \wedge Y + \hat{X} \wedge (a \rfloor Y), \\
a \rfloor (a \rfloor X) &= 0 .
\end{align}
Under the isomorphism $\Phi$ of \claimref{std-algebra.exterior-isomorphism}, $a \rfloor$ is the interior product of $\Lambda V$ with the linear form $B(a, \cdot)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
a \rfloor (b_1 \wedge \dots \wedge b_k) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} (-1)^{i-1} B(a, b_i)\, b_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \check{b}_i \wedge \dots \wedge b_k,
\end{equation}
where $\check{b}_i$ means $b_i$ is omitted.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.contraction-expansion, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 35 (The Fock representation, and the Fock space as a minimal left ideal).** Let $V = W' \oplus W$ with Witt basis $v_i \in W'$, $w_i \in W$ ($1 \le i \le k$) as in \claimref{std-algebra.witt-basis-car}, so $V \cong \R^{k,k}$. On $\Lambda W$ let $w \in W$ act by $w \wedge{}$ and $v \in W'$ by the contraction $\iota_{2B(v, \cdot)}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] this extends to an algebra isomorphism $\Cl(V, Q) \cong \End(\Lambda W) \cong \Mat_{2^{k}}(\R)$;
\item[(ii)] $f = v_1 w_1 v_2 w_2 \cdots v_k w_k$ is a primitive idempotent with $v_i f = 0$ for all $i$; the left ideal $\Cl(V, Q)\, f$ is a spinor space of dimension $2^{k}$ with basis $\{w_I f\}$, and $w_I f \mapsto w_{i_1} \wedge \dots \wedge w_{i_m}$ is an isomorphism of $\Cl(V, Q)$-modules $\Cl(V, Q)\, f \cong \Lambda W$.
\end{enumerate}
So the Fock space is the minimal left ideal generated by the vacuum idempotent $f$, and the $w_I f$ are its occupation-number basis.

*Provenance:* Transport Lab claim std-algebra.fock-representation, version 1.

**Fact (imported) 36 (Orthogonal vectors anticommute).** For $u, v \in V$: $u v = -v u$ if and only if $B(u, v) = 0$. In an orthogonal basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$, $e_i e_j = -e_j e_i$ for $i \ne j$ and $e_i^{2} = Q(e_i)$, and the product of two basis blades is $e_I e_J = \pm \bigl(\prod_{i \in I \cap J} Q(e_i)\bigr)\, e_{I \triangle J}$, the sign being $(-1)$ to the number of pairs $(i, j) \in I \times J$ with $i > j$.

**Fact (imported) 37 (Imported: the dictionary between Gauge Theory Gravity and curved-spacetime language).** Let $(\hb, \Omega)$ be a gravitational field on flat spacetime with invertible region $U$ and derived metric $g$ (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}), with frame $g_\mu = \hb^{-1}(e_\mu)$ and coframe $g^\mu = \hb^{T}(e^\mu)$. On $U$, with $\Omega$ the torsion-free field of $\hb$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Vectors.} The tangent vectors of $(U, g)$ at $x$ are the physical vectors $\hb^{-1}(a)$, and $g(a,b) = \hb^{-1}(a) \cdot \hb^{-1}(b)$. A background vector $a$ is null, timelike or spacelike for $g$ exactly when $Q(\hb^{-1}(a))$ is zero, positive or negative; the light cone of $g$ at $x$ is the image under $\hb$ of the null cone of the flat product. The components of a physical vector field $A$ are $A^\mu = g^\mu \cdot A$, and an orthonormal frame of $g$ is any orthonormal set $\{\gamma_a\}$ of $\Cl(1,3)$, with tetrad components $\gamma^a \cdot g_\mu$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Levi-Civita derivative and parallel transport.} For a physical vector field $A$, $\nabla_\mu A^\nu = g^\nu \cdot D_{e_\mu} A$, and the Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = g^\rho \cdot D_{e_\mu} g_\nu$. Parallel transport along a curve $x(\lambda)$ is $D_{x'} M = 0$; it is solved by $M(\lambda) = U M(0) \tilde U$ with $dU/d\lambda = -\tfrac12\Omega(x')U$, $U(0) = 1$, a rotor, so it preserves the flat product of physical vectors.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Geodesics.} A curve $x(\tau)$ is an affinely parametrised timelike (null) geodesic of $g$ exactly when $v = \hb^{-1}(x')$ satisfies $D_{x'}v = 0$, that is $dv/d\tau = -\Omega(x') \cdot v$, with $Q(v) = 1$ ($Q(v) = 0$).
\item[(iv)] \emph{Curvature.} The Riemann tensor of $g$ is the covariant field strength: $R(\hb(g_\mu \wedge g_\nu)) = R(e_\mu \wedge e_\nu)$, contracted with the reciprocal planes, gives $R_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}$ (Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-equivalence-einstein-equations}), so the curvature operator on bivectors is $B \mapsto \mathcal{R}(B)$. The Ricci tensor is $R_{\mu\nu} = g_\mu \cdot \mathcal{R}(g_\nu)$ with $\mathcal{R}(b) = \partial_a \cdot \mathcal{R}(a \wedge b)$, the scalar curvature is $\mathcal{R} = \partial_a \cdot \mathcal{R}(a)$, and the Einstein tensor is $\mathcal{G}(a) = \mathcal{R}(a) - \tfrac12 a\,\mathcal{R}$; the GTG field equations are Einstein's equations for $g$ (Facts \claimref{singularity.imported-equivalence-einstein-equations} and \claimref{interior.imported-equivalence-einstein-equations}). In the store's sign of the definition, $\mathcal{R}(B) = -R(\hb(B))$, the Ricci curvature of the round-sphere-positive convention on a physical null vector $k$ is $-k \cdot \mathcal{R}(k)$, and de Sitter space has $\mathcal{R}(B) = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}B$; the cited sources use $R(\hb(B))$.
\item[(v)] \emph{Weyl and Schouten.} The Schouten tensor is $\Sch(a) = \tfrac12\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a) - \tfrac16 a\,\mathcal{R}\bigr)$, and the Weyl tensor is the trace-free part of the curvature,
\[
\mathcal{W}(a \wedge b) \;=\; \mathcal{R}(a \wedge b) - \Sch(a) \wedge b - a \wedge \Sch(b) \;=\; \mathcal{R}(a \wedge b) - \tfrac12\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a) \wedge b + a \wedge \mathcal{R}(b)\bigr) + \tfrac16\,\mathcal{R}\; a \wedge b ,
\]
with $\partial_a \cdot \mathcal{W}(a \wedge b) = 0$ and $\mathcal{W}(IB) = I\,\mathcal{W}(B)$, $I$ the pseudoscalar.
\item[(vi)] \emph{Killing vectors and surface gravity.} A vector field $\xi = \xi^\mu \partial_\mu$ is a Killing field of $g$ exactly when the physical vector field $K = \xi^\mu g_\mu = \hb^{-1}(\xi^\mu e_\mu)$ makes $A \mapsto A \cdot D K$ antisymmetric for the flat product, $B \cdot (A \cdot D K) + A \cdot (B \cdot D K) = 0$; then $A \cdot D K = A \cdot F_K$ with the bivector $F_K = \tfrac12 D \wedge K$. On a Killing horizon, a locus where $Q(K) = 0$ and $K$ is normal, the surface gravity $\kappa$ is defined by $(K \cdot D)K = \kappa K$.
\end{enumerate}

**Fact (imported) 38 (Witt index is quantum capacity).** For any nondegenerate real quadratic space $(V, Q)$, the maximal number of pairwise-anticommuting fermionic mode pairs realisable by \emph{vectors} of $\Cl(V, Q)$ (families $(a_i, b_i)$ of vectors satisfying the displayed CAR of Proposition \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors}) is exactly the Witt index $\min(p,q)$ of $Q$.
