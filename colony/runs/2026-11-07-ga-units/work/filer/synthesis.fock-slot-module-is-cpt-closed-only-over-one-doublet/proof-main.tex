\emph{Notation.} As in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.cpt-closure-forces-slot-module-and-chirality}, with numbers read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$ (conventions): every vector space below is a module over $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$ and every tensor, exterior and symmetric power is taken over it; complex conjugation, which defines a CPT halving, reverses $I_{4}$. $D$ is the slot doublet, the defining module of the slot $U(2)$, so the theorem's $\Lambda^{k}$ of the doublet is $\Lambda^{k}D$, and $F_{(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2})}$ is the irreducible of highest weight $(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2})$, of weak spin $\tfrac12(\lambda_{1} - \lambda_{2})$: $F_{(1,0)} = D$, $F_{(1,1)} = \Lambda^{2}D$, $F_{(2,0)} = \mathrm{Sym}^{2}D$.

\emph{Step 1 (the one-slot space).} On $F_{1}$ the scalar $\mu I_{2}$ acts as $\mu$, so every irreducible constituent $F_{(\lambda_{1}, \lambda_{2})}$ of the polynomial representation $F_{1}$ has $\lambda_{1} + \lambda_{2} = 1$, $\lambda_{1} \ge \lambda_{2} \ge 0$: it is $F_{(1,0)} = D$. So $F_{1} \cong D \otimes M$ with $M = \mathrm{Hom}_{U(2)}(D, F_{1})$ of some dimension $b \ge 0$, on which $U(2)$ acts trivially; this is the $b\Lambda^{1}$ of clause (1) of the theorem whenever $F$ is CPT-closed.

\emph{Step 2 (the two-slot space of the exterior module).} For any $V$, $W$, the swap of $(V \otimes W) \otimes (V \otimes W) = (V \otimes V) \otimes (W \otimes W)$ is the tensor product of the two swaps, so its $-1$ eigenspace is $\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V \otimes \Lambda^{2}W \oplus \Lambda^{2}V \otimes \mathrm{Sym}^{2}W$:
\[ \Lambda^{2}(V \otimes W) \cong \mathrm{Sym}^{2}V \otimes \Lambda^{2}W \;\oplus\; \Lambda^{2}V \otimes \mathrm{Sym}^{2}W , \]
equivariantly for $GL(V) \times GL(W)$. With $V = D$, $W = M$: $F_{2} = \Lambda^{2}(D \otimes M) \cong \binom{b}{2}F_{(2,0)} \oplus \binom{b+1}{2}F_{(1,1)}$.

\emph{Step 3 (exterior case).} Let $F = \Lambda^{\bullet}F_{1}$. Then $F_{0} = \Lambda^{0}F_{1}$ is one-dimensional, so in clause (1) $a = 1$, and CPT closure requires $F_{2} \cong F_{(1,1)}$ and $F_{n} = 0$ for $n \ge 3$. For $b \ge 2$, $F_{2}$ contains $\binom{b}{2} \ge 1$ copies of $F_{(2,0)}$, a state of weak spin one, which clause (1) excludes (also $F_{3} = \Lambda^{3}(D \otimes M) \ne 0$ since $\dim(D \otimes M) = 2b \ge 4$). For $b = 0$, $F = \Lambda^{0}$ has $F_{2} = 0 \ne F_{(1,1)}$. For $b = 1$, $F = \Lambda^{0}D \oplus \Lambda^{1}D \oplus \Lambda^{2}D$, which is CPT-closed by clause (1) with $a = b = 1$. So CPT closure holds exactly for $b = 1$, and then $F = \Lambda^{\bullet}D$: the slot vacuum is unique, the wedge $D \otimes \Lambda^{n}D \to \Lambda^{n+1}D$ is onto (clause (G)), and the creation operators $a^{\dagger}(u) = u \wedge$ obey $a^{\dagger}(u)a^{\dagger}(u') = -a^{\dagger}(u')a^{\dagger}(u)$ (exclusion), the module of clause (3) of the theorem.

\emph{Step 4 (symmetric case).} Let $F = \mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}F_{1}$. For $b \ge 1$, $\dim F_{n} = \binom{2b + n - 1}{n} > 0$ for every $n$: $F$ is infinite-dimensional, outside the finite-dimensional class of clause (a), and its two-slot space $\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(D \otimes M) \supseteq \mathrm{Sym}^{2}D \otimes \mathrm{Sym}^{2}M$ already contains $\binom{b+1}{2}$ copies of $F_{(2,0)}$; any finite-dimensional graded truncation keeping $F_{2}$ fails clause (1). For $b = 0$, $F_{2} = 0$ as in Step 3. So no symmetric Fock module over the one-slot space is CPT-closed.

\emph{Step 5 (what is derived).} Steps 3 and 4 use only clause (1) of the theorem and the Fock clause; they do not use clause (G), and they derive from the Fock clause both the statistics (the symmetric module is excluded) and the number $b = 1$ of doublets. The Fock clause is a hypothesis of the statement: it replaces (G) by the requirement that the slot module be the second quantisation, of either statistics, of its one-slot space; it is not derived here and is no new postulate of the store. The checks verify Step 2 as torus characters for $\dim V, \dim W \le 3$, the content of $F_{2}$ and $F_{3}$ and the CPT-closure criterion for $b = 1, \dots, 4$, the dimensions of the symmetric module for $n \le 6$, and, realising the $2b$ slot modes as null pairs of vectors of $\Cl(2b, 2b)$ with $vw + wv = 1$ and the vacuum the null blade of the $w$'s, the eigenvalues of the weak Casimir on the two-slot states for $b = 1, 2$ (for $b = 2$: spin one on $3$ states, spin zero on $3$).
