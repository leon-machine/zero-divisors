## formal
\emph{Hypothesis (TT).} The two transverse trace-free directions of the first-order gravitational field at null momentum carry a canonical pair, as the photon's modes do in Proposition \claimref{field-theory.fock-space-maxwell-modes}. (TT) is a named hypothesis; it is not a stored premise and nothing here derives it. Clauses (1) to (10) are statements about a lattice model and hold without it. The model has two variants, a field with no variable of its own and a field with its own canonical variables; (TT) is what the second stands for, and everything said of the series in the Reading is conditional on it.

\emph{The field.} Conventions of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.first-order-gravitational-field-has-two-free-directions-at-null-momentum-and-four-constraints}: $\Cl(1,3)$, symmetric maps $h$ of the vectors, the first-order Einstein map $\mathcal{G}_{k}$. Fix a frame with timelike unit $e_{0}$ and take the gauge $h(e_{0}) = 0$, so that $h$ is a symmetric map of the three space directions, with components $h_{ij}$. The lattice is a periodic cubic lattice of period $L$. The components $h_{ii}$ sit on the sites and $h_{ij}$, $i < j$, on the plaquettes; $s_{i}$ is the forward difference along the axis $i$, $b_{i}$ the backward one, $\Delta_{i} = b_{i}s_{i}$, $\Delta = \sum_{i}\Delta_{i}$. For $i, j, k$ distinct,
\[
G[h]_{ii} = \tfrac12\bigl(\Delta_{k}h_{jj} + \Delta_{j}h_{kk}\bigr) - b_{j}b_{k}h_{jk}, \qquad G[h]_{ij} = \tfrac12\bigl(-\Delta_{k}h_{ij} + s_{i}b_{k}h_{jk} + s_{j}b_{k}h_{ik} - s_{i}s_{j}h_{kk}\bigr),
\]
\[
D h = 2\sum_{i<j}b_{i}b_{j}h_{ij} - \sum_{i}\sum_{k \neq i}\Delta_{k}h_{ii}, \qquad C_{i}[\pi] = s_{i}\pi_{ii} + \sum_{j \neq i}b_{j}\pi_{ij},
\]
and a displacement by $\xi$ (a function on the links) is $(\delta_{\xi})_{ii} = 2b_{i}\xi_{i}$, $(\delta_{\xi})_{ij} = s_{i}\xi_{j} + s_{j}\xi_{i}$. The pairing is $\langle h, h'\rangle = \sum_{x}\bigl(\sum_{i}h_{ii}h'_{ii} + 2\sum_{i<j}h_{ij}h'_{ij}\bigr)$. The six components $G[h]_{c}(x)$ are called the \emph{curvature} of $h$; they do not change under displacements. The field equations are, with step $\tau = \tfrac12$ and $p = \pi - \tfrac12\,\delta\,\tr\pi$,
\[
\pi(n+1) = \pi(n) - \tau\,G[h(n)], \qquad h(n+1) = h(n) + 2\tau\,p(n+1),
\]
and the constraints are $C_{0}(s) = (Dh)(s) - g\,\rho_{s}$ at every site and $C_{i}[\pi] = 0$ on every link. When the field has its own variables, $(h, \pi)$ are canonical and enter through the Weyl operators $W(f) = \exp(I_{4}\langle f, \pi\rangle)$, $W(f)W(f') = W(f + f')$, $[h_{c}(x), W(f)] = f_{c}(x)\,W(f)$ (the unit written as in the field theory), and an \emph{observable} is an element that commutes with every constraint. A \emph{slaved field} is a field with no variable of its own: an assignment to each component of $h$ and $\pi$ of an element of the matter algebra, satisfying the constraints.

\emph{The matter.} Two equal masses at rest on the first axis through a centre $c$, in one of two arrangements: wide, at $c \pm 2e_{1}$, or narrow, at $c \pm e_{1}$. The algebra is that of a completed pair as in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.field-slaved-to-an-operator-source-does-not-commute-at-spacelike-separation}: $\Cl(1,1)$, $v = \tfrac12(e_{1} + e_{2})$, $w = \tfrac12(e_{1} - e_{2})$, $P_{1} = vw$ (wide), $P_{2} = wv$ (narrow), $v = P_{1}vP_{2}$ (\claimref{synthesis.set-and-clear-of-a-bit-generate-the-hyperbolic-plane}). The mass density is $\rho_{s} = \rho^{\mathrm{w}}_{s}P_{1} + \rho^{\mathrm{n}}_{s}P_{2} - \bar\rho$, with $\bar\rho$ the uniform number that makes the total zero on the periodic lattice; it drops out of every commutator; it is built from the commuting numbers of the arrangements, as the source of the stored static coupling is (Proposition \claimref{field-theory.operator-newtonian-coupling}). The \emph{string} is the three sites $c - e_{1}, c, c + e_{1}$, and $f$ is the field with $f_{11} = \tfrac12$, $f_{22} = f_{33} = -\tfrac12$ on the sites of the string and zero elsewhere. For a field $f'$ put $X_{f'} = v\,W(gf') - w\,W(-gf')$, the recombiner $U_{f'} = p + q\,X_{f'}$ ($p^{2} + q^{2} = 1$) and the detection $O_{f'} = U_{f'}^{-1}P_{1}U_{f'}$; the bare ones are $U = p + q\,e_{2}$, $O = U^{-1}P_{1}U$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{(The model is the stored first-order equation in the frame's gauge; proved.)} For $h$ with $h(e_{0}) = 0$ and $k = \omega e_{0} + \kappa$, $\kappa$ orthogonal to $e_{0}$, with $\Pi[h] = \tfrac12(h - \mathrm{id}\,\tr h)$:
\[
e_{0}\cdot\mathcal{G}_{k}[h](e_{0}) = \tfrac12\bigl(\kappa\cdot h(\kappa) - Q(\kappa)\,\tr h\bigr), \qquad \mathcal{G}_{k}[h](e_{0}) - \bigl(e_{0}\cdot\mathcal{G}_{k}[h](e_{0})\bigr)e_{0} = -\omega\,\Pi[h](\kappa),
\]
and on a space direction $a$ the part of $\mathcal{G}_{k}[h](a)$ orthogonal to $e_{0}$ is $\omega^{2}\Pi[h](a) - G_{\kappa}[h](a)$ with $G_{\kappa}[h](a) = \tfrac12\bigl[(a\cdot\kappa)(h(\kappa) - \kappa\,\tr h) - Q(\kappa)h(a) + (a\cdot h(\kappa))\kappa - (\kappa\cdot h(\kappa) - Q(\kappa)\tr h)\,a\bigr]$. The stencils of $D$, $C_{i}$ and $G$ annihilate constants, those of $D$ and $G$ annihilate linear functions, and their leading symbols at $\kappa$ are these three expressions: $-\tfrac12 Dh$ for the first, $\omega\,C[\Pi[h]]$ for the second, $\omega^{2}\Pi[h] - G[h]$ for the third. The leading symbols do not tell a forward difference from a backward one; the placing of the differences is fixed by the exact identities of (2), not by this clause. With $\pi = \Pi[h']$, the prime the derivative in the time of the frame (so that $h' = 2p$), the constraints $C_{0}$, $C_{i}$ are the four first-order equations with no second derivative in that time (clause (v) of the stored theorem) for a source with mass density only, and the field equations above, $\pi' = -G[h]$ with $h' = 2p$ in steps of $\tau$, are the other six with no source.
\item[(2)] \emph{(Structure; proved, the count computed.)} $G[\delta_{\xi}] = 0$ and $D\,\delta_{\xi} = 0$; $\langle\pi, \delta_{\xi}\rangle = -2\sum\xi_{i}C_{i}[\pi]$; $C_{i}[G[h]] = 0$; $G$ is symmetric for the pairing; $\tr G[h] = -\tfrac12 Dh$; $D p = \sum_{i}b_{i}C_{i}[\pi]$. Hence the four constraints commute with one another, a step of the field equations preserves them, and each step is a canonical map. Further $G[h] = -\tfrac12\Delta h + \tfrac12\delta_{V} - \tfrac12\,\delta\,Dh$ with $V_{i} = C_{i}[h] - \tfrac12 s_{i}\tr h$, so on fields with $V = 0$ and $Dh = 0$ the field equations are the lattice wave equation $h(n+1) - 2h(n) + h(n-1) = \tau^{2}\Delta h(n)$. On the lattice of $27$ sites the fields with $Dh = 0$ modulo displacements have dimension $58 = 2\cdot 26 + 6$: two for each nonzero momentum, the transverse trace-free ones, and six at zero momentum.
\item[(3)] \emph{(Which rearrangements can be dressed; proved.)} If $f'$ vanishes outside a box on which the coordinates are single-valued, with a margin of one site, then $\sum_{x}(Df')(x) = 0$ and $\sum_{x}x_{k}\,(Df')(x) = 0$ for each coordinate $x_{k}$ of the site $x$: $Df'$ has no monopole and no dipole. So for one mass on two sites $a \neq b$, whose density difference $\delta_{a} - \delta_{b}$ has dipole $a - b$, no field supported in such a box has $Df' = \delta_{a} - \delta_{b}$. The two arrangements of the pair have the same mass and the same centre, $\rho^{\mathrm{w}} - \rho^{\mathrm{n}}$ has second moment $6$ along the axis, and $Df = \rho^{\mathrm{w}} - \rho^{\mathrm{n}}$ for the field $f$ on the string. For matter at rest the constraints $C_{i}$ ask nothing of a dressing by $W(f')$, which is a function of $\pi$.
\item[(3a)] \emph{(Curvature confined to a box; proved.)} If the curvature $G[f']$ vanishes outside a box on which the coordinates are single-valued, with a margin of one site, then again $\sum_{x}(Df')(x) = 0$ and $\sum_{x}x_{l}\,(Df')(x) = 0$, whatever the support of $f'$. The displacement by $\xi_{i} = 2x_{l}x_{i} - \delta_{il}\sum_{m}x_{m}^{2}$ is $4x_{l}\,\delta$ plus a constant field, exactly, and $Df' = -2\,\tr G[f']$. So for one mass on two sites no dressing by one Weyl operator has curvature confined to such a box: the detection dressed by any $f'$ with $Df' = \delta_{a} - \delta_{b}$ fails to commute with the curvature outside every such box. For example the dressing with no transverse trace-free part, $f' = \phi\,\delta$ with $-2\Delta\phi = \delta_{a} - \delta_{b}$, on the lattice of $729$ sites with $a$, $b$ neighbours, has $G[f'] \neq 0$ on $3590$ of the $4212$ components outside the $27$ sites about $b$ (computed). The obstruction is in the constraint $C_{0}$ alone: it does not depend on whether the field has its own variables.
\item[(4)] \emph{(Dressing; proved.)} $[C_{0}(s), U] = -g\,q\,(\rho^{\mathrm{w}} - \rho^{\mathrm{n}})(s)\,e_{1}$: the bare recombiner is not an observable when the field has its own variables. For every $f'$ with $Df' = \rho^{\mathrm{w}} - \rho^{\mathrm{n}}$: $X_{f'}^{2} = -1$, $U_{f'}^{-1} = p - q\,X_{f'}$, and $U_{f'}$ and $O_{f'} = p^{2}P_{1} + q^{2}P_{2} + pq\,(v\,W(gf') + w\,W(-gf'))$ commute with every constraint; $[h_{c}(x), O_{f'}] = g\,pq\,f'_{c}(x)\,X_{f'}$ and $[G[h]_{c}(x), O_{f'}] = g\,pq\,G[f']_{c}(x)\,X_{f'}$. On the lattice of $729$ sites the detection $O_{f}$ dressed on the string commutes with $h$ on the $4365$ components off the string and with the curvature on $4317$ of its $4374$ components; $G[f]$ is nonzero on $57$ components at $22$ sites around the string. The dressings treated are those by one Weyl operator.
\item[(5)] \emph{(The static slaved field; proved, the count computed.)} Let $\phi^{\mathrm{w}}, \phi^{\mathrm{n}}$ solve $-2\Delta\phi = \rho^{\mathrm{w}} - \bar\rho$ and $-2\Delta\phi = \rho^{\mathrm{n}} - \bar\rho$. The slaved field $h_{ij} = g\,(\phi^{\mathrm{w}}P_{1} + \phi^{\mathrm{n}}P_{2})\,\delta_{ij}$, $\pi = 0$ at one step, satisfies the constraints, has no transverse trace-free part, and its curvature $g\,\kappa\,P_{1}$ plus a number, $\kappa = G[(\phi^{\mathrm{w}} - \phi^{\mathrm{n}})\,\delta]$, is the same at every step (in this gauge $h$ itself changes, by a displacement). For every matter element $m$, $[G[h]_{c}(x), m] = g\,\kappa_{c}(x)\,[P_{1}, m]$. On the lattice of $729$ sites $\kappa \neq 0$ on $3858$ of the $4317$ components off the support of $G[f]$: there the static slaved field fails to commute with every matter element that does not commute with the mass density; for the bare detection $[G[h]_{c}(x), O] = g\,\kappa_{c}(x)\,pq\,e_{2}$.
\item[(6)] \emph{(The two parts; proved.)} With $u_{c,x}$ the solution of $\Delta u = D\epsilon_{c,x}$, $\epsilon_{c,x}$ the unit field on one component, and $w_{c} = 1$ for a diagonal component and $2$ otherwise, put $G^{L}[h]_{c}(x) = \tfrac{1}{4w_{c}}\sum_{s}u_{c,x}(s)\,(Dh)(s)$ and $G^{T} = G - G^{L}$. $G^{L}$ is a function of $Dh$ alone, so on states annihilated by the constraint it is an element of the matter algebra, and $G^{T}[h]$ is the curvature of a field with $D = 0$. Then $[G^{L}_{c}(x), O_{f}] = g\,pq\,\kappa_{c}(x)\,X_{f}$ and $[G^{T}_{c}(x), O_{f}] = g\,pq\,(G[f] - \kappa)_{c}(x)\,X_{f}$: off the support of $G[f]$ the constrained part and the transverse trace-free part each fail to commute with the dressed detection on the $3858$ components, by opposite amounts.
\item[(7)] \emph{(The constraint alone does not fix a slaved field; proved, the count computed.)} $h = g\,f\,P_{1}$ plus a number field, $\pi = 0$, is a slaved field, and its commutator with the bare detection, $g\,f\,pq\,e_{2}$ for $h$ and $g\,G[f]\,pq\,e_{2}$ for the curvature, vanishes off the string and off the support of $G[f]$. It is not static: after two steps its curvature has changed on $499$ components, while its constrained part stays $g\kappa P_{1}$.
\item[(8)] \emph{(Dynamics; computed.)} Lattice of $17^{3} = 4913$ sites, steps $n = 0, \dots, 6$, detection at $n_{d} = 3$. A step changes a component only through the stencils of $G$ and the trace; the \emph{depth} of a component of $h$ is the least number of steps after which data on the nine components where $f$ is nonzero can reach it, and an event (a curvature component at a site, at a step $n$) is \emph{outside the cone of the detection} when every component of $h$ that enters it has depth greater than $|n - n_{d}|$. This is the lattice's cone: it moves one site a step along the axes, twice the speed of the lattice wave of (2), and every cone and count of this clause and of (9) is in that sense. There are $206346$ events, $202171$ of them outside the cone. With the field's own variables, $[G[h]_{c}(x)(n), O_{f}(n_{d})] = g\,pq\,G[S(n)]_{c}(x)\,X_{f}$, with $S$ the solution of the field equations with data $h = f$, $\pi = 0$ at step $n_{d}$: it vanishes at all $202171$, and so do the commutators of $h$ and $\pi$ themselves outside the cone. The retarded slaved field, $h^{\mathrm{sl}}(n) = P_{1}h^{\mathrm{w}}(n) + P_{2}h^{\mathrm{n}}(n)$ with $h^{\mathrm{w}} - h^{\mathrm{n}} = g\,R(n)$ and $R$ the solution with data $f$, $0$ at step $0$ (the two arrangements made at step $0$ from a common one by a motion along the string), satisfies the constraints at every step and has $[G[h^{\mathrm{sl}}]_{c}(x)(n), O] = g\,G[R(n)]_{c}(x)\,pq\,e_{2}$, nonzero at $8736$ of the $202171$ events, none before step $2$ and $1304$ of them not later than the detection. Of the events outside the lattice's cone of the detection, $9310$ lie inside the lattice's cone of the source event (the string at step $0$); the failures are $8736$ of those. Against a cone of unit speed, half a site a step: let the \emph{base site} of a component be the site at which it is indexed in the stencils of (2), the plaquette components included, and let $d$ be the Euclidean distance, the least-image distance on the periodic lattice, from the base site of a curvature component to the nearest of the $22$ sites where $G[f]$ is nonzero; an event is inside the unit-speed cone of the source event when $d \le n/2$ and outside the unit-speed cone of the detection when $d > |n - n_{d}|/2$ (the inequalities as written). Then $3490$ of the failures are inside the unit-speed cone of the source event, and $5246$ have $d > n/2$, where a wave of unit speed from the source has not arrived and the lattice's field is nonzero only because the lattice's cone is wider. Of the $3490$, $671$ are not later than the detection and $3423$ are outside the unit-speed cone of the detection. So, unlike the stored lattice Maxwell model with its exact cone, this model has failures at events that a cone of unit speed would call spacelike to the source as well; the statement that survives a change of cone is the one about the $3423$ of them that are also outside the unit-speed cone of the detection, $d > |n - n_{d}|/2$ ($604$ of them not later than the detection); the other $67$ are at the step of the detection at sites where $G[f]$ is nonzero.
\item[(9)] \emph{(A coupled device; proved, the counts computed.)} Take Hypothesis (D) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.with-a-coupled-device-the-slaved-field-failure-is-a-first-order-signal-and-a-survey-finds-no-stored-bound} with the device, a number $\lambda$, coupled to one curvature component at one event: $V = \exp(-\lambda\,\iota\,G[h]_{c}(x)(n))$, $\iota$ the unit of the phases. For the retarded slaved field $\iota$ is the pseudoscalar $I$ of $\Cl(1,2) \supset \Cl(1,1)$ as in that theorem, $V = e^{-I\vartheta}P_{1} + P_{2}$ up to a phase, $\vartheta = \lambda\,g\,G[R(n)]_{c}(x)$, and in the state $\tfrac12(1 + a\,e_{1} + b\,Ie_{2})$ the detection probability changes by $pq\,[a(\cos\vartheta - 1) + b\sin\vartheta]$, of first order $\lambda\,g\,G[R(n)]_{c}(x)\,pq\,b$: nonzero at $1304$ of the $115792$ events outside the cone of the detection and not later than it. With the field's own variables $\iota = I_{4}$, $V$ is a Weyl operator of the field, $V^{-1}W(\pm gf)V = e^{\pm I_{4}\theta}\,W(\pm gf)$ with $\theta = \lambda\,g\,G[S(n)]_{c}(x)$, and $V^{-1}O_{f}V = p^{2}P_{1} + q^{2}P_{2} + pq\,(e^{I_{4}\theta}v\,W(gf) + e^{-I_{4}\theta}w\,W(-gf))$: at every event outside the cone $\theta = 0$ and the device leaves the detection unchanged, at every order in $\lambda$. The two units are not identified: $I$ multiplies matter elements in the slaved variant, $I_{4}$ is the unit of the field's Weyl operators in the other.
\item[(10)] \emph{(Two space dimensions; proved, the count computed.)} In two space dimensions the Einstein map of the space directions vanishes identically, and on the periodic square lattice of $16$ sites the fields with $Dh = 0$ modulo displacements are the three constant ones: there is no free part at nonzero momentum, and the invariant content of $h$ is $Dh$, which the constraint ties to the mass density site by site. So there a slaved field is fixed up to displacements and constants, its spatial scalar curvature $Dh$ is $g\rho_{s}$, and its invariant content commutes with every matter element away from the masses; three space dimensions is the least with a free part, in agreement with clause (vi) of the stored theorem.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Remark (a slaved field fitted to one detection; computed).} The slaved field $N(n) + S(n)P_{1}$, with $N$ a number solution completing the constraint, obeys the constraints and the field equations and commutes with the detection at step $3$ at all $202171$ events; against a detection at step $5$ it fails at $1252$ of the $198771$ events outside that detection's cone.

\emph{What the model is and is not.} It is the stored first-order field equation, in one gauge, on a lattice, with a source at rest that has mass density only, and it is exact over the rationals. It is not a model of gravity beyond first order: the field does not source itself and the four constraints commute, where the constraints of the full theory do not. The matter has no momentum and no stress, so the constraints $C_{i}$ carry no matter term and the model does not treat a detection that changes them. The cone is the lattice's own, one site a step, wider than the cone of a wave of unit speed, which moves half a site a step at $\tau = \tfrac12$: outside the lattice's cone the commutators vanish exactly, and every failure counted in (8) is outside it. The lattice has the symmetry of the cube and no rotations, so helicity two has no meaning on it; it enters only through the continuum symbol and clause (vi) of the stored theorem. The frame's gauge and the time step leave no Lorentz covariance. On the periodic lattice the total mass is cancelled by $\bar\rho$ and there are no charges at infinity: moments are defined on a box only. The motion that takes one arrangement to the other is not modelled. Neither the rotation-gauge field nor any spinor appears.

\emph{Reading (argued).} The stored lattice Maxwell model (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.two-dimensional-lattice-maxwell-coulomb-slaved-field-fails-and-string-dressed-detection-commutes}) left the step to gravity as an argument by analogy. This model takes the step at first order, and three of its conclusions are the same. A field with no variable of its own is not fixed by the constraints ((7)); the static one, the Newtonian field, fails to commute with the detection at a distance ((5)); the retarded one fails at events outside the lattice's cone of the detection and inside that of the source event ((8)), a signal of first order for a coupled device ((9)). With the field's own variables a detection dressed on the string commutes with the field outside its cone, and the device then has no effect at any order; this much holds in any local canonical lattice model once a dressing of compact support exists. One conclusion is new relative to the stored Maxwell model (it is standard; see the prior art in the note): the Hamiltonian constraint is a double divergence, so a dressing of compact support, or with curvature confined to a box, exists only for a rearrangement that keeps the mass and the centre ((3), (3a)). One mass moved alone between two sites has none: its detection dressed by any field fails to commute with the curvature outside every box. Stated against the condition registered beforehand, that with the field's own variables no dressed detection commutes at every event outside the cone: the condition is met for one mass moved alone and not met for the pair. That the former is a restriction on which detections are local, and not a failure of the field with its own variables, is a reading made after the computation, argued as follows. The mass dipole is a charge of the constraint $C_{0}$, which no dressing screens, in the slaved variant as in the other and whatever the free part is made of; a motion that takes one arrangement to the other with momentum conserved carries its recoil somewhere, and $f$ is the first-order field of such a motion: with $f = 2p$ the corresponding $\pi$ has the one component $\pi_{11} = \tfrac12$ on the string, and $C_{1}[\pi]$ is nonzero on exactly the two links that the masses cross (computed), the string carrying the momentum between them; for a mass that recoils against a distant body the dressing reaches that body. The experiment that motivates the problem, one mass on two paths (Prediction \claimref{pred.gravity-entangles-standard-phase}), is of the single-mass type: the model gives it a local dressed detection only with its recoil partner included, and the model, having no momentum of matter, does not contain that motion. The statement that a dressed detection commutes outside its cone once a dressing of compact support exists holds in any local canonical lattice model and is not specific to spin two.

## technical
An exact lattice model of the stored first-order gravitational field: the symmetric field in the gauge $h(e_{0}) = 0$ of a frame, on a periodic cubic lattice, diagonal components on sites and off-diagonal ones on plaquettes, with the four constraints and the six field equations of the stored first-order Einstein map for a source at rest. The lattice operators are invariant under displacements, satisfy the contracted identity exactly, and leave two free pairs for each nonzero momentum. Matter is a pair of equal masses in one of two arrangements about one centre, in the algebra of a completed pair. Every statement below is a statement about this model: it has the cube's symmetry and no rotations, no Lorentz covariance, commuting constraints, matter with no momentum or stress, and the lattice's own cone.

Status of the clauses: (1), (2), (3), (3a), (4), (6) and the identities of (5), (7), (9), (10) are proved; the counts in (2), (3a), (4), (5), (7), (8), (9), (10) and the Remark are computed in exact rational arithmetic; the two Readings are argued. Everything said of the series is conditional on the named hypothesis (TT), that the two transverse trace-free directions at null momentum carry a canonical pair as the photon's modes do; the model's clauses are not.

The Hamiltonian constraint is a double divergence. A field supported in a box, or with curvature confined to a box, has a constraint source with no monopole and no dipole. So one mass on two sites has no dressing of compact support and none with curvature confined to a box; its dressings, such as the Newtonian one, reach everywhere. The pair of arrangements with one mass and one centre has a dressing on the three sites between the masses. Against the condition registered before the computation (no dressed detection commutes at every event outside the cone): met for one mass moved alone, not met for the pair.

With no variable of its own: the static slaved field, the Newtonian one, differs in curvature between the arrangements on $3858$ of $4317$ components away from the string ($729$ sites) and fails to commute there with every matter element that does not commute with the mass density; a string-shaped slaved field commutes at one time and is not static; the retarded slaved field fails at $8736$ events outside the lattice's cone of the detection (one site a step, twice the wave speed), which are $8736$ of the $9310$ such events inside the lattice's cone of the source event ($4913$ sites, seven steps), and a coupled device then changes the detection probability at first order at $1304$ events not later than the detection. Against a cone of unit speed, $3490$ of the $8736$ are inside the cone of the source event, $3423$ of these also outside the unit-speed cone of the detection, and $5246$ are outside the source's, lattice precursors that a wave of unit speed would not produce.

With the field's own variables: the detection of the pair dressed on the string is an observable, commutes with the curvature at all $202171$ events outside the lattice's cone, and a coupled device leaves it unchanged there at every order, as in any local canonical lattice model once a dressing of compact support exists. The constrained part and the transverse trace-free part of the curvature fail separately and cancel.

In two space dimensions there is no free part and the invariant content of a slaved field is its source.

Ledger: transport-absent. The canonical first-order field, its constraints, the dressing of sources and the conserved dipole are standard; nothing here is new to the literature. New relative to the store: an exact model with the gravitational constraints in place of the argument by analogy from the lattice Maxwell model, the statement of which rearrangements can be dressed, and the match of the lattice operators with the stored first-order map.

## lay
An earlier result built a small exact model of the electromagnetic field on a grid and found that a field which only repeats what its source did fails a test of locality, while a field with wave-making freedom of its own passes it. It said that the step from electromagnetism to gravity was an argument by analogy.

This result takes that step for weak gravity, in a model. The model is the series' own equation for a weak gravitational field, put on a three-dimensional grid, with the four equations that tie the field to its source at every instant. A grid has no rotations and its own speed limit, twice the speed of its waves, so what follows is about the model and is not a statement about gravity itself.

Three findings carry over. In the model, a field with no freedom of its own is not pinned down by those four equations. In the model, the natural choice, the ordinary Newtonian field, fails the locality test at a distance. The choice that follows the field's law of motion from the source's earlier move fails at places that the earlier move could have influenced and the later measurement could not, and a nearby device could turn that into a signal; about two fifths of those places remain when the grid's speed limit is replaced by the speed of the waves. If the two wave-making components are quantum variables in their own right, there is a way of attaching field to matter for which the test is passed everywhere outside the measurement's reach on the grid and the device sees nothing. That last fact is not special to gravity: it holds in any model of this kind once the attached field can be kept to a small region.

One finding is new to the series, though known elsewhere. In the model (and, by an argument, in weak gravity generally) the attached field can be kept to a small region only when the matter is rearranged without shifting its centre of mass. A single mass moved alone from one place to another cannot be dressed in this way, and for it the test fails; a pair of masses moved symmetrically can. This reflects the fact that nothing moves without something else recoiling. The experiment that prompted the question has a single mass in two places, so for it the model's answer needs the recoiling partner to be included, which the model does not contain.

Whether the series gives the two components quantum variables is a hypothesis named here, not a decision taken; the model shows what follows either way, for weak fields only.
