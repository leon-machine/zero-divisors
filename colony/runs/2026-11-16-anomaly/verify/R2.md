### Points A to C

**A. Algebra.**
- The spinor module is the minimal left ideal S of Cl(0,6), real dimension 8, which is complex dimension 4 over
  span{1, J}. This is the full spinor module of S^5: the Clifford map X -> Xn is even and commutes with J, and
  Cl^+(0,6) is M_4 over span{1, J}.
- dim_R M_k = 8 C(k+4,4), so the complex multiplicity is 4 C(k+4,4). This is the classical multiplicity
  2^[n/2] C(k+n-1, k) of the round S^5 Dirac spectrum, quoted from memory.
- Eigenvalues are -(k+5/2) on M_k and +(k+5/2) on xM_k, with the store's signs (Q(e) = -1, Clifford map Xn).
  The overall sign of D is a convention (orientation and Clifford map) and only fixes the overall sign of eta.
- "Per Weyl component": by the stored claim, each complex component of V_+ is one Weyl component. W_+ is S
  tensored with V_+ over the unit, so each component carries one copy of S, which is one five-dimensional Dirac
  spinor. That matches the literature's anomaly-theory normalisation (one 5d Dirac operator per 4d Weyl field).
- The general-degree statement is **proved**, not only checked to degree 3. The Fischer-adjointness argument
  (multiplication by x is minus the adjoint of the vector derivative, and is injective) is valid at every k. I
  checked the full spectrum to k = 5.

**B. The lift of the antipodal map.**
- A = J a*, and A^2 = J^2 = -1. On M_k, A = (-1)^k J; on xM_k, A = (-1)^{k+1} J, using J x = -x J.
- J and omega commute and both square to -1, so J tensor omega is an involution.
  - On W_+ (where J tensor 1 = 1 tensor I) it is +1, so A tensor omega acts as Psi(x) -> Psi(-x) and the fields
    on RP^5 are the even fields.
  - On W_- it is -1.
- Result: eta(0) = -1/8 for even fields and +1/8 for odd fields, per complex component of V_+ (one rank-4 spinor
  bundle). No eigenvalue is zero, so xi = eta/2 exactly, with no zero-mode term: xi = -1/16 per component.

  - T RP^n + 2L is spin, but RP^n is not, iff n = 1 mod 4.
  - C(p,2) + C(q,2) = m + q mod 2 for p + q = 2m.
  - Lens spaces L^{2k+1}(4): w_2 = (k+1)u, every a in H^1(Z_2) has a^2 = 0, so L^5(4) has neither a spin nor a
    Spin-Z4 structure.
  - (iii): from w(E+)w(E-) = (1+x)^{2m}, I get w_1(E+) = w_1(E-) = t and w_2(E+) + w_2(E-) = m x^2 + t^2. Correct.
  - Definite side: class m x^2; far side: class (m+1) x^2. Exactly one side needs the twist on any flat wall,
    because the two sides' q differ by one.
- **K6: torus involutions, with my own textbook charge table.**
  - The table is Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c, nu^c with hypercharges 1/6, -2/3, 1/3, -1/2, 1, 0, and sum Y = sum Y^3 = 0.

## E. Together: which branch of the success clause, and at what level

The success clause has three branches, all on a reading without the Majorana term.

**Branch 1: "the anomaly of omega on RP^5 vanishes exactly for sixteen components per generation."**
- Not established as a stored statement.
- What is established:
  - xi = (n_+ - n_-)/16 for the structure (A, omega), up to an overall sign. This is proved in proposal 1, given
    classical analysis (A).
  - The signed count is 0 mod 16 for sixteen-component generations, and n_g mod 16 for fifteen-component ones,
    across the whole coset. This is proved arithmetic in proposal 2.
- The step "integral xi <=> anomaly vanishes" is the Dai-Freed/GEM statement, which is not imported (decision of
  9 November). So branch 1 holds only **conditionally** on that literature statement.
- Even then, "exactly for sixteen" is false as worded: fifteen-component generations pass when 16 divides n_g. It
  is true for n_g = 3. Both proposals say this.

**Branch 2: "a proof that it does not constrain the content."**
- Proposal 2's Reading (b) gives this at the **argued** level only. It rests on:
  - GEM sec. 2.1, unimported: a global anomaly is no inconsistency;
  - a stage-by-stage reading of the chain, with the u(5) citation error above.
- "Constrains no stored stage" is **argued, not proved**.

**Branch 3: "the reading is excluded."** Not addressed. The stored premise that the Majorana term is realised is a
premise, not an exclusion.

**Overlap and consistency.**
- The two proposals agree in sign. Proposal 1's xi = (n_+ - n_-)/16 with omega = -I on V_+ gives -1 for sixteen,
  matching proposal 2's count of -16.
- They agree in modulus.
- They disagree only in naming: proposal 2's (H1) calls 1/16 "the spectral asymmetry", where proposal 1 calls 1/16
  xi and 1/8 the asymmetry.
- They use different twist elements: omega of the slot module in proposal 1, j of the jump plane in proposal 2.
  Proposal 2 states that no stored result identifies the two.
- Both restate the stored clause (4) arithmetic: proposal 1 in the last sentence of (v), proposal 2 in (vi). That
  duplication is modest, and proposal 2's coset enumeration is the new part.
- Proposal 2 cites proposal 1 as hypothesis (H1). When proposal 1 is accepted, (H1) should become a `uses` edge.

**Net:** real progress, with the third attack item's fixed-point count now derived. The success clause is met only
conditionally (branch 1) or at the argued level (branch 2), never at the proved level.
