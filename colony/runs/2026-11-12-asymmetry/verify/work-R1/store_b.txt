##### synthesis.imported-jarlskog-commutator-identity | version 2 | status imported | kind claim
TITLE: Imported: the Jarlskog commutator identity (Jarlskog 1985)
FORMAL: Numbers, and the entries of the matrices, are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$ (conventions U1; the source writes $i$ for $I_{4}$, $\mathrm{Im}$ is the $I_{4}$-part and $^{*}$ conjugates $I_{4} \mapsto -I_{4}$). Let $H_{u}$, $H_{d}$ be $3 \times 3$ Hermitian matrices with eigenvalues $m_{u}^{2} < m_{c}^{2} < m_{t}^{2}$ and $m_{d}^{2} < m_{s}^{2} < m_{b}^{2}$, $H_{q} = U_{q}\,\mathrm{diag}(\cdot)\,U_{q}^{\dagger}$ with $U_{q}$ unitary, and $V = U_{u}^{\dagger}U_{d}$ (rows $u, c, t$, columns $d, s, b$). Let $J$ be defined by $\mathrm{Im}[V_{ij}V_{kl}V_{il}^{*}V_{kj}^{*}] = J\sum_{m,n}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jln}$, so that $J = \mathrm{Im}(V_{us}V_{cb}V_{ub}^{*}V_{cs}^{*})$; it does not depend on the phases of the columns of $U_{u}$ and $U_{d}$. Then
\[
\det[H_{u}, H_{d}] = 2I_{4}J\,F_{u}F_{d},\qquad F_{u} = (m_{t}^{2} - m_{c}^{2})(m_{t}^{2} - m_{u}^{2})(m_{c}^{2} - m_{u}^{2}),\quad F_{d} = (m_{b}^{2} - m_{s}^{2})(m_{b}^{2} - m_{d}^{2})(m_{s}^{2} - m_{d}^{2}).
\]
In particular, with nondegenerate masses in each sector, $J = 0$ if and only if $\det[H_{u}, H_{d}] = 0$; a common eigenvector of $H_{u}$ and $H_{d}$ gives $\det[H_{u}, H_{d}] = 0$ and so $J = 0$. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{Jarlskog1985}; the definition of $J$ as in \cite{PDG2024}.
