\begin{proof}
Throughout $c = \cos kx^{1}$, $s = \sin kx^{1}$, a prime is $d/d\eta$, and "first order" means first order in $(\phi, \psi, b)$.

\emph{The form of the field.} A first-order scalar perturbation of the derived metric has four functions (the two above the slices, $\phi$ and $b$, and two within them, $\psi$ and a second-derivative term $\partial_{j}\partial_{l}E$). A displacement within the slices, $x^{j} \mapsto x^{j} + \partial_{j}\lambda$ (clause (e) of Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}), changes $E$ by $\lambda$ and $b$ by a multiple of $\lambda'$ and leaves the slices and $\psi$ alone, and a rotor field changes the frame and not the derived metric; so the form displayed is general for a given slicing.

\emph{(i)} The torsion equation of clause (f) of the Definition, $D\wedge\hb^{T}(a) = \hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge a)$, is solved to first order by
\[
\Omega(e_{0}) = (k\phi + b' + \mathcal{H}b)\,s\,e_{0}e_{1}, \qquad
\Omega(e_{1}) = -\bigl(\mathcal{H} - (\mathcal{H}\phi + \mathcal{H}\psi + \psi')\,c\bigr)e_{0}e_{1},
\]
\[
\Omega(e_{j}) = -\bigl(\mathcal{H} - (\mathcal{H}\phi + \mathcal{H}\psi + \psi')\,c\bigr)e_{0}e_{j} + (k\psi - \mathcal{H}b)\,s\,e_{1}e_{j} \quad (j = 2, 3),
\]
(the spatial part of $\Omega(e_{j})$ carries the combination $k\Psi$ of (ii)). With $R(a\wedge b)$ of clause (c), $\mathcal{R}(B) = -R(\hb(B))$ and the contractions of clause (c), the Einstein map on the unit normal $n = e_{0} + bs\,e_{1}$ (orthogonal to the three vectors $\hb^{-1}(e_{j})$, $n^{2} = 1$ to first order) and the unit vector $m_{1} = e_{1} + bs\,e_{0}$ is
\[
n\cdot\mathcal{G}(n) = -\frac{3\mathcal{H}^{2}}{a^{2}} + \frac{2}{a^{2}}\bigl(k^{2}\psi + 3\mathcal{H}(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi) - \mathcal{H}kb\bigr)c, \qquad
m_{1}\cdot\mathcal{G}(n) = \frac{2}{a^{2}}\,k(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi)\,s .
\]
The covariant divergence of the normal, with $D = \hb^{T}(\partial_{a})D_{a}$, is
\[
\theta = D\cdot n = \frac{3\mathcal{H}}{a} + \frac{1}{a}\bigl(-3(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi) + kb\bigr)c ,
\]
so $H = \mathcal{H}/a$ and $a\kappa = -3(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi) + kb$. The slice's induced field is $\hb^{-1}(e_{j}) = a(1 - \psi c)e_{j}$ on the three spatial vectors, a field on the vectors of $\Cl(0,3)$; its scalar curvature, in the sign in which the round sphere is positive, is $-4(k/a)^{2}\psi c$ to first order (the conformal factor $1 - \psi c$ of a flat field contributes $4\nabla^{2}\psi/a^{2}$). The check computes $\Omega$ from the torsion equation, the field strength, its contractions, the divergence and the slice's curvature exactly for generic rational Taylor data of $(a, \phi, \psi, b)$ at rational points, with the lab's exact engine for these fields, and confirms each coefficient above, the Gauss relation ${}^{3}R + \tfrac23\theta^{2} + 2n\cdot\mathcal{G}(n) = 0$ at zeroth and first order, and the sign of the slice curvature on the unit sphere; each displayed expression is linear in the Taylor data of $(\phi, \psi, b)$ with coefficients rational in $(a, a', k)$, so agreement on generic samples at two backgrounds and two wavenumbers fixes it on those samples (sampled, not symbolic).

By Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(iv) these are Einstein's equations for the derived metric, and the field equation of clause (f) with $\Lambda = 0$ reads $\mathcal{G}(a) = -8\pi G\,T(a)$. Contracting with $n$ twice: the background gives $3H^{2} = 8\pi G\rho$, and first order gives $2(k/a)^{2}\psi - 2H\kappa = -8\pi G\rho\,\delta = -3H^{2}\delta$, the first relation. Contracting with $n$ and $m_{1}$: $\tfrac{2}{a^{2}}k(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi)s = -8\pi G\,m_{1}\cdot T(n) = 8\pi G(\rho + p)\,v\,s$, and $8\pi G(\rho + p) = 3(1 + w)\mathcal{H}^{2}/a^{2}$ gives $v$. (For a fluid $T(a) = (\rho + p)(a\cdot V)V - pa$ with $V = n + vs\,m_{1}$, $m_{1}\cdot T(n) = (\rho + p)(m_{1}\cdot V) = -(\rho + p)vs$ since $m_{1}^{2} = -1$, and $n\cdot T(n) = \rho$ to first order.) The slice's curvature is $-4(k/a)^{2}\psi c = (6H^{2}\delta - 4H\kappa)c = 6H^{2}(\delta - 2\delta_{H})c$. With $\Omega_{\Sigma} = 8\pi G\,n\cdot T(n)/(\theta^{2}/3)$, $\Omega_{\Sigma} - 1 = (\delta - 2\kappa/3H)c$ to first order. Given $(\psi, \delta, u)$ at one $\eta$, the first relation gives $\kappa$, the second gives $\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi = \tfrac32(1 + w)\mathcal{H}u$ and the third gives $b$; the remaining components of the field equation are evolution equations (they contain $\psi''$ or $b'$) and do not restrict the data at one $\eta$, so the three data are free.

\emph{(ii)} A first-order change of the time label, $\eta \mapsto \eta + \xi(\eta)c$, keeps the form of the field and acts by $\phi \mapsto \phi + \xi' + \mathcal{H}\xi$, $\psi \mapsto \psi - \mathcal{H}\xi$, $b \mapsto b - k\xi$ (the Lie derivative of the background field along $\xi c\,e_{0}$; the sign of $\xi$ is immaterial). Then $\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi \mapsto \psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi + (\mathcal{H}^{2} - \mathcal{H}')\xi$, and the background equations give $\mathcal{H}^{2} - \mathcal{H}' = 4\pi Ga^{2}(\rho + p) = \tfrac32(1 + w)\mathcal{H}^{2}$, so $u \mapsto u + \mathcal{H}\xi$; and the first-order part of $n\cdot\mathcal{G}(n)$ changes by $\tfrac{2}{a^{2}}\cdot 3\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}^{2} - \mathcal{H}')\xi c$, so $\delta \mapsto \delta - 3(1 + w)\mathcal{H}\xi$. The four combinations are therefore unchanged. Eliminating $\kappa$ between the first and third relations of (i), $k^{2}\psi - \mathcal{H}kb = -\tfrac32\mathcal{H}^{2}\delta - 3\mathcal{H}(\psi' + \mathcal{H}\phi) = -\tfrac32\mathcal{H}^{2}(\delta + 3(1 + w)u)$. Choosing $\xi = -u/\mathcal{H}$ gives the slicing with $v = 0$, orthogonal to the matter flow, on which $\psi$ is $\mathcal{R}$ and $\delta$ is $\delta_{c}$. The check verifies the shifts and the invariance with the engine's own output on generic backgrounds (generic $w$), not with the formulas of (i).

\emph{(iii)} For a perfect fluid the stress on the slice is isotropic. In the slicing $b = 0$ the engine gives $m_{1}\cdot\mathcal{G}(m_{1}) - e_{2}\cdot\mathcal{G}(e_{2}) = -k^{2}(\phi - \psi)c/a^{2}$, so $\phi = \psi = \Psi$. With $a = \eta$ (the radiation field, $\mathcal{H} = 1/\eta$), $m_{1}\cdot\mathcal{G}(m_{1}) = -8\pi Gp$ and $p = \rho/3$ at first order give $m_{1}\cdot\mathcal{G}(m_{1}) - \tfrac13 n\cdot\mathcal{G}(n) = 0$, and the engine's output for the left side is $-\tfrac{2}{a^{2}}\bigl(\Psi'' + 4\Psi'/\eta + \tfrac13k^{2}\Psi\bigr)c$. In $x = k\eta/\sqrt3$ this is $\Psi_{xx} + 4\Psi_{x}/x + \Psi = 0$, solved by $g$ and $d$ (spherical Bessel functions of order one over $x$), with Wronskian $g_{x}d - gd_{x} = 3/x^{4}$. In this slicing $u = (\eta\Psi' + \Psi)/2$ by (i), so $\mathcal{R} = \tfrac32\Psi + \tfrac12x\Psi_{x}$ and, by (ii), $\delta_{c} = -2x^{2}\Psi$. For $d$: $d_{x} = \cos x/x^{2} - 3d/x$, so $\mathcal{R}[d] = \tfrac32d + \tfrac12\cos x/x - \tfrac32d = \cos x/(2x)$. For $g$: $\mathcal{R}[g] = \tfrac32 - \tfrac14x^{2} + \dots$. For $\Psi = A_{0}g + Dd$,
\[
\mathcal{R}\,(\cos x + x\sin x) + \tfrac14\delta_{c}\cos x = A_{0}\bigl(\mathcal{R}[g](\cos x + x\sin x) - \tfrac12x^{2}g\cos x\bigr) + D\bigl(\tfrac{\cos x}{2x}(\cos x + x\sin x) - \tfrac12x^{2}d\cos x\bigr),
\]
and the second bracket vanishes identically since $x^{2}d = (\cos x + x\sin x)/x$. The first bracket is constant: $(\cos x + x\sin x, \tfrac12x^{2}\cos x)$ is proportional to $(d, \mathcal{R}[d])$, namely $x^{3}(d, \mathcal{R}[d])$, so the bracket is $x^{3}(\mathcal{R}[g]d - \mathcal{R}[d]g) = \tfrac12x^{4}(g_{x}d - gd_{x}) = \tfrac32$. Hence $\tfrac32A_{0} = \mathcal{R}(\cos x + x\sin x) + \tfrac14\delta_{c}\cos x$ at every $x$, which is the first form of $\zeta_{\infty}$; both sides are slice independent by (ii), and substituting $\mathcal{R} = \psi + u$, $\delta_{c} = \delta + 4u$ gives the second. The check verifies the series of $g$ and $d$, the Wronskian, $\mathcal{R}[d]$ and the projection identity on both solutions in exact rational series to order $x^{18}$. For $x_{d} \ll 1$, $\cos x_{d} + x_{d}\sin x_{d} = 1 + \tfrac12x_{d}^{2} + \dots$, and $\psi_{\Sigma} = -\tfrac32(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{2}(\delta - 2\delta_{H})$ by (i), $u = (a_{d}H_{d}/k)v$, which gives the last display. That the constant $\zeta_{\infty}$ is the curvature perturbation carried outside the Hubble length to re-entry is Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-superhorizon-zeta-conservation}; the Bardeen potential of the growing solution is $\tfrac23\mathcal{R}$, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-large-scale-potential-and-sachs-wolfe-relations}.

\emph{(iv)} Under (UE), $\kappa = 0$ and (i) gives $\psi_{\Sigma} = -\delta_{\Sigma}/(2x_{d}^{2})$ (since $(aH/k)^{2} = 1/(3x^{2})$); substituting in (iii) gives the bracket, whose series is $\tfrac{1}{2x^{2}} + (\tfrac14 - \tfrac14) + (\tfrac18 - \tfrac1{16})x^{2} + \dots = \tfrac{1}{2x^{2}} + \tfrac{1}{16}x^{2} - \tfrac{1}{144}x^{4} + \dots$. Under (LC), $\psi_{\Sigma} = 0$. With $u_{\Sigma} = 0$ as well, $\mathcal{R}_{\Sigma} = 0$ and $\zeta = \tfrac14\delta_{\Sigma}$ on the slice while $\zeta_{\infty} = -\tfrac14\delta_{\Sigma}\cos x_{d}$: such a datum is not a pure growing solution (its $\Psi = -\delta_{\Sigma}/(2x_{d}^{2})$ is mostly the decaying one), and the two values agree in magnitude at leading order. The pure growing datum on a flat slice has $u = -\tfrac14\delta(1 + O(x^{2}))$ and $\zeta_{\infty} = +\tfrac14\delta$: the field of delays of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.adiabatic-deposit-modulation-is-a-time-shift}. For $\delta_{H} = \tfrac12(1 - \lambda)\delta$, $\psi_{\Sigma} = -\lambda\delta/(2x_{d}^{2})$ and the series starts $\tfrac{\lambda}{2x^{2}} + \tfrac14(\lambda - 1)$; the two terms are equal in size where $(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{2} = |\lambda - 1|/(6|\lambda|)$.

\emph{(v)} With uncorrelated $\delta$ and $v$ the spectra add: $\Delta^{2}_{\zeta} = C_{\delta}^{2}\Delta^{2}_{\delta} + C_{v}^{2}\Delta^{2}_{v}$ with the coefficients of (iv) at leading order, $(C_{\delta}, C_{v}) = (\tfrac32(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{2}, 2a_{d}H_{d}/k)$ under (UE) and $(\tfrac14, 2a_{d}H_{d}/k)$ under (LC). A factor $(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{2m}$ on $\Delta^{2} \propto k^{3 + s}$ gives index $n_{s} - 1 = 3 + s - 2m$: $s - 1$ for $m = 2$, $s' + 1$ for $m = 1$, $s + 3$ for $m = 0$; the smaller index dominates as $k \to 0$. Under (UE) with $s = 0$, $\Delta^{2}_{\zeta} \propto 1/k$ and $\int_{k_{1}}\Delta^{2}_{\zeta}\,dk/k = \Delta^{2}_{\zeta}(k_{1})$, which exceeds one below the $k_{1}$ at which $\Delta^{2}_{\zeta}(k_{1}) = 1$: the first-order treatment holds on $k \gg k_{1}$ only.

\emph{(vi)} For a gauge-invariant quasi-free state diagonal in momentum the variance of a one-body observable is $\Tr(fEf(1 - E))$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.quasi-free-deposit-is-vacuum-plus-shot-noise}(i)); for the energy and the momentum component $P_{1}$ of a large box of volume $V$ this is $V\int\omega_{p}^{2}n_{p}(1 - n_{p})\,d^{3}p/(2\pi)^{3}$ and $V\int p_{1}^{2}n_{p}(1 - n_{p})\,d^{3}p/(2\pi)^{3}$ per state. For Fermi-Dirac occupations $n(1 - n) = -T\,\partial n/\partial\omega$ (for Bose-Einstein occupations $n(1 + n) = -T\,\partial n/\partial\omega$, with variance $\Tr(fEf(1 + E))$), and for massless quanta one integration by parts gives $\int\omega^{2}(-T\partial_{\omega}n)\,d^{3}p = 4T\int\omega n\,d^{3}p$. The occupations are those of the I-state (Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state}) read at $T_{d}$ on the descent slice. So $\mathrm{Var}(E)/V = 4\rho T$ and, by isotropy, $\mathrm{Var}(P_{1})/V = \tfrac43\rho T = T(\rho + p)$: $P_{\delta} = 4T/\rho$ and $P_{v} = T/(\rho + p)$ at $k \to 0$, flat for $k \ll aT$. Then under (UE), with $k/a_{d}$ the physical wavenumber, $\Delta^{2}_{\zeta} = \tfrac94(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{4}\,(k/a_{d})^{3}\,4T_{d}/(2\pi^{2}\rho)$, and $\rho = \tfrac{\pi^{2}}{30}gT_{d}^{4}$ gives the coefficient $\tfrac94\cdot4\cdot30/2 = 135$ over $\pi^{4}g$. With $H_{d}/T_{d} = \pi\sqrt{g/90}\,T_{d}/\bar M_{P}$ and $a_{d}T_{d}\,g^{1/3} = a_{0}T_{0}\,g_{s0}^{1/3}$ (entropy conserved, as in Proposition \claimref{synthesis.finite-range-descent-deposit-cannot-seed-cmb}(iii)), $\Delta^{2}_{\zeta}(k_{*}) = \tfrac{135}{\pi^{4}g}(H_{d}/T_{d})^{4}(g_{s0}/g)^{1/3}a_{0}T_{0}/k_{*} = \tfrac{1}{60}g^{2/3}g_{s0}^{1/3}(T_{d}/\bar M_{P})^{4}a_{0}T_{0}/k_{*}$, in which $\pi$ has cancelled. The check evaluates the sixth power of this expression in exact rationals with the record inputs of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-planck-2018-lcdm-parameters} as used in the finite-range result's check ($T_{0}$, $H_{0}$, $k_{*}$, $A_{s} = 2.1 \times 10^{-9}$; $\bar M_{P} = 2.435 \times 10^{18}$ GeV, $g_{s0} = 43/11$), at $1$ MeV (below the floor of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-bbn-reheating-temperature-floor}, as there), at the stored ceiling $7.3 \times 10^{8}$ GeV and at $10^{12}$ GeV, and confirms every bracket stated, the value at $k = a_{0}H_{0}$ (larger than the pivot's by $k_{*}/a_{0}H_{0}$, between $222$ and $223$), and the infrared end $k_{1} = k_{*}\Delta^{2}_{\zeta}(k_{*})$. Under (LC), (v) with $P_{v} = \tfrac{3}{16}P_{\delta}$ gives $\tfrac1{16}(1 + 12(a_{d}H_{d}/k)^{2})\Delta^{2}_{\delta}$; $\Delta^{2}_{\delta}(k_{*}) = \tfrac{60}{\pi^{4}g}(k_{*}/a_{d}T_{d})^{3} = \tfrac{60}{\pi^{4}g_{s0}}(k_{*}/a_{0}T_{0})^{3}$, and $a_{d}H_{d}/k_{*}$ is below $7.63 \times 10^{22}/222$ for $T_{d} \le 10^{12}$ GeV (the stored bracket; the ratio grows with $T_{d}$).

\emph{(vii) (Argued.)} Each sentence compares a stored statement with (i) to (vi). The finite-range result's clause (i) takes the covariance of $\zeta$ as its hypothesis; its closing sentence and the quasi-free result's clause (iv) pass from a deposit of matter to $\zeta$ at order one, which by (iv) is (LC) with $v_{\Sigma} = 0$. The thermal reading's conversion $P_{\Phi} = 8G^{2}k^{-4}\langle\delta\rho^{2}\rangle$ is $k^{2}\Psi = -\tfrac32\mathcal{H}^{2}\delta_{c}$ of (ii) with $\Psi$ read as the growing potential, which by (iv) is (UE) up to the factor between $\Psi$ and $\tfrac23\mathcal{R}$. On the search: the stored statements that describe the descent slice (the dating, the Hadamard premise and its Weyl-flat collar under the radiation-type descent, the radical-normal tame wall, the single frozen point) are statements about the background field; none gives a first-order datum of the slice, and no stored result gives a reason for spatial flatness of the descended field, of the whole or of a patch. For free quanta in place of a perfect fluid the stress noise is anisotropic at the level of $\delta$; in the slicing orthogonal to the flow the curvature perturbation then changes at the rate $\mathcal{H}$ times a combination of $\delta_{c}$ and the anisotropic stress over $\rho$, both of order $\delta$ outside the Hubble length, so only the order-one term of (iii) is affected.
\end{proof}
