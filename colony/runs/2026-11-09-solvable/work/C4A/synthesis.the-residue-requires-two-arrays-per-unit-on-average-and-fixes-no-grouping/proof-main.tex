\emph{Setting.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.residue-from-mode-entropy}(i), under its hypothesis $\sum_{j \in J}s_{j} = A/4$ over the counted units $J$, the residue is $\kappa_{c} = |J|/A = 1/(4\langle s\rangle)$ with $\langle s\rangle$ the mean entropy per unit. In the quasi-free state the entropy of a set of arrays is the sum of their entropies, and at the weights of (Q) version 3 each array carries exactly $\tfrac12\ln 2$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.half-integer-weight-array-carries-half-ln2}(i), given the imported theta-function identities). A unit of $u_{j}$ whole arrays therefore carries $\tfrac12u_{j}\ln 2$, and $\langle s\rangle = \tfrac12\bar u\ln 2$ with $\bar u = |J|^{-1}\sum_{j}u_{j}$.

(i) \emph{Premise by premise.} (P), (R), (R$_{\Lambda}$) and (Q) fix the marginals, the joint quasi-free KMS state and the weights of the modes of each array; (N$_{+}$) and the window reading (\claimref{synthesis.window-reading-array-killing-energy}) fix the null ground state and read each array as one chiral component of a light ray on one window; (C1) and (C3) place the modes and fix the counted epoch; (C2) (\claimref{singularity.c2-cell-density}) fixes the form of the density and leaves $\nu$ free; (IC) fixes the clock; J3 identifies channels with two-dimensional modes; the slot-module hypothesis and the results built on it fix the content, sixteen Weyl components per generation per light ray (\claimref{synthesis.slot-module-light-ray-arrays-are-weyl-components}); the foundation, tower and imported statements concern algebras and representations. Each is a statement about modes, arrays, their weights and states, or about content, and none mentions a partition of the arrays into registered units, so a regrouping, which changes nothing they mention, carries a model of all of them to a model of all of them. The models differ in $|J|$ at fixed area and fixed array count, so in $\kappa_{c} = 1/(4\langle s\rangle) = 1/(2\bar u\ln 2)$, and in the registered density, which is proportional to $\kappa_{c}$ (\claimref{pred.boundary-mode-density}); (C2) admits every value.

(ii) $\kappa_{c} = 1/(4\ln 2)$ if and only if $\langle s\rangle = \ln 2$, that is $\bar u = 2$. Per generation, a grouping of the sixteen arrays into $k$ units has $\bar u = 16/k$, so it qualifies exactly when $k = 8$, whatever the unit sizes; the check runs over the $231$ integer partitions of $16$ (groupings up to which arrays go where) and finds the $22$ with eight parts, among them the all-pairs grouping and $(9, 1, \dots, 1)$; for uniform units $1/(2u\ln 2) = 1/(4\ln 2)$ only at $u = 2$. \emph{Ladder pairs.} A boundary mode is one ladder pair, of capacity $\ln 2$ (\claimref{synthesis.capacity-per-mode-independent-of-helicity}); a mode of weight $\omega$ has entropy $s(x) = \ln(1 + e^{-x}) + x/(e^{x} + 1)$, $x = 2\pi\omega/\kappa$, even and decreasing in $|x|$, with $s(0) = \ln 2$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boundary-mode-thermal-entropy}). At the weights $\kappa(k + \tfrac12)$, $|x| = |2k + 1|\pi \ge \pi$, and with $u = e^{-x}$, $s \le u + xu$; since $e^{3} > (1 + 1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac16 + \tfrac1{24} + \tfrac1{120})^{3} > 20$ and $\pi > 3$, $e^{-\pi} < \tfrac1{20}$, so $s(\pi) < (1 + \tfrac{16}{5})/20 = \tfrac{21}{100}$, while $\ln 2 = \sum_{k \ge 1}1/(k2^{k}) > \tfrac{69}{100}$ (partial sums). No single ladder pair carries $\ln 2$; a unit carrying it must contain modes of several arrays, and among unions of whole arrays only the mean of two does. \emph{Offset $0$.} There an array carries $S_{0} = \ln 2 + 2\sum_{k \ge 1}s(2\pi k) \in (0.72039, 0.72040)$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.half-integer-weight-array-carries-half-ln2}(ii)), above $\ln 2$, so any union of $u \ge 1$ whole arrays carries more than $\ln 2$; only the zero-weight mode carries exactly $\ln 2$, leaving $S_{0} - \ln 2$ of its array outside the count, the case of \claimref{synthesis.residue-from-mode-entropy}(vi).

(iii) Under the hypothesis at a horizon of area $A$ the arrays carry $A/4$, so there are $A/(2\ln 2)$ arrays per modular period whatever the grouping (\claimref{synthesis.half-integer-weight-array-carries-half-ln2}(iii)); with $c$ arrays per light ray there are $A/(2c\ln 2)$ light rays. On the cosmological horizon the same computation applies under (R$_{\Lambda}$) with weights $\kappa_{\Lambda}(k + \tfrac12)$, the entropy of an array depending only on $x = 2\pi\omega/\kappa_{\Lambda}$; with $A/4 = \pi N^{2}$ (\claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-entropy-pi-n-squared}), the arrays number $2\pi N^{2}/\ln 2$, the light rays $2\pi N^{2}/(c\ln 2)$ and the registered units $2\pi N^{2}/(\bar u\ln 2)$, which is $\pi N^{2}/\ln 2$ exactly at $\bar u = 2$. Units per light ray are $c/\bar u = c/2$; for the fermion content $c = 16n_{g}$, so $8n_{g}$, which is $24$ at $n_{g} = 3$; fields beyond the fermions in the light-ray sea would raise $c$. The note of \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number} calls $\pi N^{2}/\ln 2$ a count of light rays, citing the boundary-mode count of Observation \claimref{standard-model.largeness-number-universes-boundary-modes}; by the above it is the count of registered units, and the light rays are $c/2$ times fewer. That proof uses only that the count is common to the towers, which holds for both, so its conclusions stand.
