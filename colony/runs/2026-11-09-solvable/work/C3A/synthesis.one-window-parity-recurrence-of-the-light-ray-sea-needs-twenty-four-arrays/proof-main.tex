\emph{Setting.} A tower level $\Cl(L, L + 2)$ carries the kernel vectors $g_{1}, g_{2}$ of square $-1$ and the planes $e_{j}, f_{j}$, $Q(e_{j}) = 1$, $Q(f_{j}) = -1$, $j = 0, \dots, L - 1$; $J = g_{1}g_{2}$ has $J^{2} = -g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{2} = -1$ and commutes with every $e_{j}$ and $f_{j}$ (it is even and its vectors are orthogonal to them). The null pairs $v_{j}, w_{j}$ are the Witt pairs of Theorem \claimref{std-algebra.witt-basis-car}, $v_{j}$ the creators, and $n_{j} = v_{j}w_{j}$. Several arrays sharing a light ray are read in one such algebra with disjoint sets of planes and the one kernel unit $J$, so that every number of the sea is read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, J\}$.

\emph{(i)} Theorem \claimref{synthesis.exact-window-sugawara-identity-and-luttinger-normal-modes-of-tower-density-waves}(ii) gives, for real $t$, $e^{JtH} = e^{JtM^{2}/2}\prod_{k}\bigl(1 + (e^{Jt(k + 1/2)} - 1)n_{k}\bigr)$, a product of commuting even factors. At $t = 2\pi$, $e^{J\pi M^{2}} = (e^{J\pi})^{M^{2}} = (-1)^{M^{2}} = (-1)^{M}$ and $e^{2\pi J(k + 1/2)} = e^{\pi J(2k + 1)} = -1$, so each factor is $1 - 2n_{k}$. Now $n_{j} = \tfrac14(e_{j} + f_{j})(e_{j} - f_{j}) = \tfrac14(e_{j}^{2} - f_{j}^{2} - e_{j}f_{j} + f_{j}e_{j}) = \tfrac12(1 - e_{j}f_{j})$, so $1 - 2n_{j} = e_{j}f_{j}$ and
\[
e^{2\pi JH} = (-1)^{M}\prod_{j}e_{j}f_{j} = \Gamma_{\mathrm{win}} .
\]
Each $e_{j}f_{j}$ squares to $-e_{j}^{2}f_{j}^{2} = 1$; bivectors on disjoint planes commute, so $\Gamma_{\mathrm{win}}^{2} = 1$; $J$ commutes with each of them. $e_{j}f_{j}$ anticommutes with $e_{j}$ and with $f_{j}$ and commutes with the vectors of the other planes, so $\Gamma_{\mathrm{win}}$ anticommutes with every $v_{j}$ and $w_{j}$ and conjugation by it is $\psi \mapsto -\psi$ on the window algebra. On the occupation state $\prod v_{j}^{o_{j}}\Omega$ (with $\Omega$ proportional to $\prod_{j}w_{j}v_{j}$) the element $n_{j}$ acts as $o_{j}$ and $e_{j}f_{j} = 1 - 2n_{j}$ as $(-1)^{o_{j}}$, so $\Gamma_{\mathrm{win}}$ acts as $(-1)^{M + \hat N} = (-1)^{\mathcal{Q}}$; on the sea ($o_{j} = 1$ for $k < 0$) it is $+1$. By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.exact-window-sugawara-identity-and-luttinger-normal-modes-of-tower-density-waves}(i), $H = \tfrac12\mathcal{Q}^{2} + T$ with $T = \sum_{j}jn_{j} - \tfrac12\hat N(\hat N - 1)$, which has integer eigenvalues on occupation states; since $\tfrac12\mathcal{Q}^{2} - \tfrac12\mathcal{Q} = \tfrac12\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{Q} - 1) \in \Z$, every eigenvalue of $H$ lies in $\tfrac12\mathcal{Q} + \Z$, which is the spectral form of the same identity. On the domain of finite excitations of the infinite band the stored clause (iii) gives $H_{\infty} = \tfrac12\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}^{2} + \sum_{q \ge 1}q\,b_{q}^{*}b_{q}$ with integer second term on each vector, so the same holds there. At the zero offset the weights are $k$ and $e^{2\pi Jk} = 1$: every factor is $1$ and the flow at one window is the identity, the contrast recorded for (Q) version 2. \emph{Computed:} on windows $L = 2, 4$ the operator identity, $\Gamma_{\mathrm{win}}^{2} = 1$, the commutation with $J$, the anticommutation with all $v_{j}, w_{j}$, the eigenvalues of $H$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{win}}$ on all $2^{L}$ occupation states, $h - \tfrac12\mathcal{Q} \in \Z$, $h \ge \tfrac12\mathcal{Q}^{2}$, $h = 0$ on the sea, and the identity at the zero offset; powers of $e^{\pi J/2} = J$ are exact.

\emph{(ii)} \emph{The constant.} The window reading (Premise \claimref{synthesis.window-reading-array-killing-energy}) measures an array's Killing energy from the null ground state over one window, and by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.null-ordering-of-counted-units-is-zeta-symmetric}(iv) that ordering is, at the half offset, the zeta-regularised symmetric one. The excitation energies above the array's sea are $\kappa H$ in every ordering (the weights $\kappa(k + \tfrac12)$ of (Q) version 3), so only the sea's constant depends on the ordering, and in the symmetric ordering it is $-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}$ times the finite parts of the particle and hole towers, $-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(\zeta(-1, \tfrac12) + \zeta(-1, \tfrac12))$, with the finite-part rule of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.counted-unit-energy-at-half-offset}(ii). For $0 < a \le 1$ the finite part of $\sum_{m \ge 0}(m + a)e^{-\epsilon(m + a)}$ is $-c_{2}$, where $\epsilon e^{-\epsilon a}/(1 - e^{-\epsilon}) = \sum_{j}c_{j}\epsilon^{j}$, and $c_{2} = \tfrac12B_{2}(a)$; at $a = \tfrac12$, $B_{2}(\tfrac12) = -\tfrac1{12}$, so $\zeta(-1, \tfrac12) = \tfrac1{24}$ and the sea is $-\kappa/24$, the value of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.null-ordering-of-counted-units-is-zeta-symmetric}(i) for the boost ground state over one window in the null ordering; $\tfrac12B_{2}(\delta) + \tfrac1{24} = \tfrac12(\delta - \tfrac12)^{2}$ vanishes only at $\delta = \tfrac12$. So the null-ordered Killing energy of one array is $\kappa(H - \tfrac1{24})$. \emph{Computed:} $c_{2}$ by exact power-series inversion at $a = \tfrac12, \tfrac13, \tfrac14, \tfrac23, \tfrac15$, the sea value and the square identity at six offsets.

\emph{The evolution.} For $n$ arrays, $H_{\mathrm{null}} = \kappa\sum_{a}(H_{a} - \tfrac1{24})$; the $H_{a}$ are even elements on disjoint planes and commute, and the constant is a real multiple of $1$, so over one window of Killing time $2\pi/\kappa$
\[
U = \exp\bigl(-\tfrac{2\pi}{\kappa}JH_{\mathrm{null}}\bigr) = e^{2\pi Jn/24}\prod_{a}e^{-2\pi JH_{a}} = e^{2\pi Jn/24}\prod_{a}\Gamma_{a}^{-1} = e^{2\pi Jn/24}\Gamma ,
\]
using (i) and $\Gamma_{a}^{-1} = \Gamma_{a}$. The constant is the band value fixed by the window reading (the zeta-regularised sea of the infinite band), not a finite-window sum: on a finite window the sea has $H = 0$ and only (i) is a statement about the window itself. \emph{Normalisation of $\Gamma$.} An element implementing the grading by conjugation is fixed only up to a central unit; among the products of the window's vectors it is fixed up to sign, and $\Gamma$ is normalised by $\Gamma = +1$ on the sea, as (i) gives. With the sign left free the condition is $U = \pm\Gamma$, that is $e^{2\pi Jn/24} = \pm1$, that is $12 \mid n$; on $n = 16n_{g}$ this is $3 \mid 4n_{g}$, again $3 \mid n_{g}$. \emph{Computed:} the values $1 \le n \le 96$ with $e^{2\pi Jn/24} = \pm1$ are the multiples of $12$. The opposite sense of time gives $e^{-2\pi Jn/24}\Gamma$ and the same conditions. \emph{Automorphism and KMS.} $e^{2\pi Jn/24} = \cos\tfrac{2\pi n}{24} + J\sin\tfrac{2\pi n}{24}$ commutes with every mode vector, so $UXU^{-1} = \Gamma X\Gamma$ for every $X$ of the window algebra: the Killing flow at one window is the parity automorphism for every $n$, which is the one-period clause of (Q) version 3. The Gibbs functional $X \mapsto \mathrm{tr}(e^{-\beta H}X)/\mathrm{tr}(e^{-\beta H})$ is unchanged by $H \mapsto H + a$ for real $a$, so the KMS state at $\beta = 2\pi/\kappa$ of (Q) and of the window reading's thermal state is the same for every value of the constant. \emph{The condition.} Since $\Gamma$ is invertible, $U = \Gamma$ exactly when $e^{2\pi Jn/24} = 1$, that is $\cos\tfrac{2\pi n}{24} = 1$, that is $24 \mid n$. Likewise $U^{m} = e^{2\pi Jmn/24}\Gamma^{m}$ equals $\Gamma^{m}$ exactly when $24 \mid mn$, and $U^{2} = e^{2\pi Jn/12}$ is $1$ exactly when $12 \mid n$. One array gives $e^{2\pi J/24}$, a counted unit of two arrays $e^{2\pi J/12}$ and a generation of sixteen $e^{2\pi J\cdot 16/24} = e^{4\pi J/3} \neq 1$, whose cube is $1$. \emph{Computed:} $e^{2\pi J/24} = c + sJ$ with $c = \tfrac14(\sqrt6 + \sqrt2)$, $s = \tfrac14(\sqrt6 - \sqrt2)$ in $\Q(\sqrt2, \sqrt3)$, $c^{2} + s^{2} = 1$, its sixth power $J$, its twelfth $-1$, the values $1 \le n \le 96$ with $e^{2\pi Jn/24} = 1$ (the multiples of $24$) and with $e^{2\pi Jn/12} = 1$ (the multiples of $12$), each power computed by repeated multiplication, not reduced; that $J$ commutes with the window's vectors is checked in (i).

\emph{Not implied by (Q), the KMS condition, (N$_{+}$) or the window reading.} The one-period clause of (Q) is stated for the automorphism ($\psi \mapsto -\psi$), the KMS condition for the state, and the identification of the array's real-time recurrence with the Euclidean sign $R_{2\pi} = -1$ is, by the text of (Q), part of its reading; the window reading fixes the constant but imposes nothing on it. \emph{(iv)} \emph{Relative to J3.} The postulate \claimref{hawking.joint-j3-s-wave-reduction} is in this result's past cone (the null-ordering result rests on the collapse result, which assumes it).
