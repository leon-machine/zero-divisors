## E1. synthesis.additive-weights-on-the-rational-projectors-of-two-modes-are-linear

**Verdict: SOUND WITH FIXES.** I started by looking for the error, as the brief asked, and found none in the
theorem (iii). As far as I can tell from the literature, it answers an open problem that Mushtari posed in 2001,
for n = 4 and in a stronger form. The fixes concern the reading against (P), the lay text, prior art for (v), and
making the proof self-contained.

### (a) Domain and orthogonality
- The domain is every projector of every rank: the self-adjoint idempotents of Cl_Q(2,2). Under m these are the
  symmetric idempotents of Mat_4(Q), because the stored blade-inverse dagger of (P) goes to the transpose (check:
  m(b^dagger) = m(b)^T on all 16 blades; m is multiplicative and of rank 16). So orthogonality is the standard
  dot product on Q^4, and it is the dagger of (P).
- Atoms are the rational rays Pi_r. The norms r.r are arbitrary, not squares: the certificate uses vectors such
  as (1,-2,-3,-1) with norm 15. Every rational subspace has an orthogonal rational basis (Gram-Schmidt without
  normalising; the form is anisotropic over Q). So frames exist through every ray, and the additivity conditions
  bite. Well-definedness of mu(E) from the atom values (two bases completed by one basis of the complement) is
  correct.

### (b) The reduction and the step from finite to general
I checked every step of the proof text by hand.
- (ii) Rays are quaternions modulo Q^x, and <x y~>_0 = r.r'. A frame is a coset {x, ax, bx, abx}, where a, b are
  orthogonal pure quaternions; mod scalars, {1, a, b, ab} is a Klein group. Correct.
- J = span of kappa_{a,b}[x] is a two-sided ideal; the conjugation identity is right. A frame function with sum 0
  is a linear map on A = Q[G]/J. R(kappa) = 0 because P_a + P_b + P_ab = 1. Correct.
- Step 1: the 24 relations have rank 15 and R(O) has rank 9, so W is isomorphic to Mat_3(Q) under R. The proof
  that G_W is a subgroup (invertibility in A) is correct.
- Step 2: the 34-frame identity puts [2+B1] in W.
- Step 3: the mod-5 table (145 classes) and the induction give every integer rotor of norm 5^t as a product of
  the six rotors 2 +- B_k and a unit.
- Step 4 is the general step: the conjugation trick. In Z[B1]/(N'), 2+B1 is invertible, so g = (2+B1)^s has
  N' | beta. Modulo 5, g is congruent to 4^{s-1}(2+B1), so sin(phi) is nonzero. The rotor
  y = 5^e alpha - (beta/N') n B1 n is an integer rotor of norm 5^{2e+s}, so it lies in G_W. I checked the algebra
  that leads to v_n R(g) = R(g) v_n. The matrix-unit formulas (E11 from R(g), and the generation of Mat_3 from
  E11, E22, E32 - E23, E13 - E31) are correct; I checked each product.
- Step 5: the trick that 1 = sum X P Y, and the three sign equations, give v_a = 1. This is correct.
- Step 6: G is generated by bivectors (x = (xm)m^{-1} with m orthogonal to the bivector part of x). Correct.
- The finite computations therefore do establish the general statement. Steps 4 to 6 are the generation argument
  the brief asked for, and they are on paper. They are not sampled.

### (c) Clause (v)
I checked the proof: the parity mod 8 argument, the odd inner products, and the four rays (1,+-2,+-2,0). It is
consistent with (iii), because the unit atoms form a sublogic with fewer frames, and the domain difference is
stated at the formal, Reading, lay and one-liner levels. But see prior art below: a two-valued weight on the unit
atoms of Q^4 exists. In my box, all-odd unit rays with entries up to 7 (248 rays, 202 frames) carry a regular
four-colouring by the residues mod 4, after normalising the sign so that r_0 = 1 (mod 4). The pattern classes
{111, 333}, {133, 311}, {313, 131}, {331, 113} each occur once in every frame (e1_two_valued.py; computed on the
box only). The Reading's example "a weight with values in {0, 1/4, 1}" is weaker than what is known.

### (d) Prior art (web, 18 November 2026)
- D. Kh. Mushtari, Lobachevskii J. Math. 9 (2001) 47-53. I read it only through a summarising fetch of the
  mathnet PDF, whose encoding is Russian, so the following is not verbatim and the maintainer should open the PDF.
  - The paper poses "Problem G": does every (non-negative, finitely additive) measure on Pi(Q^n), the orthogonal
    rational projections, or on Pi_0(Q^n), the sublogic of the rational unit sphere, have the form tr(TP)? It
    leaves the problem open.
  - It proves: no {0,1} measures on Pi(Q^3) (Prop. 1, attributed to Kochen); none on Pi_0(Q^n) for n >= 6
    (Prop. 2); none on Pi(Q^n) for n >= 4 (Prop. 3).
  - Prop. 4 gives a regular four-colouring of S(Q^4), hence a non-Gleason {0,1} measure on Pi_0(Q^4).
  - The 3-colouring of S(Q^3) that it cites is that of Godsil-Zaks and Meyer.
- D. Mushtari, Int. J. Theor. Phys. 34 (1995) 1627. According to his conference slides (maths.ucd.ie, Fq9 talk),
  every Q-valued signed measure on the rational idempotents (not only the orthogonal projections) of size n >= 3
  has the form tr(TP). The 1998 Mat. Zametki paper (abstract read) concerns the same idempotent logic. The
  domain is larger than E1's, so E1 does not follow from it.
- Conclusion: E1 contradicts nothing I found. Clause (iii) answers Mushtari's Problem G for n = 4, for signed and
  V-valued weights, so it extends the published state as of 2001. A short search found no later solution of
  Problem G, which is not proof that none exists. Clause (v) is essentially Mushtari's Prop. 4, which is stronger
  (two-valued). The stored no-two-valued result has Mushtari's Prop. 3 as prior art; that is out of scope, but
  the maintainer should note it.

### (e) "With non-negativity these are exactly the states of (P)"
I checked this against synthesis.premise-positivity-of-values@2: normalisation, positivity on xx^dagger, reality,
and the blade-inverse dagger for the e_j, f_j split. The proof of (iv) is correct: L(xx^dagger) is the sum of
c^T rho c >= 0 over the rational columns of m(x). So (iv) as a mathematical statement is right.

### (f) Is "proved" right, and does any level present a derivation of (P)?
"Proved" is right, provided the 34-frame certificate is part of the proof (see fix 3). No level claims to derive
(P), and the Reading says so. But one sentence of (iv) overstates what is left of (P):

> "So on two modes over Q the linearity that (P) asserts follows from additivity on orthogonal projectors; what
> (P) adds to 'weights on projectors exist, do not depend on the context and add' is the sign condition only."

(P) also assigns values to elements that are not rational combinations of commuting rational projectors. The
CHSH element C of (iv) itself has characteristic polynomial x^4 - 8x^2 (cspec.py), with eigenvalues 0, 0 and
+-2 sqrt 2. Its spectral projectors are not rational, so additivity on rational projectors says nothing about
"the value of C". That the value of C is L(C), the unique self-adjoint linear extension, is still an assumption,
and it is the one the CHSH results use.

### Fixes required
1. (iv) and the Reading: replace the quoted sentence with: "So on two modes over Q, additivity on orthogonal
   rational projectors forces the weights to be the restriction of one linear functional L. For an element that
   is a rational combination of pairwise commuting rational projectors, its value from the weights is L of it. For
   other elements, among them the CHSH element C (eigenvalues 0 and +-2 sqrt 2, spectral projectors not rational),
   that the value is L(x) is still part of (P). Of what (P) asserts about projectors, only the sign condition is
   not implied." Make the matching change in the technical level ("the linearity is not an extra assumption" needs
   the same qualifier).
2. Lay: "if the numbers assigned to mutually exclusive questions simply add up, the usual rule follows" should
   read "if each question gets one number, the same whichever compatible questions it is asked with, and the
   numbers of mutually exclusive questions add up, then the numbers are given by a formula of the same form as the
   usual rule (without the guarantee that it gives no negative numbers)".
3. Proof: reproduce the 34-frame certificate (frames and weights) in proof-main.tex, or as a data table that the
   proof cites, so that the proved status does not depend on reading check.py.
4. Prior art (extraction note and technical): cite Mushtari 2001 as posing the question (Problem G) open, and
   (iii) as answering it for n = 4. Cite Prop. 4 of that paper as giving a two-valued weight on the unit atoms of
   Q^4. Replace "carried to dimension four here with its proof" with "known in dimension four (Mushtari 2001,
   Prop. 4, which gives a two-valued one); the parity form is given here with its proof". Change the Reading's
   "{0, 1/4, 1}" example accordingly. Cite Mushtari 1995 (rational idempotents, signed, n >= 3) and say that its
   domain is larger. Flag all of these as read through abstracts and a summary.
5. Preliminaries: w_j = v_j^dagger and v_j = (e_j + f_j)/2 in Cl(2,2) are not in
   tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors@4, which states only the anticommutation relations in T_k. Say that
   w_j = v_j^dagger follows from the definition of the adjoint, and that the relations are checked directly.
6. State the ledger: transport-absent or transport-convenient. The rotors are quaternion arithmetic.

## E2. synthesis.pell-values-are-the-running-maxima-of-the-pure-state-chsh-values-over-the-rationals

**Verdict: SOUND.**

### (a) Number theory, checked step by step
- P^2 + R^2 = 8Q^2 forces P and R even, and lowest terms then forces Q odd. a^2 + b^2 = 2Q^2 is 2 mod 4, so a and
  b are odd.
- A prime l dividing b with l dividing Q would divide a, which contradicts gcd(a, Q) = 1. So 2 is a square mod l,
  which excludes 3 and 5, giving b = 1 or b >= 7.
- b = 1 is x^2 - 2Q^2 = -1. That the stored recursion gives all positive solutions is stated in the stored
  theorem's clause (v) at version 1.
- Q_{n+1}^2 < 34 Q_n^2 follows from (12xQ)^2 < 288 Q^4 < (17Q^2)^2. The comparison 4/Q_n^2 < 136/Q^2 < 196/Q^2
  is correct, and (iii) follows.
- My own brute force (e2_brute.py) used two routes.
  - Route A works from the definition for every Q <= 6000 and every P. Its records are 2, 14/5, 82/29, 478/169,
    2786/985 and 16238/5741.
  - Route B enumerates every lowest-terms value through primitive Pythagorean triples (a = p + q, b = |p - q|),
    for Q < 2.3e8. Its first twelve running maxima are exactly the Pell values
    2 ... 636562078/225058681, all with b = 1.
  - 2 x 158 + 1 = 317 matches the proposal's count of values up to 1000.
- Perturbations: replacing 34 by 33 makes the growth check fail ((3 + 2 sqrt 2)^2 = 33.97); requiring b >= 11
  fails at b = 7 (Q = 5, X = 2/5).

### (b) Definition and duplication
V is the set of X-coordinates of the rational points of X^2 + Y^2 = 8. It is exactly the set of pure-state values
of the sector defined in the stored clause (ii) at version 1, and E2 cites it there. E2 duplicates nothing: it
proves the last sentence of the stored clause (v), "computed, not proved beyond that range". The cleaner store
change is to fold E2 into the stored theorem as version 2 and drop "computed" there (the maintainer's choice,
which the note offers). The status "proved" is right. The function check_first_pell_value_is_the_value_of_the_even_state
checks a stored fact that no clause of E2 claims, which is harmless.

**New overclaim introduced by the fix round (following R-D's own suggestion).** The extraction note says "what
fails for every slaved field that obeys the field equation is the dynamical statement of clause (7)". The Reading
says "if it obeys the field equation it fails at spacelike events at later times ((7))", and the lay text says
"once it is made to obey the field's law of motion it fails later". All three are false.

Counterexample: take the slaved field E(n) = N(n) + g S(n) P_1. Here N is a number solution with
div N = g delta_b plus background, and S is the solution with data (chi, A = 0) at the detection step 5, which is
the check's own S. It satisfies the constraint at every step (div S(n) = div chi, verified for steps 0 to 10),
obeys the field equation, and is a matter element on each link. Its commutator with O at step 5 is g S pq e_2,
which is zero at all 2750 spacelike events. It is fitted to the detection's time: against a detection at step 7
the same field fails at 86 of 2562 spacelike events (computed). The true statement is that the retarded slaved
field (fixed by the move at step 0) fails, while a slaved field fitted to one detection commutes with that one.

Exact changes:
- Extraction note: "what fails for every slaved field that obeys the field equation is the dynamical statement of
  clause (7)" becomes "what fails in (7) is the retarded slaved field, fixed by the move at step 0; a slaved field

- Reading: "but if it obeys the field equation it fails at spacelike events at later times ((7))" becomes "and

- Lay: "but once it is made to obey the field's law of motion it fails later" becomes "but if it is made to obey
