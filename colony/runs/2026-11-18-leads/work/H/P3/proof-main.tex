\emph{(a).} That the boost time evolution on the continuum mode space of a generator is the dilation $D_{t}$ is Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boost-evolution-on-generators-is-one-unitary-group}. For $e^{\kappa t} = N$, $(D_{t}\chi_{j})(U) = N^{-1/2}\chi_{j}(U/N)$, which is $N^{-1/2}\delta^{-1/2}$ on $[Nj\delta, (Nj + N)\delta)$ and zero elsewhere: this is clause (i) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.affine-cell-boost-spreads-without-scrambling} with $m = N$, which holds for every integer $m$. The support is $N$ consecutive cells, and for $j = 0$ it is $[0, N\delta)$, the first $N$ cells; for $j \ge 1$ it starts at cell $Nj \ge N$. Solving $e^{\kappa t} = N$ gives $t_{*} = \kappa^{-1}\ln N$. By the CAR, $\{a(D_{t}\chi_{j}), a^{*}(\chi_{k})\} = \langle\chi_{k}, D_{t}\chi_{j}\rangle \in \{0, N^{-1/2}\}$, clause (ii) of the same theorem. The script checks, on functions constant on unit cells and with the squared amplitude in place of the amplitude, that the dilation by $N$ sends cell $j$ to the cells $Nj, \dots, Nj + N - 1$ with squared amplitude $1/N$ and norm one, for $N = 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$ and three starting cells; that dilations compose, $N$ then $M$ giving $NM$; and that $n$ doublings cover $2^{n}$ cells, with $n\ln 2 = \ln 2^{n}$ as formal logarithms.

\emph{(b).} With $e_{+}^{2} = 1$, $e_{-}^{2} = -1$ and $\sigma = e_{+}e_{-}$: $\sigma^{2} = 1$, and for $f = \tfrac12(e_{+} + e_{-})$, $f^{*} = \tfrac12(e_{+} - e_{-})$ one has $\sigma f = -f$, $f\sigma = f$, $\sigma f^{*} = f^{*}$, $f^{*}\sigma = -f^{*}$. So with $R = \cosh(\lambda/2) + \sinh(\lambda/2)\,\sigma$, $R^{-1} = \cosh(\lambda/2) - \sinh(\lambda/2)\,\sigma$: $RfR^{-1} = (\cosh(\lambda/2) - \sinh(\lambda/2))^{2}f = e^{-\lambda}f$ and $Rf^{*}R^{-1} = e^{\lambda}f^{*}$, and $R(\lambda)R(\lambda') = R(\lambda + \lambda')$. For several modes the rotors of different modes commute and the common boost is their product, so every mode is scaled with the same $\lambda = \kappa t$; this is the weight clause of Fact \claimref{scrambling.imported-modes-modular-inventory}, and that the intrinsic flows are of this bivector-generated kind is Lemma \claimref{scrambling.clock-inventory-boundary-modes}(i). An amplitude $1/N$ on $f^{*}$ becomes $e^{\lambda}/N$, which is one exactly at $e^{\kappa t} = N$. The script checks these identities in $\Cl(1,1)$ and $\Cl(2,2)$ at sample rapidities. That this flow is not a group of $*$-automorphisms of the windows and is the continuation of the phase flow to the KMS strip is Theorem \claimref{synthesis.phase-flow-is-time-rotor-flow-kms-continuation}.

\emph{(c).} Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises} gives, under (N$_{+}$), the thermal state at rapidity period $2\pi$ for the boost; rapidity is $\kappa t$, so the period in Killing time is $\beta = 2\pi/\kappa$ and $\kappa^{-1} = \beta/2\pi$. For $f = 1 - 2M/r$, $\kappa = f'(2M)/2 = 1/(4M)$, so $\beta = 8\pi M$ and $\beta/2\pi = 4M$ (the script, at three masses). In (a) the number of covered cells is $e^{\kappa t}$, so the support dilates at the rate $\kappa = 2\pi/\beta = 2\pi T$.

\emph{(d).} This is the last clause of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boost-evolution-on-generators-is-one-unitary-group}: the anticommutators of linear elements under $D_{t}$ are inner products of unit vectors with their unitary images, bounded by one, and for fixed cells they fall as $e^{-\kappa t/2}$ by (a); no anticommutator grows, so the exponent is $0$.

\emph{(e)} is not proved and nothing above uses it. Its three steps are named with the stored results that bear on them: Theorem \claimref{scrambling.mode-count} counts the window's modes on the stretched singularity under its premises, not on a horizon generator; Proposition \claimref{scrambling.area-law-test} gives $N = S$ exactly for $\nu\,\Delta t = M/2$, an epoch of order $M$; clause (iii) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.affine-cell-boost-spreads-without-scrambling} shows the count is absent on boost-adapted cells; and Theorem \claimref{synthesis.number-diagonal-dynamics-spreads-nothing} shows that the law in real time and Premise (L$_{E}$) cannot both hold on the windows.
