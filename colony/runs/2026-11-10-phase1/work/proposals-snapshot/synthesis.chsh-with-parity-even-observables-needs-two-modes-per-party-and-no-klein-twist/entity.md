## formal
Let $\Cl(\ell,\ell)$ be the algebra of $\ell$ modes of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.mode-split-of-the-clifford-algebra-and-its-klein-twist-to-the-tensor-product}: generators $e_{k}$, $f_{k}$ with $Q(e_{k}) = 1$, $Q(f_{k}) = -1$, mode parities $p_{k} = f_{k}e_{k}$, $\Gamma_{X} = \prod_{k \in X}p_{k}$, total parity $\Gamma$, the anti-automorphism $\dagger$ fixing each $e_{k}$ and negating each $f_{k}$, coefficients in $\Q$ or any ordered field. An \emph{observable} is an $x$ with $x^{\dagger} = x$, $x^{2} = 1$; it is \emph{even} if $\Gamma x = x\Gamma$. A \emph{state} is a linear functional $\varphi$ with $\varphi(1) = 1$, $\varphi(xx^{\dagger}) \ge 0$; it is even if $\varphi(\Gamma x\Gamma) = \varphi(x)$. For disjoint sets of modes $A$, $B$ and observables $A_{1}, A_{2}$, $B_{1}, B_{2}$ with $A_{i}B_{j} = B_{j}A_{i}$, put $C = A_{1}B_{1} + A_{1}B_{2} + A_{2}B_{1} - A_{2}B_{2}$; then $C^{2} = 4 - [A_{1},A_{2}][B_{1},B_{2}]$ and $\varphi(C)^{2} \le \varphi(C^{2})$ (classical; two lines, in the proof).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Even observables need no twist; proved.)} Even elements of $\Cl(A)$ and of $\Cl(B)$ commute, and the even part of the twisted algebra $B'$ generated by the $y\Gamma_{A}$ is the even part of $\Cl(B)$, with $(y\Gamma_{A})(y'\Gamma_{A}) = yy'$ for generators $y, y'$ of $\Cl(B)$. So for even observables the graded split and the Klein-twisted split are the same split, and any even observables $A_{i} \in \Cl(A)$, $B_{j} \in \Cl(B)$ satisfy $A_{i}B_{j} = B_{j}A_{i}$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(One mode is not enough; proved.)} The self-adjoint elements of the algebra $\Cl(1,1)$ of one mode $k$ are $s + tp_{k} + ue_{k}$; its observables are $\pm 1$ and $tp_{k} + ue_{k}$ with $t^{2} + u^{2} = 1$; its even observables are $\pm 1$ and $\pm p_{k}$, which commute. Hence if one party's two observables are even elements of the algebra of a single mode, then $C^{2} = 4$ and $|\varphi(C)| \le 2$ for every state, whatever the other party, with or without the twist.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Two modes per party are enough; proved.)} For $\ell = 4$, $A = \{1,2\}$, $B = \{3,4\}$, the elements $A_{1} = p_{1}$, $A_{2} = e_{1}f_{2}$, $B_{1} = p_{3}$, $B_{2} = e_{3}f_{4}$ are even observables with $A_{1}A_{2} = -A_{2}A_{1}$, $B_{1}B_{2} = -B_{2}B_{1}$, and $C^{2} = 4 + 4K$, $K = -f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}f_{4}$, an even observable commuting with $C$. For $8r^{2} \le 1$ the functional $\varphi_{r}(x) = \langle(1 + K)(1 + rC)x\rangle_{0}$ is an even state with $\varphi_{r}(C) = 8r$ and $\varphi_{r}(C^{2}) = 8$: over $\Q$ the supremum $2\sqrt{2}$ is approached and not attained, and it is attained when $\sqrt{2}$ is a coefficient.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(The twisted split of one mode per party; proved.)} For $\ell = 2$, $A = \{1\}$, $B = \{2\}$, the observables $p_{1}, e_{1}$ of $\Cl(A)$ and $p_{2}, e_{2}p_{1}$ of $B'$ commute across the parties and anticommute within each. On the even pure state $\rho = \tfrac14(1 + p_{1}p_{2}) + \tfrac12(w_{1}w_{2} + v_{2}v_{1})$, the pair state of clause (v) of the stored theorem at equal weights, with value $\varphi(x) = 4\langle\rho x\rangle_{0}$: $\varphi(p_{1}p_{2}) = \varphi(e_{1}\,e_{2}p_{1}) = 1$, $\varphi(p_{1}\,e_{2}p_{1}) = \varphi(e_{1}p_{2}) = 0$, and with $B_{\pm} = cp_{2} \pm s\,e_{2}p_{1}$, $c^{2} + s^{2} = 1$, the CHSH element of $(p_{1}, e_{1}; B_{+}, B_{-})$ has value $2(c + s)$, above $2$ for $c, s > 0$ (for example $\tfrac{14}{5}$ at $(\tfrac35, \tfrac45)$). The observables $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}p_{1}$ are odd: they anticommute with $\Gamma$, and the parity average $E(x) = \tfrac12(x + \Gamma x\Gamma)$ of Theorem \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average} (in its total-parity form) sends them to $0$; with every observable replaced by its parity average the value is $2c \le 2$. By (ii) no other choice of observables repairs this: a value above $2$ on one mode per party requires observables that are not even.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Without the twist; proved.)} $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}$ anticommute, $e_{1}e_{2}$ is anti-self-adjoint, and $\varphi(e_{1}e_{2}) = 0$ for every state with $\varphi(x^{\dagger}) = \varphi(x)$, in particular for every $\varphi = \langle\rho\,\cdot\,\rangle_{0}$ with $\rho^{\dagger} = \rho$. The self-adjoint part of the untwisted element $e_{1}e_{2} + e_{1}p_{2} + p_{1}e_{2} - p_{1}p_{2}$ is $H = e_{1}p_{2} + p_{1}e_{2} - p_{1}p_{2}$, with $H^{2} = 3$, so $\varphi(H)^{2} \le 3 < 4$ for every state.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
In the Clifford algebra of fermionic modes, odd elements of two disjoint sets of modes anticommute, and the stored Klein twist is what makes them commute. This result separates what the twist buys from what it does not. If the observables of a party are parity-even, the twist is idle: even elements of disjoint mode sets already commute and the twisted and untwisted even algebras coincide. With even observables a party holding one mode has only the parity itself, so every CHSH value is at most $2$; a party needs two modes, and with two modes each there are even observables with CHSH square $4 + 4K$ and even states with values $8r$ for all $8r^{2} \le 1$, of supremum $2\sqrt{2}$. On one mode per party the twisted split does give values $2(c + s)$ up to $2\sqrt{2}$ on the stored even pair state, but only through the observables $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}p_{1}$, which anticommute with the parity and which the stored parity average sends to zero. So the question whether a single entangled pair of modes shows a CHSH value above $2$ is the question whether parity-odd elements are observables, which is a choice of premise and is not settled here. Without the twist the odd-odd product has value zero in every state given by a self-adjoint element and the self-adjoint part of the CHSH expression has square $3$. All of this is classical (fermion parity superselection and the known need for two copies of a mode-entangled state); the statement in the series' algebra, with the stored twist and parity average, is what is recorded.

## lay
Two parties share particles of the kind that obey the exclusion principle, described by slots that are either empty or full. A long-standing rule says that nothing measurable can change the number of such particles from even to odd. If each party may only use measurements obeying that rule, then one shared slot each is not enough to show the quantum excess over the everyday limit of $2$: each party has only one such measurement, "is my slot full". Two slots each are enough, and then nothing special is needed to make the two parties' measurements compatible. A stored device called the Klein twist makes rule-breaking measurements of the two parties compatible, and with them one slot each does exceed $2$; whether such measurements exist is a choice this result leaves open. The mathematics is known; it is recorded here inside the series' algebra.

## one-liner
In the Clifford algebra of modes, parity-even observables of disjoint mode sets commute with no Klein twist; with them a one-mode party gives CHSH square $4$ (value at most $2$) and two modes per party give $C^{2} = 4 + 4K$ with even rational states of values $8r$, $8r^{2} \le 1$; the twisted one-mode split exceeds $2$ only through parity-odd observables, which the parity average erases.
