## A. synthesis.strict-completions-exist-exactly-when-the-signature-does-not-jump

### Verdict: OVERSTATED

True weaker statement. *With "standard outside U" read as in the stored formal text (h = G perp K outside U, K
nondegenerate on a complement, one signature of K on M \ U, K free to vary from point to point)*: (1), (2), (3), (5),
(6) hold as stated, and the four families of (4) each have an everywhere-nondegenerate strict completion. Under the
stricter reading in which the exterior model is one fixed form (K constant in some trivialisation of the complement,
or more generally with trivial positive and negative subbundles on M \ U), (1) still holds, the counter-models x^2,
x^2 + y^2 and diag(x, -x) still hold (explicit constant-K completions below), but [[x, y], [y, -x]] has NO strict
completion nondegenerate everywhere when M \ U contains a loop around the origin: the obstruction is
w_1 of the positive line bundle of G on that loop, a degree-one class. So in that reading the dichotomy (3) is false,
"the invariant is the integer signature" is false, and the conjecture's guess of a cohomological invariant is not
refuted. The conjecture's sentence "degenerates on a displaced locus on the other side" is not proved in either
reading even when the signature jumps, and is false as worded (below).

### Point 1: is the notion the stored one?

- sheaf.conservation-degeneracy-absolute-form@4, formal: "h = G perp K for a nondegenerate form K on a complement, with
  sig(K) the same on both exterior regions". Literally this admits a K that varies, so the proposal's K = -G is
  admitted. Its proof is a pure signature count, which is all (1) needs.
- transport.obstructions-are-moved-never-destroyed@3: "K nondegenerate on a complement and of the same signature on every
  region where G is nondegenerate (the reading of Theorem sheaf.conservation...)". Same literal content (signature only).
- But the store's one-liners say "with one exterior model" (sheaf.conservation-degeneracy-absolute-form one-liner) and
  the conjecture's note contrasts the strict reading with "the exterior model allowed to change across the wall", and
  explains the relaxed obstruction through rung counts and w_1 of the dying line. So the stored wording is ambiguous
  between "one signature" and "one model form". The proposal's own definition glosses "one signature" as "one exterior
  model", which hides the ambiguity.

Results under each reading (my own constructions, exact checks in /tmp/referee/a_constK.py, a_c2.py,
a_summary_check.py):

- x^2 on a line: constant K = -1, h = [[x^2, beta], [beta, -1]] (the proposal's own clause (4)(a)). Survives.
- x^2 + y^2 on a line over R^2: constant K = -1, det = -(x^2 + y^2) - beta^2 < 0 everywhere. Survives.
- diag(x, -x) over R: constant K = diag(1, -1) works with a constant coupling R = [[1, 1], [-1, 1]]:
  h = [[g, beta R], [beta R^T, K]]. Schur complement g - beta^2 R K^{-1} R^T = [[x, 2 beta^2], [2 beta^2, -x]],
  determinant -x^2 - 4 beta^4 < 0 unless x = beta = 0, which never happens. Checked exactly at 801 points: signature
  (2,2) everywhere. Survives.
- [[x, y], [y, -x]] over R^2, U bounded (so M \ U contains a circle C around 0): no constant-K completion. Proof: if h is
  nondegenerate on E over R^2 then a maximal positive subbundle E_+ exists over R^2, which is contractible, so E_+|C is
  trivial. On C, h = G perp K, and L_+(G) + K_+ is a maximal positive subbundle, isomorphic to E_+|C. K constant gives
  K_+ trivial. The positive eigenline of [[cos t, sin t], [sin t, -cos t]] is spanned by (cos t/2, sin t/2), which
  returns to its negative after one turn: L_+(G)|C is the Moebius bundle, w_1 != 0. Contradiction. (The doubling
  escapes because K = -G has K_+ = L_-(G), also Moebius.) The naive constant-coupling ansatz indeed degenerates: with
  K = diag(1,-1), coupling beta*identity, h is singular (corank 2) at (1,0) (a_c2.py). If U is chosen so that M \ U is
  simply connected (e.g. the complement of a closed half-plane) the argument does not apply; the obstruction depends on U.

So clause (4)(c), second model, and the "if and only if" of (3) depend on the literal reading; the general
fixed-model obstruction contains at least the w_1 (more generally the KO class) of the positive bundle of G over M \ U
modulo classes extending over M, which is exactly the type of "cohomological invariant" the conjecture guessed.

### Point 2: which sentence is refuted

The conjecture: "any completion that is nondegenerate of constant signature on one side of the locus degenerates on a
displaced locus on the other side, so that no completion is nondegenerate globally". The counter-models (literal
reading) refute the consequence "no completion is nondegenerate globally", and with it the first part as worded (their
completions are nondegenerate on both sides). They do not refute a fixed-model version for [[x,y],[y,-x]].

Conversely, the Reading's "By (1) that holds whenever the signature of the family differs between the sides" is too
strong: (1) proves only that every strict completion degenerates somewhere in U, on a closed set separating the
exterior points. It proves nothing about "a displaced locus on the other side", and that part is false as worded:
h = G perp K (beta = 0) is strict and degenerates exactly on Sigma, not displaced; for G = x on R,
[[x, beta], [beta, +1]] degenerates at x = beta^2 > 0 and [[x, beta], [beta, -1]] at x = -beta^2 < 0, so the side is
chosen by K. Fix: "By (1) the conclusion that no strict completion is nondegenerate globally holds whenever the
signature differs; where the degeneration sits is not determined (it can be Sigma itself, or either side)."

A very strict reading ("h = G perp K on every region where G is nondegenerate", not just outside U) makes the
conjecture trivially true (h = G perp K extends by continuity to Sigma, where it is degenerate); the U-reading is the
sensible one, but the proposal should say it chose it.
