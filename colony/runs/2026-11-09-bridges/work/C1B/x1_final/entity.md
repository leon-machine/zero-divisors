## formal
Let $e_{1}, \dots, e_{2m}$ be the signed directions of $\Cl(2m, 0)$, $Q(e_{r}) = 1$, with strings $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, 2m\}$, blades $e_{S}$, and the forms $b(S,T) = |S||T| + |S \cap T|$, $q(S) = \binom{|S|}{2} \pmod 2$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra}(ii) (commutation and square signs); adjoin a central unit $\omega$, $\omega^{2} = -1$ (the product of all generators of $\Cl(2m, 1)$ or $\Cl(2m + 1, 0)$, whichever has square $-1$), so that $h_{S} = e_{S}$ or $\omega e_{S}$ has square $+1$, and write $\mathrm{tr} = 2^{m}\langle\cdot\rangle_{0}$. A \emph{family} is a subspace $L \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{2}^{2m}$ of dimension $m$ on which $b$ vanishes, and its \emph{states} are the $2^{m}$ idempotents $\rho = 2^{-m}\prod_{r}(1 + s_{r}h_{S_{r}})$, $S_{r}$ a basis of $L$, $s_{r} = \pm 1$. For $1 \le a < m$, $b' = m - a$, let $A$ be the subalgebra generated by $e_{1}, \dots, e_{2a}$ and $\omega$, $d_{A} = 2^{a}$, $d_{B} = 2^{b'}$, $D = d_{A}d_{B}$; the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto $A$ is the projection onto the blades of $A$, $\rho_{A} = d_{B}$ times the projection of $\rho$, and $\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{n} = (|L_{A}|/d_{A})^{n-1}$ with $L_{A}$ the strings of $L$ inside $\{1, \dots, 2a\}$. A quarter turn $(1 + u)/\sqrt2$, $u = e_{w}$ or $\omega e_{w}$ of square $-1$, permutes the states and acts on strings by $x \mapsto x + b(x, w)w$. The \emph{finite average} $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is the mean over all states of all families (one orbit of the quarter turns: computed for $m \le 3$, in general by Witt's extension theorem, background); the \emph{sector average} is the mean over the states with $v = \{1, \dots, 2m\} \in L$ and a fixed sign of $h_{v}$, the parity sector, of dimension $D/2$ (one orbit of the quarter turns about even strings: computed for $m \le 4$). Haar values are those of Facts \claimref{synthesis.imported-haar-average-purity}, \claimref{synthesis.imported-haar-moments-of-random-pure-state} and \claimref{singularity.imported-page-average-entropy-subsystem}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Count by rank; proved.)} The number of families with $|L_{A}| = 2^{a - e}$ (so $\rho_{A}$ flat of rank $2^{e}$) is $N(a, a - e)\,N(b', b' - e)\,|Sp(2e, 2)|$, with $N(c, k) = \binom{c}{k}_{2}\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}(2^{c-r} + 1)$ the number of $k$-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2c}$ on which $b$ vanishes and $|Sp(2e, 2)| = 2^{e^{2}}\prod_{r=1}^{e}(4^{r} - 1)$; the total is $\prod_{r=1}^{m}(2^{r} + 1)$. At $a = b' = 1$: $9$ and $6$ families, $36$ and $24$ states.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Moments two and three agree; proved for every $a$, $b'$.)} $\langle\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{fin}} = (d_{A} + d_{B})/(D + 1)$ and $\langle\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{3}\rangle_{\mathrm{fin}} = (d_{A}^{2} + d_{B}^{2} + 3D + 1)/((D + 1)(D + 2))$, the Haar values.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Moment four does not; computed.)} $\langle\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{4}\rangle_{\mathrm{fin}}$ exceeds the Haar value for every $a + b' \le 8$: $\tfrac{13}{20}$ against $\tfrac{22}{35}$ at $a = b' = 1$, $\tfrac{3}{17}$ against $\tfrac{52}{323}$ at $a = b' = 2$. At the crossing $a = b' = c$, $d = 2^{c}$, the coefficient $d^{n-1}\langle\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{n}\rangle_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is larger at $c = 24$ than at $c = 16$ and lies within $2^{-16}$ at $c = 16$ and $2^{-32}$ at $c = 24$ below $2, 5, 16, 67, 374$ for $n = 2, \dots, 6$, the numbers $G_{n-1} = \sum_{r}\binom{n-1}{r}_{2}$ of subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n-1}$, where the Haar coefficient tends to the Catalan numbers $2, 5, 14, 42, 132$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.eviction-keeps-one-of-catalan-many-replica-saddles}(iii)). The limit exists for every $n$ and equals $\phi\sum_{k \ge 0}2^{(n-1)k - k^{2}}\phi_{k}^{-2}$, $\phi_{k} = \prod_{r=1}^{k}(1 - 2^{-r})$, $\phi = \phi_{\infty}$ (proved); that it is $G_{n-1}$ is computed for $n \le 6$ and not proved.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Parity sector; proved.)} For a unit vector drawn from the unitarily invariant measure of the parity sector, $\langle\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{n}\rangle = 2\sum_{\sigma \in S_{n}}(d_{A}/2)^{\#(\sigma^{-1}\eta)}(d_{B}/2)^{\#(\sigma)}/\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}(D/2 + l)$ (the same Schur average as the Fact, on a space that is $(\text{even} \otimes \text{even}) \oplus (\text{odd} \otimes \text{odd})$ and not a tensor product), so the mean purity is
\[
\frac{d_{A} + d_{B}}{D + 2} \quad\text{in place of}\quad \frac{d_{A} + d_{B}}{D + 1} ,
\]
and the sector average of the purity equals it for every $a$, $b'$. Computed: the sector average of $\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{3}$ equals the sector's Haar value for $m \le 4$, and that of $\mathrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{4}$ exceeds it. At $m = 2$ the six states reached from $\tfrac14(1 + h_{12})(1 + h_{34})$ by the six bivector quarter turns have purities $1, 1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12, \tfrac12, \tfrac12$, mean $\tfrac23$, against $\tfrac45$ for two unrestricted modes.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Which quarter turns; proved, with the values at $m = 4$ computed.)} The quarter turns about bivectors permute the directions, and the orbit of the family spanned by $\{1,2\}, \{3,4\}, \dots$ is the $(2m - 1)!!$ perfect matchings, equal to the $\prod_{r=1}^{m-1}(2^{r} + 1)$ families containing $v$ exactly for $m \le 3$ ($3$, $15$) and fewer for every $m \ge 4$ ($105$ of $135$ at $m = 4$, where the classes of even strings modulo $v$ fall into orbits of $28$ and $35$ and the mean purity at $a = b' = 2$ is $\tfrac{17}{35}$, against $\tfrac49$ for the sector and $\tfrac{8}{17}$ for the Fact; one quartic quarter turn added restores $135$ and $\tfrac49$). Without the adjoined unit, a quarter turn of unit norm about $e_{w}$ exists in $\Cl(2m, 0)$ exactly when $q(w) = 1$; for $m$ odd every class of even strings modulo $v$ has such a member ($e_{v}^{2} = -1$, the even subalgebra has its own unit), and for $m$ even $q$ descends to the classes and is preserved, so for even $m \ge 4$ the classes with $q = 0$ and $q = 1$ are never exchanged.
\item[(vi)] \emph{(Mean entropy; computed.)} The finite average of the entropy is $\ln 2$ times the mean of $e$: $\tfrac25\ln 2 < \tfrac{7}{25}$ at $a = b' = 1$ against Page's $\tfrac13$; at the crossing it lies below $\ln d$ by between $0.84\ln 2$ and $0.86\ln 2$ for $c = 8, 16, 24$, more than $0.56$, where Page's deficit is below $\tfrac12$.
\end{enumerate}
So the finite group of quarter turns reproduces the imported mean purity and third moment and nothing further: Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-haar-average-purity}, as used in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.eviction-keeps-one-replica-saddle}(iii), (iv), could be replaced by (ii); Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-haar-moments-of-random-pure-state}, as used at every index in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.eviction-keeps-one-of-catalan-many-replica-saddles}(iii), (iv), and Page's average, as used in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.eviction-deficit-against-page-deficit}, could not: with the finite average the gap $\ln C_{n}/(n - 1)$ would read $\tfrac13\ln 16$ at $n = 4$, and the half nat at the crossing about $0.59$. On a parity sector the Renyi-2 comparison at the crossing $d_{A} = d_{B} = d$ reads $2d/(d^{2} + 2)$, below the capacity value in $S_{2}$ by $\ln 2 - \ln(1 + 2d^{-2})$ in place of $\ln 2 - \ln(1 + d^{-2})$; the leading terms and the Catalan limit are unchanged (argued from (iv): the planar permutations contribute $C_{n}d^{1-n}$ in both). None of the stored results is contradicted: they state the Haar values for an abstract tensor product and do not use a finite group or a sector.

## technical
The signed directions generate a finite group whose quarter turns shuffle a finite set of special states, those fixed by a largest commuting set of blades. Averaging over that finite set is exact arithmetic: a reduced state always has a flat spectrum of rank a power of two, and the number of states of each rank is a product of counts of isotropic subspaces. The average purity and the average third moment come out equal to the random-state (Haar) values for every split, by a two-line double count. The fourth moment does not: one against one mode it is 13/20 where the random-state value is 22/35. At the point where the two parts have equal size the finite average replaces the Catalan numbers 2, 5, 14, 42 of the random-state moments by 2, 5, 16, 67, and the mean entropy sits about 0.59 below the maximum instead of one half. If only states of one fermion parity are allowed, the group that preserves parity still reproduces the random-state values of that sector, but those are different numbers, because a parity sector is not a product of two parts: the mean purity has D + 2 in the denominator where the unrestricted formula has D + 1. Finally, the rotations in planes of two directions are the whole parity-preserving group only for four and six directions; from eight on they reach 105 of 135 families and give a different mean.

## lay
Some of the series' comparisons use the entanglement of a quantum state picked completely at random, a result taken from the literature. This entry asks whether the same numbers can be obtained from the series' own finite group of quarter turns, by averaging over a finite list of states instead of over all of them. The answer is yes for the two simplest measures and no beyond: the finite list gives exactly the random value for the average purity and for the next moment, then departs (13/20 instead of 22/35 in the smallest case), and it gives a different average entropy. It also shows that if only states with an even number of particles are allowed, the random value itself changes slightly, and that quarter turns in planes alone are enough only for the two smallest cases. Everything here is for the finite list of states named, with the splits and sizes stated in each clause.

## one-liner
Averages over the states of the quarter-turn group of $2m$ signed directions equal the Haar moments of a subsystem at orders two and three for every split and exceed them at order four ($\tfrac{13}{20}$ against $\tfrac{22}{35}$; crossing coefficients $2, 5, 16, 67, 374$ against the Catalan numbers, computed to order six); on a parity sector the mean purity is $(d_{A} + d_{B})/(d_{A}d_{B} + 2)$; bivector quarter turns alone suffice only for $m \le 3$.
