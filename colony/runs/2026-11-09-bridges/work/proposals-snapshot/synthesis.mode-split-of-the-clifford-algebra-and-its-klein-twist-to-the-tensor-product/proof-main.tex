\emph{Preliminaries.} The blades $e_{S}$ are a basis, $e_{S}e_{T} = \pm e_{S \triangle T}$, and two blades commute or anticommute according to the form $b(S,T) = |S||T| + |S \cap T|$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.blade-square-signs-f2-quadratic-form}(ii)). For a blade, $e_{S}^{\dagger}$ is its reverse times $(-1)^{\#f}$, $\#f$ the number of generators $f_{k}$ in it, and the product of a blade with its reverse is the product of the squares of its generators, $(-1)^{\#f}$; so $e_{S}e_{S}^{\dagger} = 1$, the blades are orthonormal for $\langle xy^{\dagger}\rangle_{0}$, and $\langle xx^{\dagger}\rangle_{0} \ge 0$ with equality only for $x = 0$. From $v_{k}w_{k} + w_{k}v_{k} = 1$ (Proposition \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors}) and $w_{k}v_{k} = \tfrac14(e_{k} - f_{k})(e_{k} + f_{k}) = \tfrac12(1 + e_{k}f_{k})$, $p_{k} = 1 - 2w_{k}v_{k} = f_{k}e_{k}$ and $p_{k}^{2} = -f_{k}^{2}e_{k}^{2} = 1$.

\emph{(i).} Generators of $\Cl(A)$ and of $\Cl(B)$ are distinct orthogonal vectors and anticommute, which gives the sign $(-1)^{|x||y|}$ on products of generators and so on homogeneous elements. A blade factors uniquely, up to sign, as a blade of $\Cl(A)$ times a blade of $\Cl(B)$, so multiplication is a bijection of bases; it carries the ordinary product $(x \otimes y)(x' \otimes y') = xx' \otimes yy'$ to $xx'yy'$, while $(xy)(x'y') = (-1)^{|y||x'|}xx'yy'$, so it is multiplicative only with that sign. Since $\langle e_{S}e_{T}\rangle_{0} = 0$ unless $S = T$, the pairing $\langle xy\rangle_{0}$ with $y \in \Cl(A)$ sees only the components of $x$ on blades of $\Cl(A)$ and is nondegenerate there: this is the characterisation of $E_{A}$. Multiplication by a blade of $\Cl(A)$ permutes the blades up to sign and preserves the set of blades of $\Cl(A)$ and its complement, whence $E_{A}(yxy') = yE_{A}(x)y'$; $\dagger$ preserves each blade up to sign, whence $E_{A}(x^{\dagger}) = E_{A}(x)^{\dagger}$. For $\rho = \sum_{r}x_{r}x_{r}^{\dagger}$ and $z \in \Cl(A)$, $\langle z^{\dagger}E_{A}(\rho)z\rangle_{0} = \langle E_{A}(z^{\dagger}\rho z)\rangle_{0} = \sum_{r}\langle(z^{\dagger}x_{r})(z^{\dagger}x_{r})^{\dagger}\rangle_{0} \ge 0$; $\Cl(A)$ with coefficients in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$ is a full matrix algebra on which $\operatorname{tr}_{A}$ is the trace and $\dagger$ the adjoint (the Fock module built on the vacuum of the $v_{k}$, $k \in A$), and an element $h = h^{\dagger}$ with $\operatorname{tr}_{A}(zz^{\dagger}h) \ge 0$ for all $z$ is positive, hence a sum of terms $xx^{\dagger}$. Finally $\operatorname{tr}_{A}\rho_{A} = d_{A}d_{B}\langle\rho\rangle_{0} = 1$, and the purity is the definition written out.

\emph{(ii).} $\Gamma_{A}$ is a product of the commuting elements $p_{k}$ of square $1$, so $\Gamma_{A}^{2} = 1$; each $p_{k} = f_{k}e_{k}$ anticommutes with $e_{k}$ and $f_{k}$ and commutes with the other generators, so $\Gamma_{A}$ anticommutes with the generators of $\Cl(A)$ and commutes with those of $\Cl(B)$. For generators $y, y'$ of $\Cl(B)$ and $x$ of $\Cl(A)$: $(y\Gamma_{A})^{2} = y^{2}\Gamma_{A}^{2} = Q(y)$; $y\Gamma_{A}y'\Gamma_{A} = yy'$, so the twisted generators anticommute as the untwisted do; and $x\,y\Gamma_{A} = -yx\Gamma_{A} = y\Gamma_{A}x$. With $\omega_{k} = e_{k}f_{k} = -p_{k}$ the twisted generators are $\pm y\,\omega_{k_{1}}\cdots\omega_{k_{a}}$, those of Theorem \claimref{tower.commuting-copies} for the ground algebra $\Cl(B) \cong \Cl(b,b)$ and the $a$ planes of $A$ (the sign does not change the algebra generated), and that theorem gives $B' \cong \Cl(b,b)$, commuting with the algebra of the planes, $\Cl(A)$, and the isomorphism $\Cl(A) \otimes B' \to \Cl(\ell,\ell)$. A product of $t$ twisted generators is $\pm e_{T}\Gamma_{A}^{t}$, which is $\pm e_{T}$ for $t$ even and $\pm\Gamma_{A}e_{T}$ for $t$ odd: the statement on even and odd parts. Uniqueness: $yK$ commutes with a generator $x$ of $\Cl(A)$ exactly when $K$ anticommutes with $x$, so each blade $e_{U}$ occurring in $K$ anticommutes with the $2a$ generators of $A$ and commutes with the $2b$ generators of $B$. By the form $b$, a generator $g$ and $e_{U}$ anticommute exactly when $|U| + [g \in U]$ is odd. If $|U|$ is even, $U$ contains every generator of $A$ and none of $B$, the blade of $\Gamma_{A}$; if $|U|$ is odd, $U$ contains no generator of $A$ and all $2b$ of $B$, which is an even number, a contradiction. So $K$ is a multiple of $\Gamma_{A}$, and $(yK)^{2} = Q(y)K^{2}$ forces the multiple to be $\pm 1$; this is the count of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.joint-clifford-clause-is-a-klein-convention-on-the-composite-space}(iv) for a general pair of sets of modes.

\emph{(iii).} In a tensor product of matrix algebras the partial trace over the second factor is the map $T$ into the first with $\operatorname{tr}_{A}(T(x)y) = \operatorname{tr}(xy)$ for all $y$ in the first. By (i), $\operatorname{tr}_{A}(d_{B}E_{A}(x)y) = d_{A}d_{B}\langle xy\rangle_{0} = \operatorname{tr}(xy)$, so $T = d_{B}E_{A}$. The same argument with the roles exchanged gives $d_{A}$ times the projection onto the blades of $B'$, which by (ii) are the $e_{T}$ with $|T|$ even and the $\Gamma_{A}e_{T}$ with $|T|$ odd; $E_{B}$ keeps all $e_{T}$ instead. The two projections agree on the even $e_{T}$ and on every blade outside both lists, and differ exactly on the components listed. A blade $e_{T}$ or $\Gamma_{A}e_{T}$ with $|T|$ odd is odd ($\Gamma_{A}$ has $2a$ generators), so an even element has no such component.

\emph{(iv).} Evenness is invariance under $x \mapsto \tfrac12(x + \Gamma x\Gamma)$, the average of Theorem \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average} with the total parity $\Gamma$ in place of the single-mode $\sigma$; that theorem is stated for one mode, and the $\ell$-mode form is a definition here (applied mode by mode it would erase the coherence of the pair state of (v), which is odd in one mode alone). A projection onto blades preserves evenness, so $\rho_{A}$, $\rho_{B}$ are even; an even element of $\Cl(A)$ commutes with $\Gamma_{A}$; and $\rho_{B} = d_{A}E_{B}(\rho) = d_{A}E_{B'}(\rho)$ by (iii). For $\rho$ pure, the Schmidt decomposition in the tensor split $\Cl(A) \otimes B'$ gives equal nonzero spectra of the two partial traces, and the trace of $B'$ restricted to the common even part is $d_{B}\langle\cdot\rangle_{0}$, as for $\Cl(B)$; so the moments agree. A pure $\rho$ is a projector of rank one; commuting with the involution $\Gamma$, its range is an eigenline of $\Gamma$. $\Gamma = \Gamma_{A}\Gamma_{B}$ with $\Gamma_{B} \in B'$ even, so in the tensor split $\Gamma = \Gamma_{A} \otimes \Gamma_{B}$, and since $\operatorname{tr}_{A}\Gamma_{A} = 0$ each of its eigenspaces is the sum of two products of half-dimensional eigenspaces. The example: $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}p_{1} = e_{2}f_{1}e_{1}$ are commuting elements of square $1$ fixed by $\dagger$, so $\tfrac12(1 + e_{1})\tfrac12(1 + e_{2}p_{1})$ is a projector of trace $1$; expanding, $e_{1}e_{2}f_{1}e_{1} = -f_{1}e_{2}$ and $e_{2}f_{1}e_{1} = f_{1}e_{1}e_{2}$. Its blades in $\Cl(\{1\})$ are $1, e_{1}$ and in $\Cl(\{2\})$ only $1$. The check computes these, and the moments of an even and of a non-even pure state of three modes.

\emph{(v).} Even elements of the algebras of disjoint sets of modes commute by (i). A product of blades $e_{S_{j}}$ of the $\Cl(P_{j})$ is $\pm$ the blade on $\bigcup S_{j}$, which is scalar only if every $S_{j}$ is empty and lies in $\Cl(A)$ only if every $e_{S_{j}}$ does; hence $\langle\prod x_{j}\rangle_{0} = \prod\langle x_{j}\rangle_{0}$ and $E_{A}(\prod x_{j}) = \prod E_{A \cap P_{j}}(x_{j})$ for $x_{j} \in \Cl(P_{j})$. So $\operatorname{tr}\rho = 1$, $\rho_{A} = \prod(\rho_{j})_{A \cap P_{j}}$ (the factors $2^{|P_{j} \setminus A|}$ multiply to $d_{B}$), and since the reduced factors are even and commute, $\rho_{A}^{n} = \prod(\rho_{j})_{A \cap P_{j}}^{n}$ and the traces multiply. $\rho$ is a sum of terms $xx^{\dagger}$ because the commuting positive $\rho_{j}$ have commuting positive square roots in the even parts; it is a projector if every $\rho_{j}$ is. The pair state: with $\Omega = v_{k}w_{k}v_{k'}w_{k'}$ the vacuum projector and $\psi = c + s\,w_{k}w_{k'}$, $\psi\Omega\psi^{\dagger}$ is the projector on $c\ket{00} + s\ket{11}$; expanding with $v_{k}w_{k} = \tfrac12(1 + p_{k})$ gives the displayed form, in which $p_{k}$, $p_{k'}$, $p_{k}p_{k'}$ carry the diagonal and $w_{k}w_{k'} + v_{k'}v_{k} = -\tfrac12(e_{k}f_{k'} + f_{k}e_{k'})$ the coherence. Its blades inside $\Cl(\{k\})$ are $\tfrac14(1 + rp_{k})$, so the reduction is $\tfrac12(1 + rp_{k})$, with $\operatorname{tr}(\tfrac12(1 + rp_{k}))^{2} = \tfrac12(1 + r^{2})$. Reading the pairs of Theorem \claimref{singularity.eviction-window-thermal-product-radiation-entropy} as these pair states, its state is the product of such pair states over the injected pairs (window member and radiated partner, or both halves in the radiation after an eviction), so the statements follow from the first part and (iv). The check verifies the displayed form against the Fock construction, the product, the reductions and the purities at $c^{2} = \tfrac{9}{25}, \tfrac{25}{169}$ and at zero weight.

\emph{(vi).} By (iv) $\rho_{A}$ is the partial trace of the tensor split, so $\operatorname{tr}_{A}\rho_{A}^{n} = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho^{\otimes n}(P^{A}_{\eta} \otimes 1)\bigr)$ as in the proof of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.eviction-keeps-one-of-catalan-many-replica-saddles}(iii). Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-haar-moments-of-random-pure-state}, applied to the sector as a space of dimension $D/2$, gives $\langle\rho^{\otimes n}\rangle = \sum_{\sigma}P_{\sigma}\Pi^{\otimes n}/\prod_{l<n}(D/2 + l)$ with $\Pi = \tfrac12(1 \pm \Gamma)$ and $\Pi^{\otimes n} = 2^{-n}\sum_{U}(\pm 1)^{|U|}\Gamma_{(U)}$, the sum over subsets $U$ of the $n$ copies, $\Gamma_{(U)}$ acting as $\Gamma = \Gamma_{A} \otimes \Gamma_{B}$ on the copies in $U$. The trace of $\Gamma_{(U)}P_{\sigma}(P^{A}_{\eta} \otimes 1)$ factorises over the cycles of $\tau = \sigma^{-1}\eta$ on the $A$ side and of $\sigma$ on the $B$ side; a cycle $z$ contributes $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma_{A}^{|U \cap z|}$, which is $d_{A}$ for $|U \cap z|$ even and $0$ otherwise, and likewise $d_{B}$. So only the $U$ meeting every cycle of $\sigma$ and of $\tau$ evenly contribute, each with sign $+1$ and weight $d_{A}^{\#(\tau)}d_{B}^{\#(\sigma)}$. These $U$ are the common zeros of the indicator vectors of the cycles in $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}$. A linear relation among the indicators equates a union of cycles of $\sigma$ with a union of cycles of $\tau$, a set invariant under $\sigma$ and $\tau$, hence under $\eta = \sigma\tau$, hence empty or everything; so the indicators span a space of dimension $\#(\sigma) + \#(\tau) - 1$ and there are $2^{n + 1 - \#(\sigma) - \#(\tau)}$ such $U$. The trace is $2^{-n}2^{n+1-\#(\sigma)-\#(\tau)}d_{A}^{\#(\tau)}d_{B}^{\#(\sigma)} = 2(d_{A}/2)^{\#(\tau)}(d_{B}/2)^{\#(\sigma)}$, which is the formula; the check confirms the count by enumeration at six cases. At $n = 2$: $2\cdot\tfrac18 d_{A}d_{B}(d_{A} + d_{B})/(\tfrac14 D(D + 2))$. The inequalities at $n = 2$: with $d_{A} \le d_{B}$, $d_{A}(d_{A} + d_{B}) > D + 2$ since $d_{A}^{2} \ge 4$; the others are immediate. At $d_{A} = d_{B} = d$ a permutation with $\#(\sigma) + \#(\tau) = n + 1 - 2g$ contributes $2(d/2)^{n+1-2g}/(d^{2}/2)^{n} = 4^{g}d^{1-n-2g}$ before the factor $\prod_{l}(1 + 2l/d^{2})^{-1}$, and $d^{n+1-2g}/d^{2n}$ in the unrestricted average; $g$ is an integer because the signs of $\sigma$, $\tau$ multiply to that of $\eta$, and $g \ge 0$ with $N_{n,0} = C_{n}$ by clause (ii) of the stored theorem. The limits and the coefficients of $d^{-2}$ follow ($\sum_{l<n}2l = n(n-1)$). Lower bound: $\rho_{A}$ is a state on $d$ dimensions, so $\operatorname{tr}\rho_{A}^{n} \ge d^{1-n}$, with equality only for the flat spectrum, which is not almost sure. The two upper comparisons are verified exactly in the stated range and not proved in general.

\emph{(vii).} In the tensor split a sector is $H_{+} \oplus H_{-}$, each summand the product of an eigenspace of $\Gamma_{A}$ (dimension $m/2$) with one of $\Gamma_{B}$ (dimension $n/2$); put $k_{0} = mn/4$. A unit vector is $\sqrt{t}\,\psi_{+} + \sqrt{1-t}\,\psi_{-}$ with unit $\psi_{\pm} \in H_{\pm}$. The partial trace of a cross term $\psi_{+}\psi_{-}^{\dagger}$ vanishes, the two $B$ eigenspaces being orthogonal, so $\rho_{A} = t\rho_{+} \oplus (1-t)\rho_{-}$ on the two eigenspaces of $\Gamma_{A}$ and $S(\rho_{A}) = h(t) + tS(\rho_{+}) + (1-t)S(\rho_{-})$, $h(t) = -t\ln t - (1-t)\ln(1-t)$. Under the invariant measure (the law of a normalised Gaussian vector), $t$ is the ratio of two independent Gamma variables of shape $k_{0}$, a Beta law with parameters $(k_{0}, k_{0})$, independent of $\psi_{+}$ and $\psi_{-}$, which are independent and invariantly distributed on their summands. Hence $\langle S_{A}\rangle = \langle h(t)\rangle + \langle S\rangle_{m/2,\,n/2}$, the last term given by Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-page-average-entropy-subsystem} (zero for $m = 2$). For the Beta law $\langle t^{1+s}\rangle = \Gamma(k_{0}+1+s)\Gamma(2k_{0})/(\Gamma(k_{0})\Gamma(2k_{0}+1+s))$, whose derivative at $s = 0$ is $\langle t\ln t\rangle = \tfrac12(\psi(k_{0}+1) - \psi(2k_{0}+1)) = -\tfrac12\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{2k_{0}}1/k$, so $\langle h(t)\rangle = \sum_{k=mn/4+1}^{mn/2}1/k$. Adding the Fact's $\sum_{k=n/2+1}^{mn/4}1/k - (m/2 - 1)/n$ gives the formula. The difference from the Fact's value at $(m,n)$ is rational by inspection; with $a_{N} = \sum_{k=N+1}^{2N}1/k$ it is $\Delta = \tfrac{1}{2n} - (a_{mn/2} - a_{n/2})$, and $a_{N+1} - a_{N} = 1/((2N+1)(2N+2))$ lies strictly between $\tfrac12(\tfrac{1}{2N+1} - \tfrac{1}{2N+3})$ and $\tfrac12(\tfrac{1}{2N} - \tfrac{1}{2N+2})$; summing over $n/2 \le N < mn/2$ telescopes to $\tfrac12(\tfrac{1}{n+1} - \tfrac{1}{mn+1}) < a_{mn/2} - a_{n/2} < \tfrac12(\tfrac1n - \tfrac{1}{mn})$, the stated bounds on $\Delta$ (the upper one is below $\tfrac{1}{2mn} + \tfrac{1}{2n^{2}}$). The closed form is the two-block case of a known formula for random states on a direct sum of tensor products (note); it is rederived here from the Fact. For the deficit, convexity of $1/x$ gives $1/k < \int_{k-1/2}^{k+1/2}dx/x$ and $\int_{k-1}^{k}dx/x < \tfrac12(\tfrac{1}{k-1} + \tfrac1k)$; summing over $n/2 < k \le mn/2$: $\ln m - \tfrac{m-1}{mn} < \sum 1/k < \ln\frac{mn+1}{n+1}$, so $\delta_{\mathrm{sec}} > \tfrac{m-2}{2n} + \ln\bigl(1 + \tfrac{m-1}{mn+1}\bigr)$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{sec}} < \tfrac{m-2}{2n} + \tfrac{m-1}{mn} = \tfrac{m}{2n} - \tfrac{1}{mn}$; with $\ln(1+u) > 2u/(2+u)$ the lower bound becomes the stated one. At $m = n$: the upper bound is $\tfrac12 - m^{-2}$; the lower one is $\tfrac14 + \tfrac{6}{37}$ at $m = 4$, $\tfrac38 + \tfrac{14}{137}$ at $m = 8$ and at least $\tfrac12 - \tfrac1m \ge \tfrac{7}{16}$ from $m = 16$, all above $\tfrac25$. The lower bound exceeds $(m-1)/(2n)$ exactly when $n(2m - 4) > m + 1$, true for $m \ge 4$; for $m = 2$ the upper bound is $\tfrac{1}{n} - \tfrac{1}{2n} = \tfrac{1}{2n} = (m-1)/(2n)$, so the deficit is below it. The check evaluates the closed form, the block decomposition, the difference and all bounds for $m, n \le 64$, with $\ln 2$ bracketed by partial sums of $\sum 1/(k2^{k})$, and compares the moments of the Beta law at $m = 2$ with the formula of (vi).
