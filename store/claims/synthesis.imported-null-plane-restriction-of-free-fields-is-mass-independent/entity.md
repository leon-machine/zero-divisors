## formal
On the flat gravitational field ($\hb = 1$, $\Omega = 0$; Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}) let $N$ be a null plane.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (Leutwyler, Klauder and Streit.) The algebra generated by a free field on $N$ (or on a lightlike slab) is irreducible, and the free fields of different masses $m \ge 0$ restricted to $N$ are unitarily equivalent; on spacelike planes the free fields of different masses are inequivalent.
\item[(b)] (Wall.) For a free scalar field of mass $m \ge 0$ the restriction to a null surface, with its null stress $T_{kk} = \tfrac12(\nabla_{k}\Phi)^{2}$ and the commutator of the restricted field, involves no mass; the mass enters only the zero modes and finite-energy constraints. For the Dirac field one half of the spinor is free data on the null surface and the other half is fixed by a constraint in which the mass appears. The vacuum is the ground state of the null energy on the surface.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Provenance:} \cite{LeutwylerKlauderStreit1970}; \cite[Secs.~3.2, 4.1]{Wall2012}.

## technical
Light-front quantisation sets up a free field on a null plane instead of on a spacelike surface. Leutwyler, Klauder and Streit showed in 1970 that on such a plane the fields of different masses become unitarily equivalent, unlike on spacelike surfaces, and that they generate an irreducible algebra. Wall used the same structure on horizons: restricted to a null surface, a free scalar's algebra and null energy do not see the mass apart from zero modes, and for the Dirac field only one half of the spinor is free data on the surface, the mass appearing in the constraint that fixes the other half. The vacuum restricted to the surface is the ground state of the null energy.

## lay
A quantum field can be described by its values on a surface moving at the speed of light rather than on an instant of time. On such a surface, fields of particles of different masses look the same: the mass drops out of the description, apart from a few special modes. This is the standard light-front picture, taken from the literature.

## one-liner
On a null plane free fields of different masses are unitarily equivalent and generate an irreducible algebra (Leutwyler, Klauder and Streit 1970); restricted to a null surface a free field's algebra and null energy carry no mass except in zero modes, the Dirac field's mass entering only its constrained half (Wall 2012).
