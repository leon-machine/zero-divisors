Let $\mathcal{M}$ be such an extension; rain-boundedness is exactly $\mathcal{M} \subseteq \shW$. By local freeness there is a transition matrix $A$ over $C^\infty(\Mt_u)$ expressing an $\mathcal{M}$-frame in the $\shW$-frame, invertible in the interior, and
\[
\det G_{\mathcal{M}} \;=\; (\det A)^2 \,\det G_{\shW} \;=\; -(\det A)^2\, r ,
\]
by Theorem \claimref{sheaf.schwarzschild-degeneration-boundary-tame}(1). Fix a boundary point $x_0$. If $A(x_0, 0)$ is invertible then $A$ is invertible near $x_0$ and $\mathcal{M} = \shW$ there. If not, $\det A(x_0, u) \to 0$ as $u \to 0$, so $\det G_{\mathcal{M}}(x_0, u) = o(r)$. But by (T2$\partial$) exactly one eigenvalue of $G_{\mathcal{M}}$ vanishes at $x_0$ while the others are bounded away from zero, so the dying eigenvalue is $\det G_{\mathcal{M}}$ divided by a unit: it is $o(r)$, contradicting (T3$\partial$). Hence $A$ is invertible along all of $E$, and $\mathcal{M} = \shW$.
