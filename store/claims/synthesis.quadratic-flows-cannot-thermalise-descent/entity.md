## formal
Let $\mathfrak{A}$ be the boundary-mode CAR algebra, realised on each window by null pairs ($a_{j}$, $a_{j}^{*}$ with $\{a_{i}, a_{j}^{*}\} = \delta_{ij}$; Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-algebra-boundary-modes-car-algebra}), its numbers and one-particle matrices read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, J\}$, $J = e_{2}e_{3}$ the complex unit of the radial atom from which the boundary modes descend (\claimref{synthesis.transverse-bivector-is-the-complex-unit-of-the-radial-atom}), and $\omega_{I}$ the I-state at $\beta$ with occupations $n_{j}^{I} = (1 + e^{\beta\omega_{j}})^{-1}$ (Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state}). Hypothesis (T): the time evolution of a window is generated either by a quadratic element $H = H^{*}$, or by an element of the abelian algebra generated by the $\sigma_{k} = 2N_{k} - 1$ (the generators (L$_E$) permits, Proposition \claimref{singularity.number-diagonal-dynamics-models-scrambling-derived}), or by a sum of the boost generator $\sum_{j}\omega_{j}N_{j}$ and such an element.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Conserved charges.)} For a Hermitian one-particle matrix $k$ and $X_{k} = \sum_{ij}k_{ij}a_{i}^{*}a_{j}$, every $Q_{n} := X_{k^{n}}$ ($n \ge 0$) commutes with $X_{k}$, so $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{JtX_{k}})$ conserves the occupations of the eigenmodes of $k$; for the boost dynamics ($k = \operatorname{diag}(\omega_{j})$) these are the $N_{j}$. Every element of the algebra generated by the $\sigma_{k}$, alone or added to the boost generator, also commutes with every $N_{j}$ (for example $\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}$). A term that moves occupation between modes, such as $(a_{1}^{*}a_{2} + a_{2}^{*}a_{1})N_{3}$, does not commute with $N_{1}$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(No relaxation.)} Under (T), for every state $\omega$ of the descent and every $t$, the conserved occupations keep their initial values, and $\omega\circ\alpha_{t} \to \omega_{I}$ on a window, in any sense that implies convergence of the expectations of the $N_{j}$ (of the eigenmode occupations, for a quadratic $H$ with eigenmodes other than the $a_{j}$), holds only if those occupations already have their I-state values. For a quasi-free $\omega$ with one-particle correlation matrix $C$, a quadratic evolution acts as $C \mapsto UCU^{*}$ with $U$ unitary, which keeps the spectrum of $C$ and every $\operatorname{tr}(k^{n}C)$; under the boost dynamics it keeps the diagonal of $C$ and multiplies the coherence $C_{ij} = \omega(a_{j}^{*}a_{i})$ by $e^{-J(\omega_{i} - \omega_{j})t}$ and the pairing $\omega(a_{i}a_{j})$ by $e^{-J(\omega_{i} + \omega_{j})t}$. Gauge invariance can be reached by dephasing; the Fermi-Dirac symbol cannot.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Restriction reaches it.)} The pure pair state $c\ket{00} + s\ket{11}$ of a mode and a partner restricts on the mode to the gauge-invariant state with occupation $s^{2}$, the Gibbs state at $e^{-\beta\omega} = s^{2}/c^{2}$; it is the I-state's restriction to that mode if and only if $s^{2} = n^{I}$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(For the descent.)} Relaxing a non-equilibrium descent state to the I-state therefore needs dynamics that moves occupation between modes, which (L$_E$) excludes. So, under (T), and in particular under (L$_E$), the descent must deposit the Fermi-Dirac occupations directly, mode by mode, for instance as the restriction of pure pair states as in (iii) (the thermofield double on a horizon pair).
\end{enumerate}

## technical
Assume the time evolution of a window is generated by a quadratic element, or by the number-diagonal generators that the locality premise (L$_E$) permits, or by the boost generator plus such terms. A quadratic flow with one-particle matrix $k$ conserves the occupations of $k$'s eigenmodes; for the boost dynamics these are the mode occupations themselves, and number-diagonal products such as $\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}$ conserve every mode occupation too. So under any of these dynamics a state of the descent keeps its occupations for ever: if they are not the Fermi-Dirac values of the I-state at $\beta$, it never relaxes to the I-state. What these flows change is phases: coherences and pairings rotate and, in a large system, dephase, which can restore gauge invariance but not the thermal symbol. Only a term that moves occupation between modes, such as a hop weighted by a third occupation, breaks the conservation, and (L$_E$) excludes such terms.

The I-state is reached instead by restriction: a pure pair of a mode and a partner with amplitudes $c$, $s$ restricts to the Gibbs state at $e^{-\beta\omega} = s^{2}/c^{2}$, as the thermofield double does on a horizon pair. So, under these hypotheses, the descent must deposit the thermal occupations directly.

## lay
After the descent, the framework's black-hole-like modes sit in a particular thermal state. A natural guess is that the descent leaves them out of equilibrium and they then settle into that thermal state, as a hot cup of coffee cools. This result shows that, assuming the modes evolve only by the kinds of motion the framework allows, they cannot settle: those motions never change how many particles sit in each mode, so a state that starts with the wrong numbers keeps them for ever. Only the relative phases between modes wash out. Settling would need an interaction that moves occupation between modes, which the framework's locality premise excludes. The thermal state can still arise, but in a different way: as one half of a larger pure state, which is how the thermal radiation of a horizon arises. So the descent must produce the thermal numbers directly.

## one-liner
If the time evolution of the boundary modes is quadratic or number-diagonal (the dynamics (L$_E$) permits), it conserves mode occupations, so no descent state relaxes to the I-state unless its occupations are already Fermi-Dirac; relaxation would need occupation-moving dynamics, which (L$_E$) excludes, so the I-state must be deposited, for instance by restriction of pure pairs.
