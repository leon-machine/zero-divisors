## formal
(i) \emph{(Radiative breaking.)} In a gauge theory whose tree potential has no mass term, one-loop corrections can produce a minimum away from the origin, so that the symmetry is broken spontaneously and a mass scale appears through the running of a dimensionless coupling (dimensional transmutation) \cite{ColemanWeinberg1973}.

(ii) \emph{(The scalon.)} Let the tree potential $V_{0}(\varphi) = \lambda_{ijkl}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\varphi_{k}\varphi_{l}$ of real scalars be scale free, and let it have a flat direction: a unit vector $n$ with $V_{0}(n) = 0$ at a minimum of $V_{0}$ on the unit sphere. Along $\varphi = \rho n$ the one-loop potential is $A\rho^{4} + B\rho^{4}\ln(\rho^{2}/\mu^{2})$ with
\[
B = \frac{1}{64\pi^{2}v^{4}}\Bigl(3\sum_{\text{vectors}}M_{V}^{4} + \sum_{\text{real scalars}}M_{S}^{4} - 4\sum_{\text{Dirac fermions}}M_{F}^{4}\Bigr),
\]
the masses being the tree masses at $\rho = v = \langle\rho\rangle$ (each colour counted), and the one scalar along the flat direction (the scalon, the pseudo-Goldstone boson of broken scale invariance) has mass $m_{S}^{2} = 8Bv^{2}$:
\[
8\pi^{2}v^{2}m_{S}^{2} = 3\sum_{\text{vectors}}M_{V}^{4} + \sum_{\text{real scalars}}M_{S}^{4} - 4\sum_{\text{Dirac fermions}}M_{F}^{4},
\]
positive exactly when the bosons outweigh the fermions. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{GildenerWeinberg1976}; the displayed form as in \cite{FootKobakhidzeVolkas2007}, equations (10) to (14).

## technical
Coleman and Weinberg showed that a theory with no mass parameter at all can still break its symmetry, the scale being generated by quantum corrections and the running of a coupling. Gildener and Weinberg extended this to many scalars: if the tree-level potential, built only from quartic terms, has a flat direction along which it vanishes, the one-loop correction picks a point on that direction and the scalar along it, the scalon, gets a mass from the fourth powers of every mass the vacuum generates, with weight $3$ for each massive vector, $1$ for each real scalar and $-4$ for each Dirac fermion (per colour). Fermions push the scalon mass down; heavy bosons push it up.

## lay
Some theories contain no built-in mass scale, yet produce one through quantum effects, as the strong force does for the proton. Coleman and Weinberg found how this can work for a Higgs-like field, and Gildener and Weinberg found the formula for the mass of the resulting light scalar particle: it is set by the masses of all the other particles, with heavy force carriers raising it and heavy quarks lowering it.

## one-liner
Coleman and Weinberg: radiative symmetry breaking without a mass term; Gildener and Weinberg: on a flat direction of a scale-free potential the scalon has $8\pi^{2}v^{2}m_{S}^{2} = 3\sum_{V}M^{4} + \sum_{S}M^{4} - 4\sum_{F}M^{4}$.
