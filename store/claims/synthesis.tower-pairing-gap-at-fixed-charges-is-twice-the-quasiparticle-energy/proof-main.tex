\emph{(i).} The element is that of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.composite-scalars-mean-field-level}(2), on the Fock space of the floor's tower modes, which generate the CAR (Fact \claimref{field-theory.imported-tower-limit}). $H_{4}$ is a sum of number operators and of $P^{*}P = \sum_{b,b'}v_{c}(b)v_{a}(b)w_{a}(b')w_{c}(b')$ (with $v$ creating, as in the stored check), which moves a complete pair from block $b'$ to block $b$; so it conserves $N_{a}$ and $N_{c}$, and it annihilates any state in which block $b'$ holds $a$ or $c$ alone, while such a block cannot receive a pair. A block holding one particle alone is therefore inert for the pairing term and contributes $K$. On the remaining $\Omega$ blocks, each empty or paired, the operators $P$, $P^{*}$ and $p - \Omega/2$ close on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$: with $J_{+} = P^{*}$, $J_{-} = P$ restricted to these blocks and $J_{z} = p - \Omega/2$, each block is a quasi-spin $\tfrac12$ (Proposition \claimref{field-theory.how-close-tower-comes-ccr} is the same algebra on a whole floor), and $P^{*}P = J_{+}J_{-} = J^{2} - J_{z}^{2} + J_{z} = (j + j_{z})(j - j_{z} + 1)$ on total quasi-spin $j$, with $j_{z}$ the eigenvalue of $J_{z}$. The total quasi-spin of $\Omega$ spins $\tfrac12$ takes the values $\Omega/2, \Omega/2 - 1, \dots$, and the kinetic term is $K$ times the particle number $2p + s_{a} + s_{c}$. This is the quasi-spin (seniority) solution of the degenerate pairing model (Kerman, 1961; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-quasi-spin-pairing-and-top-condensation}(i)). The check does not rely on it: it builds $H_{4}$ on the full Fock space of the $n_{b} = 2, 3$ floors with GA's exterior model of the CAR (creation by wedge, annihilation by contraction), and in every sector $(N_{a}, N_{c})$, at eight couples $(K, G)$ including $K = 0$ and couplings on both sides of the threshold, verifies exactly that the product of $H_{4} - E$ over the predicted values vanishes on the sector (every eigenvalue is predicted) and that the product over all but one predicted value does not (every predicted value occurs), and that the lowest predicted value at $N_{a} = N_{c} = n$ is the stored band value. For $G > 0$ the energy decreases with $j$ at fixed $j_{z}$, so the band ($j = n_{b}/2$, no inert block) is the lowest level of its sector; a blocked pair of blocks lowers the available quasi-spin by one, as does a lower $j$, and higher seniorities cost more.

\emph{(ii).} At $N_{a} = N_{c} = n$, $0 < n < n_{b}$, the band level is $2Kn - Gn(n_{b} - n + 1)$. The next candidates have the same particle number and quasi-spin lowered by one: either $s_{a} = s_{c} = 1$ with $n - 1$ pairs on $n_{b} - 2$ blocks at top quasi-spin, or no inert block and $j = n_{b}/2 - 1$; both give $2Kn - G(n - 1)(n_{b} - n)$. The difference is $G[n(n_{b} - n + 1) - (n - 1)(n_{b} - n)] = Gn_{b}$, independent of $n$ and $K$. By (i) no other value lies between (the check confirms the two lowest predicted values differ by exactly $Gn_{b}$ in every such sector and that both occur).

\emph{(iii).} The one-shell mean-field energy of the stored result is $f(x) = n_{b}(K - \sqrt{K^{2} + x}) + x/G$ with $x = \Delta^{2}$; $f'(x) = -n_{b}/(2\sqrt{K^{2} + x}) + 1/G$ vanishes exactly at $\sqrt{K^{2} + x} = Gn_{b}/2$, which is positive-$x$ exactly when $G > 2K/n_{b}$ (the stored threshold), and $f''(x) = n_{b}/(4(K^{2} + x)^{3/2}) > 0$. So the mean-field quasiparticle energy is $E = Gn_{b}/2$ and $Gn_{b} = 2E = 2\sqrt{K^{2} + \Delta^{2}} \ge 2\Delta$, with equality exactly at $K = 0$, $\Delta$ being the mean-field fermion mass. Checked exactly at four above-threshold samples with rational $E$ and $\Delta$, one with $K = 0$. On a single shell the uniform scalar channel $P + P^{*}$ moves only along the band, so the level at $2E$ is the pair-breaking (two-quasiparticle) threshold and is not reached by the composite-scalar operator on a floor. Its relation to the amplitude mode of the relativistic model, where many shells are present and the bound state sits at $2m$ (Nambu and Jona-Lasinio; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-quasi-spin-pairing-and-top-condensation}(ii)), is an analogy (argued): the threshold is the floor's counterpart of the energy at which that mode sits, not the mode itself.

\emph{(iv).} Under the reading stated, with the neutral mode at or above the pair-breaking threshold, $m_{h} \ge 2E \ge 2m_{f}$, $m_{f} = \Delta$ the constituent's mean-field mass. The stored $m_{h} = 125.20$ GeV gives $m_{f} \le 62.6$ GeV and the stored $M_{Z} = 91.188$ GeV, the tree value of $m_{h}$ in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.rung-field-d-term-action-and-tree-masses-on-the-lift-locus}, gives $m_{f} \le 45.6$ GeV; the stored $m_{t} = 172.57$ GeV exceeds both bounds (checked exactly), so the top quark is excluded as the sole constituent at this level (one species, single shell). For comparison (outside; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-quasi-spin-pairing-and-top-condensation}(iii), arXiv 1906.09579, which cites the large-$N_{c}$ result): in the minimal top-condensation model the composite Higgs has $m_{\chi} = 2m_{t}$ at large $N_{c}$ and about $\sqrt{2}\,m_{t}$ with gauge and Higgs corrections, both too large. The same paper obtains its own light composite Higgs, about $125$ GeV, from two condensates (mostly bottom-antibottom with a top admixture, through axial $U(1)$ breaking), which is the several-species case left open here. The floor result supplies the analogue of the first relation inside the series. Limits of the reading: the rung field is a doublet with a charged pair, the composite here a single complex scalar of one shell; colour, gauge corrections and the running between the cutoff and the weak scale are absent; the bound concerns a composite of one species, and a composite of several species, or mixing between condensates, is not excluded by this computation.
