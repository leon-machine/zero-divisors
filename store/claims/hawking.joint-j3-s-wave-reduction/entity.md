## formal
The current boson of the s-wave theory is the s-wave reduction of each four-dimensional massless field, and the lift from the boundary modes to the field is the standard partial-wave reconstruction: the near-horizon reduction of field theory on the Schwarzschild exterior identifies each $(\omega, \ell, m, s)$ channel with a two-dimensional mode.

## technical
This modelling premise identifies the current boson of the s-wave theory with the s-wave reduction of each four-dimensional massless field, and takes the lift from the boundary modes to the field to be the standard partial-wave reconstruction. Concretely, the near-horizon reduction of field theory on the Schwarzschild exterior identifies each channel $(\omega, \ell, m, s)$ with a two-dimensional mode.

## lay
Near a black hole, a four-dimensional field can be broken into spherical-wave pieces, each behaving like a simple wave in one space dimension. This premise assumes that the framework's horizon modes are exactly these pieces. It is a modelling assumption, not a theorem.

## one-liner
Premise: by s-wave reduction on the Schwarzschild exterior, each $(\omega, \ell, m, s)$ channel of a massless field is identified with a two-dimensional boundary mode.
