## formal
Let $L_{p,q} := \varinjlim T_{k}$ be the algebraic limit of the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$ (\claimref{obj.tower.tower}), $n = p + q$, $r = p - q$, with ground frame $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ ($x_{i}^{2} = +1$ for $i \le p$, $-1$ for $i > p$) and planes $(e_{j}, f_{j})$, $e_{j}^{2} = 1$, $f_{j}^{2} = -1$, $j \ge 1$. Write $H_{\infty} := H_{1} \perp H_{2} \perp \cdots$, so that $L_{0,0} = \Cl(H_{\infty})$, and $B_{\infty} \subset L_{p,q}$ for the subalgebra generated by the planes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every $(p,q)$, with $r$ of either parity, $L_{p,q} = \Cl(\R^{p,q} \perp H_{\infty})$ and
\[
L_{p,q} \;\cong\; \Cl(H_{\infty}) \;\cong\; \Mat_{2^{\infty}}.
\]
An isomorphism $\Phi \colon L_{p,q} \to L_{0,0}$ is the extension of the frame bijection $x_{i} \mapsto e_{i}$ ($i \le p$), $x_{p+i} \mapsto f_{i}$ ($i \le q$), $e_{j} \mapsto e_{p+j}$, $f_{j} \mapsto f_{q+j}$; it carries $T_{k}$ into the floor $T'_{k + \max(p,q)}$ of the tower over $\Cl(0,0)$, and for $n \ge 1$ it does not carry $B_{\infty}$ onto the plane subalgebra of $L_{0,0}$, which is all of $L_{0,0}$. The algebra $L_{p,q}$ therefore determines neither $n$ nor $r$.
\item[(ii)] For $r$ even, with Theorem \claimref{tower.algebraic-limit-even-charge}: $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}} \cong \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$, the ground algebra being absorbed whether it is of type $\Mat_{k}(\R)$ or $\Mat_{k}(\HH)$.
\item[(iii)] For $r$ odd, $L_{p,q} \cong \Cl^{0}(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$, where $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \cong \Cl(p, q-1)$ if $q \ge 1$ and $\Cl^{0}(p,0) \cong \Cl(0, p-1)$, both of even residue; and $L_{p,q}$ is \emph{not} isomorphic to $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$. For a finite-dimensional $A$ the centre of $A \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$ is $Z(A) \otimes 1$; it is $\R$ for $L_{p,q}$, and for $A = \Cl(p,q)$ it is the two-dimensional centre $\R \oplus \R$ or $\C$ of Lemma \claimref{tower.grade-twist-when-it-inner}. Each finite floor $T_{k}$ has the two-dimensional centre $\mathrm{span}\{1, \omega_{T_{k}}\}$, and of it only $\R$ commutes with the next plane.
\item[(iv)] For the frame $*$-structure of Lemma \claimref{tower.structure-tower-c-norms}, the complexification of $\Phi$ is a $*$-isomorphism. Hence for every $(p,q)$ the complexified limit carries a unique C*-norm, and its completion is the CAR algebra, the completion of $L_{0,0}^{\C}$ by Theorem \claimref{tower.car-limit-even-charge} at $p = q = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
So for odd charge the limit is again a plain tensor product with $\Mat_{2^{\infty}}$, of the even ground algebra $\Cl^{0}(p,q)$ rather than of $\Cl(p,q)$, and no invariant of the algebra, real K-theoretic or other, separates residues or dimensions. What the top of the tower keeps of the residue is carried by the planes' subalgebra $B_{\infty}$, whose relative commutant is $\Cl^{0}(p,q)$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.plane-commutant-even-ground}).

## technical
The limit of the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$ is the Clifford algebra of an infinite quadratic space with countably many orthonormal vectors of each sign. Any two such spaces are isometric: list the positive vectors of each in one sequence and the negative vectors in another, and match the lists. So every tower's limit, whatever its ground signature, is the same algebra, the Clifford algebra of countably many hyperbolic planes, which is the algebraic $2^{\infty}$ matrix algebra. The matching shifts the planes along to make room for the ground generators, so it moves floors upwards and does not respect the planes' subalgebra; the bare algebra forgets the dimension and the residue. For even residue this shows that tensoring with $\Mat_{2^{\infty}}$ absorbs the ground algebra, quaternionic type included. For odd residue the limit is $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$ and not $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$: the centre of a tensor product with $\Mat_{2^{\infty}}$ is the centre of the finite factor, which is one-dimensional for the limit but two-dimensional for odd $\Cl(p,q)$. At each finite floor the volume element is central, but the next plane anticommutes with it, so the centre dies in the limit. The matching respects the adjoint of the frame, so after complexification every limit completes to the same CAR algebra. The residue survives at the top of the tower only through the planes' subalgebra, whose commutant is the even ground algebra.

## lay
The series builds, over every starting algebra, an infinite ladder by adding one pair of opposite directions at each rung. For starting algebras of one kind the series knew what the top of the ladder is; for the other kind it was an open question. The answer is that the top is the same in every case: with infinitely many rungs there is always room to relabel, so the starting directions are absorbed into the rungs. Which tower one started from is not visible in the top as a bare algebra; it is visible only if one also remembers which part of the top came from the rungs.

## one-liner
Every tower's algebraic limit, odd charge included, is $\Cl(H_{\infty}) \cong \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$ (completing to the CAR algebra); for $p - q$ odd it is $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$, not $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^{\infty}}$, and the residue survives only through the planes' subalgebra.
