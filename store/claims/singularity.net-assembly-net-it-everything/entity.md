## formal
For $F$ with at least two points, $J(F)$ is the whole $F$-mode algebra, so the two-level discrepancy computed against it vanishes identically. The assignment $F \mapsto J(F)$ is covariant (isotone) under inclusion: it is a net of algebras, the structure of an algebra of regions, and not a presheaf; it has no restriction maps, and the question whether it is a sheaf does not arise. Its ``stalk'' at a point, the algebra generated by the big fibre at $x$ alone, contains elements odd at every other point of $F$.

## technical
For a finite truncation $F = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\}$ of $E$, the net assembly $J(F)$ is the subalgebra of the $F$-mode algebra generated by the big fibres $B_j = \operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Ad}\sigma(x_j))$, the elements even in mode $j$. In a Jordan-Wigner representation $\operatorname{Ad}(\sigma_x)$ fixes the ladder $v_y$ for every $y \neq x$, so when $F$ has at least two points every generator of every mode lies in some other point's big fibre, $J(F)$ is the whole $F$-mode algebra, and the two-level discrepancy computed against it vanishes identically.

The assignment $F \mapsto J(F)$ is isotone under inclusion: it is a net of algebras, an algebra of regions, not a presheaf, so it has no restriction maps and the sheaf question does not arise. The algebra generated by the big fibre at a single point $x$ already contains elements, such as $v_y$ for $y \neq x$, that are odd at another point of $F$.

## lay
There are two natural ways to build what the classical level can see over a region from what it sees at each point. This result treats the second way, which collects what each point's reading leaves untouched and lets those pieces generate an algebra. With two or more points that algebra is everything, so on this reading the gap between the two levels is zero. The construction behaves like the algebras of regions used in quantum field theory, not like a sheaf.

## one-liner
On any finite set of two or more boundary points the net assembly generates the whole mode algebra, so its two-level discrepancy vanishes, and it is an isotone net of algebras rather than a sheaf.
