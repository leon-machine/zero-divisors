Throughout, the gravitational field is the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand gauge field $\hb = 1 - \beta P$ of Fact \claimref{sheaf.gauge-field-cometric}, $\beta = \sqrt{2M/r}$, on flat spacetime (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}), invertible on $r > 0$, with derived line element $dt^{2} - (dr + \beta\,dt)^{2} - r^{2}d\Omega^{2}$ read through Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(i). The field does not depend on $t$, so the background translation $t \mapsto t + \tau$ is a symmetry of the gravitational field and $K = \hb^{-1}(e_{t})$ is a Killing vector in the sense of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(vi); $Q(K) = 1 - \beta^{2} = 1 - 2M/r$ vanishes exactly on $r = 2M$, where $K$ is normal, and the surface gravity there is $\kappa = 1/(4M)$, the value fixed by Theorem \claimref{singularity.euclidean-section-period} (imaginary Killing time of period $2\pi/\kappa = 8\pi M$ closes the Euclidean section) and recomputed in (ii) below as the non-affinity of the horizon generator. Write $s = \sqrt{r/2M}$, $\rho = s^{2} = r/2M$, so $\beta = 1/s$.

\emph{(i)} Radial null directions of the derived metric satisfy $(1 - \beta^{2})\dot t^{2} - 2\beta\,\dot t\dot r - \dot r^{2} = 0$, so $dr/dt = 1 - \beta$ (outgoing) or $dr/dt = -(1 + \beta)$ (ingoing); the labels $u = t - \int dr/(1 - \beta)$ and $v = t + \int dr/(1 + \beta)$ are constant along the outgoing and ingoing worldlines respectively. With $r = 2Ms^{2}$, $dr = 4Ms\,ds$ and $1 \mp \beta = (s \mp 1)/s$,
\[
\kappa\int\frac{dr}{1 \mp \beta} \;=\; \int\frac{s^{2}\,ds}{s \mp 1} \;=\; \int\Bigl(s \pm 1 + \frac{1}{s \mp 1}\Bigr)ds \;=\; \frac{s^{2}}{2} \pm s + \ln|s \mp 1| ,
\]
the partial fractions $s^{2}/(s - 1) = s + 1 + 1/(s - 1)$ and $s^{2}/(s + 1) = s - 1 + 1/(s + 1)$ being exact identities of rational functions (checked). Hence, for $s > 1$, $e^{-\kappa u} = e^{-\kappa t}(s - 1)e^{s^{2}/2 + s}$ and $e^{\kappa v} = e^{\kappa t}(s + 1)e^{s^{2}/2 - s}$, and the functions $U = -e^{-\kappa t}(s - 1)e^{s^{2}/2 + s}$, $V = e^{\kappa t}(s + 1)e^{s^{2}/2 - s}$ of the statement equal $-e^{-\kappa u}$ and $e^{\kappa v}$ there. Both are smooth on $r > 0$ ($s$ is smooth in $r$ there). $UV = -(s - 1)(s + 1)e^{s^{2}} = -(\rho - 1)e^{\rho}$, the exponents adding to $s^{2}$ and the prefactors to $s^{2} - 1$ (checked). $U$ vanishes exactly where $s = 1$, is negative for $s > 1$ and positive for $s < 1$, since $e^{(\cdot)}$ never vanishes; $V > 0$ for all $s \ge 0$. Differentiating a product $P(s)e^{E(s)}$ by $(P' + PE')e^{E}$: $\partial_{s}\bigl[(s - 1)e^{s^{2}/2 + s}\bigr] = \bigl(1 + (s - 1)(s + 1)\bigr)e^{s^{2}/2 + s} = s^{2}e^{s^{2}/2 + s}$ (checked), so $\partial_{s}U = -s^{2}e^{s^{2}/2 + s}e^{-\kappa t} \ne 0$ for $s > 0$. The symmetry $t \mapsto t + \tau$ multiplies $U$ by $e^{-\kappa\tau}$ and $V$ by $e^{\kappa\tau}$, because $t$ enters only through $e^{\mp\kappa t}$: $\partial_{t}U = -\kappa U$, $\partial_{t}V = \kappa V$, and $UV$ is invariant.

\emph{(ii)} In the chart $(v, r)$ the derived metric is $f\,dv^{2} - 2\,dv\,dr - r^{2}d\Omega^{2}$ with $f = 1 - 2M/r$ (substituting $dv = dt_{\mathrm{S}} + dr/f$ in $f\,dt_{\mathrm{S}}^{2} - dr^{2}/f$, $t_{\mathrm{S}}$ the Schwarzschild label $t - \int\beta\,dr/(1 - \beta^{2})$). The radial direction $\partial_{r}$ at fixed $v$ has $g(\partial_{r}, \partial_{r}) = 0$: the hypersurfaces $v = \mathrm{const}$, which are the level sets of $V = e^{\kappa v}$, are null, generated by the ingoing radial null worldlines. In that chart only $r$-derivatives of the metric are nonzero, and with $g^{vv} = 0$, $g^{vr} = -1$, $g^{rr} = -f$: $\Gamma^{r}{}_{rr} = \tfrac12 g^{rd}(2\partial_{r}g_{dr} - \partial_{d}g_{rr}) = 0$, since $g_{vr}$ is constant and $g_{rr} = 0$; so $r$ is an affine parameter along $v = \mathrm{const}$, and these worldlines are free-fall null worldlines by Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(iii), crossing $r = 2M$ regularly. Likewise $\Gamma^{v}{}_{vv} = \tfrac12 g^{vr}(-\partial_{r}g_{vv}) = f'/2$, equal to $1/(4M)$ at $r = 2M$: the non-affinity of the horizon generator $\partial_{v}$, the surface gravity $\kappa$ (checked, with the tabulated value at two masses). Along $v = \mathrm{const}$, $u = v - 2r^{*}$ with $r^{*} = \int dr/f = r + 2M\ln|r/2M - 1|$, so $2\kappa r^{*} = \rho + \ln|\rho - 1|$ and $U = -e^{-\kappa u} = -e^{-\kappa v}(\rho - 1)e^{\rho}$, whose $\rho$-derivative is $-e^{-\kappa v}\rho\,e^{\rho}$ by the same product rule (checked): nonzero for $\rho > 0$ and equal to $-e^{1 - \kappa v}$ on the horizon. So $U$ is a smooth strictly monotone function of the affine parameter on each ingoing generator, with nonvanishing derivative at the horizon: a regular parameter. The level sets of $U$ are the hypersurfaces $u = \mathrm{const}$ generated by the outgoing radial null worldlines. Under the s-wave reduction premise (J3), Premise \claimref{hawking.joint-j3-s-wave-reduction}, each $(\omega, \ell, m, s)$ channel of a massless field on the exterior is a two-dimensional boundary mode, so the outgoing sector near the horizon is one chiral field per light ray, constant along the outgoing worldlines: a function of $U$ and the transverse labels, read off along any ingoing generator. This reduction is a hypothesis of the result, not derived here. Its carrier on the ingoing generator through $p \in \mathcal{H}^{+}$ is the continuum mode space $\CAR(L^{2}(\R, dU)\otimes K)$ of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.continuum-mode-space}, the mode set attached to the sheet of $p$ generated by the ingoing null direction, in the labelling by null direction of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.r-loc-needs-sheet-labelled-modes}. By (i) the symmetry acts on it through $U \mapsto e^{-\kappa\tau}U$, and the weight that preserves the anticommutator $\{\psi(U), \psi^{*}(U')\} = \delta(U - U')$ is $\tfrac12$: $\beta_{\tau}(\psi(U)) = e^{-\kappa\tau/2}\psi(e^{-\kappa\tau}U)$, the boost of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises} with $\xi = -\kappa U\partial_{U}$. On $U < 0$ (outside) the flow moves $U$ towards $0$, the future along the ingoing generator, so it is future-directed there; on $U > 0$ (inside) it moves $U$ away from $0$, the past. Both halves exist at the crossing for any hole whose late-time horizon lies in the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand field; the interior half ends at $r = 0$, where $\rho = 0$ and $U = e^{-\kappa v}$, so $U$ ranges over the half-line $(-\infty, e^{-\kappa v})$ on the generator, on which the translation group of (N$_{+}$) does not act (the generator of translations on $L^{2}$ of a half-line has no self-adjoint extension); the condition of (v) is therefore posed in the local form of (iv), whose limit object lives on the whole line.

\emph{(iii)} Fix $U_{0}$ and write $U = U_{0} + x$, $U' = U_{0}$, $a = \xi'(U_{0})$, $b = \tfrac12\xi''(U_{0})$, $c = \tfrac16\xi'''(U_{0})$. Then $\xi(U) - \xi(U') = ax + bx^{2} + cx^{3} + O(x^{4})$ and $\xi'(U)\xi'(U') = a(a + 2bx + 3cx^{2}) + O(x^{3})$, so the squared ratio of the weight-$\tfrac12$ kernels, $\xi'(U)\xi'(U')\,x^{2}/(\xi(U) - \xi(U'))^{2}$, is
\[
\frac{a^{2} + 2abx + 3acx^{2}}{a^{2} + 2abx + (b^{2} + 2ac)x^{2}} + O(x^{3}) \;=\; 1 + \frac{ac - b^{2}}{a^{2}}x^{2} + O(x^{3}) \;=\; 1 + \frac{S_{\xi}(U_{0})}{6}x^{2} + O(x^{3}),
\]
because $(ac - b^{2})/a^{2} = \tfrac16\xi'''/\xi' - \tfrac14(\xi''/\xi')^{2} = \tfrac16 S_{\xi}$. The ratio itself is therefore $1 + \tfrac{1}{12}S_{\xi}x^{2} + O(x^{3})$, and the difference of the kernels is $\bigl(\tfrac{1}{12}S_{\xi}x^{2} + O(x^{3})\bigr)/x = \tfrac{1}{12}S_{\xi}(U_{0})\,x + O(x^{2})$: continuous across the diagonal and zero on it. The $i0$ prescriptions agree because $\xi' > 0$ preserves the orientation. This covariance is specific to the kernel and its weight: the same kernel $1/(U - U' + i0)$ carried with weight $0$ or $1$ has squared ratio $x^{2}/(\xi(U) - \xi(U'))^{2} = a^{-2} + O(x)$, respectively $(\xi'(U)\xi'(U'))^{2}x^{2}/(\xi(U) - \xi(U'))^{2} = a^{2} + 2abx + O(x^{2})$, neither $1 + O(x^{2})$ unless $a = 1$ and $b = 0$, so for those weights the difference is singular on the diagonal unless $\xi' \equiv 1$ (a test of the covariance weight, not of other fields). A weight-$h$ field with its own ground-state kernel $(U - U' + i0)^{-2h}$ has ratio $(1 + \tfrac16 S_{\xi}x^{2} + O(x^{3}))^{h}$, so the difference of its kernels is $\tfrac{h}{6}S_{\xi}(U_{0})x^{2 - 2h} + O(x^{3 - 2h})$, continuous for every $h \le 1$ and equal to $S_{\xi}/6$ on the diagonal at $h = 1$ (checked: the squared ratio's $x^{2}$ coefficient is $S_{\xi}/6$, which is the weight-$1$ difference's diagonal value). Where $\xi'(U_{0}) = 0$ (for example $\xi = U^{3}$ at $0$) the ratio vanishes. The expansion is exact algebra in $a, b, c$; the check computes the squared ratio as an exact rational function of $x$ for three polynomial $\xi$ at three base points, tabulating $S_{\xi}/6$, with the covariance-weight controls. Consequently, if a kernel is $W_{0}$ in the parameter $U$ plus a remainder bounded near a point, it is $W_{0}$ in the parameter $\xi$ plus a remainder bounded there (the Schwarzian term is continuous, hence bounded), and conversely; the parameters $U$, $r$ and the affine parameter of (ii) are related by regular reparametrisations near the horizon.

\emph{(iv)} For $g \in L^{1}\cap L^{2}$ let $\psi(g) = \int g(U)\psi(U)\,dU$. By (ii), $\beta_{\tau}(\psi(g)) = \psi(g_{\varepsilon})$ with $\varepsilon = e^{-\kappa\tau}$ and $g_{\varepsilon}(U) = \varepsilon^{-1/2}g(U/\varepsilon)$, the unitary dilation of $L^{2}(\R, dU)$, whose support shrinks to $U = 0$ as $\tau \to \infty$. Then
\[
\omega\bigl(\beta_{\tau}(\psi^{*}(f)\psi(g))\bigr) \;=\; \iint \bar f_{\varepsilon}(U')g_{\varepsilon}(U)\,\bigl[W_{0}(U, U') + R(U, U')\bigr]\,dU\,dU'
\;=\; \iint \bar f(x')g(x)\,W_{0}(x, x')\,dx\,dx' \;+\; \varepsilon\iint \bar f(x')g(x)\,R(\varepsilon x, \varepsilon x')\,dx\,dx' ,
\]
substituting $U = \varepsilon x$, $U' = \varepsilon x'$: the measure gives $\varepsilon^{2}$, the two half-density factors $\varepsilon^{-1}$, and $W_{0}(\varepsilon x, \varepsilon x') = \varepsilon^{-1}W_{0}(x, x')$ (the $i0$ is unchanged for $\varepsilon > 0$), so the singular part is exactly invariant, while the remainder keeps one factor $\varepsilon$ (checked: for a polynomial remainder the scaled contribution is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ with no constant term; for weight $0$ or $1$ the singular part is multiplied by $\varepsilon^{-1}$ or $\varepsilon$ and is not invariant). Since $R$ is bounded on a neighbourhood of $(0, 0)$ by some $C$, for $\varepsilon$ small enough that $\varepsilon\,\mathrm{supp}$ lies in that neighbourhood (test functions of compact support, dense in $L^{1}\cap L^{2}$; the general case by approximation, the two-point functions being bounded on $L^{2}$), the second term is at most $\varepsilon C\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{1} \to 0$. So $\omega\circ\beta_{\tau} \to \omega_{0}$ on every two-point function; a gauge-invariant quasi-free state is determined by its two-point function (Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-quasi-free-kms-reduces-two}, the quasi-free structure), and $\omega\circ\beta_{\tau}$ is gauge-invariant quasi-free with two-point function converging to that of $\omega_{0}$, so $\omega\circ\beta_{\tau} \to \omega_{0}$ on every monomial, hence weakly on the carrier. The hypothesis is sufficient and not necessary: all the proof uses is $\varepsilon\iint\bar f g R(\varepsilon x, \varepsilon x') \to 0$, which also holds when $R$ is bounded but discontinuous at the origin, and for every finite-rank change of the symbol $T_{0} \mapsto T_{0} + \sum_{k}c_{k}|a_{k}\rangle\langle b_{k}|$ with $a_{k}, b_{k} \in L^{2}$, since the dilated test functions $f_{\varepsilon}$ tend weakly to zero as $\varepsilon \to 0$ and so $\langle a_{k}, f_{\varepsilon}\rangle \to 0$; such states lie outside the class of (iii) when the $a_{k}$ are discontinuous at $0$ and yet have the limit $\omega_{0}$. If instead $R$ is homogeneous of degree $-1$, $R(\varepsilon x, \varepsilon x') = \varepsilon^{-1}R(x, x')$, the second term equals $\iint\bar f g R$ for every $\varepsilon$ and does not vanish (checked with a rational homogeneous kernel); such an $R$ is unbounded near the origin. The ground state for the symmetry's own flow on the exterior half, the Boulware-type state, is invariant under $\beta_{\tau}$ and not equal to $\omega_{0}$ (its kernel is translation-invariant in $u = -\kappa^{-1}\ln(-U)$, not in $U$), so its remainder $W_{B} - W_{0}$ is $\beta_{\tau}$-invariant, that is homogeneous of degree $-1$ in $(U, U')$, and nonzero: it is not in the class.

\emph{(v)} At late times the ingoing generator through $p \in \mathcal{H}^{+}$ lies, on both sides of the horizon, in the region outside the collapsed matter, where the gravitational field is the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand field (the join to the matter's field is Birkhoff's theorem, literature); so (i) to (iv) apply on it. Under (N$_{+}^{\mathrm{coll}}$) the state of the outgoing modes on the sheet has kernel $W_{0} + R$ with $R$ bounded near $p$ in some regular parameter, hence in $U$ by (iii), and by (iv) its late-time limit under the symmetry's flow is $\omega_{0}$, the state of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(i) for the translations in $U$. The exterior outgoing modes are those with support in $U < 0$, and the subalgebra $\CAR(L^{2}(\R_{-}, dU)\otimes K)$ is $\beta_{\tau}$-invariant. By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(ii) the restriction of $\omega_{0}$ to the half-generator on which the boost at rate $\kappa$ is future-directed is the continuum I-state of Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state-continuum} at $\beta = 2\pi/\kappa$ for that boost, jointly KMS, even and unique; here that half is $U < 0$ by (ii), and the flow $\xi = -\kappa U\partial_{U}$ on it is exactly the stored past-half clause of (ii), the boost $\beta_{-t}$ future-directed on $\CAR(L^{2}(\R_{-}, du))$ with symbol $(1 + e^{2\pi w})^{-1}$ on $|u|^{-1/2 + iw}$. That clause is a statement of CAR algebra on $L^{2}(\R, du)$ with the translations and the weight-$\tfrac12$ dilation, and nothing in its proof refers to which null line carries $u$, so it applies verbatim with $u = U$ on the ingoing generator, although the stored theorem names only the horizons (a) to (c). With $\kappa = 1/(4M)$, $\beta = 8\pi M$, and in Killing frequency $\varpi$ the occupation is $(1 + e^{2\pi\varpi/\kappa})^{-1}$, the symbol of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(iii) (checked: the Mellin symbol at $w = \varpi/\kappa$ satisfies the two-point KMS identity at $\beta\kappa = 2\pi$ and at no other tested multiple). This is the late-time state of the near-horizon exterior outgoing modes of the collapse hole, thermal at $\kappa/2\pi = 1/(8\pi M)$ on the continuum carrier. That it is Hawking's flux at infinity uses Premise (I), Premise \claimref{hawking.premise-i-interface-identification}, which carries the occupancy of each near-horizon channel to the asymptotic channel by the exterior propagation, with the stored greybody inheritance; both are cited as hypotheses and not redone. The interior half $U > 0$ carries the restriction of $\omega_{0}$ for the reversed flow, the I-state at the same $\beta$ by the same theorem's past-half clause, and $\omega_{0}$ is pure on the whole generator (its symbol is a projection), so the exterior thermal state is the marginal of a pure state entangled across $\mathcal{H}^{+}$. The past half of $\mathcal{H}^{+}$, absent for a collapse hole, is never used: the half-generator cut is on the ingoing generator, at the horizon.

\emph{(vi)} The stored case (a) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises} poses (N$_{+}$) on the whole generators of the future horizon of the extended solution, and by the symmetry of the extended solution equally on those of the past horizon: a ground state on both sheets at the bifurcation surface, the Hartle-Hawking-type condition. (N$_{+}^{\mathrm{coll}}$) poses it on the ingoing sheet only, in the scaling limit at $\mathcal{H}^{+}$; the ingoing sector (functions of $V$) is not constrained by it and is fixed by the data of the collapse's past, which for a star collapsing from a region with vanishing gravitational field is the ground state in $v$ (no incoming flux): the Unruh-type condition. The Boulware-type state is excluded by (iv). The condition is sufficient, in the sense of (iii) and (iv): every state in the short-distance class has the ground state as its scaling limit; it is not necessary, since (iv) shows the same limit for every finite-rank change of the symbol. That a state prepared from regular data before the collapse is in the class at $\mathcal{H}^{+}$ is the propagation of short-distance regularity (Fulling, Sweeny and Wald 1978; the flux from it, Fredenhagen and Haag 1990), cited from memory and not imported: it is the justification the open problem asks for, argued here and not derived in the store; the companion result filed alongside shows it exactly for the in-vacuum of a null-shell collapse, whose ray-tracing map has a finite Schwarzian at the horizon ray.
