$\Cl(p,q)$ is semisimple, so it is $\Mat(m, D)$ or $\Mat(m, D) \oplus \Mat(m, D)$, and the commutant of an irreducible module is $D$. The centre is $\R$ when $n = p + q$ is even and $\R[\omega]$ when $n$ is odd, $\omega = e_{1} \cdots e_{n}$ the volume element, with $\omega^{2} = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(-1)^{q}$; so two components ($\omega^{2} = +1$) or a complex centre ($\omega^{2} = -1$) according to $n$ and $q$, which depend on $r$ as stated. For the division algebra, Lounesto's construction gives a primitive idempotent
\[
f \;=\; \prod_{i=1}^{k}\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + e_{T_{i}}\bigr)
\]
from a maximal set of $k$ commuting basis blades squaring to $+1$ generating a group of order $2^{k}$, and then $f\,\Cl(p,q)\,f \cong D$, of real dimension $1$, $2$ or $4$. Bott periodicity in the form $\Cl(p+1, q+1) \cong \Mat(2, \Cl(p,q))$ (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-eight-towers-mod-8-clock}) preserves $D$ and the component count, so both depend on the residue alone; the dependence within one period is the classical table (Cartan, Bott), recomputed here: the script builds every $\Cl(p,q)$ of dimension at most five in \texttt{GA.py}, computes the centre by exact linear algebra, finds a primitive idempotent by exhaustive search over blade sets and computes $\dim_{\R} f\,\Cl(p,q)\,f$ by exact rank, agreeing with the table on all twenty signatures and on the eight P3 representatives.
