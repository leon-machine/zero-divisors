## formal
Let $A$ be an associative unital ring and $(v_{a}, w_{a})$, $a = 1, \dots, m$, switches in $A$ ($v_{a}^{2} = w_{a}^{2} = 0$, $v_{a}w_{a} + w_{a}v_{a} = 1$; Theorem \claimref{synthesis.set-and-clear-of-a-bit-generate-the-hyperbolic-plane}) such that for $a \ne b$ each of $v_{a}, w_{a}$ anticommutes with each of $v_{b}, w_{b}$. Put $e_{a} = v_{a} + w_{a}$, $f_{a} = v_{a} - w_{a}$, $n_{a} = v_{a}w_{a}$, $h_{a} = 1 - 2n_{a}$, and
\[
u = e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{m} \ \ (m \text{ odd}), \qquad u = f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{m} \ \ (m \text{ even}), \qquad X(x) = u\,x\,u^{-1}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The exchange is inner.)} $u^{2} = \pm 1$, and $X(v_{a}) = w_{a}$, $X(w_{a}) = v_{a}$ for every $a$; $X^{2}$ is the identity. On the subring generated by the switches $X$ is the only ring homomorphism that exchanges set and clear in every switch. The statement is for finitely many switches: for infinitely many modes the exchange is still an automorphism of the algebra they generate, but no such product $u$ exists and nothing here makes it inner.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(What it fixes.)} $X(e_{a}) = e_{a}$ and $X(f_{a}) = -f_{a}$, so for $S, S' \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}$ the product $e_{S}f_{S'}$ of the $e_{a}$, $a \in S$, and the $f_{a}$, $a \in S'$, goes to $(-1)^{|S'|}e_{S}f_{S'}$: every blade is fixed up to sign, and $u$ is itself a blade. Hence $X$ preserves the integer span of the blades (the image of the integer Clifford algebra of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.integer-clifford-algebras-contain-no-switch}), each plane $\Z v_{a} + \Z w_{a}$ with its form $(\alpha v_{a} + \beta w_{a})^{2} = \alpha\beta$, and the parity of the number of vector factors. If $A$ has an anti-automorphism $\rho$ fixing every $v_{a}$ and $w_{a}$ (reversal), then $X\rho = \rho X$ on the subring generated by the switches, and $X\rho$ is there the anti-automorphism exchanging $v_{a}$ and $w_{a}$: the adjoint is the exchange composed with reversal.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(What it moves.)} $X(n_{a}) = 1 - n_{a}$ and $X(h_{a}) = -h_{a}$ with $h_{a} = e_{a}f_{a}$; so the number $\sum_{a}n_{a}$ goes to $m$ minus itself, $h_{1}\cdots h_{m}$ goes to $(-1)^{m}h_{1}\cdots h_{m}$, and the two idempotents $p_{\mathrm{off}} = \prod_{a}(1 - n_{a})$ and $p_{\mathrm{on}} = \prod_{a}n_{a}$ are exchanged. The volume element $h_{1}\cdots h_{m}$ is reversed exactly when $m$ is odd, which is when $u$ is a product of an odd number of the $e_{a}$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(No invariant vacuum.)} If $\psi \in A$ satisfies $w_{a}\psi = 0$ for one $a$ and $X(\psi) = \psi$, then $\psi = 0$. If $q \in A$ satisfies $X(q) = -q$ and $\omega$ is an additive map from $A$ to an abelian group with $\omega \circ X = \omega$, then $2\,\omega(q) = 0$.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Integer states.)} Call an additive map $\omega : A \to \Z$ with $\omega(1) = 1$ an integer state. No integer state satisfies $\omega \circ X = \omega$, for any $m \ge 1$ (the argument uses only additivity, $\omega(1) = 1$ and $X(n_{1}) = 1 - n_{1}$). The class is the whole content: on an algebra over $\Q$, such as $\Cl_{\Q}(m,m)$, there are no integer states at all (an additive map from a divisible group to $\Z$ is zero), so the statement says something only on a ring such as the integer span of the monomials below, where the existence half is placed. If $A$ carries the adjoint $x \mapsto x^{\dagger}$ of (ii) and $\omega(v_{a}v_{a}^{\dagger}) \ge 0$, $\omega(w_{a}w_{a}^{\dagger}) \ge 0$ for all $a$, then $\omega(n_{a}) \in \{0, 1\}$ for every $a$: the state gives each switch a definite position, its charge $q(\omega) = \sum_{a}\omega(2n_{a} - 1)$ is an integer with $|q| \le m$ and $q \equiv m \pmod 2$, and $q(\omega \circ X) = -q(\omega)$. On the integer span of the ordered monomials in the $v_{a}, w_{a}$ inside $\Cl_{\Q}(m,m)$ every such value of $q$ occurs: for each configuration $\sigma \in \{0,1\}^{m}$ the map $\omega_{\sigma}(x) = 2^{m}\langle p_{\sigma}x\rangle_{0}$, $p_{\sigma} = \prod_{a}(\sigma_{a}n_{a} + (1 - \sigma_{a})(1 - n_{a}))$, is an integer state with these two properties and $\omega_{\sigma}(n_{a}) = \sigma_{a}$, and $\omega_{\sigma} \circ X = \omega_{\bar\sigma}$ for the complementary configuration.
\item[(vi)] \emph{(With one half.)} On $\Cl_{\Q}(m,m)$ the scalar part $\tau = \langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_{0}$ satisfies $\tau \circ X = \tau$ and $\tau(n_{a}) = \tfrac12$; it is integer-valued on the integer span of the blades and not on the integer span of the monomials in the $v_{a}, w_{a}$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Reading (argued, not proved).} The foundation's distinction between nothing and something (Premise \claimref{synthesis.premise-the-bit}) is, in the algebra, the label of a blade, the set of directions present in it, and by (ii) the exchange fixes every label. The off and on of a switch are the two signs of $h_{a} = e_{a}f_{a}$, a sign the bit does not carry and the exchange reverses. So in the order that builds directions first the two positions of a switch are not distinguished by anything the foundation holds, and an invariant value assignment exists, on an algebra that contains no switch. In the order that builds the switch first, over the integers, there is none: no integer state is invariant, every integer state that is positive on the two tests $v_{a}v_{a}^{\dagger}$, $w_{a}w_{a}^{\dagger}$ puts each switch definitely on or off, and which way is a label, moved by an inner automorphism. The descent's flag (Definition \claimref{obj.dark-matter.witt-flag}) is the choice of the annihilated half in each of three pairs, and its conjugate is this exchange with $m = 3$, an odd number, which reverses the volume element (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.two-families-are-two-chiralities}(iii)). Whether the exchange is a symmetry of the stored laws is not decided here, and the stored results point the other way for the linear map: on the conformal ambient this exchange, taken in a Witt basis in which each $v_{k}$ is plus or minus the conjugate of $w_{k}$, is a real isometry of $\R^{2,4}$ carrying the flag to its conjugate, of determinant $-1$ (a product of three reflections), and by clauses (3), first sentence, and (5) of \claimref{synthesis.cpt-conjugation-pseudoscalar-rotor-times-complex-conjugation} it reverses the frozen point's action in the field-action convention; the realisation that preserves that action is antilinear (clause (4) there). So ``label'' is proved here for the algebra of the switches and not for the laws.

## technical
For any finite number of anticommuting set-and-clear pairs, exchanging set with clear in all of them is conjugation by a product of the pairs' directions, those of square $+1$ when the number of pairs is odd. It fixes every blade up to sign, the integer lattice of blades, the hyperbolic form and reversal, and composed with reversal it is the adjoint. It reverses each number operator about one half, exchanges the empty and the full idempotent, and reverses the volume element exactly for an odd number of pairs. Nothing annihilated by a clear operation is invariant, and no normalised integer-valued value assignment is invariant: an integer state that is positive on $vv^{\dagger}$ and $ww^{\dagger}$ puts every switch definitely on or off, with a total charge of the parity of the number of switches, and the exchange reverses that charge. The invariant assignment, the scalar part, gives each switch the value one half and is integral only on the blade lattice, which contains no switch. All of this is standard and elementary: the exchange is the particle-hole exchange, implemented as usual by a product of Majorana elements, and the integer statement is that $1$ is odd.

## lay
Take any number of on/off switches. Swapping the meaning of on and off in all of them at once changes nothing about the rules the switches obey: the swap can be carried out by an operation already present among the rules. So the rules cannot say which position is really on. Yet if values must be whole numbers, no assignment of values is balanced between the two positions, since balance needs the number one half; and every assignment that gives no negative value to the two tests (is it on, is it off) puts each switch definitely on or definitely off. In that setting some imbalance between on and off is unavoidable, while which side it favours is a matter of naming. For the matter in the universe this is a statement about the starting point of the theory only: it does not show that the laws built later treat matter and antimatter alike.

## one-liner
For $m$ anticommuting switches, exchanging every set with its clear is conjugation by a blade ($e_{1}\cdots e_{m}$ for odd $m$), fixes every blade up to sign and equals the adjoint composed with reversal; it reverses the volume element exactly for odd $m$, no vacuum and no normalised integer-valued state is invariant under it, and the invariant state needs $\tfrac12$ (standard algebra, for finitely many switches).
