$d\varrho = f^{-1/2}dr$ gives $d\varrho^{2} = f^{-1}dr^{2}$, and $F^{2} = f$, which is the first display. $F(0) = \sqrt{f(2M)} = 0$. By the chain rule $dF/d\varrho = (d\sqrt f/dr)(dr/d\varrho) = \bigl(f'/2\sqrt f\bigr)\sqrt f = f'/2$, an exact identity of rational functions in $r$, and $f'(2M)/2 = \kappa$ is Proposition \claimref{singularity.computed}. Since $F$ is smooth in $r$ and $\varrho$ is a smooth function of $r$ with $d\varrho/dr = f^{-1/2}$, one has $\varrho = 2\sqrt{(r - 2M)/f'(2M)}\,(1 + O(r - 2M))$, so $F = \kappa\varrho + O(\varrho^{3})$ ($F$ is odd in $\varrho$ under the reflection $\varrho \mapsto -\varrho$ that extends the metric across the origin), which is the expansion. The rest is the elementary geometry of a surface of revolution: with $\phi := \kappa\tau$ the leading metric is $d\varrho^{2} + \varrho^{2}d\phi^{2}$, the flat plane in polar coordinates, which is smooth at the origin exactly when $\phi$ has period $2\pi$; the circumference of the geodesic circle of radius $\varrho$ is $pF(\varrho) = \kappa p\,\varrho + O(\varrho^{3})$, so the total angle at the origin is $\kappa p$, and a cone of total angle other than $2\pi$ is not a smooth manifold. The sphere factor $r^{2}d\Omega^{2}$ is smooth and nondegenerate at $r = 2M$ and plays no role. The script verifies $\sqrt f\,(d\sqrt f/dr) = f'/2$ as an identity of rational functions, the vanishing of $f$ at $2M$, and $f'(2M)/2 = 1/4M$ exactly.
