\emph{(i)} Exchanging $i$ and $j$ in the relation gives $x_{j}x_{i} = c\,x_{i}x_{j}$, so $x_{1}x_{2} = c\,x_{2}x_{1} = c^{2}x_{1}x_{2}$ and $(1 - c^{2})x_{1}x_{2} = 0$; since $x_{1}x_{2} \ne 0$ and $c$ is a scalar of a field, $c^{2} = 1$. With only the relation $x_{1}x_{2} = c\,x_{2}x_{1}$, moving $x_{2}$ through $x_{1}x_{1}$ uses it twice: $x_{1}^{2}x_{2} = c\,x_{1}x_{2}x_{1} = c^{2}x_{2}x_{1}^{2}$, so $x_{1}^{2}x_{2} - x_{2}x_{1}^{2} = (c^{2} - 1)x_{2}x_{1}^{2}$, which vanishes, when $x_{2}x_{1}^{2} \ne 0$, exactly when $c^{2} = 1$. In characteristic other than $2$, $c^{2} = 1$ and $c \ne 1$ give $c = -1$. The check verifies the second statement in the normal-ordered model of the algebra with one relation $x_{2}x_{1} = c^{-1}x_{1}x_{2}$ for $c = 2, \tfrac13, -1, 1$ (the square $x_{1}^{2}$ is central exactly for $c = \pm 1$, and these are exactly the values for which the relation reads the same both ways), and in $\Cl(p,q)$ that distinct generators anticommute and every $e_{i}^{2}$ is central.

\emph{(ii)} By Proposition \claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}, $e_{S}e_{T} = \pm \prod_{i \in S \cap T} Q(e_{i})\, e_{S \triangle T} = \pm e_{S \triangle T}$, the generators having squares $\pm 1$. Reversing the $k = |S|$ letters of $e_{S}$ costs $\binom{k}{2}$ transpositions, and $e_{S}\widetilde{e_{S}} = \prod_{i \in S}Q(e_{i}) = (-1)^{|S \cap N|}$, so $e_{S}^{2} = (-1)^{\binom{k}{2}}e_{S}\widetilde{e_{S}} = (-1)^{q(S)}$. Moving $e_{T}$ past $e_{S}$ letter by letter gives $e_{S}e_{T} = (-1)^{b(S,T)}e_{T}e_{S}$ with $b(S,T) = |S|\,|T| + |S \cap T|$ (each letter of $T$ passes the $|S|$ letters of $S$, anticommuting with all but the one equal to it, if any). Then $e_{S \triangle T}^{2} = (e_{S}e_{T})^{2} = e_{S}e_{T}e_{S}e_{T} = (-1)^{b(S,T)}e_{S}^{2}e_{T}^{2}$, which is $q(S \triangle T) = q(S) + q(T) + b(S,T)$. The form $b$ is bilinear over $\mathbb{F}_{2}$, since $|S \triangle S'| \equiv |S| + |S'|$ and $|(S \triangle S') \cap T| \equiv |S \cap T| + |S' \cap T| \bmod 2$, and $q(\emptyset) = 0$; so $q$ is a quadratic form with polar form $b$. The group $\{\pm e_{S}\}$ maps onto $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}$ with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$, central; its commutators are $(-1)^{b}$ and its squares $(-1)^{q}$. The check compares the GA products with the closed forms for every pair of blades in all twenty signatures with $n \le 5$.

\emph{(iii)} $S$ lies in the radical when $b(S, \{i\}) = |S| + [i \in S] = 0$ for every $i$. If $S$ is neither empty nor everything, taking $i \in S$ and $i' \notin S$ gives $|S| \equiv 1$ and $|S| \equiv 0$; $S = \emptyset$ always lies in the radical, and $S = \{1, \dots, n\}$ does exactly when $n \equiv 1 \bmod 2$. Its square is the pseudoscalar's, whose sign is that of Proposition \claimref{primer.sign}.

\emph{(iv)} For $k \ge 0$, $(-1)^{\binom{k}{2}} = \langle (1 - j)j^{k}\rangle_{0}$ (the values for $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$ are $1, 1, -1, -1$, and both sides have period $4$ in $k$). Grouping the subsets by $a = |S \setminus N|$ and $b' = |S \cap N|$,
\[
\sum_{S}(-1)^{q(S)} = \Big\langle (1 - j)\sum_{a, b'}\binom{p}{a}\binom{q}{b'}j^{a}(-j)^{b'}\Big\rangle_{0} = \big\langle (1 - j)(1 + j)^{p}(1 - j)^{q}\big\rangle_{0},
\]
all products taken in the commutative algebra $\mathrm{span}\{1, j\}$. There $(1 + j)(1 - j) = 2$, $(1 + j)^{2} = 2j$, $(1 + j)^{8} = 16$, so with $r = p - q \ge 0$ the product is $2^{q}(1 - j)(1 + j)^{r}$, and $\langle (1 - j)(1 + j)^{s}\rangle_{0} = 1, 2, 2, 0, -4, -8, -8, 0$ for $s = 0, \dots, 7$; with $r = 8m + s$ and $n = r + 2q$ this is the stated table. For $r < 0$ the product is $2^{p}(1 - j)^{|r| + 1}$, and the automorphism $j \mapsto -j$ of $\mathrm{span}\{1, j\}$ turns its scalar part into that of $2^{p}(1 + j)^{|r| + 1}$; since $\langle (1 + j)^{t}\rangle_{0} = 1, 1, 0, -2, -4, -4, 0, 8$ for $t = 0, \dots, 7$, and $(1 + j)^{8} = 16$, so that for $|r| + 1 \ge 8$ the value is $16^{m}$ times the tabulated one, the same table follows. For $n$ even the polar form is nondegenerate by (iii) and the definition of the Arf invariant applies directly; comparing the sign of the Gauss sum with the classification (Theorem \claimref{transport.classification-real-clifford-algebras}: $p - q \equiv 0, 2$ real, $4, 6$ quaternionic, $1$ two real, $5$ two quaternionic, $3, 7$ complex matrix algebras) gives the even statement. For $n$ odd with $q(\{1, \dots, n\}) = 1$, $S \mapsto S \triangle \{1, \dots, n\}$ changes $q$ by $1$ (since $b(S, \{1, \dots, n\}) = 0$), so the Gauss sum is $0$ and by (iii) the pseudoscalar squares to $-1$, the complex case $p - q \equiv 3, 7$. Otherwise $q$ is constant on the cosets of the radical, the quotient form on $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n-1}$ has nondegenerate polar form, and the Gauss sum is twice the quotient's, $\pm 2 \cdot 2^{(n-1)/2}$, with the sign $+$ for $p - q \equiv 1$ and $-$ for $p - q \equiv 5$. The check computes $q$ from the GA squares, the radical, the Gauss sum and the scalar part of $(1 - j)(1 + j)^{p}(1 - j)^{q}$ with $j = e_{1}e_{2}$ in $\Cl(2,0)$ for all $44$ signatures with $1 \le n \le 8$, and compares each with the type of the classification.

\emph{(v)} By Theorem \claimref{tower.commuting-copies} with one plane, the twisted generators $x_{i}\omega$ satisfy the ground relations, $(x_{i}\omega)^{2} = x_{i}^{2}$ because the even element $\omega$ commutes with the ground generators and $\omega^{2} = 1$, and they commute with $e$ and $f$. So the square of the product $y_{A,B}$ of the $x_{i}\omega$, $i \in A$, with $e_{B}$ is $(-1)^{q_{(p,q)}(A)}(-1)^{q_{(1,1)}(B)}$. As $\omega^{|A|}$ is $1$ or $\pm ef$, $y_{A,B}$ is $\pm$ the blade with index set $A \triangle (\{e, f\}$ if $|A|$ is odd$) \triangle B$, which is linear in $(A, B)$ and injective; so it is an isometry $q_{(p,q)} \perp q_{(1,1)} \to q_{(p+1,q+1)}$. The Gauss sum is multiplicative on orthogonal sums, and $q_{(1,1)}$ takes the values $0, 0, 1, 0$ on $\emptyset, \{e\}, \{f\}, \{e,f\}$, Gauss sum $2 = 2^{1}$, Arf invariant $0$; so the sign of the Gauss sum, and with it the type, is unchanged, which is the division-ring clause of Theorem \claimref{tower.rigidity-clock-frozen}. For $(0,2)$, $e_{1}^{2} = e_{2}^{2} = (e_{1}e_{2})^{2} = -1$, Gauss sum $-2$, Arf invariant $1$. The check builds the twisted products in $\Cl(p{+}1, q{+}1)$ for five ground signatures and verifies the squares, the commutation and the bijection. For an anisotropic plane $e', f'$ with $Q(e') = Q(f') = -1$, $\omega' = e'f'$ has $\omega'^{2} = -1$, so $(x_{i}\omega')^{2} = -x_{i}^{2}$: the twisted ground generators have the opposite squares, and the same argument gives an isometry $q_{(q,p)} \perp q_{(0,2)} \to q_{(p,q+2)}$, not $q_{(p,q)} \perp q_{(0,2)}$; so $\Cl(2,2)$, real, comes from $\Cl(2,0)$, real, and $\Cl(0,4)$, quaternionic, from $\Cl(0,2)$, quaternionic. The check verifies this isometry for four ground signatures and the two examples.
