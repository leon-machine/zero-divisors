## formal
Write $\Cl(1,4)$ for the algebra generated by $\Cl(1,3)$ ($e_0^2 = 1$, $e_i^2 = -1$) and one more vector $e_4$ orthogonal to it with $e_4^2 = -1$, $B_a = e_0e_a$, and let $K_5 = \{P \in \R^4 : \sum_a P_a = 1 = \sum_a P_a^2\}$, the five-dimensional Kasner sphere, and $D_s = \{(p, c) \in \R^3\times\R : \sum_i p_i = 1,\ \sum_i p_i^2 + c^2 = 1\}$. For $P \in K_5$ put $k = 1 + P_4/2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{(Reduction.)} The five-dimensional vacuum Kasner metric $G = dT^2 - \sum_{a=1}^{4}T^{2P_a}(dx^a)^2$ ($P \in K_5$) is Ricci-flat, and $G = T^{-P_4}g - T^{2P_4}(dx^4)^2$ with
\[
g = T^{P_4}\Bigl(dT^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{3}T^{2P_i}(dx^i)^2\Bigr), \qquad \phi = \sqrt{\tfrac{3}{16\pi G}}\;P_4\log T .
\]
The pair $(g, \phi)$, read as the derived metric of a diagonal synchronous gravitational field on flat spacetime (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}) and a massless scalar, solves the Einstein-scalar equations exactly: in the store's sign $\Ric = -8\pi G\,d\phi\otimes d\phi$ and $\Box\phi = 0$. In the proper time $\tau = T^{k}/k$ of $g$, $g = d\tau^2 - \sum_i k^{2p_i}\tau^{2p_i}(dx^i)^2$ and $\phi = q\log\tau + \mathrm{const}$ with
\[
p_i = \frac{P_i + P_4/2}{k}, \qquad 8\pi G\,q^2 = \frac{3P_4^2}{2k^2},
\]
the Kasner-disk relations $\sum_i p_i = 1$, $\sum_i p_i^2 = 1 - 8\pi G q^2$ of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.unit-boost-residue-needs-nonpositive-exponent}(iii) holding exactly.
\item \emph{(Bijection.)} $\Pi(P) = \bigl(p,\ \sqrt{3/2}\,P_4/k\bigr)$ maps $K_5$ bijectively onto $D_s$, with inverse $P_4 = c/(\sqrt{3/2} - c/2)$, $P_i = p_ik - P_4/2$; here $c = \sqrt{8\pi G}\,q$ with the sign of $P_4$. So every Einstein-scalar Kasner wall of this diagonal form is the reduction of exactly one five-dimensional vacuum Kasner wall, the scalar's rate being the fifth exponent.
\item \emph{(The completed residue.)} With $B_w = \sum_i p_iB_i$ (minus the residue of the Kasner pole, Proposition \claimref{dark-energy.frozen-point-datum-kasner-wall}(i)) and $\sum_i p_i = 1$, the bivector $\hat B = B_w + c\,B_4$ satisfies $\hat B^2 = \sum_i p_i^2 + c^2$, so $\hat B$ is a unit boost of $\Cl(1,4)$ exactly when the Einstein-scalar equations hold at the wall; its $B_4$ component is the scalar's logarithmic rate and $1 - B_w^2 = c^2$. The five-dimensional residue $B_5 = \sum_a P_aB_a$ is a unit boost, and
\[
k\,B_w = \sum_{i=1}^{3}P_iB_i + \frac{P_4}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}B_i :
\]
the four-dimensional residue is the spatial part of $B_5$ plus the isotropic residue of the Weyl factor $T^{P_4}$, rescaled by the change of time $d\tau/dT = T^{P_4/2}$.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
The residue-sense forcing half reads the field equations at a Kasner wall as the statement that the residue of the connection's pole is a unit boost; with a massless scalar the residue squares to $1 - 8\pi Gq^2$, which raised the question whether matter is excluded at the wall or needs a new kind of wall datum. For the massless scalar (equivalently a stiff fluid) the answer is neither. A five-dimensional vacuum Kasner metric with exponents $P_1, \dots, P_4$ reduces along its fourth axis, in the Einstein frame, to a four-dimensional Kasner gravitational field with a massless scalar whose rate is fixed by $P_4$; the map from the five-dimensional Kasner sphere to the Einstein-scalar Kasner sphere $\{\sum p = 1, \sum p^2 + 8\pi G q^2 = 1\}$ is a bijection, so every diagonal Einstein-scalar Kasner wall arises this way exactly once. In the store's language the four-dimensional residue $B_w$, a boost of norm below one, completes to the unit boost $B_w + \sqrt{8\pi G}\,q\,e_0e_4$ of $\Cl(1,4)$, and the field equations at the wall are again the single statement that the completed residue is a unit boost. The relation to the five-dimensional residue is explicit: $B_w$ is its spatial part plus the isotropic pole of the conformal factor, divided by the change of time. The facts behind the reduction are standard; what is new is that the store's unit-boost reading survives matter of this kind by adding one generator. The checks verify Ricci-flatness in five dimensions, the reduced Einstein-scalar equations, the exponent map and its inverse, and the residue identities in $\Cl(1,4)$, exactly at three rational points of the five-dimensional Kasner sphere with a control off it.

## lay
Near the singularity inside a black hole or at the Big Bang, the series describes the gravitational field by a few exponents, and the laws of gravity become a single condition on them: a certain algebraic object, the residue, must have unit size. Adding the simplest kind of matter, a massless field, breaks that: the residue comes out smaller than one, which seemed to mean that matter needs its own special treatment at the singularity. This result shows it does not. The same situation is exactly what one sees in four dimensions when a matter-free gravitational field lives in five dimensions and one direction is hidden. In an algebra with one more generator the residue has unit size again, and the missing part of its size is precisely how fast the matter field grows near the singularity. Nothing here claims that a fifth dimension exists; it is a bookkeeping device that makes the matter fit the series' rule.

## one-liner
Each diagonal Einstein-scalar Kasner wall is the Einstein-frame reduction, along a fixed axis, of exactly one five-dimensional vacuum Kasner wall, and its residue $B_w$, of square $1 - 8\pi Gq^2$ in $\Cl(1,3)$, completes to the unit boost $B_w + \sqrt{8\pi G}\,q\,e_0e_4$ in $\Cl(1,4)$, so the field equations at the wall remain the statement that a residue is a unit boost.
