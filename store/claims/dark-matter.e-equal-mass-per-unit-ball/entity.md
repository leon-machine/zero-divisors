## formal
\emph{Premise (E), equal mass per unit ball.} There is one length $d$, common to all populated towers, such that every populated tower holds the same mass $\mu$ per ball of radius $d$ of its own space. With $\rho_{r}$ the mass per unit volume of the $D_{r}$-dimensional space of the tower of residue $r$, at its representative under Premise \claimref{dark-matter.p3-nearest-dimension-representatives}, and $V_{D}$ the volume of the unit ball $\B^{D}$,
\[
\rho_{r}\,V_{D_{r}}\,d^{D_{r}} \;=\; \mu \qquad \text{for every populated } r .
\]
The unit ball of the unit-ball rule is the ball of radius $d$.

\emph{What it replaces.} Premises R1 (\claimref{dark-matter.r1-equal-mass-per-quantum}, equal mass per quantum) and R2 (\claimref{dark-matter.r2-one-quantum-per-unit-ball}, one quantum per unit ball), taken in the length $d$, give E with $\mu$ the mass of a quantum, and E gives neither: it says nothing about what a tower's quanta weigh or how many there are (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.dark-to-baryon-total-uses-r1-r2-and-d-only-as-mass-per-unit-ball}(i)). R1 and R2 are one model of E, the particle model. By the decision of 19 November 2026, E and not R1 and R2 is the premise of the dark-to-baryon total (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}), with the thickness of Premise R3 (\claimref{dark-matter.r3-one-cell-per-unshared-direction}) read in the same length $d$. R1 and R2 remain premises of the stored results that use a quantum's mass or number by itself.

\emph{What it does not fix.} E does not say what $d$ is, and the total does not depend on it. In particular $d$ is not tied to the spacing of this world's baryons, as R2's unit was: the stored results that give the counted thickness a value, today's baryon spacing of about a metre or its value at the deposit, use R2 for that. The democracy premise (\claimref{dark-matter.d-democracy-total-mass}) is E with $d$ the de Sitter radius, given the stored reading of the sphere form, and the particle model is E with $d$ the common cell radius (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.dark-to-baryon-total-uses-r1-r2-and-d-only-as-mass-per-unit-ball}(i), (ii)). E can hold in one length only (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}(iv)). It presupposes that the towers it names are populated, which for the four odd towers is Premise \claimref{dark-matter.odd-towers-populated}. Whether one length serves at more than one epoch is settled as for R2 (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.unit-ball-clauses-hold-at-one-epoch-unless-hidden-direction-co-expands}(v)): only if the five-dimensional towers' hidden direction co-expands with ours, and otherwise E is a statement about the deposit.

\emph{How much it says.} For the mirror, whose space has the dimension of ours, E is equal density, which the population count gives separately (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent}). Across dimensions, E with R3 holds for some $d$ exactly when the closed form's two shares hold and the two thicknesses are equal (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}(iii)). So E is the fitted relation stated as one law: it adds no support to the fit that is independent of the fitted number, and the series supplies no mechanism for it.

\emph{Refutation condition.} E with R3 is refuted exactly when the closed form is: by a dark-to-baryon ratio that excludes $(11\pi + 16)/3\pi$, or by a composition of the dark matter other than the shares $V_{3}/V_{D}$.

## technical
Premise E says that there is one length $d$, the same for every populated tower, at which each tower holds the same mass $\mu$ per ball of radius $d$ of its own space: $\rho_{r}V_{D_{r}}d^{D_{r}} = \mu$. It is the premise of the dark-to-baryon total since the decision of 19 November 2026, with R3's thickness read in the same length. It takes the place of R1 (equal mass per quantum) and R2 (one quantum per unit ball), which together imply it and are now one model of it, the particle model; E itself says nothing about a tower's quanta.

E does not fix $d$ and the total does not depend on it: the democracy premise is E at the de Sitter radius, and the particle model is E at the common cell radius, the only one of the three that ties $d$ to the spacing of this world's baryons. Across dimensions E with R3 is equivalent to the closed form's two shares with equal thicknesses, so it is the fitted relation stated as one law and gives the fit no independent support. The series supplies no mechanism for it.

## lay
The formula for the amount of dark matter needs to know how much matter each hidden sector holds. This assumption says it in the weakest form that works: there is one length at which a ball of that radius holds the same mass in every sector, each ball taken in that sector's own number of dimensions. It does not say that particles in different sectors weigh the same, or that there are equally many of them, which the earlier form of the rule assumed. It is still an assumption, not a result, and it is no more than the formula itself put as a single rule: it holds exactly when the formula's two shares do.

## one-liner
Premise (E): there is one length $d$ at which every populated tower holds the same mass per ball of radius $d$ of its own space; it replaces R1 and R2 under the dark-to-baryon total, fixes neither $d$ nor any quantum's mass, and with R3 is equivalent to the closed form's two shares.
