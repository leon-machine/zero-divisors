## formal
Units $\hbar = c = 1$, signature $(1,3)$. Let $p$ be a point, $\Sigma$ a small spacelike two-surface element through $p$, and $\ell$, $n$ its two future null normals. The local Rindler horizons $\mathcal{H}_{p,\ell}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{p,n}$ of Premise \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-local-horizon-premise} built on $\Sigma$ (generated by $\ell$ and by $n$) share the edge $\Sigma$; call them the two sheets of the edge. Take as a hypothesis, not assumed, the temperature clause (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) of that premise: the state of a patch's boundary modes is KMS at rapidity period $2\pi$ for the patch's future-directed local boost. A \emph{shared algebra} at $p$ is a unital $*$-algebra $\mathfrak{A}$ of modes contained in the mode algebras of both patches, the inductive limit of finite matrix factors (windows) each invariant under both boost flows, on which each flow acts on a window as $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{JsH})$ with $H = H^{*}$, as the phase-acting boost of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.boundary-mode-algebra-windows-boost-dynamics} does. The windows' numbers are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, J\}$, $J = e_{2}e_{3}$ the complex unit of the completed pair of the radial plane, which commutes with its mode operators (\claimref{synthesis.transverse-bivector-is-the-complex-unit-of-the-radial-atom}); in (iii) this $J$ is the windows' scalar, not the rotation generator $e_{2}e_{3}$ in the domain of $D$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Opposite boosts.)} At leading order in the patch size the future-directed approximate boost fields of the two sheets are $\chi$ and $-\chi$, each of unit surface gravity; on the past halves $\chi = -\lambda k$ along the generators ($k = \ell, n$), the form used in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.einstein-equations-from-c-loc-and-clausius}.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Shared modes carry no temperature.)} Assume (N): the action of a boost on modes it fixes depends only on its vector field, so the flow of the sheet $n$ on a shared algebra is the inverse of that of the sheet $\ell$, $\alpha^{n}_{s} = \alpha^{\ell}_{-s}$. If a state satisfies (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) for both sheets, then on every window $\alpha^{\ell}$ is trivial and the state is the normalised trace. Hence at any nonzero weight no state satisfies (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on both sheets of an edge on a shared algebra.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Covariant lifts carry nothing.)} A Lie algebra homomorphism $D$ from the Lorentz algebra into the $*$-derivations of a finite window (every one inner, $D(B) = J[h_{B}, \cdot\,]$ with $h_{B}$ self-adjoint) is zero: the Lorentz algebra is simple and noncompact, $\operatorname{ad}$ of a boost having the real eigenvalues $\pm 2$, while $\operatorname{ad}$ of every element of the image has purely imaginary eigenvalues, in $J\R$. So a Lorentz-covariant action of the local boosts on finite windows is trivial, independently of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), and carries no boost temperature.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(The completed pair is shared.)} The completed pair of Theorem \claimref{hawking.modular-structure-completed-pair} has ladder $v = \tfrac12(e_{0} + e_{1})$, $v^{*} = \tfrac12(e_{0} - e_{1})$, the two null directions of the plane of $\sigma = e_{0}e_{1}$, that is the generators of the two sheets of the edge orthogonal to that plane, and relabelling the pair reverses the orientation. Under (N) the two sheets ask its weight-$w$ mode for the occupations $1/(1 + e^{2\pi w})$ and $1/(1 + e^{-2\pi w})$, equal only at $w = 0$.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Sheet labels suffice.)} Let the modes at $p$ be labelled by future null directions, one window algebra $\mathfrak{A}_{p,[\ell]}$ for each direction, the algebras of different directions commuting, and let a local boost with bivector $B$ whose horizon is generated by $\ell$ act on $\mathfrak{A}_{p,[\ell]}$ through the weight $c_{\ell}(B)$ defined by $\tfrac12(B\ell - \ell B) = c_{\ell}(B)\,\ell$. Then $c_{\ell}$ is a character of the stabiliser of the direction $[\ell]$ (it vanishes on the null rotations fixing $\ell$, on the rotation about $\ell$ and on all brackets), every future boost of a horizon generated by $\ell$, whatever its edge, has the same weight $c_{\ell} = -1$ in the past-half orientation of (i), and the product over directions of the states $e^{-2\pi h_{\ell}}/\operatorname{tr}e^{-2\pi h_{\ell}}$ satisfies (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) for every local Rindler horizon through $p$, at every weight.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Consequences.} (a) Read the stored Definition, as a hypothesis, as placing the hole's modes at the bifurcation surface of the Killing horizon, where Premise \claimref{singularity.r-regularity-fixed-locus} imposes its condition, with those modes in the patches of both sheets through each of its points. Then at such a point the other sheet through that point is a local Rindler horizon whose future boost is $-\xi$ for the Killing field $\xi$; under the one-mode-set identification of (C$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) and (N), (R) and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on that sheet have no common state at the frequencies $\omega_{j} > 0$ of the Definition. (b) Antipodal de Sitter observers share the bifurcation sphere of their static patches, with Killing fields $\xi$ and $-\xi$ in the stabiliser of the frozen point (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-static-boost-frozen-point}); (R$_{\Lambda}$) imposed for every observer on modes of that sphere meets (ii). (c) So, under (N), (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) quantified over every local Rindler horizon is consistent at nonzero weight only if no mode is shared by the two sheets of an edge: the modes a patch carries at its edge must be labelled by the patch's sheet (its generating null direction), not by the point or by the plane of the edge. The per-direction windows of (v) are point spectrum, so the positive-null-energy premise of the result filed alongside (synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises) cannot be posed on them; the continuum carrier of that result, modes along each generator with the edge a null set carrying no mode, is the other resolution and supplies the labels automatically.

## technical
Clause (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) of the local-horizon premise asks the boundary modes of every local Rindler horizon to be thermal at rapidity period $2\pi$ for that horizon's future boost. This result tests the clause against itself. A small spacelike surface element through a point has two future null normals, and each generates a local Rindler horizon with that element as its edge; in local inertial coordinates both have the same flat boost as approximate Killing field, future pointing on one sheet and past pointing on the other, so their future boosts are $\chi$ and $-\chi$. On a mode set where the boost multiplies modes by phases, as in the store's Definition, the boost fixes its modes, and modes at the edge belong to both patches. If a boost's action on fixed modes depends only on its vector field, the two sheets act on such modes by mutually inverse flows; a state thermal at period $2\pi$ for a flow and for its inverse is thermal at $2\pi$ and at $-2\pi$, and on a matrix factor that forces the generator to be a scalar and the state to be the trace (KMS uniqueness). A Lorentz-covariant action on finite windows is no way out: the Lorentz algebra is simple and noncompact and has no nonzero homomorphism into the compact algebra of self-adjoint generators of a window, so such an action is trivial whatever the state.

The store's completed pair is such a shared algebra: its two ladder operators are the two null directions of one timelike plane, the generators of the two sheets of the orthogonal edge, and the two sheets ask its mode for reciprocal occupations. If, reading the stored Definition, the hole's modes sit at the bifurcation surface of a Killing horizon, where (R) is imposed, and belong to the patches of both sheets, then the other sheet through the surface is a local Rindler horizon with future boost $-\xi$, so with one mode set the two premises ask one mode for two occupations. The same happens for two antipodal de Sitter observers, which share a bifurcation sphere. Labelling modes by null direction removes the conflict: every horizon generated by a given null direction, whatever its edge, has a future boost that dilates that direction with the same weight, a character of the direction's stabiliser, so one thermal state per direction satisfies the clause for every local horizon. In quantum field theory the conflict does not arise because the two sheets carry the algebras of the right and left wedges, which commute (a remark, literature from memory); on the continuum of field modes along generators the labels are automatic, and the positive-null-energy carrier filed alongside is of that kind, while the per-direction windows here, being point spectrum, cannot carry it. The null directions at a point form a sphere, which in the series' twistor correspondence is, after complexification, the incidence line of the point; this identification of the labels is a remark, argued and not proved here.

## lay
The series assumes that every small horizon, the kind an accelerated observer sees anywhere in spacetime, is warm at a definite temperature. This result shows a hidden snag. Any small patch of surface has two light-like directions leaving it, and each defines such a horizon, with the same edge. The two horizons have opposite notions of which way their boost runs. If the quantum modes that carry the temperature sit on the shared edge, do not know which of the two horizons they belong to, and each boost acts on them only through its own flow, the two horizons demand opposite temperatures from the same modes, and the only state that satisfies both is one with no temperature at all. The store's basic two-mode building block has exactly this ambiguity, and the same clash appears at the meeting surface of a black hole's horizon, if the hole's modes are read as sitting there, and at the horizons of opposite observers in de Sitter space. The fix is to label the modes by the light-like direction they belong to, which is what happens automatically in ordinary quantum field theory.

## one-liner
If a boost acts on a mode it fixes through its vector field alone, the two local horizons of one edge, having opposite boosts, admit the local temperature clause on modes they share only at zero boost weight (the trace); the completed pair is such a shared algebra, which, with the hole's modes read as sitting at the bifurcation surface, sets (R) there against (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on the other sheet; labelling modes by null direction makes the clause consistent at every weight.
