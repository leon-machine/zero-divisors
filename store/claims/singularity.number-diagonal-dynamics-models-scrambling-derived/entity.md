## formal
Under (L$_E$) every generator available to the internal dynamics of the boundary modes lies in the abelian algebra generated by $\{\sigma_k\}$, hence is diagonal in the joint number basis and its exponential is a diagonal unitary. Consequently each mode's occupation probabilities are constants of the motion of the boundary modes, so populations set at injection by the local geometry do not drift; and the diagonal scrambling of the finite-mode model, the controlled-$Z$ circuit of Section \ref{subsec:modeldef}, is not a modelling convenience but the general form of what (L$_E$) permits.

## technical
Under (L$_E$), every generator of the boundary-mode dynamics lies in the abelian algebra generated by the $\sigma_k = 2N_k - 1$, so it is diagonal in the joint number basis and its exponential is a diagonal unitary. Diagonal unitaries change only phases, so every mode's occupation probabilities are constants of the motion, and since the ascended marginals are diagonal each marginal $\varrho_k$ is constant in time.

The model's controlled-$Z$ scrambling is therefore not a modelling convenience but the general form of what (L$_E$) permits.

## lay
Under the locality premise (L$_E$), every internal motion of the boundary modes only changes phases, so the probability that each mode is occupied never changes, so the populations set when the modes are created stay fixed. The simple phase-gate scrambling used in the toy model turns out to be the general form of the dynamics the locality assumption allows.

## one-liner
Under (L$_E$) the boundary-mode dynamics is a number-diagonal unitary, so occupation probabilities are conserved and controlled-$Z$ scrambling is its general form.
