## formal
(i) \emph{(Whitney product formula.)} For real vector bundles $E$, $F$ over a paracompact space, the total Stiefel-Whitney classes satisfy $w(E \oplus F) = w(E) \cup w(F)$, $w = 1 + w_{1} + w_{2} + \cdots$, and $w(\varepsilon^{k}) = 1$ for a trivial bundle. (ii) \emph{(Projective space.)} $H^{*}(\mathbb{RP}^{n}; \mathbb{Z}_{2}) = \mathbb{Z}_{2}[a]/(a^{n+1})$ with $a = w_{1}(\gamma_{n})$ the class of the tautological line bundle $\gamma_{n} \subset \mathbb{RP}^{n} \times \R^{n+1}$; in particular $a^{n} \neq 0$. (iii) \emph{(Euler sequence.)} $T\mathbb{RP}^{n} \oplus \varepsilon^{1} \cong (n+1)\,\gamma_{n}$, so $w(\mathbb{RP}^{n}) = (1 + a)^{n+1}$. Topological statements about bundles over the space, flagged as such. \emph{Provenance:} \cite[\S 4]{MilnorStasheff1974}.

## technical
Three classical facts of bundle theory. Stiefel-Whitney classes multiply when bundles are added, so a bundle that adds to a trivial one has the inverse total class. The mod-two cohomology of real projective $n$-space is generated by the class of its tautological line, whose powers survive up to the $n$-th. And the tangent bundle of projective space plus one trivial line is $n+1$ copies of the tautological line, which gives its total class. The series uses them to bound how many rungs a wall whose dying line twists needs, and to show that the bound is reached on a projective wall.

## lay
Mathematicians attach to each family of lines or planes over a space a set of invariants that detect twisting. These facts say how the invariants combine when families are put together, and compute them for the simplest twisted space, the projective space of lines through a point.

## one-liner
$w(E \oplus F) = w(E)w(F)$; $H^{*}(\mathbb{RP}^{n}; \mathbb{Z}_{2}) = \mathbb{Z}_{2}[a]/(a^{n+1})$ with $a = w_{1}(\gamma)$; and $T\mathbb{RP}^{n} \oplus \varepsilon^{1} \cong (n+1)\gamma$ (Milnor and Stasheff, Section 4).
