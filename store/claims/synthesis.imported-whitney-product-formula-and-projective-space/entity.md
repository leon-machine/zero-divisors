## formal
(i) \emph{(Whitney product formula.)} For real vector bundles $E$, $F$ over a paracompact space, the total Stiefel-Whitney classes satisfy $w(E \oplus F) = w(E) \cup w(F)$, $w = 1 + w_{1} + w_{2} + \cdots$, and $w(\varepsilon^{k}) = 1$ for a trivial bundle. (ii) \emph{(Projective space.)} $H^{*}(\mathbb{RP}^{n}; \mathbb{Z}_{2}) = \mathbb{Z}_{2}[a]/(a^{n+1})$ with $a = w_{1}(\gamma_{n})$ the class of the tautological line bundle $\gamma_{n} \subset \mathbb{RP}^{n} \times \R^{n+1}$; in particular $a^{n} \neq 0$. (iii) \emph{(Euler sequence.)} $T\mathbb{RP}^{n} \oplus \varepsilon^{1} \cong (n+1)\,\gamma_{n}$, so $w(\mathbb{RP}^{n}) = (1 + a)^{n+1}$. (iv) \emph{(Dimension axiom.)} For a real vector bundle $E$ of rank $k$, $w_{0}(E) = 1$ and $w_{j}(E) = 0$ for every $j > k$: there is no class above the rank. Topological statements about bundles over the space, flagged as such. \emph{Provenance:} \cite[\S 4]{MilnorStasheff1974}; (iv) is one of the axioms for Stiefel-Whitney classes stated at the start of that section (the first of the four, in the numbering recalled from memory).

## technical
Four classical facts of bundle theory. Stiefel-Whitney classes multiply when bundles are added, so a bundle that adds to a trivial one has the inverse total class. A bundle has no class of degree above its rank (the dimension axiom), so if a bundle of rank $k - 1$ has total class $(1 + x)^{-1}$ then $x^{j} = 0$ for $j \geq k$. The mod-two cohomology of real projective $n$-space is generated by the class of its tautological line, whose powers survive up to the $n$-th. And the tangent bundle of projective space plus one trivial line is $n+1$ copies of the tautological line, which gives its total class. The series uses them to bound how many rungs a wall whose dying line twists needs, and to show that the bound is reached on a projective wall.

## lay
Mathematicians attach to each family of lines or planes over a space a set of invariants that detect twisting. These facts say how the invariants combine when families are put together, that a family of $k$-dimensional spaces has no invariant beyond the $k$-th, and compute them for the simplest twisted space, the projective space of lines through a point.

## one-liner
$w(E \oplus F) = w(E)w(F)$; $H^{*}(\mathbb{RP}^{n}; \mathbb{Z}_{2}) = \mathbb{Z}_{2}[a]/(a^{n+1})$ with $a = w_{1}(\gamma)$; $T\mathbb{RP}^{n} \oplus \varepsilon^{1} \cong (n+1)\gamma$; and $w_{j}(E) = 0$ above the rank of $E$ (Milnor and Stasheff, Section 4).
