## formal
Every populated tower carries the same total mass $M_{\mathrm{tot}}$ on its de Sitter sphere. This is a democracy of mass, distinct from an equality of the asymmetry per mode (the former Premise P4, retired as a postulate; the asymmetry is equal in this tower and the mirror by Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-tower-copy-one}(v)), which the odd towers do not carry.

\emph{Note (where D belongs; written on 10 and 11 November 2026, when the fitted total was stated under R1 to R3, and to be read with the note of 19 November below).} D is the premise of the de Sitter reading of the count (Propositions \claimref{dark-matter.democracy-de-sitter-radius-unit-ball}, \claimref{dark-matter.two-reductions}) and is not counted with R1 to R3: no stored statement of the fitted total with the cell radius as unit has D in its past cone, with one exception, the audit \claimref{synthesis.dark-to-baryon-total-uses-r1-r2-and-d-only-as-mass-per-unit-ball}, which sets the two readings side by side (its clause (ii) is under D, its clause (i) under R1 to R3) and so has both among its conditions, and Observation \claimref{dark-matter.status-rule}(iv) has the de Sitter reading replace R2 by D. The two readings cannot be held together with more than one quantum per tower (argued): D with R1 (\claimref{dark-matter.r1-equal-mass-per-quantum}) gives every populated tower the same number of quanta on its de Sitter sphere, and R2 (\claimref{dark-matter.r2-one-quantum-per-unit-ball}) gives a tower of dimension $D$ one quantum per ball of the common cell radius $a$, so by the equality of the ball and sphere forms the number is $(\ell/a)^{D}$ for $D = 2, 3, 4$, and $x^{2} = x^{3} = x^{4}$ holds for $x > 0$ only at $x = 1$: the cell radius is the de Sitter radius and each tower holds one quantum per de Sitter ball. D is also the one postulate of the dark-matter subject that names the de Sitter radius, so it is the one whose content depends on that radius being a constant of the laws: it shares the exposure of $w = -1$. If $w$ differed from $-1$ the unit of D would change with time and the two results under D would go with it; the fitted total, which does not use D, would not.

\emph{Note (2026-11-19).} By the decision of 19 November 2026 the fitted total rests on Premise E, equal mass per unit ball (\claimref{dark-matter.e-equal-mass-per-unit-ball}), in place of R1 and R2. D is E at the de Sitter radius, given the stored reading of the sphere form (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.dark-to-baryon-total-uses-r1-r2-and-d-only-as-mass-per-unit-ball}(ii)), and E holds in one length only (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}(iv)). So the de Sitter reading is the same law with the de Sitter radius as its length, and it can be held with E only at that length; what was said above of D with R1 and R2 stands.

## technical
This premise states that every populated tower carries the same total mass $M_{\mathrm{tot}}$ on its own de Sitter sphere. It is a democracy of mass, distinct from an equality of the asymmetry per mode, which holds for this tower and the mirror and which the odd towers do not carry. It is the premise under which the shares can be read at the de Sitter radius rather than at a microscopic spacing.

Note: D belongs to the de Sitter reading and is not among the premises of the fitted total, which since 19 November 2026 are Premise E and R3. D is E at the de Sitter radius, given the stored reading of the sphere form, and E holds in one length only, so D and E at another length are alternatives. Held together with R1 and R2, D forces the cell radius to be the de Sitter radius, one quantum per de Sitter ball (argued). It is the one dark-matter postulate that names the de Sitter radius, and so shares the exposure of $w = -1$.

## lay
This is an assumption, not a result: every populated hidden world holds the same total amount of mass, spread over its own version of the cosmic sphere. It is separate from the statement that the matter excess is the same in our world and its mirror, which the odd-dimensional worlds cannot carry. It belongs to one of two alternative ways of counting, the one that uses the size of the cosmic horizon as its unit. It is the same kind of rule as the one the formula now rests on, equal mass per ball of one size, with the horizon as that size; the rule cannot hold at two sizes at once, and combined with the particle picture (one particle per ball of a small fixed size) it would leave each world a single particle. Because it uses the horizon size, it depends on dark energy being a true constant.

## one-liner
Premise of the de Sitter reading (not a premise of the fitted total; it is Premise E at the de Sitter radius, given the stored sphere reading, and the one dark-matter postulate naming that radius): every populated tower carries the same total mass $M_{\mathrm{tot}}$ on its de Sitter sphere.
