## formal
Let $x \in \Sig$ and let $(\mathbb{W}, Q)$ be any nondegenerate quadratic space containing $(T^*_xM, G_x)$ as a subspace with the restricted form (a fibrewise completion, in the sense of the series). Then in $\Cl(\mathbb{W}, Q)$ the image of the radical line $\shL(x)$ generates the \emph{unit} ideal: the two-sided ideal generated by $k$ contains $1$. Resolution therefore converts a square-zero ideal into the unit ideal: it turns an infinitesimal into a dimension.

## technical
At $x \in \Sig$, embed $(T^*_xM, G_x)$ with its form into any nondegenerate quadratic space $(\mathbb{W}, Q)$. The radical covector $k$ stays null but is no longer radical, so there is $v$ with $Q(k, v) = \tfrac12$, and $k^* = v - Q(v)\,k$ is a null partner with $k k^* + k^* k = 1$ in $\Cl(\mathbb{W}, Q)$.

Both summands lie in the two-sided ideal generated by $k$, so that ideal contains $1$. Resolution therefore converts the square-zero ideal of the degenerate fibre into the unit ideal: an infinitesimal becomes a dimension.

## lay
On the wall, the zero-length direction generates only an infinitesimal part of the algebra, one whose products all vanish. Once the geometry is enlarged to a non-degenerate one, in any way at all, that same direction finds a partner, and together they produce the number one. So the infinitesimal turns into a genuine new dimension.

## one-liner
In any nondegenerate quadratic space containing a wall fibre with its form, the radical line generates the unit ideal of the Clifford algebra.
