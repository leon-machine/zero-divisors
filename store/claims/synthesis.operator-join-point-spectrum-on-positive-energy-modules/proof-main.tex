\emph{Setting.} Set $\hbar = 1$ in the computations (it returns by dimension). The store's components are $\omega = (Z^{0}, Z^{1})$, $\pi = (Z^{2}, Z^{3})$, with conjugates paired as $\bar\pi_{A} = \bar Z_{A}$ and $\bar\omega^{A'} = \bar Z_{2+A}$ (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.operator-join-ordering-constants}); $\hat B = \br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}} + \lambda[\omega^{1}\omega^{2}]$, $\hat B^{\dagger}$ its conjugate, and the palatial join is clause (3) there, the symmetric quantisation of the classical join $2|B_{\Lambda}|^{2} + \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}C$ of Theorem \claimref{mass.exact-join}, with $[\hat B, \hat B^{\dagger}] = \lambda\tr\hat h$.

\emph{(1).} For one spinor index put $x = \omega^{A}$, $y = \pi_{A'}$. The canonical relations ask $[x, y^{\dagger}] = [y, x^{\dagger}] = 1$ and $[x, x^{\dagger}] = [y, y^{\dagger}] = [x, y] = 0$, where the conjugate is read as the adjoint. With $x = \tfrac{s}{\sqrt2}(a + c^{\dagger})$, $y = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2 s}(a - c^{\dagger})$ and $[a, a^{\dagger}] = [c, c^{\dagger}] = 1$ one finds $[x, y^{\dagger}] = \tfrac12([a, a^{\dagger}] - [c^{\dagger}, c]) = 1$, $[x, x^{\dagger}] = \tfrac{s^{2}}{2}([a, a^{\dagger}] + [c^{\dagger}, c]) = 0$, and similarly for the rest. So the store's conjugation is the Hilbert adjoint on the Fock space of the $a$ and $c$, and $\hat B^{\dagger}$, built from $\bar Z$, is the adjoint of $\hat B$. The symmetrised helicity of one constituent is $\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha}(\bar Z_{\alpha}Z^{\alpha} + Z^{\alpha}\bar Z_{\alpha}) = N_{a} - N_{c}$ (the cross terms $a^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}$, $ca$ cancel between the $\bar\pi\omega$ and $\bar\omega\pi$ halves). The energy is $\tfrac12(N_{a} + N_{c}) + 1$, so at helicity $s$ ($N_{a} - N_{c} = 2s$) and level $n$ the states number $(n + 2|s| + 1)(n + 1)$, with lowest energy $|s| + 1$: the oscillator model of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment}. The scale $s$ is changed by the dilation $\omega \mapsto t\omega$, $\pi \mapsto \pi/t$, which preserves the canonical relations and the adjoint rule, so it is implemented by a unitary (a Bogoliubov transformation of finitely many modes, Stone and von Neumann); spectra do not depend on $s$. The check verifies the canonical relations, the adjointness of $\hat B$ and of $\hat h_{12}$ on number states, and the multiplicities for $|2s| \le 3$, $n \le 2$.

\emph{(2).} Expanding, $\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}} = \tfrac{1}{2s^{2}}\epsilon(a_{1} - c_{1}^{\dagger})(a_{2} - c_{2}^{\dagger})$ and $[\omega^{1}\omega^{2}] = \tfrac{s^{2}}{2}\epsilon(a_{1} + c_{1}^{\dagger})(a_{2} + c_{2}^{\dagger})$ with the same antisymmetric contraction $\epsilon$ on the spinor index. The cross terms $\epsilon(a_{1}c_{2}^{\dagger} + c_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2})$ carry $-\tfrac{1}{2s^{2}} + \tfrac{\lambda s^{2}}{2}$, which vanishes at $\lambda s^{4} = 1$, and the pure terms then carry $\tfrac{1}{s^{2}} = \sqrt\lambda$: $\hat B = \sqrt\lambda(A + C^{\dagger})$. (Geometrically: the de Sitter frozen point lies in the compact two-plane of the Fock structure at that scale; for $\Lambda < 0$ the matched scale is $\lambda s^{4} = -1$, where the pure terms vanish, $\hat B = -\hbar\sqrt{|\lambda|}\,\epsilon(a_{1}c_{2}^{\dagger} + c_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2})$ preserves the oscillator number, and the join has discrete spectrum with normalisable zero modes of finite energy content: at $\Lambda = -6$, $s = 1$ the check finds the join $N$-preserving with a three-dimensional kernel on oscillator number $2$, inside the internal spin $j = 1$, in the sectors with $s_{1} + s_{2} = 0$. So the statement is for $\Lambda > 0$ only.) The determinant identities $[A, A^{\dagger}] = N_{a} + 2$, $[C, C^{\dagger}] = N_{c} + 2$ give $[\hat B, \hat B^{\dagger}] = \lambda(N_{a} - N_{c}) = \lambda\tr\hat h$, the stored commutator. With $K_{0}^{a} = \tfrac12(N_{a} + 2)$, $K_{0}^{c} = \tfrac12(N_{c} + 2)$ the pairs $(A, A^{\dagger}, K_{0}^{a})$ and $(C, C^{\dagger}, K_{0}^{c})$ are commuting $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$s, and $K_{-} = A + C^{\dagger}$, $K_{0} = K_{0}^{a} - K_{0}^{c}$ is their diagonal after the automorphism $K_{\pm} \mapsto K_{\mp}$, $K_{0} \mapsto -K_{0}$ of the second. $A$ and $C$ are determinants of $2 \times 2$ matrices whose rows are the constituents, so they are invariant under the internal $SU(2)$. The check verifies $\hat B = \sqrt\lambda(A + C^{\dagger})$, $\tr\hat h = N_{a} - N_{c}$ and the commutator on number states at $(\Lambda, s^{2}) = (6, 1)$ and $(\tfrac32, 2)$, and as a control that at $(\tfrac32, 1)$ the pair form fails.

\emph{(3).} $\hat B^{\dagger}\hat B + \hat B\hat B^{\dagger} = \lambda(K_{+}K_{-} + K_{-}K_{+}) = 2\lambda(K_{0}^{2} - C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)})$ by $[K_{-}, K_{+}] = 2K_{0}$. For $\hat C$: with $E_{il} = \hat h_{il}$ a $\mathfrak{gl}(2)$ basis, $J_{+} = E_{12}$, $J_{-} = E_{21}$, $J_{z} = \tfrac12(E_{11} - E_{22})$ and $N = E_{11} + E_{22}$, one has $\sum E_{il}E_{li} = \tfrac12N^{2} + 2J^{2}$, so $\hat C = J^{2} = j(j+1)$. Then $\hat m^{2} = 2\lambda(K_{0}^{2} - C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)}) + \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}j(j+1) - \tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}$, which is (3) since $2\lambda = \Lambda/3$. The check verifies the operator identity, the $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$ relations and $\hat C = j(j+1)$ on samples, at both values of $\Lambda$.

\emph{(4).} The $a$-modes form a $2 \times 2$ matrix (rows constituents, columns spinor index). The polynomials of degree $d$ killed by $\det(\partial)$ have dimension $\binom{d+3}{3} - \binom{d+1}{3} = (d+1)^{2}$ (the map to degree $d - 2$ is onto; the check computes the rank for $d \le 4$), and they form $V_{d/2} \otimes V_{d/2}$ under internal times spinor $SU(2)$ (harmonic polynomials on $2 \times 2$ matrices, classical). Each is a lowest weight of $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)_{a}$ with $K_{0}^{a} = \tfrac{d}{2} + 1$, so the $a$-space is $\bigoplus_{j_{a}}(V_{j_{a}} \otimes V_{j_{a}}) \otimes D^{+}(j_{a} + 1)$; the same holds for the $c$-modes, and the automorphism turns $D^{+}(j_{c} + 1)$ into $D^{-}(j_{c} + 1)$. The internal spin $j$ runs over $|j_{a} - j_{c}|, \dots, j_{a} + j_{c}$, and $K_{0} \in (j_{a} + 1) - (j_{c} + 1) + \Z$, so $K_{0} \equiv j \pmod 1$.

In $D^{+}(\kappa_{1}) \otimes D^{-}(\kappa_{2})$ with orthonormal $\ket{p}\ket{q}$, $K_{-} = K_{-}^{(1)} + K_{+}^{(2)}$ with $\|K_{-}^{(1)}\ket{p}\|^{2} = p(p + 2\kappa_{1} - 1)$ and $\|K_{+}^{(2)}\ket{q}\|^{2} = (q + 1)(q + 2\kappa_{2})$. A vector of weight $K_{0} = \kappa$ is $\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\ket{p}\ket{q(p)}$ with $q(p) = p + n$, $n = \kappa_{1} - \kappa_{2} - \kappa$. If $n < 0$ the sum starts at $p_{0} = -n > 0$ and the coefficient of $\ket{p_{0} - 1}\ket{0}$ in $K_{-}v$ is $\alpha_{p_{0}}\sqrt{p_{0}(p_{0} + 2\kappa_{1} - 1)}$ alone, so $v = 0$. If $n \ge 0$, $K_{-}v = 0$ is the recursion $|\alpha_{p}|^{2}p(p + 2\kappa_{1} - 1) = |\alpha_{p-1}|^{2}(q_{p-1} + 1)(q_{p-1} + 2\kappa_{2})$, whose ratio is exactly
\[
\frac{|\alpha_{p}|^{2}}{|\alpha_{p-1}|^{2}} = 1 - \frac{2\kappa}{p} + \frac{n(n - 1 + 2\kappa_{2}) + 2\kappa(2\kappa_{1} - 1)}{p(p + 2\kappa_{1} - 1)},
\]
so by Gauss's test $\sum|\alpha_{p}|^{2} < \infty$ exactly when $2\kappa > 1$. The highest weights are the mirror image. Hence the discrete series in the product are $D^{+}(\kappa_{1} - \kappa_{2} - n)$ and $D^{-}(\kappa_{2} - \kappa_{1} - n)$ with $\kappa > \tfrac12$, so $\kappa \le |j_{a} - j_{c}| \le j$ and $\kappa \in \tfrac12\Z$ gives $\kappa \ge 1$. The check verifies the boundary obstruction and the ratio identity for $\kappa_{1}, \kappa_{2} \le 5$, $-3 \le n \le 4$, $p < 40$.

\emph{(5).} $K_{0}$ and $\hat C$ have discrete spectrum and commute with $C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)}$ and with $\hat m^{2}$, so a normalisable eigenvector of $\hat m^{2}$ has a normalisable component in some block $(j_{a}, j_{c})$ of fixed $K_{0} = m$ and $j$, and that component is an eigenvector of $C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)}$ with eigenvalue $c = m^{2} + j(j+1) - 2 - \mu$ for $\hat m^{2}$-eigenvalue $\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\mu$. Cited from memory: $D^{+}(\kappa_{1})$ is square-integrable, so $D^{+}(\kappa_{1}) \otimes D^{-}(\kappa_{2})$ is contained in a multiple of the regular representation of $SL(2,\R)$ (Fell absorption), and its decomposition is the finitely many discrete series found above plus a direct integral of principal series with absolutely continuous measure (Pukánszky, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 100 (1961); Repka, Amer. J. Math. 100 (1978)); principal series occur in the Plancherel decomposition with no atoms. So every normalisable eigenvector of $C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)}$ lies in the discrete-series part, with $c = \kappa(\kappa - 1)$ and $|m| = \kappa + p$, $p \ge 0$. Then
\[
\mu = m^{2} - \kappa(\kappa - 1) + j(j+1) - 2 = 2\kappa p + p^{2} + \kappa + j(j+1) - 2 \;\ge\; \kappa + \kappa(\kappa + 1) - 2 \;\ge\; 1,
\]
using $j \ge \kappa \ge 1$, with equality exactly at $\kappa = 1$, $p = 0$, $j = 1$. The equality case occurs: in $(j_{a}, j_{c}) = (1, 0)$, $D^{+}(2) \otimes D^{-}(1)$ contains $D^{+}(1)$ ($n = 0$), whose lowest weight $v = \sum_{p}\beta_{p}(A^{\dagger})^{p}(a_{00}^{\dagger})^{2}(C^{\dagger})^{p}\ket{0}$, $\beta_{p} = -\beta_{p-1}/p(p+3)$, is normalisable (its squared norms decay like $p^{-2}$), has internal spin $1$ and $K_{0} = 1$, and satisfies $\hat m^{2}v = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}v$; the check verifies this on its components below a cut, at $\Lambda = 6$. The check also enumerates $j_{a}, j_{c} \le 6$, $n \le 12$, $p \le 5$ and confirms $\mu \neq 0$ throughout with minimum $1$. So no normalisable zero mode exists in any sector, and with Proposition \claimref{synthesis.operator-join-seed-has-no-diagonal-matrix-elements}(3) the seed has no normalisable massless carrier among the join's states.

Every normalisable eigenvector found here is an infinite sum of number states, as $v$ is, in agreement with the result filed alongside, synthesis.operator-join-positive-energy-no-finite-energy-zero-modes (no eigenvector of finite energy content, for any eigenvalue, when $\Lambda \ge 0$); together they exclude normalisable zero modes of either kind.

\emph{Sharpness and the continuum.} The bound uses the central constant of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.operator-join-ordering-constants}(3) and the Casimir normalisation of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.operator-join-casimir-shifts}: with $-\Lambda\hbar^{2}$ in place of $-\tfrac{2\Lambda}{3}\hbar^{2}$ the vector $v$ above would be a zero mode (a control in the check). On the principal series $C_{\mathfrak{su}(1,1)} = -\tfrac14 - \nu^{2}$, so the continuous spectrum of $\hat m^{2}$ in a block is $[\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\hbar^{2}(m^{2} + j(j+1) - \tfrac74), \infty)$; it contains $0$ only for $(m, j) = (0, 0), (\pm1, 0), (\pm\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ (enumerated in the check, with the parity $m \equiv j$), and in the block $(0, 0)$ it reaches $-\tfrac{7}{12}\Lambda\hbar^{2}$. Numerical support for the cited fact (not part of the check): for $(\kappa_{1}, \kappa_{2}, m, c) = (1, 1, 0, -2), (1, 1, 1, -1), (2, 2, 0, -2), (\tfrac32, 1, \tfrac12, -1)$ the formal Casimir eigenvector solving the three-term recurrence from its boundary has partial sums of $|\alpha_{p}|^{2}$ growing like $\log P$ (for the first, $3.60$, $4.97$, $6.34$ at $P = 10^{2}, 10^{3}, 10^{4}$), while at a discrete-series value, $(3, 1, 2, 2)$, they converge ($1.66666\ldots$). \emph{Reading (argued).} Massless states of the join exist only as generalised (scattering) states in the three kinds of block above, and neighbouring blocks $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$ both contain them; whether the seed's matrix element between such continuum states means anything is a question for the condensation programme, not the join. At $\Lambda = 0$ the adapted scale degenerates; there $\hat m^{2} = 2\hat b^{\dagger}\hat b$ with $\hat b$, $\hat b^{\dagger}$ commuting, multiplication by $2|\br{\pi^{1}\pi^{2}}|^{2}$ in the momentum reading of the module, which vanishes only on the collinear null set, so there is again no zero mode (standard, argued).
