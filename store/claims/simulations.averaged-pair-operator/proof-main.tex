(i) $P_{ij}$ is symmetric in $i$ and $j$ and $\mathbf{g}_{ij} = -\mathbf{g}_{ji}$, so the force of $j$ on $i$, $-m_{i}m_{j}F_{ij}P_{ij}\mathbf{g}_{ij}$ with $F_{ij}$ the symmetric bracket, is minus the force of $i$ on $j$. The kinetic power of the pair is $-m_{i}m_{j}F_{ij}\,\mathbf{v}_{ij}\cdot P_{ij}\mathbf{g}_{ij}$, and the two internal-energy terms sum to $+m_{i}m_{j}F_{ij}\,\mathbf{v}_{ij}\cdot P_{ij}\mathbf{g}_{ij}$, since $\mathbf{v}_{ji}\cdot P_{ji}\mathbf{g}_{ji} = \mathbf{v}_{ij}\cdot P_{ij}\mathbf{g}_{ij}$ and the viscous term is split equally. (ii) With $Q(\mathbf{n}) = \mathbf{n}\mathbf{n} = 1$ (\claimref{std-algebra.vector-squares}) and $\mathbf{v}\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n} + \mathbf{v}\wedge\mathbf{n}$ (\claimref{std-algebra.product-decomposition}), $(\mathbf{v}\wedge\mathbf{n})\mathbf{n} = (\mathbf{v}\mathbf{n} - \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{v} - (\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n})\mathbf{n}$, which is $P_{ij}(\mathbf{v})$ for $\mathbf{n}_{i} = \mathbf{n}_{j} = \mathbf{n}$. (iii) For $\mathbf{n}_{i}\perp\mathbf{n}_{j}$, $P_{ij}\mathbf{n}_{i} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{i}$, $P_{ij}\mathbf{n}_{j} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{j}$ and $P_{ij}$ fixes the direction dual to $\mathbf{n}_{i}\wedge\mathbf{n}_{j}$, $(\mathbf{n}_{i}\wedge\mathbf{n}_{j})I_{3}$ with $I_{3}$ the pseudoscalar of $\Cl(3,0)$, so $P_{ij}^{2} \ne P_{ij}$; in general $P_{ij}$ is idempotent only when $(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i})\mathbf{n}_{i} = (\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{j})\mathbf{n}_{j}$ for every $\mathbf{v}$, that is $\mathbf{n}_{i} = \pm\mathbf{n}_{j}$. Not covered by (i): variable smoothing lengths without correction terms, and what acts after these equations (the integrator, the energy floor and cooling). The script checks (i) on a rational instance with distinct normals and viscosity, (ii) exactly in the blade engine, and (iii) in exact rationals.
