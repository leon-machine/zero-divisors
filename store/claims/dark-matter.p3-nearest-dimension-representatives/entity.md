## formal
Each tower is represented at the dimension nearest four; where two are equally near (the odd residues, three and five), the lower one with a single time, else the higher. \emph{Support:} P1's one metric read as minimality. Structural premise; Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.towers-are-residues-single-clock-filter}(iii) records the other windows. \emph{Note.} Premise (W) of \claimref{synthesis.frozen-point-complement-window-lowest-causal}, that every tower's spacetime is, up to sign, a nondegenerate subspace of the frozen point's complement, is recorded as a costed alternative that restates this premise's window (with the single-clock filter it would replace this premise and the residue-$4$ half of the costed single-ideal clause of Observation \claimref{dark-matter.status-rule}(vi) by one hypothesis; at residue $0$ it admits the causal $(1,1)$ where this premise has $(2,2)$); it was found with the fit known and is not adopted. This premise is unchanged.

## technical
Each tower, labelled by its residue mod $8$, is represented at the dimension nearest four. For the odd residues, where dimensions three and five are equally near, the lower is taken if it has a single time, otherwise the higher. The support offered is a reading of the one metric of Premise P1 as a minimality condition; what other windows would give is recorded separately. A geometric restatement of the window, that every tower's spacetime is up to sign a nondegenerate subspace of the frozen point's complement, is recorded as a costed alternative found with the fit known, and is not adopted.

## lay
Each hidden sector could in principle be realised with several different numbers of dimensions. This assumption picks, for each one, the number closest to our four, with a tie-break for the cases exactly in between. It is a choice the paper makes, not something it derives.

## one-liner
Premise: each tower is represented at the dimension nearest four, ties going to the lower dimension when it has a single time.
