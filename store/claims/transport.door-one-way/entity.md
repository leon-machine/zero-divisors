## formal
Let $A = \Cl(p,q)$ be nondegenerate and let $B$ be either minimal completion of this paper: the Rule-1 ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1) \cong \Mat_2(A)$, or (when $p - q \equiv 1 \bmod 4$) a Rule-2 ascent $\cong \Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$. Then there is no unital algebra homomorphism $B \to A$ whatsoever. Every descent is therefore non-multiplicative; the conditional expectation of Proposition \claimref{transport.pinching-kernel} is the canonical linear surrogate, and its loss, the erasure of the ladder sector, is forced by the algebra, not chosen by the formalism.

## technical
Let $A = \Cl(p,q)$ be nondegenerate and let $B$ be either minimal completion: the Rule 1 ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1) \cong \Mat_2(A)$, or, when $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, a Rule 2 ascent $\cong \Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$. Then there is no unital algebra homomorphism $B \to A$ whatsoever.

Every descent from such a completion to its base is therefore non-multiplicative. The pinching conditional expectation is the canonical linear surrogate, and its loss, the erasure of the ladder sector, is forced by the algebra and not chosen by the formalism.

## lay
The series repairs an algebra by moving up to a larger one. Starting from a nondegenerate algebra, this result shows that there is no way back down that respects multiplication: no structure-preserving map from either larger algebra (the second kind exists only when the signature meets a congruence condition) to the original one exists. Any return journey must lose something, and the natural way back loses the part the climb added.

## one-liner
There is no unital algebra homomorphism from either minimal completion of a nondegenerate $\Cl(p,q)$ back to $\Cl(p,q)$ (the Rule 2 one existing only when $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$), so every descent is non-multiplicative.
