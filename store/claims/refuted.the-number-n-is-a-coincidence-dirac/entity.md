## formal
The number N is a coincidence (Dirac)

## technical
Refuted at observation level only, and in part: the refuting Observation \claimref{standard-model.one-number-explain} is conjectured, unlike the refuters of the other refutations, which are proved. The corrected statement, that $N$ is one number appearing in every smallness problem, holds for the cosmological-constant problem ($G\Lambda = 3N^{-2}$) and for gravity's weakness at the Hubble scale ($N^{-2}$), which are one number by the definition of $N$. It does not hold for the mass hierarchy: $\operatorname{Im} w = N\,(m/M_{P})$, to relative order $H^{2}/m^{2}$, carries the rung unit $v/M_{P} \approx 2.0 \times 10^{-17}$, which, granting as Proposition \claimref{families.why-kinematic-attacks-had-fail} does that $m/M_{P}$ is invariant under the rescaling, is a second modulus kinematically not a function of $N$. What the series rejects is Dirac's reading of the coincidence as a law ($N$ a clock, $\Lambda$ decaying as $1/t^{2}$), on its registered and conditional prediction $w = -1$; the coincidence itself, $N$ equal to the present age of the universe in Planck units, the series holds to be a coincidence of the present epoch (Observation \claimref{standard-model.what}(iii)), and so too the Zel'dovich-Weinberg relation $m_{h}^{3} \sim M_{P}^{2}H$ (Proposition \claimref{families.why-kinematic-attacks-had-fail}).

## lay
Dirac noticed that some enormous numbers in physics have about the same size and asked whether this was chance. In this framework the tiny cosmological constant and the weakness of gravity are one number, N, the ratio of the Planck mass to the Hubble scale, though only by how N is defined, and particle masses need a second number besides. Dirac's own coincidence, that N matches the present age of the universe, is read as a feature of our era, not a law; the series rejects Dirac's law on a prediction that is itself conditional. So this entry is refuted only at the level of an observation, and only in part.

## one-liner
Refuted at observation level and in part: the cosmological-constant problem and gravity's weakness are one number $N$ by definition, the mass hierarchy needs a second, and Dirac's coincidence of $N$ with the present age is held epochal, his law rejected on the conditional $w = -1$.
