## formal
For the Schwarzschild gravitational field on flat spacetime (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}): (i) Outside the horizon locus, the static frame and the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand frame, two orthonormal frames of physical vectors, are related at every radius by $e_{0}^{\mathrm{PG}} = (e_{0} - \beta e_{1})/F$, $e_{1}^{\mathrm{PG}} = (e_{1} - \beta e_{0})/F$, a Lorentz transformation ($\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda = \eta$), that is a rotation-gauge transformation, that fixes $e_{2}, e_{3}$ and scales $\ell$ by $(1 - \beta)/F = F/(1 + \beta)$: a boost in the plane of $\ell$, generated by $e_{01}$, which lies in $\mathfrak{p}(\ell)$. The two frames' vacua coincide; on the horizon locus itself the static frame does not exist, and the comparison there is carried by the regular frame alone, whose vacuum is the same object continued. (ii) The one transport the framework selects for a boundary mode is the flow along the $t$-line, whose holonomy is the boost of the completed plane \cite[Prop.~13.2(i)]{ButlerT5}; at the pre-ascent level its class is zero in both frames (Propositions \claimref{pairing.static-frame-only-expansion-survives}, \claimref{pairing.regular-frame-class-vanishes-horizon-changes}). (iii) At the singularity paper's sample point $e^{-\omega/T} = \tfrac{9}{16}$, if the Hawking states for the weights $\omega$ and $2\omega$ are written on the fibre's two modes as $\ket{0} + A\ket{12}$ with $A = \tfrac34$ and $A = \tfrac{9}{16}$, each is the vacuum of a decomposition obtained from $W$ by the rotor $1 + A e_{12}$, whose null directions are
\[
e_{0} + \tfrac{7}{25}e_{1} + \tfrac{24}{25}e_{2} \qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{0} + \tfrac{175}{337}e_{1} + \tfrac{288}{337}e_{2},
\]
distinct: a holonomy reproducing both would be one rotor per mode.

## technical
For the Schwarzschild field on flat spacetime, outside the horizon locus the static and Painlev\'e-Gullstrand frames of physical vectors are related at each radius by $e_0^{\mathrm{PG}} = (e_0 - \beta e_1)/F$, $e_1^{\mathrm{PG}} = (e_1 - \beta e_0)/F$, a rotation-gauge transformation: a boost in the plane of $\ell$, generated by $e_{01} \in \mathfrak{p}(\ell)$, which scales $\ell$ by $(1 - \beta)/F = F/(1 + \beta)$; so the two frames have the same vacuum, and on the horizon locus the regular frame alone carries it. The one transport the framework selects for a boundary mode is the flow along the $t$-line, whose holonomy is the boost of the completed plane, and at the pre-ascent level its pairing class is zero in both frames.

At the sample point $e^{-\omega/T} = \tfrac{9}{16}$, if the Hawking states for weights $\omega$ and $2\omega$ are written on the fibre's two modes as $\ket{0} + A\ket{12}$ with $A = \tfrac34$ and $A = \tfrac{9}{16}$, each is the vacuum of the decomposition obtained by the rotor $1 + Ae_{12}$, with null directions $e_0 + \tfrac{7}{25}e_1 + \tfrac{24}{25}e_2$ and $e_0 + \tfrac{175}{337}e_1 + \tfrac{288}{337}e_2$. These are distinct, so a holonomy reproducing both would be one rotor per mode.

## lay
Outside a black hole's horizon, the two standard ways of setting up local axes, hovering and free-falling, differ only by a boost along the outgoing light direction, so they agree on what the fermion vacuum is; on the horizon only the free-falling axes exist, and they carry the same vacuum across. At the level before the ascent, the one path the framework singles out for a boundary mode, forward in time at fixed radius, does not disturb that vacuum in either set of axes. At the sample temperature used earlier, the thermal states at two different frequencies would each need their own, different tilt of the vacuum, so a single transport could reproduce them only by acting differently on each mode.

## one-liner
Outside the horizon locus the static and Painlev\'e-Gullstrand frames of the Schwarzschild field differ by a rotation-gauge boost in the plane of $\ell$ and share a vacuum, the selected $t$-line transport has zero pairing class in both at the pre-ascent level, and at the sample point the Hawking states at two weights need distinct rotated null directions, one rotor per mode.
