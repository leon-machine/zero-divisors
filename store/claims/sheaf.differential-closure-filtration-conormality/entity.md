## formal
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$ is a commutative algebra containing $\mathcal{A}$, stable under the full boundary calculus: under $\tau\partial_\tau$ and under all tangential vector fields. The differential closure of $\mathcal{A}$ therefore lies inside $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$, and by Lemma \claimref{sheaf.log-obstruction} it exceeds $\mathcal{A}$ as soon as one exponent is nonconstant.
\item The logarithms are filtered by tangential differentiation: $N$ tangential derivatives applied to $\mathcal{A}$ produce log degree at most $N$, with top log coefficient $(e')^N$ on a monomial $\tau^{e}$; radial derivatives do not raise the log degree.
\item No operation of the calculus lowers the exponent set. Consequently every element of $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$ is conormal: for b-vector fields $V_1,\dots,V_k$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, $V_1\cdots V_k a = O(\tau^{\,e_{\min}(x)-\varepsilon})$ locally uniformly.
\item The log-free part with locally constant exponents is precisely the smooth-coefficient polyhomogeneous ring of \cite{Melrose1993}, index sets generated by the exponents; $\mathcal{A}$ itself is the log-degree-zero part of $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$: a subalgebra, but not a submodule over the calculus unless the exponents are locally constant.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the variable-order algebra of powers $\tau^{e(x)}$ with smooth exponents and $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$ its log completion. Then $\mathcal{A}^{\log}$ is a commutative algebra containing $\mathcal{A}$, stable under $\tau\partial_\tau$ and all tangential vector fields, so it contains the differential closure of $\mathcal{A}$, which is strictly larger than $\mathcal{A}$ once an exponent is nonconstant. $N$ tangential derivatives raise the log degree by at most $N$, with top log coefficient $(e')^N$ on $\tau^{e}$, while radial derivatives do not raise it.

No operation of the calculus lowers the exponent set, so every element is conormal: $V_1 \cdots V_k a = O(\tau^{e_{\min}(x) - \varepsilon})$ locally uniformly, for b-vector fields $V_i$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$. The log-free part with locally constant exponents is the smooth-coefficient polyhomogeneous ring of Melrose, and $\mathcal{A}$ is the log-degree-zero part: a subalgebra, but a module over the calculus only when the exponents are locally constant.

## lay
Functions that behave like a power of the distance to a boundary, with the power allowed to vary from point to point, form a natural algebra. Differentiating along the boundary produces logarithms, so the algebra has to be enlarged to include them. This result shows that the enlarged algebra is closed under all the relevant derivatives, that the logarithms build up in a controlled way, and that no element grows faster at the boundary than its powers allow.

## one-liner
The log completion of the variable-order algebra is a commutative algebra closed under the boundary calculus, with log degree filtered by tangential derivatives, all elements conormal, and the classical polyhomogeneous ring as its log-free, locally-constant-exponent part.
