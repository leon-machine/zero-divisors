\emph{Setting.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.quasi-free-deposit-is-vacuum-plus-shot-noise} a quasi-free deposit on the boundary-mode algebra exceeds the vacuum by shot noise only, below $10^{-77}$ at the pivot for dense deposits; a deposit that carries the measured amplitude must therefore contain a classical (non-quasi-free) component, and the one considered here is a modulation of the deposit's local data on scales far outside the descent's Hubble length (by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.finite-range-descent-deposit-cannot-seed-cmb}(iii), the pivot lay outside it by more than $2 \times 10^{5}$). On such scales each patch evolves as an unperturbed descended universe with its own data (the separate-universe reading; standard, cited from memory). The stored deposit has three local data. The visible bath's temperature $T_{d}$ at the deposit. The charge density $n_{Q}$: under the charge reading of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number}(i) the stored asymmetry is a charge fixed by the descent's data and independent of the tower's temperature, and the mirror carries the same baryon charge as this tower, so one $n_{Q}$ serves both. The deposit per unit charge $\varepsilon$ (not the asymmetry $\epsilon$ of $Q = \epsilon N$ in the charge reading): by Proposition \claimref{synthesis.mirror-temperature-measures-deposit-under-r}(ii), under (R), the mirror receives $\varepsilon T_{d}$ per unit charge, thermalised at once, so its radiation density at the deposit is $\rho_{m} = \varepsilon T_{d}n_{Q}$ up to the floor, which (i) of the same proposition puts below $2.5 \times 10^{-5}$ in $\xi$ and which is neglected here. The towers exchange no energy afterwards (the same proposition's hypothesis that neither tower receives entropy beyond its copied history).

\emph{Curvature perturbations.} For a population that is decoupled and has a definite equation of state, the curvature perturbation on its own uniform-density slices, $\zeta_{i} = -\psi + \delta\rho_{i}/(3(\rho_{i} + p_{i}))$, is conserved outside the Hubble length (Wands, Malik, Lyth and Liddle 2000; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-superhorizon-zeta-conservation}). On the slice of uniform time at the deposit, taken flat ($\psi = 0$) in the separate-universe gauge (Hypothesis (LC) of the statement: the patch's curvature, a fourth local datum, is set to zero), radiation has $\zeta = \tfrac14\delta\ln\rho$ and a conserved number $\zeta = \tfrac13\delta\ln n$. So $\zeta_{\gamma} = \tfrac14\delta\ln T_{d}^{4} = \delta\ln T_{d}$, $\zeta_{Q} = \tfrac13\delta\ln n_{Q}$ for every population whose number is fixed by the deposited charge, and $\zeta_{m} = \tfrac14(\delta\ln\varepsilon + \delta\ln T_{d} + \delta\ln n_{Q})$. The entropy perturbation of population $i$ against the photons is $S_{i} = 3(\zeta_{i} - \zeta_{\gamma})$, conserved with them.

\emph{(i)} For the three local data listed (further deposited populations not fixed by the charge would add entropy directions), substituting, $S_{Q} = \delta\ln n_{Q} - 3\delta\ln T_{d}$ and $S_{m} = \tfrac34(\delta\ln\varepsilon + \delta\ln n_{Q} - 3\delta\ln T_{d}) = \tfrac34(\delta\ln\varepsilon + S_{Q})$. The matrix of $v \mapsto (S_{Q}, S_{m})$ is
\[
\begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 & 0 \\ -\tfrac94 & \tfrac34 & \tfrac34 \end{pmatrix},
\]
of rank $2$, with kernel spanned by $(1, 3, 0)$. With the entropy density $s \propto T_{d}^{3}$, $Y_{B} \propto n_{Q}/T_{d}^{3}$, so $\delta\ln Y_{B} = S_{Q}$; and with equal numbers of light species at the deposit, $\xi^{4} = \rho_{m}/\rho_{\gamma} \propto \varepsilon n_{Q}T_{d}^{-3}$, so $\delta\ln\xi^{4} = \delta\ln Y_{B} + \delta\ln\varepsilon = \tfrac43 S_{m}$, the differentiated form of the stored law $\xi^{4} = \tfrac43 Y_{B}\varepsilon$. Along $(1, 3, 0)$ every $\zeta_{i}$ equals $\delta\ln T_{d}$. A patch whose descent happens later by $\delta t$ and is otherwise identical has, on a common slice of uniform time after both deposits, expanded less and so has a higher temperature, $\delta\ln T = H\delta t$, with the same charge per unit entropy and the same $\varepsilon$: that is the direction $(1, 3, 0)$, the adiabatic mode. The check computes the matrix from the definitions, its rank and kernel with the store's exact linear algebra, a control grid on which every other direction is non-adiabatic, and the consistency with the deposit law.

\emph{(ii)} Reading the matrix: (a) $v = (1, 0, 0)\,\delta\ln T_{d}$ gives $S_{Q} = -3\zeta_{\gamma}$, $S_{m} = -\tfrac94\zeta_{\gamma}$; (b) $v = (0, 0, 1)\,\delta\ln\varepsilon$ gives $\zeta_{\gamma} = \zeta_{Q} = 0$ and $S_{m} = \tfrac34\delta\ln\varepsilon$, a pure isocurvature mode of the mirror's radiation, which perturbs the photons only gravitationally after re-entry; (c) $v = (0, 1, 0)\,\delta\ln n_{Q}$ gives $\zeta_{\gamma} = 0$, $S_{Q} = \delta\ln n_{Q}$. The mirror's share of the radiation at the deposit, with equal numbers of light species ($g' = g$, the proposition's assumption), is $\xi^{4}/(1 + \xi^{4})$, below $0.0388$ at the dark-radiation cap $\xi < 0.448$ of the same proposition (iii) and below $1.7 \times 10^{-7}$ at the upper edge $\xi = 2 \times 10^{-2}$ of the helium interval.

\emph{(iii)} The measured perturbation, of amplitude $A_{s} = (2.100 \pm 0.030) \times 10^{-9}$ at the pivot (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-planck-2018-lcdm-parameters}), so $|\zeta_{\gamma}|$ of order $4.6 \times 10^{-5}$, is carried by the photons and the matter together: Planck 2018 (results X; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-planck-2018-isocurvature-and-isotropy-bounds}) bounds the non-adiabatic contribution to the observed temperature variance by $1.3$ percent for matter isocurvature and $1.7$ percent for neutrino density isocurvature at $95$ percent confidence. In (b) and (c) the photons carry no curvature perturbation of their own, and in (a) the charge carriers carry an isocurvature three times the curvature perturbation; a general modulation decomposes as a multiple of $(1, 3, 0)$ plus a part on which $(S_{Q}, S_{m})$ is injective, and that part must be small against $\zeta_{\gamma}$. So the deposit per unit charge and the charge per unit entropy are uniform up to the allowed isocurvature, and the carrier of the spectrum, if it is a modulation of the deposit, is the time-shift field $\delta t(\vec x)$. Its two-point function is a slice correlator of $\zeta = H\delta t$ (the sign of the time shift above), to which Theorem \claimref{synthesis.finite-range-descent-deposit-cannot-seed-cmb} applies unchanged: a delay field of finite range gives index $3$, and the amplitude needs correlations over one comoving length; nothing stored fixes such a field.

\emph{(iv) (Argued: large-scale estimate.)} On large angular scales the temperature anisotropy of mixed adiabatic and matter-isocurvature modes is proportional to $\zeta + 2S_{\mathrm{eff}}$, with $S_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the matter-weighted entropy perturbation of the matter species (Langlois and Riazuelo 2000; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-large-scale-potential-and-sachs-wolfe-relations}; the relative sign depends on conventions and both signs are treated). In (a), every population fixed by the deposited charge has $S_{Q} = -3\zeta$; the baryons and the mirror's baryons (the same charge in the same number of baryons, the copied content giving the same mass per baryon) make up at least $f_{Q} = 2\Omega_{b}h^{2}/(\Omega_{b}h^{2} + \Omega_{c}h^{2})$ of the matter, with $\Omega_{b}h^{2} = 0.02237$ and $\Omega_{c}h^{2} = 0.1200$ (Planck 2018 VI; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-planck-2018-lcdm-parameters}), so $f_{Q} > 0.314$ and the non-adiabatic amplitude $6f_{Q}|\zeta| = \tfrac{26844}{14237}|\zeta| > 1.88|\zeta|$. For a fully correlated mode the non-adiabatic share of the variance is $1 - (1 \pm 6f_{Q})^{-2}$, which is above $0.88$ or below $-0.27$, in magnitude more than twenty times $0.013$. Any further dark matter fixed by the deposited charge raises $f_{Q}$. The full-spectrum bound is the Planck one; the estimate here is the Sachs-Wolfe limit only.
