\emph{(ii)} Write $L = c_{1}L_{1} + c_{3}L_{3} + c_{5}L_{5}$ as a function of $V$ and $\nabla V$ (with $\nabla$ and $I$ held fixed). For a variation $\delta V$, $\delta\int L = \int\bigl(\ip{\delta V}{\partial L/\partial V} + \ip{\nabla\delta V}{\partial L/\partial\nabla V}\bigr)$, and since $\nabla$ is metric, $\ip{\nabla\delta V}{P} = d\ip{\delta V}{P} - \ip{\delta V}{\nabla P}$ up to the sign of the form degree, so $E_{V} = \partial L/\partial V \mp \nabla(\partial L/\partial\nabla V)$. Every term of $\partial L/\partial\nabla V$ is $\epsilon(I, V, \cdot, \ldots)$ with one vector slot open; contracting that slot with $V$ puts $V$ in two slots of the antisymmetric $\epsilon$, so $\ip{V}{\partial L/\partial\nabla V} = 0$ identically, and by the Leibniz rule $\ip{V}{\nabla(\partial L/\partial\nabla V)} = -\ip{\nabla V}{\partial L/\partial\nabla V}$ up to the same sign. Hence $\ip{V}{E_{V}} = \ip{V}{\partial L/\partial V} + \ip{\nabla V}{\partial L/\partial\nabla V} = \tfrac{d}{ds}L(sV, s\nabla V)|_{s=1}$, and $L_{k}$ is homogeneous of degree $k$ in $(V, \nabla V)$, which gives $c_{1}L_{1} + 3c_{3}L_{3} + 5c_{5}L_{5}$.
