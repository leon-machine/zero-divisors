Throughout, the modes are tower planes with $a_{x}a_{y}^{*} + a_{y}^{*}a_{x} = \delta_{xy}$, $a_{x}a_{y} + a_{y}a_{x} = 0$ (Theorem \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors}; distinct planes are orthogonal and anticommute, \claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}), $J$ commutes with every mode, and a vector is written by its occupation pattern relative to the sea $\Omega$.

\emph{(i).} The rotation is $a_{k,\alpha} \mapsto e^{-\theta w_{\alpha}J}a_{k,\alpha}$, so $a_{k,\alpha}^{*} \mapsto e^{\theta w_{\alpha}J}a_{k,\alpha}^{*}$ ($J^{*} = -J$) and $a^{*}_{k,\alpha}a_{l,\beta} \mapsto e^{\theta(w_{\alpha} - w_{\beta})J}a^{*}_{k,\alpha}a_{l,\beta}$. A pair mode $\rho_{q}(M)$ has helicity $h$ exactly when $(w_{\alpha} - w_{\beta} - h)M_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ for all $\alpha, \beta$, which is $[W, M] = hM$ (\claimref{synthesis.helicity-one-pair-modes-on-a-null-generator-are-quasi-spins}(iii)). The weights of the canonical part of a spinor on the ray are $\pm\tfrac12$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.light-ray-good-part-of-a-spinor-is-two-chirality-arrays}(iv)), so $w_{\alpha} - w_{\beta} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ and $M = 0$ for $|h| \ge 2$. The rotation is an automorphism, so helicities add under products.

\emph{(ii), two flavours.} A term of $\rho_{b}(E^{f}_{+-})\Omega$ with $b \ge 1$ needs the level $k + b$ of the channel $(-, f)$ filled and the level $k$ of $(+, f)$ empty, that is $k + b < 0 \le k$, which is impossible; so $X_{q}\Omega = 0$ and $\omega_{\Omega}([X_{q}, X_{q}^{*}]) = |X_{q}^{*}\Omega|^{2}$. Now $\rho_{b}(E^{f}_{+-})^{*}\Omega = \sum_{k=-b}^{-1}a^{*}_{k+b,(-,f)}a_{k,(+,f)}\Omega$ is a sum of $b$ vectors with distinct occupation patterns, each of norm one (for $N \ge b$ all levels are in the window). The two flavours involve different modes, and pair modes are even, so they commute; $X_{q}^{*}\Omega$ is the sum over $a + b = q$ and over the $a$ and $b$ patterns of the two flavours, all distinct, hence orthonormal. So $|X_{q}^{*}\Omega|^{2} = \sum_{a=1}^{q-1}a(q - a) = \binom{q+1}{3}$, the coefficient of $t^{q}$ in $(t/(1 - t)^{2})^{2}$.

\emph{(ii), one species.} $V_{q}$ removes two fermions of the channel $-$ at $l_{1} < l_{2}$ and puts two in the channel $+$ at $k_{1} < k_{2}$; on $\Omega$ this needs $l_{2} < 0 \le k_{1}$, so $l_{1} + l_{2} - k_{1} - k_{2} \le -4$, which is not $q \ge 1$: $V_{q}\Omega = 0$. $V_{q}^{*}\Omega$ is the sum of the vectors with holes in $+$ at $k_{1} < k_{2} < 0$ and particles in $-$ at $0 \le l_{1} < l_{2}$, with coefficients $\pm(k_{2} - k_{1})(l_{2} - l_{1})$; distinct quadruples give distinct patterns, so $|V_{q}^{*}\Omega|^{2} = \sum(k_{2} - k_{1})^{2}(l_{2} - l_{1})^{2}$ over the quadruples with $l_{1} + l_{2} - k_{1} - k_{2} = q$. The levels that occur lie in $[2 - q, q - 3]$, inside the window for $N \ge q - 2$. Put $x_{i} = l_{i}$, $y_{1} = -k_{2} - 1$, $y_{2} = -k_{1} - 1$: then $0 \le x_{1} < x_{2}$, $0 \le y_{1} < y_{2}$, $x_{1} + x_{2} + y_{1} + y_{2} = q - 2$ and the summand is $(x_{2} - x_{1})^{2}(y_{2} - y_{1})^{2}$. With $d = x_{2} - x_{1} \ge 1$,
\[
\sum_{0 \le x_{1} < x_{2}}(x_{2} - x_{1})^{2}t^{x_{1} + x_{2}} = \sum_{x_{1} \ge 0}t^{2x_{1}}\sum_{d \ge 1}d^{2}t^{d} = \frac{1}{1 - t^{2}}\cdot\frac{t(1 + t)}{(1 - t)^{3}} = \frac{t}{(1 - t)^{4}} ,
\]
so the sum is the coefficient of $t^{q-2}$ in $t^{2}/(1 - t)^{8}$, which is $\binom{q+3}{7}$. \emph{Least momentum.} A vector with the same number of fermions as the sea and two units of helicity relative to it has two fermions moved between channels of opposite weight; each moved fermion leaves a level $k < 0$ and takes a level $k \ge 0$. With two flavours the least cost is $1 + 1$ (levels $-1 \to 0$ in each flavour). With one species the two holes are at $-1, -2$ at best and the two particles at $0, 1$, cost $0 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 4$. The value $q$ of a pair mode is the case $a = q$ of the count above (\claimref{synthesis.fluctuation-forms-of-the-tower-and-density-wave-bosons-of-the-null-vacuum}(vi) for the density modes).

\emph{(iii).} Exact computation in the Fock representation on the window $N = q + 2$ (the check); the vectors $\psi_{1}$, $\psi_{2}$ differ from $\Omega$ only at the levels $-2, \dots, 1$, so they are edge-clean at depth $q \le 3$ on that window. The values are normalised expectations, $\langle\psi, [X_{q}, X_{q}^{*}]\psi\rangle/\langle\psi, \psi\rangle$; $\psi_{1}$ has squared norm $2$ and $\Omega$, $\psi_{2}$ have $1$. A number would have the same normalised expectation in all three vectors, as the commutator of the normal pair mode $\rho_{q}(\id)$ with its adjoint has ($q\operatorname{tr}\id = 4q$, clause (ii) of the helicity-one result). That the commutator of a composite is not a number is general: it holds for every composite of free fields, the square of a free boson field included. For the second quantum at $q = 2$: $X_{2}^{*} = \rho_{1}(E^{1}_{+-})^{*}\rho_{1}(E^{2}_{+-})^{*}$ and $(\rho_{1}(E^{f}_{+-})^{*})^{2}\Omega = 0$ (the quasi-spin of one site, clause (iv) there); at $q = 3$ the cross term $\rho_{1}^{*}\rho_{2}^{*}\Omega$ of one flavour vanishes as well, both terms needing the same level twice. At $q = 4$ the square does not vanish; its squared norm $36$ is computed on the window $N = 4$ (the check), against $2\cdot 10^{2}$ for a canonical mode.

\emph{(iv).} $n^{2} = \bar n^{2} = 0$, $n\cdot\bar n = \gamma_{0}^{2} - \gamma_{3}^{2} = 2$, and $n, \bar n$ are orthogonal to the transverse vectors, so $Q(\alpha\bar n + p_{\perp} + \beta n) = 4\alpha\beta - |p_{\perp}|^{2}$, zero for $\beta = |p_{\perp}|^{2}/4\alpha$. For two null vectors $Q(p_{1} + p_{2}) = 2\,p_{1}\cdot p_{2} = 4(\alpha_{1}\beta_{2} + \alpha_{2}\beta_{1}) - 2\langle p_{1\perp}, p_{2\perp}\rangle$ with the Euclidean product of the transverse parts, which is
\[
\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}|p_{2\perp}|^{2} + \frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}|p_{1\perp}|^{2} - 2\langle p_{1\perp}, p_{2\perp}\rangle = \frac{|\alpha_{2}p_{1\perp} - \alpha_{1}p_{2\perp}|^{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}} .
\]
It vanishes exactly when $p_{2\perp} = (\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})p_{1\perp}$, and then $\beta_{2} = (\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})\beta_{1}$, so $p_{2} = (\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1})p_{1}$.

\emph{(v).} The hypotheses are that the elements $\phi^{0}$ and the unit $J$ commute with the matter factor, that $J$ commutes with every $\phi^{0}$, and that $J^{2} = -1$, $J^{*} = -J$; they are what makes each kick a unit with inverse its adjoint and what lets an element that commutes with $\phi^{0}_{0}(t')$ commute with $K_{t'}$. $P_{1}P_{2} = 0$, $P_{1} + P_{2} = 1$ (\claimref{synthesis.set-and-clear-of-a-bit-generate-the-hyperbolic-plane}), so $K_{t'} = P_{1}\exp(-gc_{1}J\phi^{0}_{0}(t')) + P_{2}\exp(-gc_{2}J\phi^{0}_{0}(t'))$ is a unit with inverse $K_{t'}^{*}$. In the interaction picture the evolution to after the last kick is $W = W_{\ge t}W_{<t}$ (later kicks to the left), the detection is $O_{H} = W^{-1}OW$, and the field at $(n, t)$ has met the kicks before $t$ only, as in the stored model ($G = 0$ for $t \le t'$): $\phi_{n}(t) = W_{<t}^{-1}\phi^{0}_{n}(t)W_{<t}$. Hence
\[
[\phi_{n}(t), O_{H}] = W_{<t}^{-1}\bigl[\phi^{0}_{n}(t),\ W_{\ge t}^{-1}OW_{\ge t}\bigr]W_{<t} .
\]
$\phi^{0}_{n}(t)$ commutes with $O$ and with $P_{1}, P_{2}$; if it commutes with $\phi^{0}_{0}(t')$ for every source time $t' \ge t$ it commutes with $W_{\ge t}$ and the bracket is zero. To first order $K^{-1}OK = O + gJ[J_{\mathrm{s}}\phi^{0}, O] = O + gJ[J_{\mathrm{s}}, O]\phi^{0}$, which gives the first-order form; it is the expression in the proof of clause (4) of \claimref{synthesis.field-slaved-to-an-operator-source-does-not-commute-at-spacelike-separation} before the commutator is set equal to a number. No property of the commutators $[\phi^{0}, \phi^{0}]$ was used. \emph{Model.} $h_{x}$ is self-adjoint, even, and $h_{x}^{3} = h_{x}$ (on the two modes it joins it exchanges the two one-particle patterns and annihilates the others), so $\exp(-aJh) = 1 + (\cos a - 1)h^{2} - \sin a\,Jh$, rational at the two angles. $h_{x}$ and $h_{y}$ share no mode unless $y \in \{x - 1, x, x + 1\}$ on the ring of five, and $[h_{x}, h_{x+1}] = b_{x}^{*}b_{x+2} - b_{x+2}^{*}b_{x} \ne 0$. The element at $(n, t)$ is $h_{n-t}$ and the source elements are $h_{0}$ (time $0$) and $h_{-1}$ (time $1$): for $t = 0$ the field element fails to commute with a later source element at $n \in \{1, 4\}$ (with $h_{0}$) and $n \in \{0, 3\}$ (with $h_{-1}$), for $t = 1$ at $n \in \{0, 3\} + 1 = \{1, 4\}$, and for $t \ge 2$ nowhere; the check finds the bracket nonzero at exactly those six events. For the response, on the one-particle patterns $\exp(-aJh_{0})$ sends the pattern at site $0$ to $\cos a$ times itself minus $J\sin a$ times the pattern at site $1$ and fixes the pattern at site $2$; applied to $(b_{0}^{*} + Jb_{2}^{*})$ on the empty medium this gives the patterns $0, 1, 2$ with coefficients $\cos a$, $-J\sin a$, $J$, and the mean of $h_{1}$, twice the $\mathrm{span}\{1\}$ part of the product of the conjugate coefficient at $1$ with the coefficient at $2$, divided by the norm $2$, is $-\sin a$. In the empty medium every $h_{x}$ annihilates the vector and the mean is $0$. So the response depends on the state and is not proportional to $a$.
