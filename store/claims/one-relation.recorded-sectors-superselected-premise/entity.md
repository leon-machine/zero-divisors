## formal
\emph{Premise (recorded sectors are superselected).} Grant Premise \claimref{one-relation.recording-irreversible-stated-so-it-can}: the records a laboratory measurement writes within the modular period are never unwritten. Then, in the recording limit, the flag sectors labelled by those records are superselected: interference between them is as inaccessible as inter-tower coherence, the superselection of the dark-towers paper (Fact \claimref{one-relation.imported-flag-arrow-superselection}) operating in miniature at laboratory scale, so that a record chain reads only its own records.

\emph{Status and cost.} Premise, flagged. The step from unwritten records to inaccessible interference is the decoherence argument \cite{Zeh1970, JoosZeh1985}, which is emergent (irreversibility on the modular period, approximate in any finite laboratory) and not a theorem of charge like the towers' exact algebraic superselection; this premise takes it as exact in the recording limit. That is the first gap Proposition \claimref{one-relation.record-chain-votes-measurement-without-collapse} names. What the premise does not say: why one outcome obtains rather than all persisting in parallel record chains; the recording is compatible with both readings, and no present instrument distinguishes them.

\emph{Refutation condition.} The premise is false if interference between the outcomes of a measurement whose records were written within the modular period is recovered, so that the recorded sectors are not mutually inaccessible.

## technical
Granting that laboratory records are never unwritten, the premise takes the sectors those records label to be superselected in the recording limit: interference between them is as inaccessible as coherence between the dark towers, so a record chain reads only its own records. The step from permanent records to inaccessible interference is the decoherence argument, which is approximate in any real laboratory; the premise takes it as exact in the limit of ideal recording, and that is its cost. It says nothing about why one outcome is experienced. It is refuted if interference between recorded outcomes is ever recovered.

## lay
Once a measurement has left a permanent record, this assumption says the different possible outcomes can no longer interfere with each other at all: each record sees only its own history, just as the hidden mirror worlds of the theory cannot see one another. In real laboratories this is only approximately true, so the assumption idealises it. It would be overturned by an experiment that made recorded outcomes interfere again.

## one-liner
Premise: under irreversible recording, the sectors labelled by a measurement's records are superselected in the recording limit, interference between them as inaccessible as inter-tower coherence, the decoherence step taken as exact.
