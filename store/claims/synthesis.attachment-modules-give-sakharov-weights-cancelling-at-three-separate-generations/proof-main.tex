\emph{Setting.} The attachment (Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment}) gives, for each helicity $s$, a positive-energy module whose conformal energies are $k = |s| + 1 + n$, $n \ge 0$, with multiplicity $(n+1)(n+2|s|+1)$. Writing $a = |s|$ and $k = a + 1 + n$, $(n+1)(n+2a+1) = (k - a)(k + a) = k^{2} - s^{2}$. The two helicity states $\pm s$ of a field have the same spectrum. Bosons ($s = 0, 1$) have integer energies, fermions ($s = \tfrac12$) half-integer ones. A cut-off on conformal energy needs the attachment's conformal energy, which the tractor transport carries only on Weyl-flat fields (\claimref{synthesis.attachment-transport-is-flat-only-where-weyl-vanishes}), and it singles out the conformal frame in which that energy is read; every statement below is on such a field, in such a frame.

\emph{(i) Character.} Since $(n+1)(n+2a+1) = (n+1)(n+2) + (2a-1)(n+1)$ and $\sum_{n}(n+1)(n+2)q^{n} = 2(1-q)^{-3}$, $\sum_{n}(n+1)q^{n} = (1-q)^{-2}$, with $q = e^{-t}$ we get the closed form. Put $1 - e^{-t} = t\,u(t)$ with $u(0) = 1$ a power series with rational coefficients. Then $\chi_{s} = t^{-3}e^{-t(a+1)}\bigl[2u^{-3} + (2a-1)t\,u^{-2}\bigr]$, whose Laurent coefficients are rational. Alternatively, by the Hurwitz expansion $\sum_{j \ge 0}(j + b)^{p}e^{-t(j+b)} = p!\,t^{-p-1} + \sum_{r \ge 0}\zeta(-p-r, b)(-t)^{r}/r!$ for $p = 0, 2$ and $b = a + 1 > 0$, the only poles are $2t^{-3}$ from $\sum k^{2}e^{-tk}$ and $t^{-1}$ from $\sum e^{-tk}$, so the polar part is $2t^{-3} - s^{2}t^{-1}$ with no $t^{-2}$ term, for every $s$. The check computes the series exactly to order $t^{5}$ for $s = 0, \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac32, 2$ and confirms the closed form against the multiplicities for thirty energies.

\emph{(ii) Dictionary.} On the Einstein static field $\mathbb{R} \times S^{3}$ of unit radius the conformal energy is the energy (the conformal embedding of Minkowski space, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-luscher-mack-conformal-energy-on-the-einstein-static-field}), and for a conformally invariant field the energies are $\sqrt{P}$ for a Laplace-type operator $P$ on $S^{3}$ whose heat trace is $(4\pi\sigma)^{-3/2}(A_{0} + A_{1}\sigma + \dots)$, $A_{1} = \int\mathrm{tr}\,a_{1}$. The Mellin transform of the wave trace, $\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-t\sqrt{P}} = \frac{1}{2\pi I_{4}}\int\Gamma(u)t^{-u}\zeta_{P}(u/2)\,du$ (numbers, the contour variable $u$ among them, are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$), has poles of $\zeta_{P}(u/2)$ at $u = 3$ and $u = 1$ with residues $2(4\pi)^{-3/2}A_{0}/\Gamma(3/2)$ and $2(4\pi)^{-3/2}A_{1}/\Gamma(1/2)$, so the polar part is $A_{0}/(\pi^{2}t^{3}) + A_{1}/(4\pi^{2}t)$. With $c_{-3} = 2$ per state, $A_{0} = 2\pi^{2}$ per state, the volume of $S^{3}$, as it must be. Hence for a field the density is $\mathrm{tr}\,a_{1} = A_{1}/\mathrm{Vol} = 2c_{-1}$, and with $R = 6$ on the ultrastatic product (the four-dimensional and three-dimensional scalar curvatures agree), $a_{1} = \tfrac13 c_{-1}R$. By (i): conformal scalar (one state) $0$; Weyl field (two states, $c_{-1} = -\tfrac12$) $-R/6$; gauge vector (two states, $c_{-1} = -2$) $-2R/3$.

Independently, the heat-kernel coefficient is $\mathrm{tr}(E + R/6)$ for $P = -(\nabla^{2} + E)$ (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-sakharov-induced-gravity-coefficients}(a)). For the Dirac square, $E$ is the Lichnerowicz term $\tfrac12\sum_{a<b}e_{a}e_{b}\,R(e_{a} \wedge e_{b})$ (the spin curvature acting by left multiplication); for a constant-curvature field $R(B) = \kappa B$ in $\Cl(4,0)$ the check computes it as $-3\kappa = -R/4$ with $R = 12\kappa$ computed from the same curvature, so a two-component fermion has $2(R/6 - R/4) = -R/6$. For the gauge vector in Feynman gauge, $E = -\Ric$ on four components less two ghost scalars: $4R/6 - R - 2R/6 = -2R/3$. The ghost subtraction is the statement that the physical one-particle space of the covariantly gauge-fixed vector is its two helicities (\claimref{synthesis.self-adjoint-brst-charge-needs-a-krein-form-and-the-tower-supplies-the-ghosts}), clause (iv)), which is what the module count uses. A conformally coupled scalar has $E = -R/6$, giving $0$. These agree with the module values, so the module route and the heat-kernel route give the same $a_{1}$.

\emph{(iii) Smooth cut-offs.} Let $g(x) = (x^{2} - s^{2})\,x\,f(x/L)$ for $x \le L$ and $g = 0$ beyond, and sum over the grid $k \in a + 1 + \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$. If $f \in C^{3}[0,1]$ and $f(1) = f'(1) = 0$, then $g \in C^{1}$, $g''$ has a jump of size $O(L)$ at $x = L$ and $g''' = O(1)$ on $[a+1, L]$. The Euler-Maclaurin formula with the periodic Bernoulli remainder gives $\sum_{k}g(k) = \int_{a+1}^{\infty}g + \tfrac12 g(a+1) - \tfrac1{12}g'(a+1) + \rho$ with $\rho = -\tfrac12\int\tilde{B}_{2}g''$; integrating by parts once more ($\tilde{B}_{3}' = 3\tilde{B}_{2}$) gives a boundary term $O(L)$ from the jump and $\tfrac13\int\tilde{B}_{3}g''' = O(L)$, so $\rho = O(L)$. The endpoint terms at $a + 1$ are $O(1)$. The integral is $\int_{a+1}^{L}(x^{3} - s^{2}x)f(x/L)\,dx = L^{4}\int_{0}^{1}x^{3}f - s^{2}L^{2}\int_{0}^{1}xf + O(1)$. This proves the expansion. The upper edge contributes nothing at orders $L^{3}$ and $L^{2}$ because $g(L) = g'(L) = 0$; that is why the grid offset (integer or half-integer) cannot enter there. For $f = (1 - x^{2})^{m}$ and integer $L$ the sum is a finite sum of powers of $k$, hence (Faulhaber) a Laurent polynomial in $L$ of degrees $4$ to $-2m$; the check determines it exactly from $2m + 5$ values and confirms it at four more, for $m = 2, 3$ and $s = 0, \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac32$: the $L^{4}$ and $L^{2}$ coefficients are $\int x^{3}f = 1/(2(m+1)(m+2))$ and $-s^{2}\int xf = -s^{2}/(2(m+1))$, and there is no $L^{3}$ or $L$ term. For the exponential cut-off the regulated sum is $-\chi_{s}'(t)$, with polar part $6t^{-4} - s^{2}t^{-2}$ by (i).

\emph{(iv) The series' content.} The zero-point energy of the free field is $\tfrac12\sum\omega$ for each boson state and $-\tfrac12\sum\omega$ for each fermion state. By (iii) the $L^{2}$ coefficient of the weighted sum is $\tfrac12\bigl(2N_{V}(-1) + N_{S}\cdot 0\bigr)M_{1} - \tfrac12\bigl(2N_{W}(-\tfrac14)\bigr)M_{1} = -(N_{V} - N_{W}/4)M_{1}$, $M_{1} = \int_{0}^{1}xf > 0$, and the $L^{4}$ coefficient is $\tfrac12(2N_{V} + N_{S} - 2N_{W})M_{3}$. The gauge algebra of the connection on the hierarchy bundle is $\su(3) \oplus \su(2) \oplus \uu(1)$ (Theorem \claimref{standard-model.connection-forced}), of dimension $8 + 3 + 1 = 12$, so $N_{V} = 12$; each generation has sixteen Weyl components (\claimref{synthesis.slot-module-light-ray-arrays-are-weyl-components}), so for $N_{g}$ generations read as separate slot modules $N_{W} = 16N_{g}$. Then $N_{V} - N_{W}/4 = 12 - 4N_{g}$, zero exactly at $N_{g} = 3$. The count needs each generation to be its own slot module with its neutral singlet (the right-handed neutrino): read as faces of one slot space the three faces give at most 40 states, not 48 (\claimref{synthesis.faces-of-one-slot-space-are-not-three-generations-under-one-gauge-group}). With fifteen Weyl components per generation $N_{V} - N_{W}/4 = 12 - \tfrac{15}{4}N_{g}$, which no integer $N_{g}$ zeroes; at $N_{g} = 3$ and $f = (1 - x^{2})^{2}$ the coefficient is $-\tfrac34 \cdot \tfrac16 = -\tfrac18$. The rung scalars enter the attachment as helicity-zero modules, which are conformally coupled, so their number does not matter. The check confirms these coefficients exactly for $N_{g} = 1, \dots, 5$ and $N_{S} = 0, 4, 8$ at $m = 2, 3$, the exponential cut-off for $N_{g} = 2, 3, 4$, that fifteen components per generation never cancel, and that a gauge algebra of dimension 21 (that of Pati-Salam) does not cancel at three generations.

\emph{(v) Sharp floors.} For $f = 1$ below $L$ the summand does not vanish at the edge, and the Euler-Maclaurin endpoint term at the top depends on where the grid stops. On the integer grid with $k < L$ the last energy is $L - 1$, a whole step below $L$, and the sum is $\int^{L} - \tfrac12 g(L) + \dots$, giving $-\tfrac12L^{3}$; on the half-integer grid the last energy is $L - \tfrac12$, the sum is a midpoint sum, and no $L^{3}$ term appears. The check finds exactly $L^{3}$ coefficients $-\tfrac12, 0, -\tfrac12$ for $s = 0, \tfrac12, 1$ and an $L$ coefficient $\tfrac12$ for $s = 1$; for the content of (iv) the weighted $L^{3}$ coefficient is $-7$ with four scalars. This agrees with the floor regulator's own account (\claimref{synthesis.floor-regulator-keeps-the-charge-and-the-central-term-below-the-cut-off}), where the conformal-energy cut keeps rotations but not boosts. For $f = 1 - x^{2}$ ($f(1) = 0$, $f'(1) = -2$) the $L^{2}$ coefficients are $-\tfrac16, \tfrac1{48}, -\tfrac5{12}$, which differ from $-s^{2}/4$ by $-\tfrac16, \tfrac1{12}, -\tfrac16$, an edge term set by the grid offset; the weighted $L^{2}$ coefficient for the series' content is then not zero.

\emph{Reading.} On the Einstein static field of radius $r$, the energies are $k/r$ and a physical cut-off $\Lambda_{c}$ corresponds to $L = \Lambda_{c}r$, so the regulated energy is $r^{-1}E(\Lambda_{c}r) = M_{3}'\Lambda_{c}^{4}r^{3} + M_{2}'\Lambda_{c}^{2}r + O(r^{-1})$. The integral of the scalar curvature over a constant-time section is $6r^{-2}\cdot 2\pi^{2}r^{3} = 12\pi^{2}r$, so if the regulated effective action is local and covariant (a hypothesis, argued, not shown for a frame-dependent cut-off) the $\Lambda_{c}^{2}r$ term is the induced Einstein-Hilbert term; on this single field it cannot be separated from frame-dependent terms such as the integral of the spatial scalar curvature, which also scales as $r$ and its coefficient is the induced $1/(16\pi G)$ up to a positive factor and a sign fixed by the regulator; $\Lambda_{c}^{3}r^{2}$ and $\Lambda_{c}$ match no local invariant of the field. The ratio scalar : Weyl : vector $= 0 : 1 : -4$ of (ii) and (iv) is the standard one (induced coefficient proportional to $N_{0}(1 - 6\xi) + N_{1/2} - 4N_{1}$; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-sakharov-induced-gravity-coefficients}(a), (b), after Broda and Szanecki). The same $48 = 4 \times 12$ (Weyl fields against gauge vectors, three generations with right-handed neutrinos) appears in Boyle and Turok's cancellation of the vacuum energy and both Weyl-anomaly coefficients by dimension-zero scalars (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-sakharov-induced-gravity-coefficients}(c)); their conditions do not mention $a_{1}$. In the locally covariant scheme the renormalisation freedom of a massless field's stress tensor has no Einstein-tensor term at all (only the two quadratic-curvature tensors; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-sakharov-induced-gravity-coefficients}(d), after Tichy and Flanagan); that holds for every massless content, so it does not bear on the zero found here, which concerns cut-off regulators only. Mass terms then give $1/G$ of order $(12\pi)^{-1}\sum_{i}c_{i}m_{i}^{2}\ln(\Lambda_{c}^{2}/m_{i}^{2})$ with $c_{i}$ the weights above; with the top quark (six Weyl fields at $173$ GeV) and $\Lambda_{c}$ at the Planck scale this is of order $10^{5}$ GeV$^{2}$, about $10^{-33}$ of the observed $1/G$ (computed, order of magnitude; its sign depends on the regulator). The rung field's departure from conformal coupling adds $N_{S}(1 - 6\xi)$ to the bracket, a species count. So, under the hypotheses stated, Sakharov's induced gravity with this content and this cut-off does not produce $G$: Newton's constant stays an input (the registered $N$), and the cancellation says only that the series' matter does not renormalise it quadratically. Three generations is a conjecture of the store (\claimref{families.three-generations-faces-maximal-composite}); the vanishing at $N_{g} = 3$ is the standard Sakharov count for the Standard Model with three right-handed neutrinos, a consequence of the counts, and is not offered as evidence for that conjecture, since no premise of the series requires the induced coefficient to vanish. The mechanism is standard one-loop physics (transport-absent); the attachment supplies the modules from which the coefficients are read (transport-convenient).
