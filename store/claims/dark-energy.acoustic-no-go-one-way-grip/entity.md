## formal
In the current-algebra description of the boundary modes (Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-s-wave-current-algebra}) let $\varphi$ be a state invariant under the $U(1)$ gauge group of the currents, under charge conjugation $C$ ($J \mapsto -J$, $T \mapsto T$), and under the modular flow generated by the zero mode $L_0$ of the Sugawara stress tensor $T$: the conformal vacuum is such a state, and so is the induced state of Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-s-wave-current-algebra} under premise (Q), with charge conjugation implemented on the mode array $\psi_k$ (the fermion modes of the singularity paper's array, Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-s-wave-current-algebra}) as the particle-hole reflection about the sea level, $C\psi_kC^{-1} = \psi^\dagger_{-k}$, so that $J \mapsto -J$ and $T \mapsto T$; the proof shows that this state has the three invariances, and the proposition is conditional on (Q) wherever it is applied to it. The graviton mode is $T$. Let $O$ be a probe that is gauge-charged (a fermion field, a condensate order parameter, any operator of nonzero $U(1)$ charge) or neutral and $C$-odd (the charge and spin currents), or, more generally, that lies in a nontrivial irreducible representation of any symmetry of the algebra that $\varphi$ respects and $T$ is invariant under. Then the linear susceptibility of the graviton mode to $O$ vanishes identically: $\varphi(T\,O) = 0$, and likewise for every mode $T_n$. At first order, nothing couples to the pair correlations except the $C$-even neutral subalgebra; in the conformal vacuum that subalgebra's overlap with $T$ is carried by $T$ alone, while in the thermal state it is the whole $C$-even neutral subalgebra, generated by the current bilinears and strictly larger than the conformal family of $T$, that can couple. In the conformal vacuum the statement is strengthened by Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-conformal-orthogonality-sugawara-construction}: every quasi-primary of dimension other than two is orthogonal to $T$ as well, so the only quasi-primary with nonzero overlap is $T$. The equivalence principle thereby acquires its converse at this level: fields differ in how strongly they couple, and the reduced theory proves there is no other channel through which to couple. \emph{Level:} the orthogonality is proved below for any state with the stated invariances; that the induced state of Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-s-wave-current-algebra} has them is proved in the proof from premise (Q), and is conditional on (Q); that the thermal $C$-even neutral subalgebra is strictly larger than the conformal family of $T$ is a remark, not used.

## technical
In the current-algebra description of the boundary modes, let $\varphi$ be a state invariant under the $U(1)$ gauge group of the currents, under charge conjugation $C$ ($J \mapsto -J$, $T \mapsto T$) and under the modular flow of the zero mode $L_0$ of the Sugawara stress tensor $T$. The conformal vacuum is such a state, and so is the induced state under premise (Q). For a probe $O$ that is gauge-charged, neutral and $C$-odd, or in a nontrivial irreducible representation of any symmetry that $\varphi$ respects and $T$ is invariant under, the linear susceptibility of the graviton mode vanishes: $\varphi(T\,O) = 0$, and likewise for every mode $T_n$. For a probe of charge $q$, invariance gives $\varphi(TO) = e^{iq\vartheta}\varphi(TO)$ for all $\vartheta$; for a $C$-odd probe, $\varphi(TO) = -\varphi(TO)$.

So at first order only the $C$-even neutral subalgebra can couple. In the conformal vacuum, conformal orthogonality of quasi-primaries of unequal dimension strengthens this: $T$ is the only quasi-primary with nonzero overlap with $T$. Wherever the result is applied to the induced state it is conditional on (Q).

## lay
If the graviton is a collective, sound-like mode of the correlations among boundary modes, one can ask whether anything couples to it more efficiently than ordinary energy does. This result says no, at first order: in any state that respects the gauge symmetry, charge conjugation and the time flow the modes generate, symmetry forces the response of the graviton mode to any charged probe, or to any probe that flips sign under charge conjugation, to vanish exactly. Only probes sharing the symmetries of the stress tensor can couple, which is the sense in which gravity has one way to grip. The vacuum is such a state; for the thermal state induced from the modes the result rests on a stated premise about their joint state, premise (Q).

## one-liner
In a state invariant under the gauge group, charge conjugation and the modular flow, every gauge-charged or charge-conjugation-odd probe has zero linear overlap with the graviton mode $T$, so only the neutral, charge-conjugation-even sector can couple at first order.
