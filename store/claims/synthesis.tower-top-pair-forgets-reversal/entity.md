## formal
Let $T_{k} = \Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k)$ be the floors of the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$, with ground generators $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ ($n = p + q$, $r = p - q$) and planes $(e_{j}, f_{j})$, $e_{j}^{2} = 1$, $f_{j}^{2} = -1$, $j \ge 1$; let $L_{p,q} = \varinjlim T_{k}$ (the algebraic limit) and $B_{\infty} \subset L_{p,q}$ the subalgebra generated by all plane generators. A pair isomorphism is an algebra isomorphism $\Phi \colon L_{p,q} \to L_{p',q'}$ with $\Phi(B_{\infty}) = B_{\infty}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Reversal.)} Let $y_{1}, \dots, y_{n}$ be the ground generators of the tower over $\Cl(q,p)$, numbered so that $y_{i}^{2} = -x_{i}^{2}$. The assignment
\[
x_{i} \mapsto y_{i}e_{1}, \qquad e_{1} \mapsto e_{1}, \qquad e_{j} \mapsto e_{1}f_{j-1} \ (j \ge 2), \qquad f_{j} \mapsto e_{1}e_{j+1} \ (j \ge 1)
\]
extends to a pair isomorphism $\Phi \colon L_{p,q} \to L_{q,p}$, whose inverse is given by the same formula read from $(q,p)$ to $(p,q)$. It carries $T_{k}$ into $T'_{k+1}$ and $B_{k}$ into $B'_{k+1}$, and $\Phi^{-1}$ carries $T'_{k}$ into $T_{k+1}$; no floor goes onto a floor. On the planes' commutants it is $x_{i}x_{j} \mapsto -y_{i}y_{j}$, the isomorphism $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \cong \Cl^{0}(q,p)$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.plane-commutant-even-ground}(iii). $\Phi$ is a $*$-isomorphism for the frame $*$-structure (every generator unitary, Theorem \claimref{tower.structure-tower-c-norms}), so its complexification extends to an isomorphism of the C*-completions of the limits (Theorem \claimref{tower.car-limit-even-charge} for $p - q$ even) carrying the closure of $B_{\infty}$ onto the closure of $B_{\infty}$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Classification of tower-top pairs.)} For $n, n' \ge 1$ the pairs $(L_{p,q}, B_{\infty})$ and $(L_{p',q'}, B_{\infty})$ are isomorphic if and only if $n' = n$ and $r' \equiv \pm r \pmod 8$. For $n \ge 1$ and $n' = 0$ they are never isomorphic, since $B_{\infty}$ is the whole limit when $n' = 0$ and a proper subalgebra when $n \ge 1$. In particular the pair, as an inclusion, does not distinguish $r$ from $-r$: it records exactly what the planes' commutant records.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Where the sign lives, over $\R$.)} Grade the real algebra $L_{p,q}$ by its grade involution $\alpha$. The elements that supercommute with every plane generator are exactly $\Cl(p,q) \subset T_{0}$, so the graded pair $(L_{p,q}, B_{\infty}, \alpha)$ determines $\Cl(p,q)$ up to isomorphism and separates $r$ from $-r$ whenever $r \not\equiv 0, 4 \pmod 8$. The reversal $\Phi$ carries $\alpha$ not to the grade involution $\alpha'$ of $L_{q,p}$ but to $\theta' = \operatorname{Ad}(e_{1}) \circ \alpha'$, the automorphism that negates $e_{1}$ and fixes every other generator, whose implementer has $e_{1}\,\alpha'(e_{1}) = -1$; the supercommutant of the planes for $\theta'$ is the mirror copy generated by the $y_{i}e_{1}$. Over $\R$ the sign of $r$ at the top of the tower is therefore carried by the grading and by nothing in the ungraded pair. After complexification the supercommutant is $\Cl(p,q)_{\C}$, which depends on $n$ alone, so there neither the pair nor the grading carries the sign.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Rule 2 at the tower top.)} For $p - q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ the two repairs $\Cl(p{+}1,q)$ and $\Cl(p,q{+}1)$ of Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}, isomorphic as algebras, have residues $r + 1$ and $r - 1$, which are never congruent up to sign modulo $8$; so the tower-top pairs built over the two repairs are never isomorphic. The sign of the adjoined generator, which the fused algebra forgets, is recorded by the ungraded tower-top pair, through the planes' commutant (its centre is $\C$ for one repair and $\R \oplus \R$ for the other).
\end{enumerate}

## technical
The top of the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$ and the top of the tower over the reversed signature $\Cl(q,p)$ are isomorphic even when the plane subalgebra is required to go to itself. The isomorphism multiplies each ground generator by the first plane generator $e_{1}$, which reverses every ground square, and re-pairs the infinitely many plane generators: the elements $e_{1}$ and $e_{1}w$, for $w$ the other plane generators, again form an infinite supply of hyperbolic planes generating the plane algebra. The re-pairing shifts the planes along by one, like a hotel with infinitely many rooms, which is why no finite floor is mapped onto a floor and why the finite floors, which do separate the two signatures, give no obstruction. Together with the even ground algebra already known to sit in the planes' commutant, this classifies tower-top pairs: they are determined by $n$ and the residue up to sign, and the residue's sign is not recorded. Over the reals it is recorded once the grading is part of the data: the elements supercommuting with the planes are then the whole ground algebra, and the reversal isomorphism does not respect the grading. After complexification, as in the store's completed limit, even the grading no longer carries the sign, because the complexified ground algebra depends only on the number of generators. The isomorphism respects the adjoint, so it passes to the C*-completion. The same comparison applied to Rule 2 goes the other way: the two repairs of a split extension are isomorphic algebras, but their towers' tops are not isomorphic pairs.

## lay
Every tower of algebras ends at the same top, and the question was whether the way the top is put together, the position of the blocks added on the way up, still remembers whether the tower started from a signature or from its mirror image. It does not. An explicit relabelling, using the infinitely many added blocks to absorb a sign change, carries one top onto the other block for block. The mirror-image information survives only if one also keeps track of which elements count as odd, the grading that the physics of fermions treats as real. So the series' premise that a tower and its mirror are alike is an exact symmetry of the tower top with its plane algebra, and over the real numbers the one thing that tells them apart is the grading.

## one-liner
The tower-top pair $(\varinjlim T_{k}, B_{\infty})$ over $\Cl(p,q)$ is isomorphic to the one over $\Cl(q,p)$ (explicitly, $x_{i} \mapsto y_{i}e_{1}$ with the planes re-paired), so such pairs are classified by $n$ and $\pm r \bmod 8$; over $\R$ the sign of $r$ is carried by the grading alone.
