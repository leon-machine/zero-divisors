By Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(ii) the rotation-gauge field $\Omega$ is the torsion-free connection of the interior gravitational field, so it is computed here directly from the torsion equation. Take the background labels $(\tau, x^1, x^2, x^3) = (\tau, t, \theta, \varphi)$ of \claimref{interior.exact-parallelism-convergence-rate}, with background frame $\bar e_\tau, \bar e_1, \bar e_2, \bar e_3$ and reciprocal frame the gradients $\nabla\tau, \nabla x^i$. The gauge field of the parallel frame is $\hb^{-1}(\bar e_\tau) = e_0$, $\hb^{-1}(\bar e_i) = a_i e_i$, with $a_2 = r$ and $a_3 = r\sin\theta$, so the reciprocal physical frame is $e^a = \hb^{T}(f^a)$ for the background vector fields $f^0 = \nabla\tau$, $f^i = a_i\nabla x^i$, and $\sigma_i = \partial_\tau a_i/a_i$. Write $\omega(e_b) := \Omega(\hb(e_b))$. A constant physical vector has $D_{\hb(e_b)}e^a = \omega(e_b)\cdot e^a$, so the torsion equation of the Definition, $D\wedge\hb^{T}(f) = \hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge f)$, reads for $f = f^a$
\[
\hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge f^a) \;=\; \sum_b e^b\wedge\bigl(\omega(e_b)\cdot e^a\bigr),
\]
and it fixes $\omega$ uniquely (the frame form of the uniqueness of the torsion-free metric connection). The left side, with the outermorphism $\hb^{T}(\nabla x^\mu\wedge\nabla x^\nu) = e^\mu\wedge e^\nu/(a_\mu a_\nu)$ ($a_0 = 1$): $\nabla\wedge f^0 = 0$; $\nabla\wedge f^i = \partial_\tau a_i\,\nabla\tau\wedge\nabla x^i$ for $i = 1, 2$, so $\hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge f^i) = \sigma_i\,e^0\wedge e^i$; and $\nabla\wedge f^3 = \partial_\tau a_3\,\nabla\tau\wedge\nabla\varphi + r\cos\theta\,\nabla\theta\wedge\nabla\varphi$, so $\hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge f^3) = \sigma_3\,e^0\wedge e^3 + \frac{\cot\theta}{r}\,e^2\wedge e^3$. The right side for the displayed $\omega$: since $(e_0\wedge e_i)\cdot e^0 = -e_i$, $(e_0\wedge e_i)\cdot e^i = e_0$, $(e_2\wedge e_3)\cdot e^3 = e_2$ and $(e_2\wedge e_3)\cdot e^2 = -e_3$, for $a = 0$ every term is a multiple of $e^i\wedge e_i = 0$; for $a = i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ the boost term gives $-\sigma_i\,e^i\wedge e_0 = \sigma_i\,e^0\wedge e^i$; and the rotation term $\frac{\cot\theta}{r}R_{23}$ of $\omega(e_3)$ gives $\frac{\cot\theta}{r}\,e^3\wedge e_2 = \frac{\cot\theta}{r}\,e^2\wedge e^3$ for $a = 3$ and a multiple of $e^3\wedge e_3 = 0$ for $a = 2$. Both sides agree term by term, and uniqueness closes the argument. (In components, a labelled reading: this is the first structure equation $d\theta^a = -\omega^a{}_b\wedge\theta^b$ of the coframe $\theta^0 = d\tau$, $\theta^i = a_i\,dx^i$, solved by $\omega^i{}_0 = \sigma_i\,\theta^i$ (no sum) and $\omega^3{}_2 = \frac{\cot\theta}{r}\,\theta^3$, which the sign of the Definition, $D_a M = a \cdot \nabla M + \Omega(a) \times M$, assembles into $-\sigma_i B_i$ and $+\frac{\cot\theta}{r}R_{23}$.) The last sentence is the standard statement that the induced connection on the constant-$\tau$ spheres is that of $S^2$ of radius $r$, whose coefficients in an orthonormal frame scale as $1/r$; the $\cot\theta$ divergence at the poles is an artifact of the spherical frame, removable by a change of patch.
