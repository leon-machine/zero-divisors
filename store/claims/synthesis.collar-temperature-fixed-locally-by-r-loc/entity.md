## formal
Work on the collar of Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-interior-collar-kasner-form} in units $2M = 1$, with the rates of Proposition \claimref{singularity.modular-flow-boundary-modes-holonomy-universal}: $\rho(r) = 1/(2r^{2})$ per unit Killing time and $\rho_{\mathrm{H}}(r) = \tfrac13 S(r)(1 - r)^{-1/2}$ per unit Hubble-normalised parameter, $S(r) = \sum_{k \ge 0}\binom{2k}{k}4^{-k}\tfrac{3}{2k + 3}r^{k}$. At a collar point use the adapted frame $e_{0}$ (along $\partial_{\tau}$, $Q(e_{0}) = 1$), $e_{1}$ (the $t$-direction), $e_{2}$, $e_{3}$ (the sphere), and $B_{i} = e_{0}e_{i}$. Take as hypotheses, not assumed: (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), the temperature clause of Premise \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-local-horizon-premise}, for the local Rindler horizon generated by $e_{0} + e_{1}$ at each collar point; (S$_{1}$) the completed pair at each collar point is attached to that one sheet (a choice of $t$-orientation, which the symmetry $t \to -t$ of the geometry does not make), or the pairs are doubled by null direction so that each sheet has its own; and (A) the $t$-line holonomy of the slice $r$ acts on those modes as the phase flow of $B_{1}$ at rate $f(r)$, $f = \rho$ or $f = \rho_{\mathrm{H}}$, the flow form assumed in Theorems \claimref{synthesis.collar-kms-extension-three-flows} and \claimref{synthesis.collar-kms-extension-completion-locus}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The t-plane horizons.)} $B_{1}^{2} = 1$; $e_{0} \pm e_{1}$ are null and $\tfrac12\operatorname{ad}_{B_{1}}$ dilates them with weights $\mp 1$, fixing $e_{2}$ and $e_{3}$. So $B_{1}$ is the unit boost of the local Rindler horizons through the point generated by $e_{0} \pm e_{1}$, with edge along the sphere; likewise $B_{2}$, $B_{3}$ for the horizons generated by $e_{0} \pm e_{2}$, $e_{0} \pm e_{3}$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(The state is fixed point by point.)} At every collar point the modes of the horizon generated by $e_{0} + e_{1}$ are in the state of rapidity period $2\pi$. Its inverse temperature on the slice $r$ is $\beta = 2\pi/\rho(r) = 4\pi r^{2}$ in Killing time and
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{H}}(r) \;=\; \frac{2\pi}{\rho_{\mathrm{H}}(r)} \;=\; \frac{6\pi\sqrt{1 - r}}{S(r)} \;=\; 6\pi\Bigl(1 - \tfrac45 r + O(r^{2})\Bigr)
\]
in Hubble-normalised time. At the curvature cutoff of Premise \claimref{singularity.c1-curvature-cutoff}, $r_{c} = (2M)^{-2/3}$ in these units, $\beta_{\mathrm{H}} = 6\pi\bigl(1 - \tfrac45(2M)^{-2/3} + O((2M)^{-4/3})\bigr)$, and as $r \to 0$, at the exceptional set, $\beta_{\mathrm{H}} \to 6\pi$, independent of $M$. This is the state of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collar-kms-extension-three-flows}(iv) under the slice-product reading and the state $\omega_{P}$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collar-kms-extension-completion-locus}(ii) under hypothesis (E).
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Conflict with (R) on one mode set.)} The extension of the horizon's state along the Killing holonomy flow, Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collar-kms-extension-three-flows}(ii), has rapidity period $2\pi/r^{2}$ on the slice $r$ and violates (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on every slice with $r < 1$. Under the one-mode-set clause of (C$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), which identifies the hole's horizon modes with the modes Premises (C1) and (C2) place at the cutoff, and under (A), this extension is what (R) gives the cutoff-placed modes: KMS for the Killing flow at $\beta = 4\pi$, run at rapidity rate $\rho(r_{c})$ there, hence rapidity period $2\pi/r_{c}^{2} \neq 2\pi$. So (R) at the cutoff placement and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) at collar points are incompatible on one mode set under (A).
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Shared atoms; the collar instance of the shared-mode result filed alongside, synthesis.r-loc-needs-sheet-labelled-modes (ii), (iv).)} If one completed pair per point is read by both $t$-plane horizons, generated by $e_{0} + e_{1}$ and $e_{0} - e_{1}$ with the same edge, and each acts on it by the flow of its own future boost, $B_{1}$ and $-B_{1}$, then (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) for both holds only at zero weight; and on the pointwise frame atom $\Cl(1,3)_{\C}$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collar-isotropy-selects-t-boost} no state is KMS at period $2\pi$ for both $\tfrac12 B_{1}$ and $\tfrac12 B_{2}$.
\end{enumerate}
So under (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), (S$_{1}$) and (A) the number $6\pi$ is the inverse temperature, in Hubble-normalised time at the exceptional set, of the modes of one $t$-plane local horizon, fixed locally by the clause and not by extending the horizon's state; on one mode set this is incompatible with (R) at the cutoff placement; and the remaining choice between the slice-product and completion-locus algebras decides only whether this state is in addition KMS for the global holonomy flow, which the value does not need.

## technical
The series found that the boost along the $t$-lines inside a black hole, measured in the Hubble-normalised time of the collar near the singularity, runs at rate $\tfrac13$ at the exceptional set, so a state of rapidity period $2\pi$ would read inverse temperature $6\pi$ there, for every mass. Whether the horizon's thermal state reaches the collar with that period was left open, with three candidate collar algebras giving three verdicts. The local-horizon premise, now adopted, selects the value without that extension. It applies at every point of spacetime, including points of the collar, and at such a point the $t$-boost $B_{1}$ is exactly the unit boost of the local Rindler horizons generated by $e_{0} \pm e_{1}$, whose edge lies along the sphere. The temperature clause then puts the modes of such a horizon at rapidity period $2\pi$ at every collar point. Under the flow form both stored collar theorems assume, the inverse temperature is $4\pi r^{2}$ in Killing time and $6\pi\sqrt{1 - r}/S(r)$ in Hubble time, $6\pi(1 - \tfrac45 r + \cdots)$, already within a relative $(2M)^{-2/3}$ of $6\pi$ at the curvature cutoff where the counting premises place the modes, and $6\pi$ in the limit.

These values are those of the rapidity-flow state of the stored slice-product theorem; what is new is that the clause selects that state, given the flow form and the attachment of each collar point's pair to one $t$-plane sheet (a single pair read by both sheets admits only zero weight, as the shared-mode result filed alongside shows). The value is exactly as strong as the clause, which is independent of the present premises: in the model filed alongside (synthesis.r-loc-independent-sets-matter-coupling) the collar, which meets no Killing horizon, gets $6\pi c$. The alternative in which the horizon's state is carried inward by the Killing holonomy has period $2\pi/r^{2}$ on each slice and violates the clause everywhere inside the horizon; under the one-mode-set clause and the flow form it is what (R) gives the modes placed at the cutoff, so (R) at that placement and the clause at collar points conflict, the same obstruction as at the bifurcation surface. The clause also rules out two readings at nonzero weight: a single completed pair read by both $t$-plane horizons through a point, whose future boosts are opposite, and a frame atom read by both the $t$-plane and the sphere-plane horizons, whose boosts do not commute. What the clause leaves open is whether the collar state is also KMS for the global holonomy flow, which is the completion-locus reading and fails in the slice-product reading; the value $6\pi$ does not depend on it.

## lay
Deep inside a black hole, near the singularity, the series found a natural clock whose rate, in the local cosmological-style time, is one third, which would make the temperature there correspond to the number $6\pi$, the same for every black hole. It was unclear whether the hole's thermal state actually reaches that deep. The series has since adopted an assumption that every small horizon anywhere is warm at a standard rate, and that applies inside the hole too. With it, and with two technical assumptions about how the inner region's modes are organised, the temperature at every point of the inner region is fixed on the spot, and near the singularity it gives $6\pi$ without carrying any state inward from the horizon. The other option, carrying the horizon's state inward along the hole's own time, gives a different temperature everywhere inside. If the modes deep inside are the same modes as those at the horizon, as the series assumes, the horizon's own temperature rule carries them in exactly that other way, so the two temperature rules disagree there: one of them, or the identification of the modes, has to give.

## one-liner
At a collar point the $t$-boost is the unit boost of the local Rindler horizons generated by $e_{0} \pm e_{1}$, so under the flow form (A) and with each collar point's pair attached to one $t$-plane sheet the local temperature clause fixes its modes at rapidity period $2\pi$ on every slice: Killing-time period $4\pi r^{2}$, Hubble-normalised $6\pi\sqrt{1 - r}/S(r) = 6\pi(1 - \tfrac45 r + \cdots)$, tending to $6\pi$ at the exceptional set with no extension of the horizon's state; the Killing extension (period $2\pi/r^{2}$), which (R) gives the cutoff-placed modes on one mode set, violates the clause, so (R) there and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) conflict.
