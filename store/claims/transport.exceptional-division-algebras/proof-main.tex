By Theorem \claimref{transport.classification-real-clifford-algebras}, $\Cl(p,q)$ is $\Mat_k(\mathbb{F})$ or $\Mat_k(\mathbb{F})^{\oplus 2}$. A matrix algebra $\Mat_k(\mathbb{F})$ with $k \geq 2$ contains the nilpotent matrix unit $E_{12} \neq 0$; a direct sum contains the zero divisors $(x, 0)$; and a division algebra contains neither. So freedom from zero divisors forces a single factor with $k = 1$: $\mathbb{F}$ itself. Dimension count fixes the signatures: $\dim = 2^n = \dim_\R \mathbb{F}$ (\claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}) gives $n = 0, 1, 2$ with $\mathbb{F} = \R, \C, \HH$, i.e.\ $(0,0), (0,1), (0,2)$ (the candidates $\Cl(1,0)$ and $\Cl(2,0)$, of the right dimensions, are $\R \oplus \R$ and $\Mat_2(\R)$ by the classification, not $\C$ and $\HH$). Freedom from nilpotents allows in addition direct sums of division algebras: $\Mat_k(\mathbb{F})^{\oplus 2}$ is nilpotent-free iff $k = 1$, and dimension count $2^n = 2\dim_\R \mathbb{F}$ within $d \in \{1,5\}$ gives exactly $\Cl(1,0) \cong \R \oplus \R$ ($n=1$) and $\Cl(0,3) \cong \HH \oplus \HH$ ($n=3$).
