The metric below is the derived metric of the gravitational field, its Levi-Civita connection is the field's rotation-gauge field written in the coframe, and the field equations of the gravitational field are Einstein's equations for the derived metric (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(ii), (iv)). The script computes the Ricci tensor $\Ric_{f}$ of the derived metric from its jets by the coordinate formula of the stored Bianchi II check (positive for the round sphere); the store's sign is $\Ric = -\Ric_{f}$, as calibrated in \claimref{synthesis.scalar-kasner-wall-is-reduced-five-dimensional-vacuum-wall}, so the field equations read $\Ric_{f} = 8\pi G(T - \tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}T)g)$ with $T$ the physical stress. In the script $8\pi G$ is kept as the rational $\kappa = \tfrac32$, so that no identity holds by an accident of the value $1$.

(i) \emph{Ricci in harmonic time.} With $a_{l}' = a_{l}\alpha_{l}'$, $a_{l}'' = a_{l}(\alpha_{l}'' + \alpha_{l}'^{2})$ and $N = a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}$, the script finds at two generic rational jets, exactly, that the frame components of $\Ric_{f}$ are $N^{2}\Ric_{f}(e_{l}, e_{l}) = \alpha_{l}''$ and $\Ric_{f\,TT} = -\sum_{l}\alpha_{l}'' + 2C$, with vanishing off-diagonal components (the values $\tfrac{229}{105}$ and $\tfrac23$ of $\Ric_{f\,TT}$ are pinned). This is the standard form of the diagonal Bianchi I equations in this time (Misner's minisuperspace, cited from memory, and not used as a premise).

\emph{Maxwell.} In the spacetime algebra the stress of a Maxwell field is $T(a) = -\tfrac12 FaF$ (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-maxwell-stress-spacetime-algebra}). For $F = (E + IB)e_{1}e_{0}$ with $I = e_{0}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}$ the script computes $T(e_{a})\cdot e_{b}$ by geometric products in $\Cl(1,3)$ and finds $\mathrm{diag}(\rho, -\rho, \rho, \rho)$ with $\rho = \tfrac12(E^{2} + B^{2})$ at three pairs $(E, B)$, trace-free; for a misaligned field ($E$ along $e_{1}$, $B$ along $e_{2}$) the component $T(e_{0})\cdot e_{3}$ is nonzero (the Poynting term), so only the aligned field keeps the diagonal class. Maxwell's equations for a field depending on $T$ alone (in coordinates, as a labelled reading: $dF = 0$ and $d{*F} = 0$) say that $F_{23}$ and $\sqrt{|g|}F^{T1} = -F_{T1}/a_{1}^{2}$ are constant; the frame components are $B = F_{23}/(a_{2}a_{3})$ and $E = F_{T1}/(Na_{1}) = F_{T1}/(a_{1}^{2}a_{2}a_{3})$, so $Ea_{2}a_{3} = Q_{e}$ and $Ba_{2}a_{3} = Q_{m}$ are constant, and $N^{2}\rho = \tfrac12(Q_{e}^{2} + Q_{m}^{2})a_{1}^{2}$. Since $T$ is trace-free the field equations are $\Ric_{f} = 8\pi G\,T$; in frame components, $\alpha_{1}'' = -V$, $\alpha_{2}'' = \alpha_{3}'' = V$ and $-\sum\alpha'' + 2C = V$, that is $C = V$, with $V = 8\pi G\,N^{2}\rho = 4\pi G(Q_{e}^{2} + Q_{m}^{2})e^{2\alpha_{1}}$. The gradient of $V$ is $2V(1, 0, 0)$ and $G^{-1}$ maps $(1, 0, 0)$ to $(\tfrac12, -\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$, so these are $\alpha'' = -G^{-1}dV$, $C = V$. The script confirms that $\Ric_{f} - \kappa(T - \tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}T)g)$ vanishes identically on jets built this way (three choices of $(Q_{e}, Q_{m})$, with $\alpha_{3}'$ solved from $C = V$) and is nonzero when $\alpha_{1}''$ or $\alpha_{3}'$ is perturbed.

\emph{Fluid.} For $T = \mathrm{diag}(\rho, w\rho, w\rho, w\rho)$ in the frame, $\operatorname{tr}T = (1 - 3w)\rho$, and the field equations in frame components are $\alpha_{l}'' = 8\pi G N^{2}(1 - w)\rho/2$ for each $l$ and $-\sum\alpha'' + 2C = 8\pi G N^{2}(1 + 3w)\rho/2$, that is $C = V$ with $V = 8\pi GN^{2}\rho$. With $\rho = \rho_{0}N^{-(1 + w)}$, $V = 8\pi G\rho_{0}e^{(1 - w)\sum\alpha}$, whose gradient $(1 - w)V(1, 1, 1)$ is mapped by $-G^{-1}$ to $\tfrac12(1 - w)V(1, 1, 1)$, as required. The script confirms the vanishing of the residual for $w = 0, \tfrac13, 1$ (with $N = \tfrac{27}{8}$, so that $N^{1/3}$ is rational) and its failure when the jets or the density are perturbed.

\emph{Bianchi II.} The equations of \claimref{synthesis.kasner-curvature-wall-is-negative-weight-helicity}(iv), $2\alpha_{1}'' = -a_{1}^{4}$, $2\alpha_{2}'' = 2\alpha_{3}'' = a_{1}^{4}$ and $4C = a_{1}^{4}$, are $\alpha'' = -G^{-1}dV$, $C = V$ with $V = \tfrac14e^{4\alpha_{1}}$, since $dV = 4V(1, 0, 0)$.

\emph{Propagation.} Along $\alpha'' = -G^{-1}dV$, $C' = -G(\alpha'', \alpha') = dV(\alpha') = V'$, so $C - V$ is constant. The script checks the identity $C' = \sum_{l}\alpha_{l}''(S - \alpha_{l}')$ against $V' = 2\alpha_{1}'V$ (Maxwell) and $V' = (1 - w)SV$ (fluid), and that a fluid density with another exponent would not propagate the constraint; for the fluid this is the conservation law $\rho \propto N^{-(1 + w)}$.

(ii) Let $V = ce^{2\omega(\alpha)}$, $c > 0$, $\gamma = G^{-1}(\omega, \omega) > 0$, and $u = \omega(\alpha)$. Then $u'' = -2c\gamma e^{2u}$, and for every linear form $\lambda$ with $G^{-1}(\lambda, \omega) = 0$, $\lambda(\alpha)'' = 0$. The first is Liouville's equation, with first integral $u'^{2} + 2c\gamma e^{2u} = \gamma^{2}v^{2}$ for some $v > 0$ and solutions $u' = -\gamma v\tanh(\gamma v(T - T_{0}))$; so $e^{2u} \to 0$ and $u' \to \mp\gamma v$ as $T \to \pm\infty$, and $V \to 0$ at both ends, so $C \to 0$: both limiting velocities lie on the null cone of $G$. The forms $\lambda$ with $G^{-1}(\lambda, \omega) = 0$ together with $\omega$ span the dual space, $\lambda(v_{-}) = \lambda(v_{+})$ and $\omega(v_{-}) = -\omega(v_{+})$; the reflection $R_{\omega}$ has exactly these properties ($\lambda(\omega^{\sharp}) = 0$ and $\omega(\omega^{\sharp}) = \gamma$), so $v_{-} = R_{\omega}v_{+}$. Replacing $\omega$ by $k\omega$ multiplies $\omega(v)\omega^{\sharp}$ and $\gamma$ by $k^{2}$, so $R_{k\omega} = R_{\omega}$. For $\omega = \alpha_{1}$: $\omega^{\sharp} = (\tfrac12, -\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$, $\gamma = \tfrac12$, and $R_{\omega}v = (-v_{1}, v_{2} + 2v_{1}, v_{3} + 2v_{1})$. If $p = v_{+}/S_{+}$ has $p_{1} < 0$, then $S_{-} = S_{+}(1 + 2p_{1})$, positive since $1 + 2p_{1} \ge \tfrac13$ on the circle, so the volume grows with $T$ at both ends and the singularity is at $T \to -\infty$; dividing gives the BKL law, and on $p(u) = (-u, 1 + u, u(1 + u))/(1 + u + u^{2})$ it is $p(u - 1)$ up to order for $u \ge 2$ and $p(1/(u - 1))$ for $1 < u < 2$ (\claimref{synthesis.bkl-bounces-no-framewise-invariant}). In the explicit form used by the script, $\alpha_{1}' = -vs$ with $s = \tanh(kv(T - T_{0}))$ for $\omega = k\alpha_{1}$, $V = v^{2}(1 - s^{2})$, $\alpha_{1}'' = -kV$, $\alpha_{2}' = -\alpha_{1}' + B$, $\alpha_{3}' = -\alpha_{1}' + B_{3}$ and $C = BB_{3} - v^{2}s^{2}$, so $C = V$ is $BB_{3} = v^{2}$, independent of $k$ and $c$; the limiting velocities are $(-v, B + v, B_{3} + v)$ and $(v, B - v, B_{3} - v)$ for both $k = 1$ (Maxwell) and $k = 2$ (curvature). The script checks these identities at three $(k, c)$, three $(v, B)$ and five rational $s$, the $G$-reflection of the late velocity in $\ker\alpha_{1}$ computed with both $\omega = \alpha_{1}$ and $\omega = k\alpha_{1}$, the BKL law and $p_{1}' > 0$, and the $u$-form at four values of $u$ (one pinned: $p_{1}' = \tfrac{52}{133}$ at $u = \tfrac{13}{4}$).

(iii) On an epoch, $\alpha' \to Sp$ with $S > 0$, so $\alpha = SpT + \mathrm{const} + o(1)$ and the proper time $\tau = \int N\,dT$ is a constant times $e^{ST}(1 + o(1))$. The Hamiltonian constraint $C = V$ divided by $S^{2}$ compares $V/S^{2} \propto e^{2S\omega(p)T} \propto \tau^{2\omega(p)}$ with the order-one term $C/S^{2}$; its source term tends to zero exactly when $\omega(p) > 0$. Maxwell along axis $l$: $\omega = \alpha_{l}$, order $\tau^{2p_{l}}$. Curvature wall: $\omega = 2\alpha_{l}$, order $\tau^{4p_{l}}$, as in \claimref{synthesis.kasner-curvature-wall-is-negative-weight-helicity}(ii). On the open arc exactly one weight is negative (\claimref{synthesis.unit-boost-residue-needs-nonpositive-exponent}(2)), so both are active exactly on that axis; after the bounce the first weight is $-p_{1}/(1 + 2p_{1}) > 0$, and since the aligned field has no component along the other axes it is subleading for all earlier times (the script checks the signs at $u = 1, \tfrac32, 2, \tfrac72$). Fluid: $\omega = \tfrac12(1 - w)\sum\alpha$, order $\tau^{(1 - w)\sum p} = \tau^{1 - w}$, decaying for $w < 1$; $G^{-1}((1, 1, 1), (1, 1, 1)) = 3 - \tfrac92 = -\tfrac32$, so the form is timelike and (ii) does not apply: there is no reflection. Stiff matter: $V$ is constant, and $C = V$ divided by $S^{2}$ is $\tfrac12(1 - \sum p_{l}^{2}) = V/S^{2}$; the residue $B_{w} = \sum_{l}p_{l}B_{l}$ has $B_{w}^{2} = \sum_{l}p_{l}^{2}$ (the script computes the square by geometric products, a pure scalar) $= 1 - 2V/S^{2}$. For a massless scalar $\phi(T)$ the field equations in harmonic time are $\phi'' = 0$, $\alpha_{l}'' = 0$ and $C = 4\pi G\phi'^{2}$ (its frame stress is $\mathrm{diag}(\rho, \rho, \rho, \rho)$ with $\rho = \phi'^{2}/(2N^{2})$; the script checks that the residual of the field equations vanishes on jets with $\alpha'' = 0$ and $C = 4\pi G\phi'^{2}$ and not on perturbed jets); with $q = \tau\,d\phi/d\tau = \phi'/S$ this is $\sum p_{l}^{2} = 1 - 8\pi Gq^{2}$, the value of \claimref{synthesis.scalar-kasner-wall-is-reduced-five-dimensional-vacuum-wall}, and the stiff fluid gives the same with $\rho_{0} = \tfrac12\phi'^{2}$. The difference is only that $\phi'$ is a velocity (a fifth slot of the DeWitt form, which is why its residue completes to a unit boost of $\Cl(1,4)$), while $\rho_{0}$ is a constant: the stiff fluid's completion $B_{w} + c\,e_{0}e_{4}$ with $c^{2} = 2V/S^{2}$ exists algebraically but has no dynamical slot.

The conclusion follows: among these sources only stiff matter changes the residue, and as the scalar does; the aligned Maxwell field is subleading (vacuum residue, a unit boost) or a wall reflecting by the BKL law. Not covered: fluids with $w > 1$, misaligned fields (non-diagonal), inhomogeneous matter walls, the dilaton-coupled Maxwell field of the five-dimensional reduction (whose wall form is shifted by the scalar's rate), and existence beyond the homogeneous model.
