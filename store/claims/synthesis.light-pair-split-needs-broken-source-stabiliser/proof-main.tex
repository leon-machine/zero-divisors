\emph{(i)} Since $\hat{U}$ is unitary with $\hat{U}a_{iA}^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \sum_{j}U_{ji}a_{jA}^{\dagger}$, taking adjoints gives $\hat{U}a_{iA}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \sum_{j}\bar{U}_{ji}a_{jA}$. The face operators are $\hat\Omega_{k} = \tfrac12\sum\epsilon_{kij}\epsilon_{AB}a_{iA}a_{jB}$ (Theorem \claimref{families.skeleton-rank-one-plus-ordering-shift}), so $\hat{U}\hat\Omega_{k}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \tfrac12\sum_{i'j'}\bigl(\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{kij}\bar{U}_{i'i}\bar{U}_{j'j}\bigr)\epsilon_{AB}a_{i'A}a_{j'B}$. The cofactor identity $\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{kij}X_{i'i}X_{j'j} = \sum_{m}\epsilon_{m i'j'}\,\mathrm{cof}(X)_{mk}$ with $\mathrm{cof}(X) = \det(X)\,(X^{-1})^{\mathsf{T}}$, applied to $X = \bar{U}$ with $\bar{U}^{-1} = U^{\mathsf{T}}$, gives $\mathrm{cof}(\bar{U}) = \overline{\det U}\,U = N(U)$, hence $\hat{U}\hat\Omega_{k}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \sum_{m}N_{mk}\hat\Omega_{m}$ and $\hat{U}\hat\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \sum_{m}\bar{N}_{mk}\hat\Omega_{m}^{\dagger}$. Therefore $\hat{U}\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \sum_{m}\bigl(\sum_{k}\bar{N}_{mk}\bar{c}_{k}\bigr)\hat\Omega_{m}^{\dagger} = \hat\Omega(Nc)^{\dagger}$, and $\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat\Omega_{l}\hat{U} = \sum_{m}\bar{N}_{lm}\hat\Omega_{m}$ (as $N$ is unitary), so
\[
H_{kl}(\hat{U}\psi) = \tfrac12\bigl\langle\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\hat\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\hat\Omega_{l} + \hat\Omega_{l}\hat\Omega_{k}^{\dagger})\hat{U}\bigr\rangle_{\psi} = \sum_{mn}N_{km}H_{mn}(\psi)\bar{N}_{ln} = (NHN^{\dagger})_{kl}.
\]
The check verifies both identities exactly on two generic states for four rational unitaries, two of determinant one and two not (a phase-diagonal times a Pythagorean rotation, and a permutation with a phase).

\emph{(ii)} If $\hat{U}\psi = \psi$ then by (i) $H = NHN^{\dagger}$, so $H$ commutes with $N(U)$. For $U$ with $Uc = c$ and $\det U = 1$, $N(U) = U$ fixes $c$, and on $c^{\perp}$ these $U$ form all of $SU(2)$, which acts irreducibly; so the commutant of $N(G_{c})$ is $\mathrm{span}\{P_{c}, 1 - P_{c}\}$ (the trivial character on $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_4\}\,c$ and the irreducible doublet on $c^{\perp}$ are inequivalent), and the Hermitian $H$ lies in it: $H = h_{c}P_{c} + h_{\perp}(1 - P_{c})$. Two elements suffice when their commutant is already this span, which the check confirms for the two used, by solving the linear equations $XN = NX$ over $\mathbb{Q}(I_4) = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{1, I_4\}$ and finding complex dimension $2$. For the covered states: by (i), $\hat{U}\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger}\hat{U}^{\dagger} = \hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger}$ for $U \in G_{c}$; $\hat{V} = \sum_{k}\hat\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\hat\Omega_{k}$ is invariant for every $U$ because $N$ is unitary; and $\hat{U}\ket{0} = \ket{0}$. So any polynomial in these applied to the vacuum is $G_{c}$-invariant, and so is the evolution of the vacuum under a Hamiltonian built from them, since the Hamiltonian commutes with every $\hat{U}$, $U \in G_{c}$. Mixed states that are averages of invariant states are covered by linearity of $H$ in the density matrix (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.face-mass-form-unit-plus-gram}).

\emph{(iii)} The check works in the six-mode Fock space with exact complex-rational amplitudes (in its code the families and the face index run over $0, 1, 2$, so $e_{3}$ is index $2$ and $a_{11}, a_{22}$ are modes $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$). For the two stated states it verifies $\hat{U}\psi = \psi$ for two non-commuting elements of $SU(2)$ on families $1, 2$ (both fix $e_{3}$ and have $N(U) = U$), that the normalised mass form is $\mathrm{diag}(h_{\perp}, h_{\perp}, h_{e_{3}})$ with $h_{e_{3}} \neq h_{\perp}$ (for the first state $h_{\perp} = \tfrac{361}{145}$, $h_{e_{3}} = \tfrac{461}{29}$), and that the discriminant of its characteristic polynomial vanishes. Both states have zero means, and the check computes the ordered truncated four-point function $\langle x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}\rangle - \langle x_{1}x_{2}\rangle\langle x_{3}x_{4}\rangle - \langle x_{1}x_{3}\rangle\langle x_{2}x_{4}\rangle - \langle x_{1}x_{4}\rangle\langle x_{2}x_{3}\rangle$ for $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{4}) = (a_{11}^{\dagger}, a_{22}^{\dagger}, a_{11}, a_{22})$ and finds it nonzero; for a zero-mean Gaussian state it vanishes by Wick's theorem (Fact \claimref{families.imported-squeezed-vacuum-bosonic-pairing}), so neither state is Gaussian. For the complex $c = (1, I_4, \tfrac12 + \tfrac{I_4}{3})$ the state $(1 + \hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger} + \tfrac23\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger 2} + \tfrac15\hat{V}\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger 2})\ket{0}$ is checked invariant under two non-commuting products of reflections $1 - 2ww^{\dagger}/|w|^{2}$ in vectors $w \perp c$ (each of determinant one and fixing $c$), and its mass form is checked to act as one scalar on three vectors spanning $c^{\perp}$ and as a different scalar on $c$.

\emph{(iv)} For the four control states the check computes the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of the normalised mass form exactly: nonzero (three distinct eigenvalues, the light pair split) for the stabiliser-breaking state and for the real-structure words at $c = (1, I_4, \tfrac12 + \tfrac{I_4}{3})$ and at the isotropic $c = (1, I_4, 0)$; zero for the real-structure words at the real $c = (1, 2, -\tfrac12)$ and for the same words with $\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger}$ repeated in place of $\hat\Omega(\bar{c})^{\dagger}$ at the complex $c$. Why: for real orthogonal $U$, $N(U) = \det(U)\,U$ is real, with $\det N = 1$, so $N\bar{c} = \overline{Nc}$ and the words are covariant under real rotations but not under $U(3)$. If $c$ is real up to a phase, $c = e^{I_4\phi}r$, then $\bar{c} = e^{-2I_4\phi}c$, the words are polynomials in $\hat\Omega(c)^{\dagger}$ alone, and (ii) applies: the pair stays degenerate. If $c$ is not real up to phase, $\mathrm{Re}\,c$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,c$ are independent, a real $N$ with $Nc = c$ fixes both and so fixes a plane pointwise, and $N \in SO(3)$ is then the identity: no symmetry constrains the light block, and the computed instances split. The statement about the coherent vector is clause (ii) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.coherent-face-vector-real-slices}: for $\lambda > 0$ no complex multiple of it lies in the fixed slice of any $S_{3}$-equivariant real structure.
