## formal
Let the base be the spacetime algebra $\Cl(1,3)$, or its mirror $\Cl(3,1)$. By Theorem \claimref{rule2.where-obstruction-sits-commuting-square}(a) its obstructed one-dimensional extension is $\Cl(p,q{+}1)$ in either case ($\Cl(1,4)$, resp. $\Cl(3,2)$), and by (c) and (d) the two Rule 2 repairs are the opposite-sign repair, the Rule 1 ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ ($\Cl(2,4)$, resp. $\Cl(4,2)$, both with $\nu^{2} = +1$), and the same-sign repair $\Cl(p,q{+}2)$ ($\Cl(1,5)$, resp. $\Cl(3,3)$, both with $\nu^{2} = -1$). Write $\so(p,q) = \mathfrak{k} \oplus \mathfrak{p}$ for the Cartan decomposition by the bivectors $e_{i}e_{j}$, rotations ($(e_{i}e_{j})^{2} = -1$) spanning $\mathfrak{k}$ and boosts spanning $\mathfrak{p}$. Call a continuous unitary representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ \emph{of positive energy} if it is nontrivial on the identity component $\mathrm{Spin}^{+}(p,q)$ and some nonzero element $X$ of the Lie algebra acts by a generator $-i\,d\pi(X)$ that is bounded below (the spectrum condition).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] (Exact.) The centre of $\mathfrak{k}$ is one-dimensional for $\so(2,4)$ and $\so(4,2)$, and zero for $\so(1,5)$, $\so(3,3)$ and the base $\so(1,3)$. It is spanned by the rotation $Z$ of the plane of the two generators that square alike and are in the minority: the base time direction and whichever of the extension generator and $\nu$ squares like time. In $\Cl(2,4)$ that is $Z = e_{0}\nu$; in $\Cl(4,2)$ it is the time direction times the extension generator, while there $e_{0}\nu$ is a boost. In each case $Z^{2} = -1$ and $\mathrm{ad}(Z)^{2} = -4$ on $\mathfrak{p}$, so $\tfrac12\mathrm{ad}(Z)$ is a complex structure on $\mathfrak{p}$.
\item[(ii)] (Existence.) $\mathrm{Spin}^{+}(2,4) \cong SU(2,2)$ has representations of positive energy: the series constructs them in the twistor attachment (Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment}), where the conformal energy on the helicity-$s$ module is $|s| + 1 + n$. That this energy is generated by the central rotation $Z$ of (i) (the conformal Hamiltonian) is the standard identification, argued and not proved here; existence does not depend on it.
\item[(iii)] (Non-existence, by one import.) By the classification of invariant cones (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-invariant-cone-classification}: a simple real Lie algebra contains a closed invariant convex cone other than $0$ and itself exactly when the centre of its maximal compact subalgebra is nonzero), neither $\mathrm{Spin}(1,5)$ nor $\mathrm{Spin}(3,3)$ has a representation of positive energy.
\end{enumerate}
So, with that imported classification, the spectrum condition imposed on the symmetry group of the completed algebra admits the opposite-sign repair, the Rule 1 ascent $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$, and excludes the same-sign one, in both signature conventions; for our tower it returns $\Cl(2,4)$, where $\nu$ is a second direction squaring like time and pairs with $e_{0}$ into the compact central plane. The selection is by a physical requirement on the dynamics (a generator bounded below), not by the representation theory of the fermions, and it is a condition on the spin group built from a chosen vector slot rather than a property of the abstract fused algebra; it is not derived from equations of motion.

## technical
The two Rule 2 repairs of the obstructed extension of the spacetime algebra differ only in the sign of the adjoined generator: $\Cl(2,4)$ for $\nu^{2} = +1$ and $\Cl(1,5)$ for $\nu^{2} = -1$ (in the mirror convention $\Cl(4,2)$ for $\nu^{2} = +1$ and $\Cl(3,3)$ for $\nu^{2} = -1$). As algebras they are isomorphic, but their bivector Lie algebras are different real forms of $\so(6,\C)$: $\so(2,4) \cong \su(2,2)$, while $\so(1,5) \cong \mathfrak{sl}(2,\HH)$ and $\so(3,3) \cong \mathfrak{sl}(4,\R)$.

The difference that matters physically is whether the group can act with positive energy. By the imported invariant-cone classification, a simple real Lie algebra has a nontrivial unitary representation with a generator bounded below only when its maximal compact subalgebra has a centre; the remaining step, that a representation with all generators bounded is trivial on the identity component, is proved here by a scaling argument. The exact computation finds that centre one-dimensional for $\so(2,4)$ and $\so(4,2)$, spanned by the rotation of the base time direction with whichever new generator squares like time, with $\tfrac12\mathrm{ad}(Z)$ a complex structure on the boosts; for $\so(1,5)$ and $\so(3,3)$ it is zero. The series' twistor attachment already realises positive-energy representations of $SU(2,2)$.

So, with the imported classification, imposing the spectrum condition on the completion's symmetry selects the Rule 1 ascent, $\nu^{2} = +1$ in both conventions; in the $\Cl(1,3)$ convention the repair direction pairs with time into a compact plane, the conformal-time circle. The same invariant cone underlies the causal order of compactified Minkowski space, so positivity of energy and causal order read one structure two ways. The classification is the one step taken from outside the series.

## lay
To finish building its basic algebra the series must add one extra direction, and the mathematics alone does not say which of two signs that direction carries. Both choices give the same algebra on paper. This result asks a physical question instead: can the resulting symmetry act on quantum states with an energy that never runs to minus infinity? A theorem from the theory of Lie groups, taken from the literature, says that this is possible only when the symmetry contains a special rotation that commutes with all the other rotations. An exact computation shows that such a rotation exists only for the series' choice of sign; in the series' usual conventions it is the rotation that carries ordinary time into the new direction. So, given that theorem, the requirement that energy be bounded below picks the series' choice. The one step taken from outside the series is that Lie-group theorem.

## one-liner
With the imported invariant-cone classification, the spectrum condition (nontrivial on the identity component) fixes the Rule 2 sign: $\so(2,4)$ and $\so(4,2)$ have a central rotation in their compact part and admit positive energy, $\so(1,5)$ and $\so(3,3)$ do not, so the repair is the Rule 1 ascent, $\nu^{2} = +1$, and $\Cl(2,4)$ for our tower.
