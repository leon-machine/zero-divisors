## formal
A pre-descent $\theta$-term sources the matter asymmetry

## technical
The refuted claim is that a $\theta$-term present before the descent sources the matter asymmetry through the topology of gauge bundles. A $\theta$-term integrates a characteristic class of even degree, so the odd-dimensional towers carry no continuous $\theta$. In this tower a non-relaxing angle of order one at the descent is excluded by the neutron electric dipole bound, which gives $|\theta| \lesssim 10^{-10}$ today; an angle that relaxes or rolls is not excluded. The framework instead inherits its asymmetry as initial data, the Witt flag being the one mechanism it supplies itself.

## lay
One proposed explanation for why the universe has more matter than antimatter is a special angle in the laws of particle physics, set before the universe took its present form. The paper rules out the simplest version: such an angle cannot exist in the odd towers, and in our world measurements of the neutron show the angle is far too small, unless it changed over time. The framework instead treats the imbalance as part of the universe's starting conditions.

## one-liner
Refuted: a non-relaxing pre-descent $\theta$-term cannot source the matter asymmetry, since the odd towers carry no continuous $\theta$ and the neutron dipole bound excludes an angle of order one in this tower.
