\emph{Setting.} The hypothesis puts each quark Dirac matrix in the form of the charged-lepton matrix of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.solar-angle-light-plane-sum-rule}, with its own $c_{q}$, $\delta_{q}$ and one-type $P_{12}$-odd $X_{q}$, at the order of Lemma \claimref{neutrino.breaking-relation}. Clause (i) of that proposition is a statement about every texture of this form: the light states are the left singular vectors of the compression $B_{q}$ of $E + X_{q}$ to the light plane, in the basis $(\hat{b}_{e}, \hat{b}_{o})$, $B_{q} = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} 2/3 & x_{q} \\ y_{q} & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, and for a Dirac sector the light masses are proportional to the singular values $\sigma_{1} > \sigma_{2}$. For complex one-type and Hermitian stages $|x_{q}| = |y_{q}|$ (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.series-family-structures-give-no-lopsided-second-stage}(iii)). The common factor $c_{q}$ and the heavy democratic state drop out of the light-plane overlaps. The mass ratios and $|V_{us}|$ are data; the ratio windows are hypotheses of (ii)-(iii), chosen to cover the lattice averages of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-flag-2024-light-quark-mass-ratios} by more than three standard deviations.

\emph{(i)} Write $H_{q} = B_{q}B_{q}^{\dagger} = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ \bar{b} & d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, $a = k^{2} + |x|^{2}$, $b = k\bar{y}$, $d = |y|^{2}$, $k = \tfrac23$. Read in the Pauli algebra $\Cl(3,0)$, whose paravectors $\mathrm{span}\{1, e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}\}$ are the $2 \times 2$ Hermitian matrices (the matrix trace being $2\langle\cdot\rangle_{0}$), $H = \tfrac{a + d}{2} + h$ with the vector $h = \operatorname{Re}b\,e_{1} - \operatorname{Im}b\,e_{2} + \tfrac{a - d}{2}\,e_{3}$; its eigenprojectors are $\tfrac12(1 \pm \hat{h})$, $\hat{h} = h/|h|$ and the angle $\theta$ of the light state from the odd axis has $\cos 2\theta = (a - d)/(2|h|)$, $\sin 2\theta = |b|/|h|$. The eigenvalues are $\sigma_{1}^{2}$, $\sigma_{2}^{2}$ with $\sigma_{1}^{2} + \sigma_{2}^{2} = \operatorname{tr}H$ and $\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2} = |\det B| = |x||y|$. The relation $\tan^{2}\theta = \sigma_{2}/\sigma_{1}$ is equivalent to $\cos 2\theta = (\sigma_{1} - \sigma_{2})/(\sigma_{1} + \sigma_{2})$, that is $\cos^{2}2\theta = (\operatorname{tr}H - 2\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})/(\operatorname{tr}H + 2\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})$ with $\cos 2\theta > 0$; for $|x| = |y|$ the left side is $((a - d)/2)^{2}/(((a - d)/2)^{2} + k^{2}|x|^{2})$ and the right side $(k^{2} + 2|x|^{2} - 2|x|^{2})/(k^{2} + 4|x|^{2})$ after the substitution $\operatorname{tr}H = k^{2} + 2|x|^{2}$, and $((a - d)/2)^{2} = k^{4}/4$, so both equal $k^{2}/(k^{2} + 4|x|^{2})$. This is the stored clause (ii) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.solar-angle-light-plane-sum-rule} for $y = \pm x$ and clause (iv) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.series-family-structures-give-no-lopsided-second-stage} for complex stages; the computation above only restates it in the form used below, and it is the relation of \cite{GST1968} for the texture of \cite{Fritzsch1978}. The check verifies the identity exactly on five Gaussian-rational $x$ (in $\mathbb{Q}(I_4)$, numbers being read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_4\}$) with $y = \pm x$ and $y = \bar{x}$ (tabulated value $\cos^{2}2\theta = \tfrac{1}{46}$ at $x = y = 1 + 2I_4$), and that $y = 2x$ violates it. Hence $\sin^{2}\theta_{q} = m_{1q}/(m_{1q} + m_{2q})$.

The Cabibbo element is the overlap of the light up state with the second down state in the light plane: $|V_{us}|^{2} = \operatorname{tr}(P^{u}_{-}P^{d}_{+}) = \tfrac12(1 - \hat{h}_{u}\cdot\hat{h}_{d})$, and $h_{u}\cdot h_{d} = z_{u}z_{d} + \operatorname{Re}(b_{u}\bar{b}_{d})$, $z = (a - d)/2$, so $\hat{h}_{u}\cdot\hat{h}_{d} = \cos 2\theta_{u}\cos 2\theta_{d} + \sin 2\theta_{u}\sin 2\theta_{d}\cos\phi$ with $\phi = \arg b_{u} - \arg b_{d}$ (the check verifies the dot-product identity on four exact instances and that an imaginary $x_{u}$ against a real $x_{d}$ gives $\operatorname{Re}(b_{u}\bar{b}_{d}) = 0$, a quarter turn). Then $|V_{us}|^{2} = s_{u}^{2}c_{d}^{2} + c_{u}^{2}s_{d}^{2} - 2s_{u}c_{u}s_{d}c_{d}\cos\phi$, and with $s^{2} = t/(1 + t)$, $c^{2} = 1/(1 + t)$, $t = x$ or $y$, this is the displayed formula; it is the formula of Fritzsch and Xing (1999) in the heavy-quark limit (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-fritzsch-texture-cabibbo-formula}); the new step is only that the store's one-type light-plane block has that form. Invariance of $\phi$: a common rephasing $\operatorname{diag}(e^{I_4\beta_{1}}, e^{I_4\beta_{2}})$ of the left-handed family basis multiplies both $b_{u}$ and $b_{d}$ by $e^{I_4(\beta_{1} - \beta_{2})}$; a right-handed rephasing of one sector multiplies the columns of $B_{q}$ by phases and leaves $b = k\bar{y}$ unchanged, since $k$ and $y$ share the first column, and an overall phase of $c_{q}$ cancels in $H_{q}$. For real $X_{q}$, $b_{q}$ is real and $\cos\phi = \pm1$.

\emph{(ii)} For fixed $\cos\phi = c$ the numerator $x + y - 2c\sqrt{xy}$ and the denominator $(1 + x)(1 + y)$ are bounded separately over the boxes $x \in [\tfrac{1}{22}, \tfrac{1}{18}]$, $y \in [\tfrac{1}{580}, \tfrac{1}{440}]$ (each increasing in $x$ and $y$ apart from the $\sqrt{xy}$ term, which is bounded by its corner values), with $\sqrt{\cdot}$ by integer square roots at $10^{-30}$. This gives safe bounds: $|V_{us}| < 0.196$ at $c = 1$, $< 0.2163$ at $c = \tfrac12$, $> 0.2301$ at $c = -\tfrac12$, $> 0.247$ at $c = -1$. The window $W$ is $(0.22431 - 3 \times 0.00085, 0.22501 + 3 \times 0.00068)$ from the stored quantity and its note; all four bounds are outside it.

\emph{(iii)} The same bounds at $c = 0$ give $(0.2111, 0.2350)$, which contains $W$. At the central lattice ratios, $x_{0} = 2/(27.227 \times 1.465)$ and $y_{0} = 2 \times 0.465/(11.766 \times 27.227 \times 1.465)$, exactly, $|V_{us}|^{2}$ lies between $0.22256^{2}$ and $0.22257^{2}$. These central ratios are those of the $N_{f} = 2+1+1$ averages; at the $N_{f} = 2+1$ central ratios, $x_{3} = 2/(27.42 \times 1.485)$, $y_{3} = 2 \times 0.485/(11.82 \times 27.42 \times 1.485)$ ($m_{s}/m_{d} = 20.36$, $m_{c}/m_{u} = 496.2$), the check certifies $|V_{us}| \in (0.22054, 0.22055)$, below $W$; both points lie in the windows. So the quarter turn is compatible only within the spread of the lattice averages, and the certified content is the range of $\cos\phi$ below. (Computed in floating point, orientation only: the phase that reproduces the global-fit central value at the $N_{f} = 2+1+1$ central ratios is $\phi \approx 93^{\circ}$; the down sector alone gives $\sin\theta_{d} = 0.2185$.) Solving the formula for $\cos\phi = (x + y - |V_{us}|^{2}(1 + x)(1 + y))/(2\sqrt{xy})$ and bounding over the boxes and $W$ (numerator extremes at opposite corners, denominator at its least value for the safe direction) gives $\cos\phi \in (-0.42, 0.36)$. The check also confirms that the central ratios of the stored $M_{Z}$ running masses, $18.97$ and $487$, lie in the windows.

\emph{Reading.} The Cabibbo pass of Proposition \claimref{families.faces-ordered-what-identification-requires-first}(2) compares $\tan\theta_{d} = \sqrt{m_{d}/m_{s}}$ with $|V_{us}|$; at the order used here the down sector alone gives $\sin\theta_{d}$, and the measured value is reached through the up-sector term at a non-real $\phi$. Proposition \claimref{synthesis.residual-s2-decouples-first-family-from-ckm-and-pmns} showed that couplings respecting the residual $S_{2}$ give $V_{us} = 0$, so a quark second stage must break $S_{2}$; this result adds that a one-type such stage reaches the measured $|V_{us}|$ only with an up-down relative phase within $30^{\circ}$ of a quarter turn. That phase is one free number selected by one observable. The five candidate phases with rational cosine were fixed before the comparison, so the quarter turn is the data's choice among them (look-elsewhere count $5$; chance of one fit about $0.3$, an estimate), not a prediction. Nothing stored fixes $\phi$: it is a combination of the phases of couplings that enter the two sectors' stages independently. The result says nothing about lopsided quark stages, about $V_{cb}$, $V_{ub}$ or the Jarlskog invariant (outside the light plane at this order), or about whether the quark sectors share the lepton textures. Transport-absent.
