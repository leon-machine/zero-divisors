## formal
The constituent slots are fermionic modes on the same footing as the pairs, so that the composite's state space is the exterior algebra of slot and pair modes \cite[Sec.~5]{ButlerT12}. Used only where stated.

## technical
Hypothesis (S) takes the constituent slots of a tower's composite to be fermionic modes on the same footing as its ladder pairs, so the composite's state space is the exterior algebra of the slot and pair modes. It is the Standard-Model paper's slot hypothesis carried from our tower to every tower, without that paper's chirality clause, and it is used only where a result says so. In the three-dimensional towers the slot-level states it produces are not representations of the global form (the kernel of the action on the hierarchy is discrete there), as \claimref{note.dark-tower-matter.why-s-fails-three-dimensions-not} records.

## lay
This is an assumption, not a result: the places a composite's constituents occupy are treated as quantum modes of the same kind as their internal pairs. Our own tower's particle physics uses the same assumption; here it is carried to the dark towers, and only the results that state it rely on it.

## one-liner
Hypothesis (S), a premise available in every tower and used only where a result states it: the constituent slots are fermionic modes like the pairs, so the composite's state space is the exterior algebra of slot and pair modes (in three dimensions these slot-level states do not represent the global form).
