(i) By Theorem \claimref{standard-model.yukawa-pattern-two-rung-module}, $w_{4}$ couples exactly $(L, e^{c})$ and $(Q, d^{c})$ and $v_{4}$ exactly $(L, \nu^{c})$ and $(Q, u^{c})$, with symmetric forms under the pairing of Lemma \claimref{standard-model.invariant-pairing-five-modes} on the sixteen of Proposition \claimref{standard-model.hyp-s-slot-statistics}. For a diagonal $g$, $B(\psi, w_{4}g\varphi) = g(\varphi)B(\psi, w_{4}\varphi)$, so on a coupled pair with $\psi$ the doublet and $\varphi$ the singlet the symmetric part is $\tfrac12(g(\varphi) + g(\psi))s$ and the antisymmetric part $\tfrac12(g(\varphi) - g(\psi))s$, $s$ the $10$ entry; likewise for $v_{4}$. By Proposition \claimref{standard-model.generation-ten-under-pati-salam} and $T_{3R} = \tfrac12(N_{R} - 1)$ (Proposition \claimref{standard-model.automorphisms-preserve-slot-pair-split-are}), the doublets have $T_{3R} = 0$, $e^{c}$ and $d^{c}$ have $\tfrac12$, $\nu^{c}$ and $u^{c}$ have $-\tfrac12$, and $B - L$ takes the same value on $e^{c}$ and $\nu^{c}$ and on $d^{c}$ and $u^{c}$. Write $h = h_{e} + h_{o}$, even and odd in $T_{3R}$; then $h_{o}(0, \cdot) = 0$ and $h_{o}(-\tfrac12, y) = -h_{o}(\tfrac12, y)$, so the down-type and up-type symmetric weights are $\tfrac12(h(0, x) + h_{e}(\tfrac12, y)) \pm \tfrac12h_{o}(\tfrac12, y)$ and the antisymmetric ones $\tfrac12(h_{e}(\tfrac12, y) - h(0, x)) \pm \tfrac12h_{o}(\tfrac12, y)$, with $x$, $y$ the values of $B - L$ on the doublet and the singlet: $e + o$ and $e - o$ in both parts, with the same $o = \tfrac12h_{o}(\tfrac12, y)$. This extends Proposition \claimref{synthesis.up-down-split-under-conjugate-reading-needs-su2r-odd-weighting}(i) to the antisymmetric part. The listed values follow from $B - L = \pm1$ on leptons and $\pm\tfrac13$ on quarks (opposite on doublet and singlet), $\chi = -(-1)^{N_{R}}$, $\mu_{P} = -\tfrac{3}{16}(B - L)$ and $\mu_{R} = \tfrac14T_{3R}$ (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.georgi-jarlskog-majorana-moment-map}(i)), and $X_{4} = 8\mu_{R}\mu_{P}$, which vanishes on doublets. The Dirac entry for the pair $(Q_{i}, d^{c}_{j})$ is $Y_{ij}B(Q, Xd^{c}) + Y_{ji}B(d^{c}, XQ)$, which is twice the symmetric part times $Y_{ij}$ for symmetric $Y$ and twice the antisymmetric part times $Y_{ij}$ for antisymmetric $Y$ (the $120$ coupling is antisymmetric in the families, Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-spinor-bilinears}); relative to the $10$ this gives $M_{d} = \sum_{k}b_{k}d_{k}Y_{k}$, and through the per-sector conjugate reading of Corollary \claimref{standard-model.one-vev-vector-relations-breaking-pattern}, $M_{u} = \sum_{k}\bar{b}_{k}u_{k}Y_{k}$. This is the form of Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-clebsch-structure-seesaw}, each source a doublet vev pair.

(ii) If $o_{k} = 0$ and $b_{k} = \rho_{k}b$ with $\rho_{k}$ real, then $M_{d} = bK$ and $M_{u} = \bar{b}K$ with $K = \sum_{k}\rho_{k}e_{k}Y_{k}$, so $bM_{u} = \bar{b}M_{d}$; as $|b| = |\bar{b}|$, $H_{u} = H_{d}$, so the spectra agree and every eigenbasis of $H_{d}$ diagonalises $H_{u}$, giving $V = 1$ as in Proposition \claimref{synthesis.second-doublet-of-one-family-coupling-gives-no-quark-mixing}(ii), now for any number of independent family matrices on one rung vector. The lepton sector is the same with lepton weights.

(iii) If $o_{k} = 0$ and $Y_{k} = \omega R_{k}$ with $R_{k}$ real, put $K = \sum_{k}b_{k}e_{k}R_{k}$; then $M_{d} = \omega K$ and $M_{u} = \omega\sum_{k}\bar{b}_{k}e_{k}R_{k} = \omega\overline{K}$, so $\bar{\omega}M_{u} = |\omega|^{2}\overline{K} = \omega\overline{M_{d}}$. Hence $H_{u} = |\omega|^{2}\overline{K}K^{\mathsf T} = \overline{H_{d}}$, and a Hermitian matrix and its complex conjugate (its transpose) have the same characteristic polynomial. The eigenvectors of $H_{u}$ are the conjugates of those of $H_{d}$, so $V = U_{d}^{\mathsf T}U_{d}$ up to phases, which is not $1$ in general: in the exact instance of the check $\det[H_{u}, H_{d}] \ne 0$, so by Proposition \claimref{synthesis.second-doublet-of-one-family-coupling-gives-no-quark-mixing}(i) the mixing is not a permutation.

(iv) $\tr H_{u} = \sum_{k,l}u_{k}u_{l}\bar{p}_{kl}t_{kl}$ and $\tr H_{d} = \sum_{k,l}d_{k}d_{l}p_{kl}t_{kl}$, with $t$ and $p$ Hermitian in $(k, l)$. For a real symmetric $x_{kl}$, $\sum x_{kl}\bar{p}_{kl}t_{kl} = \sum x_{kl}(\operatorname{Re}p\operatorname{Re}t + \operatorname{Im}p\operatorname{Im}t)$ and $\sum x_{kl}p_{kl}t_{kl} = \sum x_{kl}(\operatorname{Re}p\operatorname{Re}t - \operatorname{Im}p\operatorname{Im}t)$, the imaginary parts cancelling between $(k, l)$ and $(l, k)$. With $u_{k}u_{l} - d_{k}d_{l} = -2(e_{k}o_{l} + o_{k}e_{l})$ and $u_{k}u_{l} + d_{k}d_{l} = 2(e_{k}e_{l} + o_{k}o_{l})$ the law follows. For $S$ symmetric and $A$ antisymmetric, $\tr(SA^{\dagger}) = \sum_{i,j}S_{ij}\bar{A}_{ij}$, which is odd under $i \leftrightarrow j$ and so zero.

(v) By (iv) with $o = 0$, the CP channel needs $\operatorname{Im}t_{kl} \ne 0$ with $k \ne l$ ($t_{kk}$ is real), and the only pair here has $t = 0$; so $\tr H_{u} = \tr H_{d}$. For the determinant, $\det(xS + yA)$ is even in $y$ because $(xS + yA)^{\mathsf T} = xS - yA$; $\det A = 0$ in dimension three, and the coefficient of $xy^{2}$ is $\tr(\operatorname{adj}(A)S) = a^{\mathsf T}Sa$, since $\operatorname{adj}(A) = aa^{\mathsf T}$. So $\det M_{d} = b(b^{2}D + \gamma^{2}q)$ and $\det M_{u} = \bar{b}(\bar{b}^{2}D + \bar{\gamma}^{2}q)$ with $D = \det S$, $q = a^{\mathsf T}Sa$, and
$|\det M_{u}|^{2} - |\det M_{d}|^{2} = |b|^{2}\cdot 2\operatorname{Re}(D\bar{q}(\bar{b}^{2}\gamma^{2} - b^{2}\bar{\gamma}^{2})) = -4|b|^{2}\operatorname{Im}(D\bar{q})\operatorname{Im}((\bar{b}\gamma)^{2})$, with $\det H = |\det M|^{2}$. It vanishes when $\bar{b}\gamma$ is real or imaginary.

(vi) Computed: the check gives the characteristic polynomials and $\det[H_{u}, H_{d}]$ of an exact Gaussian-rational instance, and confirms that aligning the frame coefficients or making the family matrices real removes the split, as (ii) and (iii) require.

The check computes (i) on the $32 \times 32$ rational module for ten weightings and both parts, and (ii) to (vi) on fixed exact Gaussian-rational instances: one rung vector with five composites and independent complex family matrices (and the split restored by one $T_{3R}$-odd composite), three rung vectors with relatively real matrices, the trace law on a mixed instance and on each channel alone, the $10$ with a $120$ (trace, the determinant identity and law, and the phase $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ case), and two $10$s.
