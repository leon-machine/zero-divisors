(i) The dark towers are the five of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.towers-are-residues-single-clock-filter}(ii): the mirror, two three-dimensional and two five-dimensional. That the four odd ones hold matter is Premise \claimref{dark-matter.odd-towers-populated}, and that the towers of residues $0$ and $4$ add nothing to the sum is Premise \claimref{dark-matter.single-ideal-at-residues-zero-and-four}. The shares are read in the sourcing rule of Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones}, a reading of P1: a tower's contribution is its energy per unit of the shared three-volume. By Premise E (\claimref{dark-matter.e-equal-mass-per-unit-ball}) the mass density of the tower of residue $r$ in its own space is, in the unit $d$,
\[
\rho_{r} \;=\; \frac{\mu}{V_{D_{r}}}, \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{us}} \;=\; \frac{\mu}{V_{3}}.
\]
By R3 (\claimref{dark-matter.r3-one-cell-per-unshared-direction}), whose unit is the same length $d$, that density counts per unit of our three-volume without a further factor: a five-dimensional tower's density per unit four-volume, taken through one unit of the extra direction, is a density per unit three-volume; a three-dimensional tower's density per unit area, with sheets one unit apart, is a density per unit three-volume. Ours is $\rho_{\mathrm{us}}$, the mirror's too. Divide; $\mu$ cancels, and $d$ with it, since each density is $\mu/(V_{D}d^{D})$ and the thickness supplies $d^{D-3}$. The values are those of the table:
\[
\frac{V_{3}}{V_{2}} \;=\; \frac{4\pi/3}{\pi} \;=\; \frac{4}{3}, \qquad
\frac{V_{3}}{V_{4}} \;=\; \frac{4\pi/3}{\pi^{2}/2} \;=\; \frac{8}{3\pi}, \qquad
1 + 2(1.3333) + 2(0.8488) \;=\; 5.3643 .
\]
The script evaluates the closed form in $(5.36431, 5.36433)$ by rational bounds on $\pi$.

(ii) The proof of (i) used a tower's quanta nowhere: it used the mass per unit volume, which E fixes. So any filling with $m_{r}\nu_{r}V_{D_{r}}d^{D_{r}} = \mu$ on every populated tower, $m_{r}$ the mass of a quantum and $\nu_{r}$ their number per unit volume, has the shares of (i). Under R1 (\claimref{dark-matter.r1-equal-mass-per-quantum}), $m_{r} = m$, and R2 (\claimref{dark-matter.r2-one-quantum-per-unit-ball}) with its common length $d$, $\nu_{r}V_{D_{r}}d^{D_{r}} = 1$, that product is $m$ on every tower, which is E with $\mu = m$. The script takes fillings whose quanta are heavier and rarer by a factor, different in the three- and five-dimensional towers, and finds the shares unchanged.

(iii) Suppose E holds at $d$ and $s = t = d$. Then $\rho_{r}V_{D_{r}}d^{D_{r}} = \rho_{6}V_{3}d^{3}$ for every dark tower, so $f_{r} = \rho_{r}/(d\rho_{6}) = V_{3}/V_{2}$ at $r = 1, 7$, $f_{2} = 1$, and $f_{r} = \rho_{r}d/\rho_{6} = V_{3}/V_{4}$ at $r = 3, 5$. Conversely suppose $s = t$ and the five shares, and put $d = s$. Then $\rho_{r} = (V_{3}/V_{2})\,d\,\rho_{6}$ gives $\rho_{r}V_{2}d^{2} = \rho_{6}V_{3}d^{3}$ at $r = 1, 7$; $\rho_{2} = \rho_{6}$ is the mirror's equation; and $\rho_{r} = (V_{3}/V_{4})\,\rho_{6}/d$ gives $\rho_{r}V_{4}d^{4} = \rho_{6}V_{3}d^{3}$ at $r = 3, 5$. That is E at $d$ on all six towers, with $\mu = \rho_{6}V_{3}d^{3}$. Each share is needed: with the mirror at twice our density and the other four shares and $s = t$ as stated, the mirror's ball holds twice ours at every length and E fails. For the particular statement: $\rho_{1}V_{2}d^{2} = \rho_{6}V_{3}d^{3}$ has the one positive solution $d = \rho_{1}V_{2}/(\rho_{6}V_{3})$, and then $\rho_{1}/(d\rho_{6}) = V_{3}/V_{2}$ identically in the two densities; the remaining equations of E are $\rho_{7} = \rho_{1}$, $\rho_{2} = \rho_{6}$ and $\rho_{r}V_{4}d = \rho_{6}V_{3}$ at $r = 3, 5$. The script checks both directions at sample densities on all six towers, the mirror counterexample, the identity for the three-dimensional share at several pairs of densities, and that a five-dimensional density off the condition breaks E and moves the share.

(iv) If $\rho_{D}V_{D}d^{D} = \mu$ and $\rho_{D}V_{D}d'^{D} = \mu'$ at $D = 2, 3, 4$, then $(d'/d)^{D} = \mu'/\mu$ at all three, so $(d'/d)^{3} = (d'/d)^{2}$ and $d' = d$. Two populated dimensions suffice; with only three-dimensional towers populated E would hold at every length.
