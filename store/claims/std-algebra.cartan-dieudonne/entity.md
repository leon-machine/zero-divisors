## formal
Let $(V, Q)$ be a nondegenerate real quadratic space of dimension $n$. Every isometry of $(V, Q)$ is a product of at most $n$ reflections $s_u$ in non-null vectors (\claimref{std-algebra.reflection}). Nondegeneracy is needed: every reflection fixes $\operatorname{rad} V$ pointwise, so an isometry of a degenerate space that moves a radical vector (for instance $e_2 \mapsto 2 e_2$ on $\R^{1,0,1}$) is not a product of reflections.

## technical
In a nondegenerate quadratic space every isometry can be written as a composite of mirror reflections, at most as many as the dimension. The theorem fails for degenerate spaces, because reflections never move radical vectors while isometries may rescale them.

## lay
Every rigid symmetry of a nondegenerate space, including rotations and Lorentz boosts, is a sequence of mirror reflections, never more than the number of dimensions. This is why products of arrows can describe all of them.

## one-liner
In a nondegenerate $n$-dimensional real quadratic space every isometry is a product of at most $n$ reflections; this fails for degenerate forms.
