\emph{Setting.} By Fact \claimref{field-theory.imported-tower-limit} the planes of a floor give mode operators with $\{a_j, a_k^{*}\} = \delta_{jk}$, $a_j^2 = 0$; distinct planes anticommute in the algebra, so no ordering signs need inserting. Label the $2N$ planes of a floor by $k = -N, \dots, N - 1$ and read $k$ as the momentum of a right-moving mode of one chirality on a circle (in units of $2\pi/L$); this labelling is the model, not a premise. Write $n_k = a_k^{*}a_k$, $H = \sum_k k\,n_k$, $\mathcal{N} = \sum_k n_k$ and $\rho(p) = \sum_k a_{k+p}^{*}a_k$ over the $k$ with $k$ and $k + p$ in the window, so $\rho(p)^{*} = \rho(-p)$ and $\rho(\pm 2N) = 0$ (no pair of window indices differs by $2N$).

\emph{(i).} From the CAR, $[n_j, a_{l}^{*}a_k] = (\delta_{jl} - \delta_{jk})\,a_l^{*}a_k$. Hence $[H, a_{k+p}^{*}a_k] = ((k + p) - k)\,a_{k+p}^{*}a_k = p\,a_{k+p}^{*}a_k$ and $[\mathcal{N}, a_{k+p}^{*}a_k] = 0$ term by term, so $[H, \rho(p)] = p\,\rho(p)$ and $[\mathcal{N}, \rho(p)] = 0$ with no boundary correction, and $[\mathcal{N}, H] = 0$ since both are sums of commuting numbers. For $1 \le |p| \le 2N - 1$ the sum defining $\rho(p)$ is not empty, so $\rho(p) \ne 0$. The check confirms these identities as algebra elements for $N = 1, 2, 3$.

\emph{(ii).} Using $[a_{k+p}^{*}a_k, a_{l-p}^{*}a_l] = \delta_{k,l-p}\,a_{k+p}^{*}a_l - \delta_{l,k+p}\,a_{l-p}^{*}a_k$ and summing over window indices, the terms with $l = k + p$ give $\sum_k (n_{k+p} - n_k)$ over the $k$ for which both $k$ and $k + p$ lie in the window, that is $k \in \{-N, \dots, N - 1 - p\}$ for $1 \le p \le 2N$. Shifting the first sum, this is $\sum_{k = -N + p}^{N - 1} n_k - \sum_{k = -N}^{N - 1 - p} n_k$, and the common terms cancel, leaving $\sum_{k \ge N - p} n_k - \sum_{k < -N + p} n_k$ (for $p > N$ the two ranges overlap and the same cancellation holds, leaving the same expression). Each $n_k$ has scalar part $\tfrac12$ in the floor's algebra and the two sums have $p$ terms each, so the scalar part is zero; this is the trace argument of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.no-canonical-commutation-relations-tower}, which forbids any commutator on a floor from having a nonzero scalar part. The check confirms the identity and the vanishing scalar part for $N = 1, 2, 3$ and every $p \le 2N$.

\emph{(iii).} On $\Omega$ the modes $k < 0$ are filled and $k \ge 0$ empty. A term $a_{k-p}^{*}a_k$ of $\rho(-p)$ ($p \ge 1$) needs $k$ filled and $k - p$ empty, impossible when $k < 0$, so $\rho(-p)\Omega = 0$. By (ii), $[\rho(-p), \rho(p)] = \sum_{k < -N + p} n_k - \sum_{k \ge N - p} n_k$ acts on $\Omega$ as the number of filled modes among the bottom $p$ minus the number among the top $p$: for $p \le N$ these are $p$ and $0$; for $N < p \le 2N$ they are $N$ and $p - N$. In both cases the value is $\min(p, 2N - p)$. By (i), $\rho(p)\Omega$ is an eigenvector of $H$ with eigenvalue $E_\Omega + p$, and $\rho(-p)\rho(p)\Omega = [\rho(-p), \rho(p)]\Omega = \min(p, 2N - p)\,\Omega$, which is the squared norm of $\rho(p)\Omega$ for the floor's inner product. So the spectral measure of $\rho(p)$ in $\Omega$ is a single point at energy $p$ of weight $\min(p, 2N - p)$; the one-loop density response of the free field (the particle-hole bubble, exact for a free field) is determined by it. In the continuum (the CAR limit, with the sea filled below every momentum) the same computation gives weight $p$ for every $p$, the central term of the density algebra (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-schwinger-term-and-mattis-lieb-density-algebra}: Schwinger's commutator term, and the boson algebra of Tomonaga and of Mattis and Lieb). Hence the floor reproduces the continuum exactly for $p \le N$, and $b_p = \rho(-p)/\sqrt{p}$ obeys $[b_p, b_p^{*}] = 1$ on $\Omega$ there. With the top mode also occupied, (ii) at $p = 1$ gives $n_{N-1} - n_{-N} = 1 - 1 = 0$, so the value is not a property of the algebra alone but of the state at the edges; the check confirms all of (iii) for $N = 1, 2, 3$.

\emph{(iv).} Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment} states that on the attachment the tower's floors are energy cut-offs, the energy being the conformal energy of the palatial storey. In $\Cl(2,4)$ with $Q(e_+) = 1$, $Q(e_-) = -1$, $n_\infty = e_+ + e_-$ and $n_0 = e_+ - e_-$ (null; any normalisation), $P_0 + K_0 = \gamma_0(n_\infty + n_0) = 2\gamma_0e_+$, and $(\gamma_0e_+)^2 = -1$, so $\gamma_0e_+$ generates the compact one-parameter group whose generator is the conformal Hamiltonian. The check computes the kernel of $B \mapsto [\gamma_0e_+, B]$ on the fifteen bivectors exactly: it is seven-dimensional (the compact $\mathfrak{so}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{so}(4)$: $\gamma_0e_+$, the three $\gamma_i\gamma_j$, the three $\gamma_ie_-$), its intersection with the Lorentz bivectors $\gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu$ is the span of the three rotations, and it has zero intersection with the span of the boosts $\gamma_0\gamma_j$, of the translations $\gamma_\mu n_\infty$ and of the special conformal generators $\gamma_\mu n_0$. A generator commuting with the conformal Hamiltonian preserves each of its eigenspaces, so the spatial rotations preserve every floor. Invariance of a single spectral subspace is weaker than commutation, so the converse needs one more step. The boosts, translations and special conformal generators lie in the eight-dimensional complement of the commutant (the noncompact part of $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)$); in the positive-energy modules their complex combinations raise or lower the conformal energy by one, as in the oscillator model of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment}, and a nontrivial unitary representation of a noncompact simple group has no nonzero finite-dimensional invariant subspace (standard). A floor is the span of the finitely many lowest energies of a module whose energies are bounded below, so a nonzero noncompact generator, whose raising part moves the top energy of the floor out of it, does not preserve the floor. Hence a floor is invariant under the spatial rotations and under no boost.

\emph{(v).} This clause was first stated as clause (vii) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.one-loop-vacuum-polarisation-extends-without-a-regulator-and-ward-preserving-extensions-differ-only-by-the-renormalisation-constants}; it is merged here with its proof. Every basis bivector of $\Cl(2,4)$ either commutes or anticommutes with $H = \gamma_0e_+$ (a product of basis vectors); the commuting ones span the seven-dimensional $\mathfrak{k}$ of (iv), the anticommuting ones an eight-dimensional complement $\mathfrak{p}$, and $\mathfrak{k}$ is closed under the commutator (it is a centraliser). The check computes the Lie closure of $\mathfrak{k}$ together with each of the eight anticommuting basis bivectors ($\gamma_0\gamma_j$, $\gamma_0e_-$, $\gamma_je_+$, $e_+e_-$) and each of the eleven generators $\gamma_0\gamma_j$, $\gamma_\mu n_\infty$, $\gamma_\mu n_0$ exactly, by ranks of rational vectors: each time it is all fifteen bivectors. For an arbitrary $X \notin \mathfrak{k}$, write $X = X_{\mathfrak{k}} + X_{\mathfrak{p}}$ with $X_{\mathfrak{p}} \neq 0$; the closure contains $X_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and the $\mathfrak{k}$-module it generates, which is all of $\mathfrak{p}$ when $\mathfrak{p}$, the module $\R^{2}\otimes\R^{4}$ of $\mathfrak{so}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(4)$, is irreducible, and then $[\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{p}]$ supplies the rest (argued). Let $f$ be a bounded function and $f(H)$ the weighting of the modes of an irreducible unitary module by conformal energy. Every generator in $\mathfrak{k}$ commutes with $H$, hence with $f(H)$; if $f(H)$ also commutes with one boost, it commutes with the Lie algebra they generate, the whole conformal algebra, so by Schur's lemma (cited from memory) it is a multiple of the identity on that module and regulates nothing. The attachment's one-particle modules are irreducible under the conformal algebra (Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment}; irreducibility cited from memory), so no weighting of the floors, sharp as in (iv) or smooth, keeps a boost.

\emph{The reading.} Nothing in (i) to (iii) uses more than a finite truncation of a chiral band, so they hold for any sharp mode cut-off; the truncated-band origin of the central term is standard (Mattis and Lieb; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-schwinger-term-and-mattis-lieb-density-algebra}(ii)). A sharp cut-off on conformal energy also leaves non-local terms in the regulated zero-point energy, which a smooth cut-off vanishing with its slope at the edge removes (\claimref{synthesis.attachment-modules-give-sakharov-weights-cancelling-at-three-separate-generations}). The first two parts concern the global charge, which a floor keeps exactly; the local Ward identity of a gauge-invariant regulator requires the central term to be the scalar $p$, which (ii) excludes on any floor and (iii) restores on the filled sea for momenta up to the cut-off. In two dimensions the chiral anomaly and the photon mass of the Schwinger model are fixed by that central term through bosonisation (standard, cited from memory); the floors therefore reproduce them for the modes below the cut-off and not above. The four-dimensional analogue (conformal-energy modules of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.twistor-attachment} in place of momenta on a circle) is argued, not computed: the trace argument again excludes a scalar central term on a floor, and (iv) shows that the regulator is not Lorentz-invariant. The comparison with the quasi-spin of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.how-close-tower-comes-ccr}, where $[b, b^{*}] = 1 - 2k/n$ reaches the CCR only on states of bounded number, is a parallel, not a step of the proof.
