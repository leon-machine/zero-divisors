\emph{(1)} A window algebra is $\End(\Lambda^\bullet\C^F)$, a matrix factor, and its boost dynamics is $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{itH_F})$. By Theorem \claimref{singularity.kms-uniqueness-factor} its only KMS state at $\beta$ is $\varrho_I = e^{-\beta H_F}/Z_F$. If (R$_{\mathrm{inst}}$) holds at two instants with the same window $F$ and the same weights, the window's state is $\varrho_I$ at both, so $S(\varrho)$ and $\Tr(\varrho H_F)$ agree. (The check solves the KMS equations directly on a two-mode window: with $G = e^{\beta H}$, the condition $\Tr(\varrho\,A\,e^{-\beta H}Be^{\beta H}) = \Tr(\varrho BA)$ for all $A, B$ is, by cyclicity, $G\varrho A G^{-1} = A\varrho$ for all $A$; over the sixteen matrix units this has a one-dimensional solution space spanned by $e^{-\beta H}$.)

\emph{(2)} $e^{-\beta H_{F'}} = e^{-\beta H_F}\otimes e^{-\beta\omega_j N_j}$ for $F' = F \cup \{j\}$ (the boost Hamiltonian is a sum of commuting number terms), so the KMS state of the larger window is the product of the smaller window's KMS state and the new mode's marginal $\mathrm{diag}(1 - n(x_j), n(x_j))$; von Neumann entropy and the expectation of a sum of number operators are additive on product states. Repeating, a window $F$ at the later instant and $F_0$ at the earlier, with $F \setminus F_0 = C$ and $F_0 \setminus F = D$ and the common modes in the same state by (1), have $\delta S = \sum_C s(x_j) - \sum_D s(x_j)$ and $\delta E = \sum_C\omega_j n(x_j) - \sum_D\omega_j n(x_j) = T(\sum_C x_j n(x_j) - \sum_D x_j n(x_j))$. The marginal entropy is $s(x) = \ln(1 + e^{-x}) + x\,n(x)$ by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boundary-mode-thermal-entropy}.

\emph{(3)} By (Cl), $\delta Q = T\,\delta S$, so $\delta Q - \delta E = T\sum_C(s - xn)(x_j) - T\sum_D(s - xn)(x_j)$ with $s(x) - x\,n(x) = \ln(1 + e^{-x}) > 0$ for every real $x$. At a common weight $x$ with net number $\delta m = |C| - |D|$, $\delta S = s(x)\delta m$, so $\delta Q \neq 0$ forces $\delta m \neq 0$ and then $\delta Q - \delta E = T\ln(1 + e^{-x})\,\delta m \neq 0$; the ratio $\delta E/\delta Q = x\,n(x)/s(x)$ is below $1$ because $\ln(1 + e^{-x}) > 0$, and it is $0$ at $x = 0$, where $s(0) = \ln 2$ and $\delta Q = T\ln 2\,\delta m$. If $D = \emptyset$ and $\delta Q \neq 0$, then $\delta S \neq 0$, so $C \neq \emptyset$ and $\delta Q - \delta E = T\sum_C\ln(1 + e^{-x_j}) > 0$. In both settings (E), $\delta Q = \delta E$, fails.

\emph{Why the weights are held fixed.} If the weights of kept modes could move, a mode moving from $x$ to $x + \delta x$ changes its entropy by $s'(x)\delta x = -x\,n(1 - n)\delta x$ and its Killing energy by $T\,\delta(x n) = T(n - x\,n(1 - n))\delta x$, so $T\delta S - \delta E$ changes by $-T\,n\,\delta x$, and a suitable increase of weights could offset the deficit of created modes. The boundary-mode Definition fixes the weights as mode labels, and the statement assumes it; a dynamics that moved them in response to matter would be a further structure of the same kind as the one the conclusion names.

\emph{(4)} If (R$_{\mathrm{inst}}$) holds only at the earlier instant, Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boundary-mode-first-law-of-entanglement}(i)-(ii) gives the entropy gain $\beta\,\delta E$ of a fixed window at first order, short by $\rel{\varrho}{\varrho_{I}}$ at second order; that route to Clausius is the one treated in the result filed alongside, synthesis.clausius-is-window-first-law-plus-boost-charge-conservation, and is not repeated here. What (1) adds is that $\delta E \neq 0$ forces $\varrho \neq \varrho_I$, so the new state is not KMS at $\beta$. In the example, the basis is $|n_w n_m\rangle$, the initial state is $\mathrm{diag}(0, 0, 3/4, 1/4)$ on $(|00\rangle, |10\rangle, |01\rangle, |11\rangle)$ (window mode thermal with $n = 1/4$, matter mode occupied), and the exchange is the rotation of $(|10\rangle, |01\rangle)$ by $\cos\theta = 3/5$, $\sin\theta = 4/5$, the identity on $|00\rangle$ and $|11\rangle$; it commutes with the total number. The window's occupation becomes $\sin^2\theta\cdot\tfrac34 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac{73}{100}$, total occupation stays $\tfrac54$, the window's reduced state stays diagonal, and $\tfrac{73}{100}$ is not $n(x) = \tfrac14$.

\emph{Conclusion.} Under (R$_{\mathrm{inst}}$) with fixed weights, (H1) can hold only if boost energy $\delta Q - \delta E \neq 0$ goes to a carrier other than matter and the boundary modes' Killing energy, together with a rule creating modes as heat arrives ($\delta m = \delta Q/(T s(x))$ at a common weight). Per mode the gap is $T\ln(1 + e^{-x}) = T\ln Z_{\mathrm{mode}} = -F_{\mathrm{mode}}$, minus the free energy of a mode created in its Gibbs state: in $T\,dS = dE - \mu\,dN$ it is the chemical-potential term with $\mu = -T\ln(1 + e^{-x})$, standard thermodynamics. If the weights may move, the computation above shows the ledger can balance, so a dynamics of the weights is a third way out, which the boundary-mode Definition does not provide; if (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) is weakened to the earlier instant, a fixed window can absorb the heat, at the price of leaving its KMS state at first order. At zero weight the carrier takes $T\ln 2$ per created mode, the whole heat; this is the per-mode form of the c-number label that carries a hole's mass in the model of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.central-label-carries-mass-at-zero-weight} and that the Gibbs reading forces in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.hole-mass-is-ground-energy-label}. Those results are cited for comparison only; the argument does not use them.

\emph{The check.} Exact rational arithmetic, with logarithms of rationals written as exact coefficient vectors over $\ln 2, \ln 3, \ln 5$: the KMS equations on a two-mode window with $e^{-\beta\omega} = 1/3, 1/5$ (rank $15$ on sixteen unknowns, kernel $e^{-\beta H}$); the exchange example; the factorisation of the Gibbs state of a window plus one created mode; and the per-mode ledger at $e^{x} = 3$ (entropy $2\ln 2 - \tfrac34\ln 3$, Killing energy over $T$ equal to $\tfrac14\ln 3$, difference $\ln\tfrac43 > 0$) and at $x = 0$ (entropy $\ln 2$, Killing energy $0$), and the common-weight deficit $\delta m\ln\tfrac43$ for $\delta m = 2, -1, 0$.
