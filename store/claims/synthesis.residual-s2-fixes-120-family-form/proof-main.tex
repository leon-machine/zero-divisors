(i) A transposition $P$ acts on the antisymmetric matrices $e_{i} \wedge e_{j}$ by permuting the indices and changing the sign when it reverses their order. For the exchange $P_{12}$ of $e_{1}, e_{2}$: $e_{1} \wedge e_{2} \mapsto -e_{1} \wedge e_{2}$ and $e_{1} \wedge e_{3} \leftrightarrow e_{2} \wedge e_{3}$, so the invariants are the multiples of $(e_{1} + e_{2}) \wedge e_{3}$. The exchange $P_{23}$ sends $(e_{1} + e_{2}) \wedge e_{3}$ to $(e_{1} + e_{3}) \wedge e_{2} \neq (e_{1} + e_{2}) \wedge e_{3}$, so no nonzero antisymmetric matrix is $S_{3}$-invariant (the antisymmetric matrices carry the standard representation plus the sign representation, and no trivial one). Writing $e_{1} + e_{2} = \tfrac23 u' + \tfrac13 s$ and $e_{3} = \tfrac13 u' - \tfrac13 s$ gives $(e_{1} + e_{2}) \wedge e_{3} = -\tfrac13\,u' \wedge s$; and $(u' \wedge s)a = u'(s \cdot a) - s(u' \cdot a) = 0$. For symmetric matrices, the $S_{3}$ statement is Lemma \claimref{neutrino.invariant-symmetric-matrices}; under $P_{12}$ the six symmetric basis matrices fall into the fixed ones $E_{11} + E_{22}$, $E_{33}$, $E_{12} + E_{21}$, and the pairs $\{E_{13} + E_{31}, E_{23} + E_{32}\}$, $\{E_{11}, E_{22}\}$, giving four invariants, and $u'u'^{\mathsf{T}}$, $ss^{\mathsf{T}}$, $aa^{\mathsf{T}}$, $u's^{\mathsf{T}} + su'^{\mathsf{T}}$ are four independent $P_{12}$-invariant symmetric matrices. The check computes the invariant dimensions $0, 1, 2, 4$ as exact kernels over all group elements.

(ii) $\J a = 0$ and $e_{3} \cdot a = 0$, so $(\J + \varepsilon e_{3}e_{3}^{\mathsf{T}})a = 0$ for every $\varepsilon$, while $(\J + \varepsilon e_{3}e_{3}^{\mathsf{T}})s = -2\varepsilon e_{3} \neq 0$ for $\varepsilon \neq 0$. On the invariant plane spanned by $u'$ and $s$ the matrix has two nonzero eigenvalues for small $\varepsilon \ne 0$, one near $3$ (the third family, mostly $u'$) and one of order $\varepsilon$ (the second, mostly $s$), and $a$ stays massless until $S_{2}$ is broken.
