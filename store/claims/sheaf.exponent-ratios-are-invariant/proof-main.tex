Ratio invariance is $(a e_i)/(a e_j) = e_i/e_j$. To make one exponent $e$ locally constant requires $a = c/e$, well defined by the strict positivity of $e$, and then every other exponent becomes $c\,e_i/e$: locally constant iff the ratios are. The Kasner ratio and its strict monotonicity are exact rational arithmetic, checked in the script together with the separation statement: the ratio $p_1/p_2$ takes distinct values at distinct parameter values, so a nonconstant $u(x)$ forces nonconstant ratios.
