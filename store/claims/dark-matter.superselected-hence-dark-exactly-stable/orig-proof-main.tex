(1) Under the sector decomposition of Definition \claimref{obj.dark-matter.frozen-phase-operators} every frozen-phase local operator is a section of one tower's soldered bundle and acts within that tower's sector, so its matrix elements between distinct residues vanish: the tower charge is superselected, exactly. The hypothesis cannot be weakened to an algebraic one: an operator straddling two sectors would need a fibrewise map between $(A_{r}, V_{r})$ and $(A_{r'}, V_{r'})$ respecting both solderings, and although such maps exist abstractly (Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.residue-soldered-invariant-not-algebraic-one}(ii)) none carries $V_{r}$ to $V_{r'}$. The shared metric is not a counterexample: gravity couples through stress-energy, which is built from the soldering and is residue-diagonal. (2) is the two imported theorems at their proved levels, the ascent's residue-invariance (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-eight-towers-mod-8-clock}) and the sheaf paper's conservation of degeneracy. (3) is the conjunction: no operator, no reachable wall, hence no channel; the descent, which built all the towers at once, is the one shared event. This is the series' one-way-door argument run tower-wise, with soldered locality in place of locality.
