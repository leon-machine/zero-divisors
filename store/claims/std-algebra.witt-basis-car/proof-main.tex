By \claimref{std-algebra.vector-squares}, $xy + yx = 2B(x, y)$ for vectors, which gives the three relations at once. Then $N_i^{2} = w_i v_i w_i v_i = w_i (1 - w_i v_i) v_i = w_i v_i - w_i^{2} v_i^{2} = N_i$ since $w_i^{2} = Q(w_i) = 0$. For $i \ne j$, $N_i$ and $N_j$ commute because each of $v_i, w_i$ anticommutes with each of $v_j, w_j$, and $N_i$, $N_j$ are products of two. $N_i + v_i w_i = w_i v_i + v_i w_i = 1$. For the hyperbolic planes: $Q(e_i \pm e_{k+i}) = 1 - 1 = 0$, $B(v_i, w_i) = \tfrac14(Q(e_i) - Q(e_{k+i})) = \tfrac12$, and vectors from different planes are orthogonal.
