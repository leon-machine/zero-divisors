\emph{(i).} The two vectors span the incidence line $L_{x}$ of Proposition \claimref{one-relation.spacetime-manifold-vacuum-lines}, and by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.incidence-vacua-thermofield-real-capacity}(ii), in the occupation basis of the pair $(a, b)$, $\Omega_{x}/\|\Omega_{x}\| = (\ket{00} - u\ket{11})/\sqrt{1 + u^{2}}$ and $\Psi_{x}/\|\Psi_{x}\| = (\ket{01} - v\ket{10})/\sqrt{1 + v^{2}}$, each tensored with a definite spectator state, so the $(a, b)$ state is pure and the entanglement entropy of $a$ with $b$ is the von Neumann entropy of the ascent marginal, $\operatorname{diag}(1, w^{2})/(1 + w^{2})$ with $w = u$ or $w = v$ (the check rebuilds both vectors in a two-mode Jordan-Wigner representation and computes the marginal). With $n = w^{2}/(1 + w^{2})$ and $1 - n = 1/(1 + w^{2})$,
\[
s(w) = -n\ln n - (1 - n)\ln(1 - n) = \ln(1 + w^{2}) - n\ln w^{2} .
\]
For $w \neq 0$ the marginal is faithful and, by Theorem \claimref{state-selection.partner-purification-are-canonical}, the vector is the thermofield double of the Gibbs state with $e^{-\beta} = w^{2}$, modular Hamiltonian $\beta N$. Differentiating, $ds/dw = \frac{2w}{1 + w^{2}} - \frac{dn}{dw}\ln w^{2} - n\cdot\frac{2}{w}$, and the first and third terms cancel since $n\cdot 2/w = 2w/(1 + w^{2})$; so $ds/dw = -\ln w^{2}\,\frac{dn}{dw} = \beta\,\frac{dn}{dw}$, with $dn/dw = 2w/(1 + w^{2})^{2}$. This is the Gibbs identity $dS = \beta\,d\langle N\rangle$ for the one-parameter family of Gibbs states of the fixed operator $N$, and holds for any such family. As a function on the chart, $S_{\Omega}$ depends on $x$ only through $u$; the partner $b(x)$ is defined after the spatial rotation taking $x$ into the $(e_{1}, e_{2})$ plane, so it varies with the spatial direction of $x$. The sign of $ds/dw$ is the sign of $(1 - w^{2})\,w$ (as $\beta > 0$ exactly when $w^{2} < 1$, and $dn/dw$ has the sign of $w$), so the critical points are $w = 0$, where $n = 0$ and $s = 0$ by continuity ($n\ln w^{2} \to 0$), and $w = \pm 1$, where $n = \tfrac12$, $\beta = 0$ and $s = \ln 2$; $s$ increases with $|w|$ on $[0, 1]$ and decreases beyond. The check verifies the cancellation as an identity of rational functions and the signs at forty rational points.

\emph{(ii).} With $r \geq 0$, $u = t - r = 1$ is $r = t - 1$, $t \geq 1$, the future light cone of the event $(t, r) = (1, 0)$, that is of $e_{1}$; $u = -1$ is the future light cone of $-e_{1}$; $v = 1$ is the past light cone of $e_{1}$; $v = -1$ the past light cone of $-e_{1}$. So $\{|u| = 1\}\cup\{|v| = 1\}$ is the union of the light cones of $\pm e_{1}$, which is $\{(t - 1)^{2} = r^{2}\}\cup\{(t + 1)^{2} = r^{2}\}$ (checked on a grid of rational points). The causal diamond of the unit ball $\{t = 0, r \leq 1\}$ is $\{u \geq -1,\ v \leq 1\}$, with tips $\pm e_{1}$; its past null boundary $u = -1$ is where $\Omega_{x}$ is maximal, its future null boundary $v = 1$ where $\Psi_{x}$ is maximal. On $t = 0$, $u = -r$ and $v = r$, so both entropies are $s(r)$, and (i) gives the monotonicity.

\emph{(iii).} A boost of the $(t, r)$ plane by rapidity $\eta$ sends $(u, v) \mapsto (e^{-\eta}u, e^{\eta}v)$, and a dilation by $\lambda$ sends $(u, v) \mapsto (\lambda u, \lambda v)$; neither preserves $\{|u| = 1\}$ unless $\eta = 0$, $\lambda = 1$. In the Hermitian structure of the theorem, $g^{*} = g^{-1}$ on generators, so $e_{1}^{*} = e_{1}$, $e_{2}^{*} = -e_{2}$, $e_{\pm}^{*} = \pm e_{\pm}$; hence $(e_{1}e_{2})^{*} = e_{2}^{*}e_{1}^{*} = -e_{2}e_{1} = e_{1}e_{2}$ and likewise $(e_{+}e_{-})^{*} = e_{+}e_{-}$. Both square to one, so $\exp(\sigma g) = \cosh\sigma + g\sinh\sigma$ is self-adjoint and $\exp(\sigma g)^{*}\exp(\sigma g) = \exp(2\sigma g) \neq 1$ for $\sigma \neq 0$: neither group is unitary, as Theorem \claimref{synthesis.incidence-vacua-thermofield-real-capacity}(iv) records for dilations. The check verifies the adjoints, the squares, and the non-unitarity at a rational point of the hyperbola. So the maximal set, and with it the diamond's radius and rest frame, are fixed by the Hermitian structure; and by (i) the profile is a function of $u$ and $v$ alone.
