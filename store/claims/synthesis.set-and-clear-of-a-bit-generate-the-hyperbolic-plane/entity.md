## formal
Let $R$ be a commutative ring. A \emph{bit over $R$} is the free module $M = R\,s_{\mathrm{off}} \oplus R\,s_{\mathrm{on}}$ on two states; \emph{set} is the $R$-linear map $v$ with $v\,s_{\mathrm{off}} = s_{\mathrm{on}}$, $v\,s_{\mathrm{on}} = 0$, and \emph{clear} the $R$-linear map $w$ with $w\,s_{\mathrm{on}} = s_{\mathrm{off}}$, $w\,s_{\mathrm{off}} = 0$. A \emph{switch} in an associative unital ring is a pair $(a, b)$ with $a^{2} = b^{2} = 0$ and $ab + ba = 1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $(v, w)$ is a switch. The products $vw$ and $wv$ are idempotents with $vw + wv = 1$ and $vw \cdot wv = wv \cdot vw = 0$; $vw$ fixes $s_{\mathrm{on}}$ and annihilates $s_{\mathrm{off}}$ (the test ``is on''), and $wv$ the reverse.
\item[(ii)] The $R$-algebra generated by $v$ and $w$ is $\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)$, free with basis $1, v, w, vw$.
\item[(iii)] For $x = \alpha v + \beta w$ with $\alpha, \beta \in R$, $x^{2} = \alpha\beta$. With $L = Rv \oplus Rw$ and $Q(x) = \alpha\beta$ (the hyperbolic quadratic module): for every associative unital $R$-algebra $B$ and $R$-linear $\phi : L \to B$ with $\phi(x)^{2} = Q(x)\,1$ there is exactly one unital $R$-algebra homomorphism $\Phi : \mathrm{End}_{R}(M) \to B$ extending $\phi$. So $\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)$ is the Clifford algebra of the hyperbolic plane over every $R$, with no hypothesis on $2$.
\item[(iv)] $e_{+} = v + w$ and $e_{-} = v - w$ satisfy $e_{+}^{2} = 1$, $e_{-}^{2} = -1$, $e_{+}e_{-} = -e_{-}e_{+} = wv - vw$; $e_{+}$ exchanges the two states and $e_{+}e_{-}$ is $+1$ on $s_{\mathrm{off}}$ and $-1$ on $s_{\mathrm{on}}$.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
Take a two-state system and only the two operations of setting it and clearing it. They square to zero, their anticommutator is the identity, and their two products are the tests for on and for off: the relations of one fermionic mode, with no sign and no fraction put in. They generate every linear operation on the two states, over any commutative ring of coefficients, and the square of a combination $\alpha v + \beta w$ is $\alpha\beta$, the hyperbolic form; the algebra has the universal property of the Clifford algebra of that form. The sum and difference of set and clear are two anticommuting directions of squares $+1$ and $-1$, so the orthonormal description of the hyperbolic plane is obtained from the switch, where the usual construction obtains the switch from the orthonormal pair with a factor one half.

## lay
Think of a light switch with two operations: switch on and switch off. Switching on something that is already on does nothing useful, and at any moment the light is either on or off. Written as algebra, those two facts are exactly the rules physicists use for a single particle of matter. From the two operations alone, by adding and subtracting them, one gets two directions, one that squares to plus one and one that squares to minus one, which is the pair of directions the series adds at each step. No halves are needed along the way.

## one-liner
Over any commutative ring, set $v$ and clear $w$ on a two-state module satisfy $v^{2} = w^{2} = 0$, $vw + wv = 1$, generate $\mathrm{End}(M)$ with $(\alpha v + \beta w)^{2} = \alpha\beta$ (the Clifford algebra of the hyperbolic plane, no $\tfrac12$ needed), and $v \pm w$ are orthonormal of squares $\pm 1$.
