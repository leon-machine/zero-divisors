## formal
Let $V = F \otimes \Lambda^{\bullet}W$, $F = \Lambda^{\bullet}D$, be the composite space of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.joint-clifford-clause-is-a-klein-convention-on-the-composite-space} with its Fock inner product, $\tilde a_{1}, \tilde a_{2}$ the Klein-twisted slot annihilators, $a_{1}, a_{2}$ the untwisted ones and $b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}$ the pair annihilators. For five operators $\alpha_{1}, \dots, \alpha_{5}$ on $V$ with adjoints $\alpha_{k}^{*}$ put $\gamma_{2k-1} = \alpha_{k} + \alpha_{k}^{*}$, $\gamma_{2k} = \alpha_{k} - \alpha_{k}^{*}$, and let $G$ be the group generated by $\gamma_{1}, \dots, \gamma_{10}$ and $-1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The clause is the postulates; proved.)} The $\alpha_{k}$ satisfy the canonical anticommutation relations of one system of five modes exactly when $\gamma_{1}, \dots, \gamma_{10}$ with $c = -1$ satisfy Premise (F2), in the form of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra}, with $\gamma_{2k-1}^{2} = 1$, $\gamma_{2k}^{2} = c$; then $|G| = 2^{11}$, the $2^{10}$ blades $\gamma_{S}$ are trace-orthogonal on $V$, $c$ acts as $-1$ (Premise (F3)), and $\Cl_{\Z}(5,5)$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra} acts faithfully on $V$. For $(\alpha_{k}) = (\tilde a_{1}, \tilde a_{2}, b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3})$ both hold. The sign bits of the slot and the pair systems are both the operator $-1$ of $V$, so beyond the two separate systems the clause adds only (F2c) for the $24$ slot-pair pairs: clause (f) says exactly that the ten slot and pair Majorana directions are distinct directions of one group of signed directions whose sign bit acts as $-1$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(The signature; proved.)} (F2) allows every signature; for Majoranas of modes it is forced, one direction of square $1$ and one of square $c$ per mode, so $(5,5)$. Its Gauss sum $\sum_{S}\gamma_{S}^{2}$ is $2^{5}$, as for $(10,0)$, so $\Cl_{\Z}(5,5) \cong \Cl_{\Z}(10,0)$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra}(viii)), the class of the $\Cl(10)$ of clause (f).
\item[(iii)] \emph{(The adjoint; proved.)} On $V$, $g^{*} = g^{-1}$ for every $g \in G$, so on the image of $\Z[G]/(c + 1)$ the adjoint is the anti-involution induced by $g \mapsto g^{-1}$; and the commutant of $G$ on the rational span of the occupation basis is the scalars, so the $G$-invariant symmetric form is the Fock form up to a scalar. The adjoint in the CAR is the one the group fixes.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(What the clause chooses; proved.)} The untwisted family ($a_{1}, a_{2}$ with the $b_{p}$) generates the same group $G$, with the same squares, since the pair parity is $-\gamma_{5}\gamma_{6}\cdots\gamma_{10} \in G$ and the untwisted slot Majoranas are the twisted ones times it; but its $24$ slot-pair pairs commute, so it is not a family of signed directions. The content of clause (f) beyond (F2) and (F3) is the choice of the ten directions inside $G$, canonical on $V$ up to a central unit (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.joint-clifford-clause-is-a-klein-convention-on-the-composite-space}(iv)).
\item[(v)] \emph{(Computed.)} In the occupation basis of $\Lambda^{\bullet}\C^{5}$: $|G| = 2048$, $-1$ central, all $90$ ordered pairs of distinct twisted directions anticommuting, squares $1, -1$ alternating, the $1023$ nonempty blades traceless and the $1024$ blades distinct up to sign; $\tfrac12(\gamma_{2k-1} \pm \gamma_{2k})$ equal to the Fock operators, adjoint to each other, with the $50$ relations of the CAR; $g^{\mathsf T} = g^{-1}$ on all $2048$ elements; commutant of dimension $1$ (and $2$ for nine directions); the untwisted family generating $G$ with all $24$ cross pairs commuting; blade squares equal to GA's for the sign vector $(1, -1, \dots)$, Gauss sum $32$, against $32$ for $(10,0)$ and $-32$ for $(0,10)$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Reading.} Once the ten Klein-twisted slot and pair Majoranas are declared the directions (the postulates do not choose them, (iv)), clause (f) is the instance $n = 10$ of Premises (F2) and (F3); Premise (F1) enters only as the index set, the configurations $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{10} = G/\langle c\rangle$ of the ten direction labels, not the $2^{5}$ occupation configurations of $V$, and reading these labels as the foundation's distinctions is a reading, not a theorem; it asks for no adjoint and no signature beyond what the modes and the group supply. Together with the Klein-convention result, the clause has no content on $V$ beyond a choice of directions fixed up to a central unit.

## technical
Clause (f) of the slot-module premise asks that the slot and pair operators form one system of five fermionic modes, the slot operators anticommuting with the pair operators. Written in their ten real Majorana combinations, the anticommutation relations of five modes are exactly the relations of a group of signed directions, the foundation's minimal non-commutativity premise for ten directions, with the sign bit acting as minus one: distinct directions anticommute, and each mode supplies one direction squaring to one and one squaring to the sign. The group has order $2^{11}$ and its blades are trace-orthogonal, so the integer Clifford algebra of signature $(5,5)$ acts faithfully, and its Gauss sum puts it in the class of $(10,0)$. Neither an adjoint nor a signature is extra: the Fock adjoint is the group inverse, the invariant form is unique up to scale, and the signature comes from the modes. The untwisted slot operators generate the same group but commute with the pairs, so the clause's only content is which ten elements of the group are the directions, which the Klein convention fixes up to a central unit.

## lay
One assumption of the series about how a composite particle's internal states are built says that two families of internal switches, the slot and pair switches, behave as one combined set of fermionic switches. The series' foundation starts from even simpler assumptions: independent yes-or-no distinctions, which become directions whose order matters by a single sign. This result shows that, once ten particular combinations of the switches are chosen as the directions, the assumption about the switches is exactly the foundation's assumption about directions applied to those ten, and nothing more: the rule for adjoints and the pattern of squares that the assumption seems to need are already supplied by the directions themselves. What the assumption fixes is only which ten elements of a given group are called directions, a choice made canonical earlier.

## one-liner
On the composite space, the CAR of the five slot and pair modes are exactly Premise (F2) for their ten Majoranas with the sign bit $-1$ (order $2^{11}$, signature $(5,5)$, Gauss sum $2^{5}$ as for $(10,0)$); the Fock adjoint is the group inverse and the invariant form unique; the untwisted operators generate the same group but are not signed directions: once the twisted Majoranas are declared the directions (a choice the postulates do not make), clause (f) is an instance of (F2) and (F3), (F1) entering only as the index set.
