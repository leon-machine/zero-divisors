## formal
Work on flat spacetime, the vectors of $\Cl(1,3)$ with their fixed product, with gravity the pair of gauge fields $(\hb, \Omega)$ (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}), and in the conformal model of the stored positivity result (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.positive-conformal-energy-gives-positive-null-translations}): $\Cl(2,4)$ with orthonormal $e_{0}$ ($e_{0}^{2} = 1$), $e_{1}, e_{2}, e_{3}$ (square $-1$), $\nu$ ($\nu^{2} = 1$), $e_{-}$ ($e_{-}^{2} = -1$); $\nInf = \nu + e_{-}$, $\nO = \tfrac12(\nu - e_{-})$, $X(x) = \nO + x - \tfrac12 Q(x)\nInf$, $P_{a} = \tfrac12 a\nInf$, $Z = e_{0}\nu$. Let $\Lambda = 3H^{2} > 0$, $I = \nInf + \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\nO$, $\sigma(x) = \ip{X(x)}{I} = 1 - \Lambda Q(x)/12$, and take the de Sitter gravitational field of Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point}: $\hb(a) = \sigma a$, $\Omega(a) = -(a \wedge \nabla\sigma)/\sigma$, on the invertible region $\sigma > 0$ (derived metric $\sigma^{-2}\eta$, a reading). Its physical vectors are $\hb^{-1}(a) = a/\sigma$, so a background vector is physically null exactly when $Q(a) = 0$ (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(i)). Let $n$ be a future null vector, $m$ null with $\ip{n}{m} = 1$, and $x_{\perp}$ the part of $x$ orthogonal to $n$ and $m$, with $|x_{\perp}|^{2} = -Q(x_{\perp})$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The horizon.)} The background null plane $N_{n} = \{x : \ip{x}{n} = 0\} = \{x_{\perp} + s\,n\}$ has physically null normal, and on it $\sigma = 1 + \tfrac{\Lambda}{12}|x_{\perp}|^{2} > 0$, constant along each background line $s \mapsto x_{\perp} + s\,n$. The background boost $n \wedge m$ commutes with $I$, so it fixes $\sigma$ and is a symmetry of the gravitational field (a displacement with its induced gauge transformation under which $(\hb, \Omega)$ is unchanged). It rescales $n$ and fixes the transverse directions, so $N_{n}$ is a Killing horizon of that symmetry in the sense of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(vi): one sheet of a bifurcate horizon, the future horizon of one static patch, the edge $\{s = 0\}$ and the past horizon of the opposite patch.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(The physical affine parameter.)} Along each line $x_{\perp} + s\,n$, $\Omega(n)\cdot n = -\,n\,(n \cdot \nabla\sigma)/\sigma = 0$, so the physical tangent $v = \hb^{-1}(x')$ of every constant-speed parametrisation satisfies $D_{x'}v = 0$: the lines are free-fall null worldlines (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(iii)), and their physical affine parameters are the affine functions of $s$. The parameter $\lambda = s/\sigma(x_{\perp})$ is the one whose physical tangent $\hb^{-1}(dx/d\lambda)$ is the background vector $n$ on every generator. With $W = \nInf - \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\nO$ and $e_{A}$ the transverse frame, the rotors of $\mathrm{span}\{e_{A}, W\}$ commute with $I$ and with $n \wedge m$ (the rotations of that static patch, symmetries of the gravitational field); they fix $n$, carry generators of $N_{n}$ to generators and its edge to itself, and preserve $\lambda$. So the uniform affine translations of the horizon are $\lambda \mapsto \lambda + a$ on every generator.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(A background conformal null rotation.)} $nI = n \wedge I$ is nilpotent, and the $\Cl(2,4)$ rotor $T_{a} = 1 + \tfrac{a}{2}\,nI$ maps $X(x_{\perp} + s\,n)$ to a multiple of $X(x_{\perp} + (s + a\,\sigma(x_{\perp}))\,n)$: it is the uniform translation $\lambda \mapsto \lambda + a$. Its generator is
\[
\tfrac12\,nI \;=\; P_{n} + \frac{\Lambda}{12}\,n\nO ,
\]
a background null translation plus a background null special conformal transformation. It fixes $n$ and moves $I$, so it is not a symmetry of the gravitational field and does not preserve its invertible region (at $\Lambda = 3$, $a = \tfrac34$ it carries $x = e_{0}$, where $\sigma = \tfrac34$, to a point where $\sigma = 0$ and $\hb$ is not invertible); it maps $N_{n}$ to itself and preserves $\sigma$ there. The null translation $P_{n}$ alone shifts $\lambda$ by $a/\sigma(x_{\perp})$, which is not uniform.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Positivity.)} Let $\pi$ be a continuous unitary representation of $\mathrm{Spin}^{+}(2,4)$ and $C_{\pi}$ its cone of nonnegative generators, as in the stored result, with $Z \in C_{\pi}$. Then $e_{0}\nO \in C_{\pi}$, the limit as $\rho \to 0$ of $\rho^{2}\mathrm{Ad}_{D_{\rho}}Z = \rho^{4}P_{e_{0}} + e_{0}\nO$; $n\nO \in C_{\pi}$, the limit of $2\rho^{-2}\mathrm{Ad}_{B_{\rho}}(e_{0}\nO) = (e_{0} + e_{1})\nO + \rho^{-4}(e_{0} - e_{1})\nO$ followed by a rotation; and $P_{n} \in C_{\pi}$ by the stored result. So $\tfrac12 nI \in C_{\pi}$: the uniform affine translations along the generators of $N_{n}$ have a nonnegative generator. The cone is invariant and the symmetries of the gravitational field act transitively on its static patches, so the same holds for every cosmological horizon. In particular, at $\Lambda = 3$ the rotor $1 - e_{1}e_{W}$, $e_{W} = W/H$, fixes $I$ and carries $n = e_{0} + e_{1}$ to $2v_{\Lambda}^{*}$, the null vector of the static patch of the observer at $x = 0$ (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.cosmological-horizon-completed-pair}).
\item[(v)] \emph{(What it gives for (N$_{+}$).)} If moreover the GNS vector is invariant under $\exp(t\cdot\tfrac12 nI)$ (true for a conformally invariant vacuum, and implied by invariance under the translations by the Howe-Moore theorem, cited from memory), the state is a ground state for the uniform affine translations along the generators of every de Sitter cosmological horizon. With the horizon's modes read as those of a conformally invariant field coupled to $(\hb, \Omega)$, which for $\hb = \sigma$ differ from the field's modes on the vanishing gravitational field by a power of $\sigma$, constant on each generator (argued, not derived; conformally invariant fields only: the conformally coupled massless scalar, massless spinors and the Maxwell field, not the minimally coupled massless scalar), this is the positivity and invariance clause of Premise (N$_{+}$) in its case (b), from nonnegative conformal energy. (N$_{+}$) does not fix the normalisation of $u$ on each generator: for $u = s$ the uniform translation is $P_{n}$, nonnegative by the stored result; $\lambda$ is the normalisation the static patch selects; both are nonnegative.
\item[(vi)] \emph{(The Schwarzschild field admits no such gauge.)} The argument uses a gauge in which the gravitational field is a pure dilation of the background, $\hb(a) = \sigma a$: there the Weyl part of the field strength vanishes for every $\sigma$ (for de Sitter, $\mathcal{R}(B) = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}B$ is pure trace, computed; for general $\sigma$ standard, cited from memory), so the $\Cl(2,4)$ tractor gauge field, whose field strength on an Einstein field, where the Cotton term of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.gtg-tractor-gauge-field-reproduces-tractor-connection}(iv) vanishes, is minus that Weyl part, is flat, and background $\Cl(2,4)$ rotors act on the field's null structure. The Schwarzschild field has $\mathcal{R}(B) = -\tfrac{M}{r^{3}}S(B)$ with $S = 1 - 3\Pi$ (Proposition \claimref{singularity.curvature-gauge-field-exact-shape-decoupling}), whose Ricci contraction vanishes, so its Weyl part is $\mathcal{R}$ itself, nonzero at every $r > 0$. The Weyl part transforms covariantly under both gauge freedoms, so no gauge brings the Schwarzschild field to a pure dilation, and the argument of (iii) has no counterpart at its horizon.
\end{enumerate}
Beyond one flat chart: in the conformal compactification the null ray of $n$, with $\ip{n}{I} = 0$, is a point of the de Sitter field's conformal boundary and $N_{n}$ is its light cone; the generator through $\nInf$ lies outside the chart, and the transitivity over all static patches uses the compactified model.

## technical
The stored positivity result shows that nonnegative conformal energy forces a nonnegative generator for every null translation of flat spacetime with vanishing gravitational field, and so supplies the ground-state condition (N$_{+}$) on the local Rindler horizons there. It said the horizons of a gravitational field of nonvanishing field strength are not reached. For the de Sitter field that is too pessimistic. In Gauge Theory Gravity the background is flat, and the store writes de Sitter as the gravitational field $\hb = \sigma$, a pure dilation of the background with $\sigma = 1 - \Lambda Q(x)/12$ the scale of the frozen point. Physical null directions are then the background null directions.

A background null plane $x \cdot n = 0$ is a sheet of the Killing horizon of a boost that is a symmetry of the gravitational field, and $\sigma$ is constant along each of its lines. Those lines are free-fall null worldlines, and the physical affine parameter whose physical tangent is $n$ on every line is $\lambda = s/\sigma$; the rotations of the static patch preserve it. Shifting $\lambda$ by the same amount on every line is not a background translation, since a translation shifts $\lambda$ by $a/\sigma$, but it is a background conformal transformation of $\Cl(2,4)$: the null rotation generated by $\tfrac12 n \wedge I$, a null translation plus a null special conformal transformation. The dilation and boost limits that put the null translations in the cone of nonnegative generators also put the null special conformal transformations there, so this generator is nonnegative too.

For a conformally invariant field in its conformal vacuum the cosmological horizon's modes are therefore in a ground state for the uniform affine translations along its generators: the positivity and invariance half of (N$_{+}$) for the de Sitter field follows from nonnegative conformal energy. The identification of the horizon's modes with the field on the null plane is argued, not derived. The Schwarzschild field stays outside, and the reason is a property of the gauge field, not of a curved background: its field strength is pure Weyl and nonzero, a gauge-covariant fact, so no gauge makes it a pure dilation, and the tractor gauge field, whose field strength is minus the Weyl part, is not flat. Reading $n$ as a point of de Sitter's conformal boundary and $N_{n}$ as its light cone needs the compactification, beyond one flat chart. The content is standard for conformal fields; the store's gain is the form in $\Cl(2,4)$ and the correction to the stored scope.

## lay
In this series spacetime is flat everywhere, and gravity is a pair of fields living on it that change how lengths and directions are read. A universe driven by a cosmological constant is, in this language, the simplest such field: it rescales every length at a point by the same factor and leaves the directions of light alone. Such a universe has a horizon, light rays that an observer can never quite receive, and the series needs the quantum fields there to have no negative energy along those rays. An earlier result got that for flat spacetime with no gravity from one positivity requirement, the energy that goes with the symmetries of light cones, and said it did not reach the cosmological horizon. It does. Because the field only rescales lengths, the horizon is an ordinary sheet of light rays of the flat background, and sliding everything along those rays by the same amount is one of the symmetries of light cones, so the same requirement covers it. This holds for massless fields of the kind unchanged by rescaling distances. It does not reach black holes: a black hole's gravitational field also stretches and squeezes by different amounts in different directions, a tidal effect no change of description removes.

## one-liner
On the de Sitter gravitational field $\hb = \sigma$ on flat spacetime a cosmological horizon is a background null plane whose lines are free-fall null worldlines with $\sigma$ constant along them, and the uniform shift of the physical affine parameter $s/\sigma$ is the background conformal null rotation generated by $\tfrac12 nI = P_{n} + \tfrac{\Lambda}{12}n\nO$, which the stored dilation and boost limits place in the cone of nonnegative generators; so nonnegative conformal energy gives the positivity half of (N$_{+}$) on every de Sitter cosmological horizon for a conformally invariant field in its conformal vacuum (argued identification of the horizon modes), while the Schwarzschild field, whose field strength is pure nonzero Weyl, admits no gauge in which the argument runs.
