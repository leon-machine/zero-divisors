(1) $I$ commutes with every even element, in particular with itself and with scalars, so $\R \oplus \R I$ is a commutative subalgebra; $I$ is the pseudoscalar $I_4$ and $I^2 = -1$, so the subalgebra is $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_4\}$, the store's complex field (conventions, the unit principle U1), presented as $\R[X]/(X^2 + 1)$ by $X \mapsto I$; in it $z\tilde z = (x + Iy)(x - Iy) = x^2 + y^2 = |z|^2$ is real, so $z$ is invertible, with inverse $\tilde z/(x^2 + y^2)$, if and only if $x^2 + y^2 \neq 0$; and inverses computed in the subalgebra are inverses in $\Cl(1,3)$ because the subalgebra is unital in the ambient algebra. (2) The shape computation of Proposition \claimref{interior.exact-scale-decoupling} used only $\sigma_r^2 = 1$, the commutation of $I$ with even elements, and the (anti)commutation of the six frame planes with $\sigma_r$; all of it applies verbatim to the principal plane pair of Kerr (\claimref{interior.imported-kerr-curvature}), with the scalar prefactor now valued in $\R \oplus \R I$. The eigenvalues are $-2\mathfrak M$ on the plane pair and $\mathfrak M$ on the mixed planes, ratio $2 : (-1)$ as before. (3) By (1), $z = r - Ia\cos\theta$ fails to be invertible exactly where $r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta = 0$, which for real $r$, $\theta$ and $a \neq 0$ means $r = 0$ and $\cos\theta = 0$ simultaneously: the ring (at $a = 0$ the locus is $\{r = 0\}$, item (4)). On the disc interior $r = 0$, $\cos\theta \neq 0$, the base is $z = -Ia\cos\theta$ with $z^3 = I\,(a\cos\theta)^3$ and hence $z^{-3} = -I\,(a\cos\theta)^{-3}$, so the scale is $\mathfrak M = -M z^{-3} = M\,I\,(a\cos\theta)^{-3}$ (the script checks the exact value), finite and purely pseudoscalar: the curvature is finite there, and purely dual. (4) Set $a = 0$: $\mathfrak M = -M/r^3$ and $R(B) = -(M/r^3)\,S(B)$, which is Proposition \claimref{interior.exact-scale-decoupling}.
