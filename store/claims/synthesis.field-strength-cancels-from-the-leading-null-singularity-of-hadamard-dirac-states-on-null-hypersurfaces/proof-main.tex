\emph{Conventions.} Physical vectors are those of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(i), $g(a, b) = \hb^{-1}(a)\cdot\hb^{-1}(b)$, signature $(+,-,-,-)$; geodesics and parallel transport are as in (ii), (iii) there, and the field strength along $k$ enters the Jacobi equation through the tidal function $T(w) = -(\mathcal{R}(w\wedge k)\cdot k)_{\perp}$ on the screen plane (span of $f_{1}, f_{2}$) in the store's sign (iv), self-adjoint for the screen product $w\star w' = -w\cdot w'$ because the Levi-Civita field strength has the pair symmetry. $J$, $A$, $B$, $T$ are linear functions on the screen plane (or from label displacements to it), identified along the generator by the parallel frame; $\bar X$ is the adjoint for $\star$, and $X^{T}$ in displayed formulas is the matrix reading of $\bar X$ in the frame $\{f_{1}, f_{2}\}$. The points lie in a geodesically convex neighbourhood of the cut, so $\sigma$, $\Delta$ and the connecting geodesics are smooth; boundary values carry the $I_{4}0$ of a time function (an infinitesimal shift of the spacetime separation, written in scalar form as a shift of $\sigma$ by a number: the square of a shifted vector is a number, Theorem \claimref{std-algebra.complexification-is-the-odd-extension}(iii); every value, the null-vacuum kernel and the logarithms included, is a number read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, with the field theory's unit $I_{4}$), and the independence of the bulk distributions from the choice of time function is Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-hadamard-parametrix-of-the-squared-dirac-operator-leads-with-van-vleck-root-times-spin-transport}(c) (Kay and Wald 1991; Sahlmann and Verch 2001, Sec.~5.1); the $\epsilon$-regularised restriction to $\mathcal{H}$ used in (ii) depends on the time function by a smooth kernel, so it is defined modulo smooth kernels (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.restricted-dirac-hadamard-parametrix-on-a-null-hypersurface-is-the-null-vacuum-plus-a-bounded-kernel}(ii)). A rotation gauge transformation (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}) makes the parallel frame along one generator the constant background frame, $k = e_{0} + e_{3}$, $n = \tfrac12(e_{0} - e_{3})$, $f_{a} = e_{a}$, with $\Omega(k) = 0$ on that generator; spinors transform by the rotor and the Hadamard form is covariant, so the computations of Steps 4 and 5 are made in that gauge.

\emph{Step 1 (deviation vectors).} $E_{a} = \partial_{y_{a}}X$ is a Jacobi field along each generator. It is orthogonal to $k$: $\partial_{u}(k\cdot E_{a}) = k\cdot D_{k}E_{a} = k\cdot D_{E_{a}}k = \tfrac12\partial_{a}(k\cdot k) = 0$, and $k\cdot E_{a} = 0$ where $\mathcal{H}$ is null. Its component along $k$ is affine in $u$ and drops out of everything below; its transverse components form $J$, with $J'' = -TJ$, and $D_{E}k = B(E)$ with $B = J'J^{-1}$. $B$ is symmetric: it is the null second fundamental form of $\mathcal{H}$, $f_{a}\cdot D_{f_{b}}k$, symmetric because $k$ is normal to $\mathcal{H}$ (twist-free).

\emph{Step 2 (Hessian of $-\sigma$ on $\mathcal{H}$; proved).} Fix $x' = X(u', y')$ and put $c(t) = X(u, y' + t\eta)$, so $\dot c(0) = E(\eta) = E_{a}\eta^{a}$. On the generator $\sigma(X(u, y'), x') = 0$ and $\nabla\sigma(X(u, y'), x') = (u - u')k$, the tangent of the connecting geodesic scaled by its parameter length. First variation: $\tfrac{d}{dt}\sigma = (u - u')\,k\cdot E(\eta) = 0$. Second variation:
\[
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\sigma\Big|_{0} = \nabla\nabla\sigma(E, E) + (u - u')\,k\cdot D_{E}E,
\qquad k\cdot D_{E}E = -E\cdot D_{E}k = -E\cdot B(E),
\]
the last from $E(k\cdot E) = 0$ along $\mathcal{H}$. For a Jacobi field $Y$ along the generator with $Y(u') = 0$, $\nabla\sigma$ at the varied endpoint is $(u - u')$ times the varied tangent, so $\nabla\nabla\sigma(Y(u), \cdot) = (u - u')D_{k}Y(u)$; with $Y = A(\cdot, u')w$ and $Y(u) = J(u)\eta$, $w = A^{-1}J\eta$, this gives $\nabla\nabla\sigma(E, E) = -(u - u')\eta^{T}J^{T}A'A^{-1}J\eta$. Hence $\tfrac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\sigma = -(u - u')\eta^{T}J^{T}(A' - BA)A^{-1}J\eta$. The Wronskian $W(X, Y) = X^{T}Y' - X'^{T}Y$ of two solutions of $Z'' = -TZ$ is constant ($T$ symmetric); with $X = J$, $Y = A(\cdot, u')$ it is $J(u')^{T}$ at $u'$, so $J(u)^{T}A'(u, u') - J(u)^{T}B(u)A(u, u') = J(u')^{T}$ ($J'^{T} = J^{T}B$). Therefore $J^{T}(A' - BA) = J(u')^{T}$ and
\[
\sigma(x, x') = -\tfrac12\eta^{T}M\eta + O(|\eta|^{3}),\qquad M = (u - u')J(u')^{T}A(u, u')^{-1}J(u).
\]
On the flat field $T = 0$, $B = 0$, $J = 1$, $A = (u - u')1$ and $M = 1$: $\sigma = -\tfrac12|\eta|^{2}$ on a null plane.

\emph{Step 3 (Van Vleck on the generator; proved).} $\Delta = -\det(-\nabla\nabla'\sigma)/\sqrt{g\,g'}$, normalised to $1$ at coincidence. Fix $x = X(u, y')$ and vary $x'$ by $w$: the Jacobi field $Y$ with $Y(u) = 0$, $Y(u') = w$ gives $\nabla\nabla'\sigma(\cdot, w) = (u - u')D_{k}Y(u)$. In the parallel null frame the Jacobi equation is block-triangular: the $n$-coefficient $k\cdot Y$ obeys $(k\cdot Y)'' = 0$; the transverse part obeys $Y_{\perp}'' = -TY_{\perp}$ plus a source proportional to $k\cdot Y$; the coefficient $n\cdot Y$ along $k$ is driven by the others and drives nothing, since the field strength annihilates $k\wedge k$. The determinant is the product of the diagonal blocks. The two longitudinal blocks are curvature-free and give the flat value; the transverse block maps $w$ to $(u - u')\tilde A(u', u)^{-1}w$, $\tilde A(\cdot, u)$ the Jacobi map based at $u$, and $\det\tilde A(u', u) = \det A(u, u')$ (from the constant Wronskian of $A(\cdot, u')$ and $\tilde A(\cdot, u)$, $\tilde A(u', u) = -A(u, u')^{T}$, and $\det(-X) = \det X$ for $2\times 2$ matrices). So $\Delta = (u - u')^{2}/\det A(u, u')$, which is $1$ on the flat field.

\emph{Step 4 (the identity; proved).} $\det M = (u - u')^{2}\det J(u')\det J(u)/\det A$, so $\Delta^{1/2}/\sqrt{\det M} = (|\det J(u)||\det J(u')|)^{-1/2} = (a(u)a(u'))^{-1/2}$. Neither $T$, nor $B$, nor $u - u'$ survives. This is (i).

\emph{Step 5 (the leading restricted kernel; computed at leading order).} By Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-dirac-hadamard-form-propagates-and-adiabatic-states-are-hadamard}(a) the two-point functions of $\omega$ near $\mathcal{H}$ are $D_{x}G$ (with the mass term) plus a smooth kernel, $G = U/\sigma + V\log\sigma + W$ a Hadamard parametrix of the squared Dirac operator. That its leading coefficient is $U = \Delta^{1/2}R$, $R$ the parallel transport of the spin connection along the geodesic, is the standard Hadamard construction for normally hyperbolic operators on bundles, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-hadamard-parametrix-of-the-squared-dirac-operator-leads-with-van-vleck-root-times-spin-transport}(a), (b) (Friedlander 1975; B\"ar, Ginoux and Pf\"affle 2007, Ch.~2); the Hadamard-form Fact does not state it. The restricted kernel is computed here as the $\epsilon \to 0$ limit of the transverse-smeared, $\epsilon$-regularised parametrix; that this agrees with the restriction defined through the characteristic-data embedding of the generator field is not shown (a pullback is not available, since the wave-front set of the kernel meets the conormal of $\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}$ along the generators). In the gauge of the conventions, along the generator through $y'$, the canonical projector is $\Lambda = \tfrac12(1 + e_{0}e_{3}) = \tfrac12 nk$ and satisfies $\Lambda k = 0$, $\Lambda n = n$, $n\Lambda = 0$, $e_{0}\Lambda = \tfrac12 k$, $(1 - \Lambda)e_{0}\Lambda = \tfrac12 k$, $\Lambda e_{0}\Lambda = 0$, $\Lambda f_{a} = f_{a}\Lambda$ and $\Lambda\,p\,e_{0}\Lambda = (p\cdot k)\Lambda$ for every vector $p$ (computed; the last is the identity of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.bulk-hadamard-states-restrict-to-the-generator-condition-on-flat-null-planes}(ii)). So in $\Lambda(D_{x}G)e_{0}\Lambda'$, $G = U/\sigma + V\log\sigma + W$, writing the Dirac operator in the null frame, the derivative along $n$ is removed by $\Lambda k = 0$, the transverse derivatives meet $\Lambda G e_{0}\Lambda'$, which vanishes at coincident labels where $R = 1$ (its first-order part in $\eta$ does not vanish; see below), and the derivative along $k$ meets $(1 - \Lambda)Ge_{0}\Lambda' = \tfrac12 k\,G$ where $R = 1$, giving $\Lambda\,\partial_{k}G$: the flat statement that only $\ip{p}{k}$ survives. On the generator, $R$ is the spinor transport along it, $1$ in the parallel frame. The transverse integration that produces $\delta^{2}(y - y')$: with $\sigma = -\tfrac12\eta^{T}M\eta + O(|\eta|^{3})$ and the $I_{4}0$ of a time function $\tau$, $\sigma_{\epsilon} = \sigma + I_{4}\epsilon s$, $s = (u - u')\,d\tau(k) + O(\eta)$, the substitution $\zeta = L\eta$, $L^{T}L = M$, gives
\[
\int\frac{f(\eta)\,d^{2}\eta}{-\tfrac12\eta^{T}M\eta + I_{4}\epsilon s} = \frac{2\pi f(0)}{\sqrt{\det M}}\bigl(\log\epsilon + \log(-I_{4}s)\bigr) + (\text{terms regular as } \epsilon \to 0 \text{ or carrying } f - f(0)),
\]
so the coefficient of $\delta^{2}(y - y')\log(-I_{4}(u - u'))$ in the restriction of $U/\sigma$ is $2\pi\Delta^{1/2}/\sqrt{\det M}$, and $\partial_{u}$ of $\log(-I_{4}(u - u'))$ is the only source of $(u - u' + I_{4}0)^{-1}$; the mass term carries no derivative along $k$ and contributes no such term. \emph{Log-order terms.} The derivative along $k$ also acts on the coefficient: with the canonical factor, $(a(u)a(u'))^{1/2}\partial_{u}\bigl[2\pi(a(u)a(u'))^{-1/2}(\log\epsilon + \log(-I_{4}s))\bigr] = 2\pi(u - u' + I_{4}0)^{-1} - \pi\theta(u)(\log\epsilon + \log(-I_{4}s))$, $\theta = a'/a = \mathrm{tr}\,B$ the expansion, a $\delta^{2}(y - y')$ term of log order that is nonzero wherever $\theta \neq 0$ (in the exact models, at every sample with $u \neq 0$). The transverse part of the projected Dirac operator contributes at the same (log) order through the spin connection $\tfrac12 f^{a}\Omega(f_{a})$ and through the first-order transport $R = 1 + O(\eta)$ multiplied by the transverse derivatives of $1/\sigma$. For the connection term the projection is an exact Clifford identity: if $\Omega(f_{a})\cdot k = B(f_{a})$ (the parallel frame along the generators and $D_{E}k = B(E)$), then $\Lambda f^{a}\Omega(f_{a})e_{0}\Lambda = \mathrm{tr}(B)\Lambda = \theta\Lambda$, and the shear (the trace-free part of $B$) and every other bivector component of $\Omega(f_{a})$ ($f_{1}\wedge f_{2}$, $f_{a}\wedge k$, $k\wedge n$) drop out (computed). So the projected transverse terms carry the expansion only, the same structure as the $-\pi\theta\log$ term, consistent with $(\partial_{u} + \tfrac12\theta)a^{-1/2} = 0$; the bookkeeping of the first-order transport term is not done here, and the cancellation of the $\log\epsilon$ terms is argued, not computed. None of these terms has a derivative along $k$, so none produces $(u - u' + I_{4}0)^{-1}$: the coefficient asserted in (ii) is not affected; what they affect is whether the computation defines a finite restricted kernel, which is part of the argued bound of Step 7. On the flat field the same computation, with coefficient $2\pi$, yields $W_{0}\,\delta^{2}(y - y')\Lambda$ for the canonical field $\sqrt{2}\Lambda\psi$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.bulk-hadamard-states-restrict-to-the-generator-condition-on-flat-null-planes}(ii), whose momentum-space computation fixes the constant and the sign of $I_{4}0$). On $\mathcal{H}$ the coefficient is multiplied by $\Delta^{1/2}/\sqrt{\det M}$ and by the canonical factor $(a(u)a(u'))^{1/2}$ of $\psi_{\mathcal{H}}$, whose product is $1$ by Step 4, for all $u, u'$. This is (ii), at leading order: that no other term of the parametrix gives a $(u - u' + I_{4}0)^{-1}\delta^{2}$ term is the power counting of Step 7, argued. The canonical normalisation is the one the CAR fixes: the flux of the Dirac current $\psi e_{0}\tilde\psi$ through $\mathcal{H}$ is $(\psi e_{0}\tilde\psi)\cdot k = 2\langle(\Lambda\psi)^{\dagger}\Lambda\psi\rangle_{0}$, which depends on $\Lambda\psi$ only (computed), times the area element $a\,d^{2}y\,du$; so $\psi_{\mathcal{H}}$ smeared with $F$ in $L^{2}(du\,d^{2}y)\otimes V_{\Lambda}$ has CAR norm $\|F\|$, and, given the characteristic-data embedding, the restricted state exists for every state (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-dimock-dirac-car-algebra-on-a-gravitational-field}; the passage from characteristic data on $\mathcal{H}$ to a solution of finite norm is cited from memory).

\emph{Step 6 (state independence; proved, given the Facts and the characteristic-data embedding cited from memory).} By Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-microlocal-hadamard-condition}(b) the two-point functions of two Hadamard states differ by a smooth kernel $D$ on $U\times U$; since both are bisolutions, $D$ is a smooth bisolution. The restricted kernels are defined through the generator field $\psi_{\mathcal{H}}(F)$, $F \in L^{2}(du\,d^{2}y)\otimes V_{\Lambda}$: by the characteristic-data embedding (cited from memory), $\psi_{\mathcal{H}}(F) = \psi(\Phi_{F})$ with $\Phi_{F}$ the solution whose canonical data on $\mathcal{H}$ are $F$, of norm $\|F\|$, and the pairing of a solution with $\Phi_{F}$ is the flux integral over $\mathcal{H}$ of Step 5. The restricted kernel is linear in the state, so $W_{\mathcal{H},1} - W_{\mathcal{H},2}$ is the pairing of $D$ with $\Phi_{F}$ and $\Phi_{F'}$. Because $D$ is a smooth bisolution, that pairing is the flux integral of $D$ itself over $\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}$: $W_{\mathcal{H},1} - W_{\mathcal{H},2} = (a(u)a(u'))^{1/2}\,2\Lambda D e_{0}\Lambda'$ restricted to $\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}$, a genuine pullback of a smooth kernel, hence smooth. Smeared with compactly supported transverse profiles it is a smooth function $R(u, u')$, so $|\lambda R(u_{0} + \lambda x, u_{0} + \lambda x')| \le \lambda\sup|R|$ on compact sets: it scales to zero at rate $\lambda$ (computed for polynomial $R$), and, being smooth, it changes neither the generator microlocal condition nor, by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.microlocal-condition-on-a-generator-gives-the-local-null-ground-state}(i), (ii), the scaling limit. This is (iii).

\emph{Step 7 (what remains; the equivalence proved, the bound argued).} With $W_{\mathcal{H}} = W_{0}\otimes\id_{K} + R_{\omega}$, the leading term is invariant under $\lambda W(u_{0} + \lambda x, u_{0} + \lambda x')$ (weight $\tfrac12$ per leg; computed), so the scaling limit is $W_{0}\otimes\id_{K}$ exactly when $\lambda R_{\omega}(u_{0} + \lambda\cdot, u_{0} + \lambda\cdot) \to 0$, and by Step 6 this holds for all Hadamard states if it holds for one. This is the equivalence in (iv); it uses (iii) only. By Step 5, $R_{\omega}$ contains no multiple of $(u - u' + I_{4}0)^{-1}\delta^{2}(y - y')$ with a field-strength coefficient. \emph{Argued, not proved:} the remaining terms (the $O(|\eta|^{3})$ part of $\sigma$ and the $O(|\eta|)$ parts of $\Delta^{1/2}$ and of $R$ in the transverse integral, which give kernels homogeneous of degree $\ge -2$ in $\eta$ without a derivative along $k$, hence at most $\log|u - u'|$ in $u$; $V\log\sigma$ and its derivative, bounded times $\log$; the mass term, at most $\log|u - u'|$; the log-order expansion and connection terms of Step 5; the smooth part) are locally integrable in $(u, u')$ with at most logarithmic growth, and for such a kernel $r$, $\lambda\int|f(x)g(x')|\,|r(u_{0} + \lambda x, u_{0} + \lambda x')|\,dx\,dx' \le C\lambda(1 + |\log\lambda|) \to 0$. The $\log\epsilon$ terms of Step 5 are expected to cancel (consistency argument: the generator field is bounded, given the characteristic-data embedding, so the restricted kernel is finite; this controls the kernel defined through the embedding, not the regularised integral). A proof needs this power counting made uniform in compactly supported transverse profiles; it is the one ingredient (HS)(b) still lacks.

\emph{Step 8 (the exact model; computed).} The plane-wave field $\hb^{-1}(a) = a + \tfrac12 H(a\cdot k)k$, $k = e_{0} + e_{3}$, has $g(a, b) = a\cdot b + H(a\cdot k)(b\cdot k)$ (computed in the spacetime algebra); with $u = x\cdot k$, $v = x\cdot m$, $m = \tfrac12(e_{0} - e_{3})$, $x_{i} = -x\cdot e_{i}$ (coordinate reading) $g = 2\,du\,dv + H\,du^{2} - dx_{1}^{2} - dx_{2}^{2}$. Take $H = h_{1}x_{1}^{2} + h_{2}x_{2}^{2}$, $h_{i} = -c_{i}/(1 + u)^{2}$, $c = (2, 6)$: the tidal matrix along $\partial_{u}$ is $\mathrm{diag}(h_{1}, h_{2})$, with nonzero trace (Ricci) and trace-free (Weyl) parts, and $x'' = -hx$ has the rational solutions $(1 + u)^{r}$, $r(r - 1) = c_{i}$. The Synge function is $\sigma = (u - u')(v - v') - \tfrac{u - u'}{2}\sum_{i}[x_{i}(K_{i}'x_{i}' + A_{i}'w_{i}) - x_{i}'w_{i}]$, $w_{i} = (x_{i} - K_{i}x_{i}')/A_{i}$; it satisfies $g^{-1}(d\sigma, d\sigma) = 2\sigma$ in either point, is symmetric and tends to $\tfrac12 g(d, d)$ at coincidence (computed exactly with jets). $\mathcal{H} = \{v = \tfrac12\sum P_{i}x_{i}^{2}\}$, $P = J'J^{-1}$, $J(0) = 1$, $J'(0) = 0$, is null, generated by affine null geodesics, with $B = P$ zero at $u = 0$ and with nonzero expansion and shear elsewhere (computed). At sample points ($u > u'$ and $u < u'$, central and off-central generators) $\sigma$ vanishes on generators with no linear term, the Hessian is $M$ of Step 2, $J A' - J'A = J(u')$, $\Delta = (u - u')^{2}/\det A \ne 1$ (for example $139968/308693$ at $u = \tfrac12$, $u' = -\tfrac13$), and $\det M = \Delta\,a(u)a(u')$ exactly, with $\det M \ne 1$, $a \ne 1$. Second model (computed): the tidal function rotated off the label basis, $h = -\rho\,\mathrm{diag}(2, 6)\rho^{T}/(1 + u)^{2}$ with the rational rotation $\rho$ of cosine $\tfrac35$, and $J(u) = K(u, 0) + A(u, 0)B_{0}$ with $B_{0}$ self-adjoint and not commuting with $h$ (expansion and shear nonzero at $u = 0$ too): the closed form solves the eikonal equation and is symmetric, $\mathcal{H}$ is null, $J$ is not self-adjoint and does not commute with $T$, the Hessian is $(u - u')J(u')^{T}A^{-1}J(u)$ while the reordered forms $(u - u')J(u')^{T}J(u)A^{-1}$ and $(u - u')J(u)^{T}A^{-1}J(u)$ fail, $\Delta = (u - u')^{2}/\det A$ and $\det M = \Delta\,a(u)a(u')$. The projected connection identity of Step 5 is checked in the spacetime algebra for three expansion-shear functions and all other bivector components.
