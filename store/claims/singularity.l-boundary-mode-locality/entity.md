## formal
\emph{Premise (L$_{E}$), boundary-mode locality.} On every finite window of the boundary modes, $v_{1}, \dots, v_{n}$ satisfying the CAR, the internal dynamics $\alpha_{t}$ of the boundary modes commutes with the descent at all times:
\[
\alpha_{t}\circ\mathcal{E} \;=\; \mathcal{E}\circ\alpha_{t} \quad \text{for all } t, \qquad \mathcal{E} = E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{n}, \quad E_{k}(x) = N_{k}xN_{k} + (1 - N_{k})x(1 - N_{k}),
\]
$\mathcal{E}$ the descent of the window (Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases}).

\emph{The version 1 wording, equivalent.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boundary-mode-locality-is-descent-covariance}, on every finite window this holds if and only if the dynamics is generated by elements of the abelian algebra $\mathcal{D}$ that the $\sigma_{k} = 2N_{k} - 1$ generate, diagonal in the joint number basis: the internal dynamics is generated by the geometric data the framework supplies on $E$, the boost bivector field $x \mapsto \sigma_{r}(x)$ and its products. Cross-point products such as $\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}$ correlate phases without moving occupation; hopping $v_{j}^{*}v_{k} + v_{k}^{*}v_{j}$, pairing $v_{j}^{*}v_{k}^{*} + v_{k}v_{j}$ and every other generator with a component outside $\mathcal{D}$ are excluded by the one clause. Within the class of generators the framework supplies, the exclusion of hopping is a theorem (Theorem \claimref{singularity.class-relative-no-go-no-supplied}); what the premise adds is the exclusion of unsupplied generators, pairing terms and the possible differential dynamics, which Proposition \claimref{singularity.l-stabiliser-tangent} places in the tangent of the vacuum orbit and in its stabiliser.

\emph{What it forbids.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.number-diagonal-dynamics-spreads-nothing}, every dynamics the premise admits carries an evolved mode operator only to the modes directly coupled to it, and every local commutator and out-of-time-order correlator is almost periodic and bounded: the premise forbids exponential real-time commutator growth. The real-time spreading law $t_{*} = \kappa^{-1}\ln N$ of Proposition \claimref{scrambling.spreading-law-chaos-bound-saturated} is therefore not realised under it, and the scrambling time is a modular-time statement: the growth $e^{\kappa t}$ is a rate of the rotor flow, the KMS-strip continuation of the phase flow that is the windows' time evolution (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.phase-flow-is-time-rotor-flow-kms-continuation}). The premise is kept over fast real-time scrambling; the two cannot both hold.

## technical
Postulate: on every finite window of boundary modes, the internal dynamics commutes with the descent at all times. This is equivalent to the original wording, that the dynamics is generated by the geometric data on $E$, the boost bivector field and its products, all diagonal in the joint number basis, so that cross-point products correlate phases while hopping, pairing and any unsupplied generator are excluded by a single clause. Within the class of generators the framework supplies, the exclusion of hopping is a theorem; what the premise adds is the exclusion of unsupplied generators.

The premise has a cost: under it no operator spreads beyond the modes it is directly coupled to and nothing grows exponentially in real time, so the scrambling law $t_{*} = \kappa^{-1}\ln N$ has no real-time reading. The scrambling time survives as a statement in modular time, the imaginary-time continuation of the windows' evolution. The series keeps the premise and gives up fast real-time scrambling.

## lay
This assumption says that the quantum modes at a black hole's singularity evolve in a way that never disturbs what a measurement of their occupations would find: they change phases and build correlations, but never move particles between points or create them in pairs. Part of this is proved for the operators the framework supplies; ruling out any other operators is assumed. The assumption has a price: under it, information placed in one mode does not spread rapidly through the others in real time, so the fast scrambling often attributed to black holes holds here only as a statement about the modes' thermal clock, not about their actual evolution.

## one-liner
Postulate: on finite windows the boundary-mode dynamics commutes with the descent at all times (equivalently, it is number-diagonal), which forbids exponential real-time commutator growth, so the scrambling time is a modular-time statement.
