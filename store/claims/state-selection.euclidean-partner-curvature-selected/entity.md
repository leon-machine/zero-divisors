## formal
Let $\sigma_r$ be the unit boost bivector of the radial plane and $I$ the pseudoscalar. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(I\sigma_r)^{2} = -1$, and $\{\sigma_r,\, I\sigma_r\}$ is precisely the doubled eigenplane pair of the shape operator, $S(B) = -2B$ on it, of \cite[Thm.~6.1]{ButlerT5};
\item the rotor $R_\theta := \exp(\theta\, I\sigma_r/2) = \cos(\theta/2) + I\sigma_r \sin(\theta/2)$ satisfies $R_{2\pi} = -1$ and $R_{4\pi} = +1$, so the rotor has period $4\pi$ while its conjugation action $x \mapsto R_\theta x R_\theta^{-1}$ has period $2\pi$;
\item $R_\theta$ is the analytic continuation of the boost rotor $\exp(\lambda\sigma_r/2)$ under $\lambda \mapsto I_{4}\theta$, the unit of the continuation being $I_{4} = I$, which commutes with the even element $\sigma_r$, in the sense that both are one-parameter rotor flows in the same eigenplane pair, the hyperbolic one generated by the member with square $+1$ and the elliptic one by the member with square $-1$.
\item On spinors, each chiral half: with the continuation taken instead as $\lambda \mapsto I_{6}\theta$, its unit the conformal spinors' unit $I_{6} = I_{4}E$ (with $I_{4} = I$ and $E = e_{+}e_{-}$ the bivector of the added plane), the continued boost acts on the two chiral halves of the conformal spinors as $R_\theta$ and $R_{-\theta}$, the sense fixed by the chirality (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.wick-rotor-chiral-angular-rotor}(ii)).
\end{enumerate}
The two units act in different places, $I_{4}$ in (3) on the even elements of $\Cl(1,3)$ and $I_{6}$ in (4) on the conformal spinors, and are not identified: $I_{6}$ equals $I_{4}$ on the chiral half $E = +1$ and $-I_{4}$ on $E = -1$, which is the difference between $R_\theta$ and $R_{-\theta}$.

In its action on spinors, on each chiral half, the Euclidean rotation whose angle carries the periodicity is therefore not an imported device: it is the second member of the pair that Step 4b's curvature equation already selects, and it is distinguished from the boost by exactly the sign of its square, the same criterion \cite[Cor.~6.4]{ButlerT5} uses to pick out $\sigma_r$. On vectors, and for every holonomy of a real connection, it is not: there $R_\theta$ rotates along the bifurcation sphere, not around it, and the Euclidean rotation around the sphere is realised by no real rotor of $\Cl(1,3)$ (Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies}(3)).

## technical
With $\sigma_r$ the unit boost bivector of the radial plane and $I$ the pseudoscalar, $(I\sigma_r)^2 = I^2\sigma_r^2 = -1$, and $\{\sigma_r, I\sigma_r\}$ is exactly the doubled eigenplane pair of the shape operator, $S(B) = -2B$. The rotor $R_\theta = \exp(\theta I\sigma_r/2) = \cos(\theta/2) + I\sigma_r\sin(\theta/2)$ has $R_{2\pi} = -1$ and $R_{4\pi} = +1$, so the rotor has period $4\pi$ while its conjugation action has period $2\pi$.

$R_\theta$ is the continuation of the boost rotor $\exp(\lambda\sigma_r/2)$ under $\lambda \mapsto I_{4}\theta$, with the unit $I_{4} = I$ acting on the even elements, in the sense that both are one-parameter rotor flows in the same eigenplane pair, the hyperbolic one generated by the member of square $+1$ and the elliptic one by the member of square $-1$. With the continuation's unit taken instead as the conformal spinors' unit $I_{6} = I_{4}E$, a different unit, not identified with $I_{4}$, the continued boost acts on each chiral half of the conformal spinors as $R_{\pm\theta}$, the sense set by the chirality (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.wick-rotor-chiral-angular-rotor}). In that action on spinors the Euclidean rotation carrying the periodicity is the second member of the curvature-selected pair, distinguished from the boost by the sign of its square. On vectors it is not: $R_\theta$ rotates along the bifurcation sphere, not around it, and no real rotor of $\Cl(1,3)$ realises the Euclidean rotation there (Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies}(3)).

## lay
The curvature of a black hole picks out a pair of planes. One carries the boost of the horizon's time symmetry; this result shows that the other carries an ordinary rotation, which returns to itself after a full turn when acting on geometric quantities. Read as the imaginary-time partner of the boost, that rotation is the one whose period thermal arguments demand when it acts on spinors, the quantities that describe fermions, so there it is supplied by the curvature rather than brought in from outside. Acting on ordinary directions in spacetime it is not that rotation: it turns along the horizon sphere, not around it.

## one-liner
The dual member $I\sigma_r$ of the curvature-selected pair squares to $-1$ and generates a rotor of period $4\pi$ whose conjugation action has period $2\pi$, the elliptic partner of the boost $\sigma_r$ in the same eigenplane pair; it realises the Euclidean rotation on each chiral half of the conformal spinors, not on vectors.
