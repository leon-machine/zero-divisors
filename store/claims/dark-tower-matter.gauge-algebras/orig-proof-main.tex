\textit{(i) The two factors.}\enspace The constituent count is two (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.where-mass-begins}(i)) and the field is $\R$ (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.constituents-field}), so by U2 the slot group is $O(2)$ (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.ladder}). The pairs are real (Proposition \claimref{dark-tower-matter.pairs}(i)), and by U2 their group is the orthogonal group of the real pair space, again $O(2)$.

The norm clause of U2 matters here. Without it, the pair mixings preserving the canonical relations alone would be $GL(2,\R)$, acting by $B$ on the $w_{i}$ and by $B^{-T}$ on the $v_{i}$. That is the mechanism of Remark \ref{rem:kleinian}, where the sterile tower has no time direction and so no adjoint.



\emph{(i) The kernel.}  An automorphism of $\R^{2} \otimes \R^{2}$ respecting both factors is $A \otimes B$, and
\[
A \otimes B = 1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A = \lambda 1, \;\; B = \lambda^{-1}1,
\]
with $\lambda$ real and orthogonal, so $\lambda = \pm 1$. The kernel $\{(1,1), (-1,-1)\}$ is discrete, no generator is lost, and the algebra is $\so(2) \oplus \so(2)$ (checks E1, E2).



\emph{(i) The charges.}  Write $s$ and $p$ for the weights of the slot and pair rotations. The element $(-1, -1)$ acts as $(-1)^{s + p}$, so the group's representations are those with $s + p$ even, on which
\[
q_{\pm} = \frac{s \pm p}{2}
\]
are the integral charges of $U(1)_{\pm}$ (F2).



\emph{(ii) The slot.}  The constituent count is one (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.where-mass-begins}(ii)). The slot group of one constituent is $Sp(1)$ (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.ladder}), acting on the spinor part through H1's identification of the two fields. The pair unit of Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.third-pairs-unit-five-dimensions} lies in that field, and only the circle it generates commutes with the pair algebra, so the slot is one complex line with group $U(1)$.



\emph{(ii) The pairs and the kernel.}  By U2 the three complex pairs carry $U(3)$. The action $(\lambda, B) \mapsto \lambda B$ has the common scalar as its kernel, as in T12's proof of its Theorem 4.1, so the group is
\[
\frac{U(1) \times U(3)}{U(1)} \;\cong\; U(3)
\]
(E3; the control $\C^{2} \otimes \C^{3} \mapsto 12$ is E4).
