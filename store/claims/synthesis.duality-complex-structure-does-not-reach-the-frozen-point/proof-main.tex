\emph{Setting.} As in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.frozen-point-integrand-on-tractor-transport-is-weyl-squared}: generators $e_{0}, \dots, e_{5}$ with squares $(+,-,-,-,+,-)$, $\nO = \tfrac12(e_{4} + e_{5})$, $\nInf = e_{4} - e_{5}$, $i_{6} = e_{0}\cdots e_{5}$, commutator product $x \times y = \tfrac12(xy - yx)$, four-form value of a pairing $\beta$ on two-forms $\tfrac14\sum_{p}\sgn(p)\beta(F_{p_{0}p_{1}}, G_{p_{2}p_{3}})$.

\emph{(i) Commutants.} $J$ is the Hodge star of Lemma \claimref{correspondence.lorentzian-duality-complex-structure}: $i_{4}^{2} = -1$ and $i_{4}$ commutes with every even element of $\Cl(1,3)$, so $J(B) = i_{4}B$ maps spacetime bivectors to spacetime bivectors, $J^{2} = -\id$ and $J[x, B] = [x, JB]$ for $x \in \so(1,3)$: $J$ is in the commutant. The commutant is computed as the null space of the linear conditions $T\,\mathrm{ad}_{x} = \mathrm{ad}_{x}T$ for $x$ running over a basis (structure constants from the commutator product, exact over the rationals): dimension two for $\so(1,3)$ (containing $\id$ and $J$), one for $\so(2,4)$. The stabiliser of $I = \nInf + \rho\nO$ (for $\rho \neq 0$ the frozen point of Theorem \claimref{dark-energy.frozen-point-makes-stelle-west-vector}, of de Sitter or anti-de Sitter type) is the null space of $B \mapsto B \times I$ on the fifteen bivectors, of dimension ten; its commutant has dimension one at $\rho = \tfrac37$, $-\tfrac25$ and $0$. These three values cover every case: the boost $\exp(\tfrac{\theta}{2}\nO\wedge\nInf)$ rescales $\nInf$ and $\nO$ by inverse factors, so it maps $I_{\rho}$ to a multiple of $I_{\rho e^{\mp 2\theta}}$ and conjugates the stabilisers of all $\rho$ of one sign; $\rho = 0$ is the null case. A complex structure $T$ with $T[x, y] = [x, Ty]$ lies in the commutant and squares to $-\id$, impossible in $\mathbb{R}\,\id$. The parabolic algebra (bivectors $B$ with $B \times X$ a multiple of $X$, eleven-dimensional) also has a one-dimensional commutant. In the computed bases, four of the ten elements of the stabiliser and five of the eleven of the parabolic algebra are sent by $i_{4}$ outside the bivectors, so $J$ acts on neither. This is the algebraic statement that $\so(1,3)$, with $J$ as its complex unit, is a complex Lie algebra ($\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ over $\mathrm{span}\{\id, J\}$) while the de Sitter, anti-de Sitter and Poincar\'e algebras and $\so(2,4)$ are not.

\emph{(ii) Pairings.} The check computes the Gram matrices of $\epsilon(I, X, B, B')$ and $\langle B i_{4} B'\rangle_{0}$ on the six spacetime blades at two values of $\rho$ and finds them opposite; since $I \wedge X = \nInf \wedge \nO$ does not depend on $\rho$, neither does $\epsilon_{X}$ (as in the stored theorem (ii)). Then $\epsilon_{X}(B, B') = -K(B, JB')$, and with $j^{2} = -1$, $\epsilon_{X} + jK = jK(B, (1 + jJ)B')$ and $\epsilon_{X} - jK = -jK(B, (1 - jJ)B')$; $(1 \pm jJ)/2$ are complementary projectors because $(jJ)^{2} = \id$.

\emph{(iii) The slot Hessian.} The spacetime block of the field strength is $-\mathcal{R} + T_{S}$ with $T_{S}$ linear in $S$ (proof of the stored GTG tractor theorem (iv), slot left free). For each pairing the Hessian in $S$ is the Gram matrix of $T$ on the sixteen matrix units. In the basis of lowered-index symmetric (ten) and antisymmetric (six) slots, the check finds the $K$ Hessian zero on symmetric slots and the cross blocks of both Hessians zero; the $\epsilon_{X}$ Hessian has rank sixteen and the $K$ Hessian rank six. On the antisymmetric block $\det(H_{\epsilon} + \tau H_{K})$ is a polynomial of degree at most six in $\tau$; it equals $-4096(1 + \tau^{2})^{3}$ at the eight integers $-3, \dots, 4$, hence identically. It has no real zero, and its zeros are $\tau = \pm j$, each of multiplicity three. At $\tau = \pm j$ the complex Hessian $H_{\epsilon} \pm jH_{K}$ is represented by the real $32 \times 32$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} H_{\epsilon} & \mp H_{K} \\ \pm H_{K} & H_{\epsilon}\end{pmatrix}$, of rank twenty-six (thirty-two at $\tau = 2j$), so the complex kernel has dimension three, in the antisymmetric slot.

\emph{(iv) Stationarity at the Schouten slot.} At $S = \Sch$ the spacetime block of a torsion-free field is $-\mathcal{W}$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.gtg-tractor-gauge-field-reproduces-tractor-connection}(iv)). The slot gradients are $2\beta(-\mathcal{W}, T_{\delta})$ for each pairing $\beta$ and slot direction $\delta$, linear in the curvature. The algebraic curvature tensors are spanned by the one hundred Kulkarni-Nomizu products of the ten basis symmetric matrices; on each the check forms the Schouten slot and finds both gradients zero in all sixteen directions, so they vanish for every torsion-free curvature (complete). With a curvature antisymmetric in each pair only (the curvature of a rotation gauge with torsion) the $\epsilon_{X}$ gradient still vanishes at the Schouten slot of the transposed Ricci contraction, while the $K$ gradient does not. So for torsion-free fields $\Sch$ is stationary for $\epsilon_{X} + \tau K$ for every $\tau$, unique by (iii) for real $\tau$ and unique up to the three-dimensional kernel at $\tau = \pm j$, which lies in the antisymmetric slot and does not change the symmetric part. The value there is $\epsilon_{X}(\mathcal{W} \wedge \mathcal{W}) + \tau K(\mathcal{W} \wedge \mathcal{W}) = \tfrac14 W_{abcd}W^{abcd} + \tau K(\mathcal{W} \wedge \mathcal{W})$ by the stored theorem (iii); $K(F \wedge F)$ is the Chern form there (stored theorem (iv)), here evaluated on the Weyl block, the Pontryagin-type density. The real case $\tau = 0$ with the slot eliminated is the conformal-gauge construction of conformal gravity (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-conformal-gravity-bach-equations-and-conformal-gauge-form}(b)), proved in the series as Theorem \claimref{synthesis.frozen-point-reduces-the-connection-only-when-infinitely-stiff}(iii); (iv) extends it to the whole chiral family. At $\tau = \pm j$ the kernel directions change neither the density nor its equations (they lie in the kernel of the Hessian and the gradient is zero there), so the equations are those of the Schouten slot while the slot itself is fixed only up to that kernel.

\emph{Scope.} Nothing here concerns the simplicity constraints of Plebanski's action or the reality conditions of Ashtekar's variables (cited from memory); those formulations take an independent Lorentz connection and frame as input, the first-order field space, so they adopt it rather than derive it. The statement here is that the complex structure such formulations need lives only on the Lorentz algebra, which the normal tractor and the reduced field spaces share, and that the chiral form of the stored integrand, with the slot as a field, selects the same slot as the real form.

\emph{Status.} (i) is standard Lie theory ($\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is complex; $\so(1,4)$, $\so(2,3)$, the Poincar\'e algebra, the parabolic algebra and $\so(2,4)$ are not), and the content of (iv) is the standard fact that the chiral Weyl square is the Weyl square plus a Pontryagin term; the series' objects enter as notation only, so the result is transport-absent.
