(iv) An isomorphism of algebras restricts to an isomorphism of centres; the centre of an odd Clifford algebra is spanned by $1$ and $\omega$: a basis blade commutes with $e_{i}$ exactly when $e_{i}$ anticommutes with an even number of its factors, which for a blade of grade $k$ containing $e_{i}$ requires $k - 1$ even and for one not containing it requires $k$ even, and only the scalar and the volume element satisfy both conditions for every $i$; since $x \mapsto e_{i}xe_{i}^{-1}$ is diagonal in the blade basis, an element commutes with every $e_{i}$ exactly when each of its blades does, so the centre is spanned by those two (the script confirms dimension two over every odd signature of dimension at most five, and dimension one over every even signature of dimension at most six), and is $\R \oplus \R$ when $\omega^{2} = +1$ and $\C$ when $\omega^{2} = -1$; Corollary \claimref{odd-extensions.four-extensions-oriented-pair} gives the signs.
