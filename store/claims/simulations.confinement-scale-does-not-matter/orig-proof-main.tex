For $\lambda \ge 0.21$ fm, Table \ref{tab:sidm} gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}/m \ge 9.5 \times 10^{7}\ \cmg$ in every cluster region and compression. Smaller composites barely lower this: a hard disk of radius $a = 10^{-30}$ fm still has $\lambda \approx \pi^{2}/\{k\,[\ln(ka/2) + \gamma_{E}]^{2}\} \approx 0.08$ fm (with $\ln(ka/2) = -73.5$), so $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}/m \ge 3 \times 10^{7}\ \cmg$, and the matter remains a fluid on cluster scales. The size of a composite enters $\lambda$ only through the logarithm (Computation \claimref{simulations.cluster-bound-two-dimensions}(i) and (iii)), so no physical confinement scale reaches the bound. The phase-shift limits are Computation \claimref{simulations.cluster-bound-two-dimensions}(iii) at the isotropic spacing and, since $\lambda \propto \sin^{2}\delta_{0}$ and the bound on $\lambda$ scales as $1/s$, a factor $(4.4 \times 10^{10}/9.5 \times 10^{7})^{1/2} \approx 22$ larger at the normal-compression spacing.
