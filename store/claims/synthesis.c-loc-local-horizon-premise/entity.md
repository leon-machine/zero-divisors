## formal
\emph{Premise (C$_{\mathrm{loc}}$).} Units $\hbar = c = 1$. Let $(\hb, \Omega)$ be a gravitational field on flat spacetime (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}), $p$ any point of its invertible region and $\ell$ any future physically null direction at $p$; let $\mathcal{H}_{p,\ell}$ be the local Rindler horizon through $p$ generated by $\ell$ (the null boundary of the past of a small spacelike two-surface element through $p$ orthogonal to $\ell$, with vanishing expansion and shear at $p$; in a normal gauge at $p$, $\hb(p) = 1$ and $\Omega(p) = 0$, it is at leading order a piece of the null hyperplane through $p$ orthogonal to $\ell$), with approximate boost flow of acceleration $a$. Everything is to leading order in the size of the patch, the order at which the boost flow is defined when the field strength $\mathcal{R}$ is nonzero. The patch's modes are the field on its generators' light rays: the continuum mode space of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.continuum-mode-space}, one carrier per light ray, identified with the matter field on those light rays (the horizon modes and the matter crossing the patch are one field). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Loc)] \emph{(Entropy, integrated reading.)} The entropy of a cut $\Sigma$ of the patch's horizon is a functional of the cut, $S[\Sigma] = \int_{\Sigma}\eta\,dA$, where the density $\eta$ depends only on the patch's flat tangent data at each point (the null ray and the edge plane: a physical null vector up to scale and a physical bivector, compared with the flat product), and not on the curvature (the field strength $\mathcal{R}$), the matter stress tensor or the boost normalisation there. Horizon entropy contains no Euler (Jacobson-Myers) term, on local and on closed horizons alike; the Euler term stays in the action. By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-entropy-clause-curvature-independent-density}(i) such a density is one universal constant, so $\delta S = \eta\,\delta A$; calibrated on the Schwarzschild family under the series' temperature premises it is $\eta = 1/4$ in Planck units, with no mode count (part (ii) there).
\item[(R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$)] \emph{(Temperature, before the crossing.)} Before matter crosses the patch, the state of the patch's modes on the past half of each generator (the half ending on the edge) is jointly KMS for the local boost future-directed there at rapidity period $2\pi$, on the patch's whole mode algebra. It is a condition on the state before the crossing: after a crossing with heat $\delta Q \neq 0$ the state departs from it at the order of the heat (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.clausius-is-window-first-law-plus-boost-charge-conservation}(2)). In Minkowski space (a vanishing field strength) this continuum form follows from Premise \claimref{synthesis.n-plus-null-ground-state} (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(iii), case (c)), with the positivity of the null translations derived there from a nonnegative conformal energy (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-conformal-energy-gives-positive-null-translations}); for a gravitational field of nonzero field strength it follows from the local form of (N$_{+}$), argued at leading order. By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.local-boost-temperature-is-local-regularity} the rapidity period $2\pi$ gives $T = a/2\pi$ in the proper time of the boost observers at leading order, the period at which the local boost's Euclidean section is regular.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Note (the Euler exclusion in normalisation form).} The exclusion of the Euler term in (Loc) has an equivalent normalisation form. With the geodesic-curvature edge term of a patch (the two-dimensional Gibbons-Hawking term), the Euler part of the Wald entropy of the series' action on every patch $P$ of a cut is the topological constant $2\pi\alpha\chi(P)$, $\alpha = -3/(4\Lambda)$, so it changes no entropy difference along a local horizon, and excluding it is the normalisation (Z) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.sds-clausius-fixes-area-constant} (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.euler-entropy-of-a-horizon-patch-is-an-edge-term}). The adopted reading is that horizon entropy is the entropy of (Loc), not the action's Wald entropy. This note records a reading of the clause; what the premise asserts is unchanged.

\emph{Note (status of the two clauses on the joint set with the gravitational action).} Suppose, besides this premise and the Clausius relation, that the field equations are those of the store's gravitational action on the field space, with one normalisation $G$ for every admissible field and $\Lambda \neq 0$, taken as a stated hypothesis. Then at a point where matter crosses (the stress is not a multiple of the metric) the Clausius relation at rapidity period $2\pi c$ holds on every patch exactly when $\eta = c/(4G)$, Jacobson's identification read with $G$ given by the action, and on matter-free regions it holds for every $\eta$ and $c$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.clausius-on-the-action-shell-ties-entropy-density-to-period}). So on that joint set, with the calibration $\eta = \tfrac14$ at the action's $G$ ($G = 1$ in the same units) taken as an independent input, the one-coupling clause of (Loc) and the period $2\pi$ of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) imply each other where matter crosses (the direction from the period to the one coupling, $\eta = 1/(4G)$ on every field that carries matter, does not need the calibration), and the Clausius relation is then a constraint on the thermal data, not a derivation of the field equations, which the action supplies. This note records the status of the clauses on that joint set; the action is not a premise of (C$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), and what the premise asserts is unchanged.

\emph{One mode set, per light ray.} On a hole's horizon the modes on each light ray and the modes premises (C1) and (C2) place at the curvature cutoff are one set described twice (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-is-a-second-placement-of-the-count}: independent placements would double the entropy, $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = 2G$, under the total-entropy reading). The counted windows of the cutoff placement, and the point-spectrum mode sets on which the series' thermal premises are worded, are discretisations of the per-light-ray carrier, recovered only for modes of vanishing Mellin width (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(iv)). The point-spectrum wording could not carry this clause: with a phase-acting flow the two local horizons of one edge admit (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on modes they share only at zero weight (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.r-loc-needs-sheet-labelled-modes}), and (R) at the cutoff placement and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) at the collar's points ask one mode set for different periods (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collar-temperature-fixed-locally-by-r-loc}); on the per-light-ray carrier the past halves of the two sheets of an edge are different null half-planes and the edge is a null set carrying no mode. The cutoff premises (C1), (C2) and (IC) are read as a Schwarzschild calibration only: away from Schwarzschild the horizon placement with the flat-data density of (Loc) governs, since read with a horizon's modular period on a Schwarzschild-de Sitter hole the literal cutoff count is not a curvature-independent entropy per unit area (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.cutoff-count-is-not-a-local-density-on-sds}).

\emph{Status of the clauses.} (Loc) is not derived on either reading of the entropy. On the differences reading of version 2 it is independent of the Clausius relation, of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) and of every $\Lambda$-vacuum horizon datum of the store (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.loc-independent-of-clausius-and-killing-data}). On the integrated reading adopted here the Clausius relation on open patches forces $\eta$ to be constant along each gravitational field (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.einstein-equations-from-c-loc-and-clausius}(6)), and within the series' entropy family it admits the Euler term only where the field strength at the point is of constant-curvature type, $\mathcal{R}(B)$ a multiple of $B$, and forces the curvature-independent density (Theorems \claimref{synthesis.euler-entropy-first-order-rate-on-local-patches} and \claimref{synthesis.series-wald-entropy-changes-without-flux-on-local-horizons}); a density built from scalars of the field strength that is constant on each admissible gravitational field still escapes, and its failures are confined to fields with no calibrated vacuum point (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.loc-independent-of-clausius-and-killing-data}). The continuum form of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) follows from (N$_{+}$) in Minkowski space and is argued for nonzero field strength; without (N$_{+}$), (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) with modes labelled by null direction is independent of the store's other premises, and what it fixes off Killing horizons is the matter coupling (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.r-loc-independent-sets-matter-coupling}).

\emph{What it delivers.} With the Clausius relation for the boost-energy flux of matter, a separate hypothesis and not a clause of this premise, and the geometric hypotheses stated there, Theorem \claimref{synthesis.einstein-equations-from-c-loc-and-clausius} proves Einstein's equations, the field equations $\mathcal{G}(a) + \Lambda a = -8\pi G_{\mathrm{cl}}\,T(a)$ of the gravitational field in the store's sign, with $\Lambda$ an integration constant and $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = 1/(4\eta)$ (Jacobson's argument, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-jacobson-clausius-1995}). With $\eta = 1/4$ from the Schwarzschild calibration this gives $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = G$, a consistency and not a determination of $G$, since the calibration contains $G$. What selects Einstein's equations over their Ricci-polynomial extensions on this route is the curvature-independence in (Loc), not locality. Toward the Clausius relation itself: with the horizon modes and the crossing matter one field on the light rays, the conservation of the patch's boost charge is an identity, and given Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-null-plane-modular-hamiltonian-first-law} the first law of the modes in the state of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) holds on the continuum for entropy differences, so the Clausius relation at first order follows once the entropy change is read through (Loc), under the hypotheses of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.clausius-is-window-first-law-plus-boost-charge-conservation}.

\emph{Not part of this premise.} Two readings of version 1 are recorded as what Jacobson's 2016 small-diamond route (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-jacobson-entanglement-equilibrium-2016}) would additionally need: the mode-count reading of the entropy, $\eta = 4\pi\nu\langle s\rangle$ at the epoch density of Theorem \claimref{scrambling.epoch-modular-period-two-unknowns-become}, and the geometric reading of the modular flow on nested sub-patches, which contradicts a point-spectrum mode set (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-modular-clause-moves-no-nested-patch}) and is not established on the continuum carrier. Version 1 stated the premise as three clauses, (i) count, (ii) temperature, (iii) modular flow, on local Rindler horizons and small diamonds; version 2 read the entropy as differences only and posed (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on the point-spectrum windows; the results that analyse those versions refer to them.

\emph{Refutation condition.} The premise is false if any of the following is established: (a) a result showing that the entropy of the cuts of some class of local horizons is not a universal constant times the area at leading order, for example that its density depends on the acceleration, on the matter crossing the patch or on the field strength at the point, or that horizon entropy carries an Euler term; the literal counts of the cutoff placement for spinning holes (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.kerr-ring-count-misses-area-law}) and on Schwarzschild-de Sitter holes (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.cutoff-count-is-not-a-local-density-on-sds}) are counts on the cutoff placement read away from Schwarzschild, in conflict with the horizon area law, and count against that placement, not against (Loc); (b) a result showing that no state of the patch's per-light-ray modes is jointly KMS for the local boost at rapidity period $2\pi$ at leading order before matter crosses; (c) a confirmed departure from Einstein's equations with a cosmological constant in a regime where local horizons are defined, since the premise with the Clausius relation gives those equations and nothing else (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.black-hole-data-do-not-fix-field-equations} shows the Killing-horizon data alone cannot exclude Ricci-polynomial corrections, and the curvature-independence in (Loc) is what does).

## technical
The series places its boundary modes, their temperature and their entropy on Killing horizons: the Schwarzschild horizon and the de Sitter cosmological horizon. This premise extends them to local Rindler horizons through every point, at leading order in the patch size, in the two forms Jacobson's 1995 argument uses, with the modes taken as the field running along each light ray of the patch and identified with the matter field there. (Loc): the entropy of a cut of the horizon is the integral of a density $\eta$ that depends only on the flat tangent data and not on the field strength (curvature) or matter; such a density is one universal constant, fixed at $1/4$ by the Schwarzschild calibration without counting modes, and horizon entropy carries no Euler term (that term stays in the action); equivalently, since with the patch's edge term the Euler part adds only a topological constant per patch, excluding it is a normalisation, and horizon entropy is read as the entropy of (Loc), not the action's Wald entropy. If the store's gravitational action with one coupling $G$ and $\Lambda \neq 0$ is also in force, the Clausius relation where matter crosses reduces to $\eta = c/(4G)$ at period $2\pi c$, so, given the calibration $\eta = \tfrac14$ at the action's $G$, the one-coupling part of (Loc) and the period of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) imply each other there and the Clausius relation becomes a constraint on the thermal data rather than a derivation of the field equations (a status note; the premise is unchanged). (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$): before matter crosses, the modes on the light rays ending on the edge are jointly thermal for the local boost at rapidity period $2\pi$, which gives the temperature $a/2\pi$. In Minkowski space this follows from the null ground-state premise (N$_{+}$), which has no temperature in it; for a nonzero gravitational field strength it follows from that premise's local form, argued at leading order.

The modes on a hole's horizon and the cutoff modes are one set, now per light ray; the counted windows are discretisations of it. On point-spectrum windows the temperature clause could not stand (the two sheets of an edge would share modes only at zero weight, and the cutoff and collar placements would ask one mode set for two periods), and the continuum carrier removes both conflicts. The cutoff premises are a Schwarzschild calibration only, since on a Schwarzschild-de Sitter hole the literal cutoff count is not a local area density. (Loc) is not derived on either reading of the entropy: on the differences reading an explicit model shows it independent; on the integrated reading Clausius forces the density to be constant on each gravitational field and, within the series' family, excludes the Euler term, but a density constant on each field and varying between fields with no calibrated vacuum point still escapes.

With the Clausius relation for matter's boost energy, kept as a separate hypothesis, the store proves Einstein's equations from this premise, with $\Lambda$ an integration constant and $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = 1/(4\eta)$; $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = G$ is a consistency check, since the calibration of $\eta$ contains $G$. With the horizon modes and the matter one field, the boost-charge conservation behind Clausius is an identity, and given an imported fact on null-plane modular Hamiltonians the first law of the modes gives the Clausius relation at first order once the entropy change is read through (Loc). It is a Tier C premise, refuted by an entropy density on local horizons that depends on the field strength, matter or acceleration or carries an Euler term, by the absence of a jointly thermal state for the local boost before matter crosses, or by an observed departure from Einstein's equations where local horizons make sense. The literal Kerr and Schwarzschild-de Sitter counts are not refutations: they count against the cutoff placement away from Schwarzschild.

## lay
The series computes a temperature and an entropy for the edges of black holes and of the universe's horizon, using quantum modes it places on those edges. Jacobson showed in 1995 that Einstein's equations of gravity follow if every small piece of horizon around every point, including the horizon seen by an accelerating observer, has a temperature set by the acceleration and an entropy proportional to its area, by the same amount everywhere. This assumption says exactly that, with the quantum modes running continuously along the light rays of each small horizon and being the same field as the matter that falls through it. It has two parts: the entropy of a slice of the horizon is its area times a fixed number that does not depend on how strong the gravitational field is there, and before matter falls through, the modes are thermal at the accelerating observer's temperature. Where there is no gravitational field the second part now follows from a simpler assumption with no temperature in it; the first part is still assumed. With it, the store proves Einstein's equations, with the strength of gravity set by the entropy per area and the cosmological constant left free. The assumption would fail if the entropy per area turned out to depend on the strength of the gravitational field or on matter, or if gravity were found to deviate from Einstein's equations.

## one-liner
Premise (C$_{\mathrm{loc}}$): on every local Rindler horizon, with the modes the field on the light rays, the entropy of a cut is $\int\eta\,dA$ with $\eta$ independent of the field strength and matter and no Euler term (Loc, not derived on either reading), and before matter crosses the modes are jointly KMS for the local boost at rapidity period $2\pi$ (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$, following from (N$_{+}$) in Minkowski space); with the Clausius relation it gives Einstein's equations with $G_{\mathrm{cl}} = 1/(4\eta)$.
