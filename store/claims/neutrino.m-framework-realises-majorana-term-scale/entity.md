## formal
The neutral bilinear of (2) is realised by the framework, is nonzero, and its scale $M$ lies far above $v$. What (2) proves is that nothing forbids the term; gauge invariance says nothing about whether it forms or about its magnitude ($M = 0$, Dirac neutrinos; $M \sim v$; $M \sim$ eV are all consistent with it), and the framework's own mass bracket, computed only on slot states, undefined for excited composites, and of unsettled relation to the order parameter (Fact \claimref{neutrino.imported-mass-bracket-quantum-numbers}), has not been identified with it, nor shown to differ from it. This paper takes (M) as a premise, values $M$ from the observed masses in Remark \ref{rem:valuation}, and names its derivation as a successor problem.

## technical
Premise (M) asserts that the framework realises the neutral Majorana bilinear of the singlet, that it is nonzero, and that its scale $M$ lies far above the electroweak scale $v$. Gauge invariance only permits the term: it says nothing about whether it forms or how large it is ($M = 0$, Dirac neutrinos; $M \sim v$; $M \sim$ eV are all consistent with it). The framework's own mass bracket, computed only on slot states, undefined for excited composites and of unsettled relation to the order parameter, has been neither identified with it nor shown to differ from it.

The paper therefore takes (M) as a premise, values $M$ from the observed masses, and names its derivation as a successor problem.

## lay
Neutrinos could carry a special kind of mass that only electrically neutral particles can have. Nothing forbids it, but nothing in the framework yet shows that it forms or how big it is. The paper assumes that it does, at a very high scale, and leaves the explanation for later work.

## one-liner
Premise: the framework realises the singlet's Majorana mass term, nonzero and at a scale $M \gg v$.
