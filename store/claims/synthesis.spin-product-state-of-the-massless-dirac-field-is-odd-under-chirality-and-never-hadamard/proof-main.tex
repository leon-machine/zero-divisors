\emph{Conventions.} Units $\hbar = c = 1$; $\Cl(1,3)$ with $e_{0}^{2} = 1$, $e_{i}^{2} = -1$, $I_{4} = e_{0}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}$. Spinors are acted on by left multiplication, and the field theory's unit is the right unit $\psi \mapsto \psi I_{4}$ (the store's convention for Dirac lines). Matrix forms are parenthetical readings. The real part of the Dirac adjoint pairing is $s(\psi, \phi) = \langle e_{0}\tilde\psi\phi\rangle_{0}$ (the reading of $\mathrm{Re}\,\bar\psi\phi$) and the real part of the current pairing is $h(\psi, \phi) = \langle e_{0}\tilde\psi e_{0}\phi\rangle_{0}$ (the reading of $\mathrm{Re}\,\psi^{\dagger}\phi$); the complex forms are recovered from the real parts with the right unit. The check verifies on all $16 \times 16$ pairs of basis blades of $\Cl(1,3)$, used as the module (complete by bilinearity, and inherited by every left ideal closed under the maps below), that $h$ is symmetric and positive on the basis and that every vector $e_{\mu}$ acting on the left is symmetric for $s$ (the Dirac adjoint property $\bar\gamma_{\mu} = \gamma_{\mu}$).

\emph{Set-up.} Let $N$ be a globally hyperbolic region of a gravitational field $(\hb, \Omega)$ (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}) with a time function $t$, and let $\mathcal{H}$ be the space of solutions of the Dirac equation of mass $m \ge 0$ with the conserved current product $(\psi|\phi)$, the one-particle space of the CAR algebra (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-dimock-dirac-car-algebra-on-a-gravitational-field}). For a weight $F \ge 0$, a function of $t$, put $\langle\psi|\phi\rangle_{F} = \int_{N}F\,\bar\psi\phi\,\mathrm{dvol}$ and suppose it bounded on $\mathcal{H}$ (it is on the collars of (iii), (iv) and on the ultrastatic slabs of \cite{FewsterLang2015}); $A_{F}$ is the bounded self-adjoint operator with $(\psi|A_{F}\phi) = \langle\psi|\phi\rangle_{F}$, $Q_{F} = \chi_{(0,\infty)}(A_{F})$ and $\Pi_{F}$ the projection onto $\ker A_{F}$. The spin-product state $\omega_{F}$ is the gauge-invariant quasi-free state whose two-point function $\omega_{F}(\Psi(f)\bar\Psi(h))$ is the pairing of $Sh$ with $Q_{F}Sf$ ($S$ the causal propagator) and whose other ordering carries $1 - Q_{F}$. This is the weighted spin-product state of Fewster and Lang (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-fewster-lang-spin-product-states-of-the-dirac-field}; \cite{FewsterLang2015}, Sec. 3), the finite-time form of the fermionic projector state of Finster and Reintjes, whose construction on a whole spacetime uses a family of masses and does not exist at $m = 0$ ($F$ the characteristic function of the collar gives their unsoftened state, $F$ smooth with compact support their softened ones); it is the Dirac analogue of the Sorkin-Johnston state.

\emph{(i) Chirality.} Let $\Gamma\psi = I_{4}\psi I_{4}$, left multiplication by $I_{4}$ (the matrix $\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}$) composed with the unit (the factor $i$): the matrix $\gamma_{5}$. Left and right multiplication commute, so $\Gamma^{2}\psi = I_{4}^{2}\psi I_{4}^{2} = \psi$ and $\Gamma$ commutes with the right unit; on the whole algebra $\Gamma$ is minus the grade involution (checked), since $I_{4}$ commutes with even and anticommutes with odd elements. Because $I_{4}$ anticommutes with every vector and commutes with every bivector, $\Gamma(e_{\mu}\psi) = -e_{\mu}\Gamma\psi$ and $\Gamma(B\psi) = B\Gamma\psi$ for bivectors $B$ (checked on all basis elements). With torsion ($\Omega$ independent of $\hb$, the definition's Einstein-Cartan choice) the connection term of the Dirac operator, $\hb(\partial_{a})$ acting with $\Omega(a)$, is a vector plus a trivector, both odd, so $\Gamma$ anticommutes with it too and (i), (ii) hold on every gravitational field of the definition. The Dirac operator on $(\hb, \Omega)$ is the left action of the vector derivative $\hb(\partial)$ plus the connection, a bivector acting on the left, so $\Gamma$ anticommutes with it; the massless equation is therefore $\Gamma$-invariant, while the massive one, whose mass term is the right unit times $m$ against a left vector, is not (checked: $\Gamma$ does not commute with left $e_{0}$). Under $\Gamma$ on both arguments, $s$ changes sign and $h$ is unchanged (checked on all pairs): in $s$ one factor $e_{0}$ meets the grade involution, in $h$ two do. So for $m = 0$, $\Gamma$ is a unitary involution of $\mathcal{H}$ with $\Gamma A_{F}\Gamma = -A_{F}$. Hence $\Gamma$ maps the spectral subspace of $A_{F}$ for $(0, \infty)$ onto that for $(-\infty, 0)$ and preserves $\ker A_{F}$, which is the identity $Q_{F} + \Gamma Q_{F}\Gamma = 1 - \Pi_{F}$. (Proved.)

\emph{(ii) No massless spin-product state is Hadamard.} Let $m = 0$ and $\Pi_{F} = 0$. The causal propagator obeys $S\Gamma = -\Gamma S$ on test sections: with $D$ the massless Dirac operator, $D\Gamma = -\Gamma D$, so $\Gamma S_{\mathrm{ret}}\Gamma$ is a retarded fundamental solution of $-D$ and equals $-S_{\mathrm{ret}}$ by uniqueness, and likewise for the advanced one. So $(Sh|\Gamma Q_{F}\Gamma Sf) = (S\Gamma h|Q_{F}S\Gamma f)$, the two signs cancelling: the two-point function of $\Gamma Q_{F}\Gamma = 1 - Q_{F}$ is that of $Q_{F}$ with both arguments transformed by the smooth bundle isomorphism $\Gamma$ over the identity map. Hence $\mathcal{G}^{(-)}_{\omega_{F}}$ is $\mathcal{G}^{(+)}_{\omega_{F}}$ composed with $\Gamma \times \Gamma$, and a bundle isomorphism over the identity of the base does not change a wave-front set (standard, cited from memory as \cite[Thm.~8.2.4]{Hormander1983} for the pullback by the identity map, the fibre maps being smooth and invertible). If $\omega_{F}$ were Hadamard on an open set $O \subset N$, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-microlocal-hadamard-condition}(b) would give $\mathrm{WF}'(\mathcal{G}^{(+)}) = C^{+}$ and $\mathrm{WF}'(\mathcal{G}^{(-)}) = C^{-}$ over $O \times O$, so $C^{+} = C^{-}$ there; but these sets are disjoint and nonempty (each contains $(x, \xi; x, \xi)$ with $\xi$ null and future-, respectively past-directed). So $\omega_{F}$ is Hadamard on no open subset of $N$. (Proved, given the Fact and the cited invariance.) Without $\Pi_{F} = 0$ the same argument shows that a Hadamard $\omega_{F}$ would need the kernel projection $\Pi_{F}$ to carry the whole singularity of the causal propagator.

\emph{(iii) Homogeneous collars.} On a flat slab, and for $m = 0$ on a spatially flat Robertson-Walker collar $\hb(b) = b/a(\eta)$ in conformal time, the field splits into momentum-helicity blocks. Each block is the stored two-level system $iu' = Hu$, $H = Ms_{3} + ks_{1}$, $M = ma$, in the Pauli algebra with $i = I = s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.massive-dirac-instantaneous-vacuum-and-non-smooth-descents-are-not-hadamard}, formal statement), with the current product the Hermitian product of $\mathbb{C}^{2}$, adjoint the reversion. The mass term of the Dirac Hamiltonian is $m\bar\psi\psi$, so the spin-product density of a block is $\partial H/\partial M = s_{3}$ (Hellmann-Feynman reading; checked: $H$ is linear in $M$ with that slope). For $m = 0$ on the Robertson-Walker collar, $\psi = a^{-3/2}\chi$ with $\chi$ a flat massless solution and the current product preserved (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-conformal-covariance-of-massless-fields-and-local-conformal-flatness}(b); Theorem \claimref{synthesis.deposit-over-the-conformal-vacuum-is-hadamard-exactly-when-every-occupation-moment-is-finite}), and $\bar\psi\phi\,\mathrm{dvol} = a^{-3}\bar\chi\chi'\,a^{4}\,d\eta\,d^{3}x$ (exponent $-\tfrac32 - \tfrac32 + 4 = 1$, checked), so the collar's weight is the flat weight $Fa$. With $U(\eta) = e^{-IH\eta}$ for constant $H$,
\[
A_{F}\big|_{\mathrm{block}} \;=\; \int F(\eta)a(\eta)\,\tilde U(\eta)\,s_{3}\,U(\eta)\,d\eta,
\]
a vector of the Pauli algebra (the operator $\vec A\cdot\vec\sigma$), whose square is the scalar $|A|^{2}$; its positive spectral projection is $\tfrac12(1 + \hat A)$, the pure state with Bloch vector $\hat A = A/|A|$. The conformal vacuum (on the slab, the vacuum) is the positive-frequency projection $\tfrac12(1 + \hat h)$, $\hat h = (ks_{1} + Ms_{3})/\omega$, $\omega = (k^{2} + M^{2})^{1/2}$, and the Bogoliubov coefficient between the two pure states is $|\beta_{k}|^{2} = \tfrac12(1 - \hat A\cdot\hat h)$ as in that theorem. The rotor $U$ turns $s_{3}$ about $\hat h$ through the angle $2\omega\eta$: the part $(s_{3}\cdot\hat h)\hat h = (M/\omega)\hat h$ is fixed and the perpendicular part, of length $k/\omega$, turns (checked with exact rational rotors $c - I\hat h s$, $c^{2} + s^{2} = 1$, on Pythagorean $(k, M, \omega)$ and weighted sums of up to seven turns). With $\hat F(\lambda) = \int F e^{i\lambda t}dt$ for the flat weight,
\[
A\cdot\hat h = \frac{M}{\omega}\hat F(0), \qquad |A|^{2} = \Xi^{2} = \frac{M^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\hat F(0)^{2} + \frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}|\hat F(2\omega)|^{2}, \qquad 1 - 2|\beta_{k}|^{2} = \frac{M\hat F(0)}{\omega\,\Xi},
\]
which is the mixing angle $\cos 2\theta = \hat F(0)m/(\lambda\Xi)$ of \cite{FewsterLang2015} (standard, recomputed here; the check verifies the two identities exactly for discrete weights $\sum_{j}w_{j}\delta(\eta - \eta_{j})$, to which the integral is linear). For $m = 0$: $\hat h = s_{1}$, and $\tilde U s_{3}U = \cos(2k\eta)s_{3} + \sin(2k\eta)s_{2}$ has no $s_{1}$ part, so $A \perp \hat h$ for every weight: wherever $\widehat{Fa}(2k) \ne 0$ the spin-product state's Bloch vector is perpendicular to the conformal vacuum's and $|\beta_{k}|^{2} = \tfrac12$ (checked, including weights $F a$ with $a = \eta$). The block chirality is $s_{1}$ (it commutes with $H = ks_{1}$ and anticommutes with $s_{3}$, checked), $s_{1}As_{1} = -A$ and $Q + s_{1}Qs_{1} = 1$ (checked), the block form of (i). If $F$ has compact support in $\eta$ and is not zero, $\widehat{Fa}$ is entire and $\widehat{Fa}(0) = \int Fa > 0$, so its zeros are isolated and the blocks with $A = 0$ lie on finitely many spheres in every bounded set of momenta, a null set: $\Pi_{F} = 0$, and (ii) applies. Directly: the energy of a block above the vacuum is $\omega(1 - \hat A\cdot\hat h) = 2\omega|\beta_{k}|^{2} = k$, so the expected energy density over the conformal vacuum is $a^{-4}$ times a multiple of $\int k\,d^{3}k$, divergent at every point, which no Hadamard state has (argued; this is the route of the massive-Dirac theorem). (Proved; the energy statement argued.)

\emph{(iv) Massive flat slab.} For the characteristic function of $(-T/2, T/2)$, $\hat F(0) = T$ and $\hat F(2\omega) = \sin(\omega T)/\omega$. On the momenta with $\omega T = (n + \tfrac12)\pi$, $(1 - 2|\beta_{k}|^{2})^{2} = M^{2}T^{2}\omega^{2}/(M^{2}T^{2}\omega^{2} + k^{2})$, which decreases to $M^{2}T^{2}/(M^{2}T^{2} + 1)$ as $k \to \infty$; at $MT = \tfrac34$ the limit is $\tfrac{9}{25}$ and $|\beta_{k}|^{2} \to \tfrac15$, and for $k \ge 3M$ already $|\beta_{k}|^{2} \ge \tfrac12(1 - 2^{-1/2})$ (checked exactly on the rational expression). The same lower bound holds on intervals of momenta around these points of fixed width in $\omega$, so the relative energy density diverges and the unsoftened state is not Hadamard, in agreement with \cite[Thm.~3.4]{FewsterLang2015}, which is proved for ultrastatic slabs with compact spatial sections; on the flat slab the mode-wise statement is the one computed here (computed and argued; standard). For a weight $F$ smooth with compact support, $1 - (1 + x)^{-1/2} \le x/2$ for $0 \le x \le 3$, from the polynomial identity $(1 + x)(1 - \tfrac{x}{2})^{2} = 1 - \tfrac34x^{2} + \tfrac14x^{3}$ (checked), gives, with $x = (1 - 2|\beta_{k}|^{2})^{-2} - 1 = k^{2}|\hat F(2\omega)|^{2}/(M^{2}\hat F(0)^{2})$, the bound $|\beta_{k}|^{2} = \tfrac12(1 - (1 + x)^{-1/2}) \le x/4 = k^{2}|\hat F(2\omega)|^{2}/(4M^{2}\hat F(0)^{2})$ once $x \le 3$, which holds at large $k$; $\hat F$ decreases faster than any power, so $|\beta_{k}|^{2}$ does, and the softened states are Hadamard by \cite[Thm.~3.5]{FewsterLang2015} (imported, not re-proved; proved there for compact spatial sections and carried to the flat slab mode by mode, argued; the bound is the mode-wise reason). At $m = 0$ the bound is void ($\hat F(0)M = 0$), as (iii) requires.

\emph{(v) The deposit.} (HD) (Premise \claimref{synthesis.hadamard-deposit-premise}) asks that the deposit be Hadamard on some Cauchy collar of the descent, with the occupation profile over an admissible reference vacuum free. The spin-product prescription is the one selection built only from the Dirac field's own indefinite form, its conserved current and a collar; by (ii) and (iii) it gives, for the massless Dirac field, a state that is Hadamard on no open subset of any collar for which $A_{F}$ is injective, every compactly supported weight on the radiation-type collar included, with occupation $\tfrac12$ above the conformal vacuum; by (iv) for massive fields it gives (HD) only for smooth weights, a choice the form does not make. So it does not supply the occupation profile, and (HD) is not derived by it (argued, from (ii) to (iv)). The contrast: the twistor form on the conformal spinors is graded by $Z = e_{0}\nu$ against the Hilbert form, and its positive part selects the attachment's Fock vacuum (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.attachment-conformal-energy-is-generated-by-z}), on a Weyl-flat collar the conformal vacuum, which is Hadamard there; so of the two indefinite forms on massless Dirac spinors, the conformal one selects a Hadamard state and the spacetime one does not.

Standard: the per-mode form of the fermionic projector state and the massive conclusions (Fewster and Lang).
