## formal
On the one-generation module, a vector $c = a v_{4} + b w_{4}$ of the rung plane has mass form with entries of magnitude $|a|$ on $(L, \nu^{c})$, $(Q, u^{c})$ and $|b|$ on $(L, e^{c})$, $(Q, d^{c})$, so at tree level $m_{\nu} = m_{u}$ and $m_{e} = m_{d}$, the relations of a single $10$; and three no-go theorems follow. (i) With the three families carried by a tensor factor on which the family structure acts by any matrix $F$, and with $y_{e}$, $y_{d}$ now denoting the entries of the $10$ form of $c$ on $(L, e^{c})$ and $(Q, d^{c})$, $M_{e} = y_{e}F$ and $M_{d} = y_{d}F$ with $|y_{e}| = |y_{d}|$ have identical spectra in magnitude for every $F$: no operation in family space, the breaking $S_{3} \to S_{2} \to 1$ included, produces $m_{\mu} \neq m_{s}$ or $m_{e} \neq m_{d}$ at tree level. (ii) The algebra generated by the frozen point and the rung components of its lifts is eight-dimensional and contains no colour-singlet doublet direction beyond the $10$. (iii) The algebra generated by the frozen point, its lifts, the mass bracket and its conjugate is thirty-two-dimensional, its colour-singlet part is the rung Clifford algebra, and every symmetric doublet mass form it produces is a multiple of the $10$ form. The observed second family needs $m_{\mu} \approx 3 m_{s}$ at the unification scale, the Georgi-Jarlskog factor \cite{GeorgiJarlskog1979}. \emph{Provenance:} \cite[Cor.~9.25, Thms.~9.31, 9.33, 9.35]{ButlerT12}, proved there on the module and conditional on Hypothesis (S) for the species reading; the factor three is the literature's, at record level.

## technical
On the one-generation module a rung-plane vector $c = a v_{4} + b w_{4}$ has a mass form with magnitude $|a|$ on $(L, \nu^{c})$ and $(Q, u^{c})$ and $|b|$ on $(L, e^{c})$ and $(Q, d^{c})$, so at tree level $m_{\nu} = m_{u}$ and $m_{e} = m_{d}$, the relations of a single $10$. With the families carried by a tensor factor on which any matrix $F$ acts, $M_{e} = y_{e}F$ and $M_{d} = y_{d}F$ with $|y_{e}| = |y_{d}|$ have the same spectra in magnitude, so no operation in family space produces $m_{\mu} \neq m_{s}$ or $m_{e} \neq m_{d}$ at tree level.

The algebra of the frozen point and the rung parts of its lifts is eight-dimensional with no colour-singlet doublet direction beyond the $10$; adding the mass bracket and its conjugate gives a thirty-two-dimensional algebra whose colour-singlet part is the rung Clifford algebra, and every symmetric doublet mass form it produces is a multiple of the $10$ form. The observed second family needs $m_{\mu} \approx 3m_{s}$ at the unification scale, the Georgi-Jarlskog factor. These results are imported from the Standard-Model paper, proved there on the module and conditional on Hypothesis (S) for the species reading.

## lay
In the simplest version of the series' mass mechanism, at tree level each charged lepton gets the same mass as the corresponding down-type quark, and each neutrino the same as the corresponding up-type quark, at very high energy. Earlier work in the series proved, conditional on Hypothesis (S), which reads the algebra's slots as named particles, that at tree level no rearrangement among the three families, and none of the obvious extra ingredients built from the frozen point, can break those equalities. Yet the observed muon and strange-quark masses need a factor of about three between them at that scale, a figure taken from the literature. This sets up the problem the following results address.

## one-liner
Under (S), the rung vev gives the single-$10$ relations $m_{\nu} = m_{u}$ and $m_{e} = m_{d}$ at tree level, which neither family-space operations nor the frozen point's lifts and mass bracket can dress at tree level, while the second family needs the Georgi-Jarlskog factor three.
