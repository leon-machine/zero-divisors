Conventions. $a(f) = \int \overline{f(u)}\,\psi_{m}(u)\,du$ on one generator (antilinear in $f$), $a^{*}(f) = a(f)^{*}$; a flow is a $*$-automorphism group when it preserves the anticommutator and the $*$-operation. Take $k = e_{0} + e_{1}$ and $m = \tfrac12(e_{0} - e_{1})$ (every other pair is carried to this one by a rotor, which leaves every statement below covariant); then $\ip{k}{m} = 1$, $k \wedge m = e_{1}e_{0}$ and $B_{\lambda} = \cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2} + e_{1}e_{0}\sinh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}$. The check sets $e^{\lambda/2} = r$ rational, so $\cosh$ and $\sinh$ of $\lambda/2$ are rational and every identity in (i), (ii) and (iv) is an exact identity of multivectors.

\emph{(i)} $\Lambda(m) = m\gamma_{0}/(2m_{0}) = \tfrac12(1 + e_{0}e_{1})$ is idempotent and self-adjoint for the frame $*$-structure of Theorem \claimref{tower.structure-tower-c-norms}, $m\Lambda(m) = 0$ since $Q(m) = 0$, and $\Lambda(k) + \Lambda(m) = 1$. Every vector is $a = \ip{a}{m}k + \ip{a}{k}m + a_{\perp}$ with $a_{\perp}$ orthogonal to $k$ and $m$, hence $\nabla = k\,(m\cdot\nabla) + m\,(k\cdot\nabla) + \nabla_{\perp}$. Direct multiplication gives $\Lambda(m)k\gamma_{0}\Lambda(m) = 0$, $\Lambda(m)e_{j}\gamma_{0}\Lambda(m) = 0$ for $j = 2, 3$ and $\Lambda(m)m\gamma_{0}\Lambda(m) = \Lambda(m)$. The field is that of Definition \claimref{obj.field-theory.single-algebra-field}. Compressing the anticommutator $\chi(\nabla D)\gamma_{0}$ of Proposition \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation}(2) to $\Lambda(m)$ therefore leaves $\chi\,(k\cdot\nabla D)\,\Lambda(m)$: only the derivative along the generators of $N_{k}$ survives. On the null plane that derivative of the Pauli-Jordan function is a constant multiple of $\delta(u - v)\delta^{2}(x_{\perp} - y_{\perp})$ (the light-front restriction, cited from memory; only the constant depends on it), so the canonical component $\psi_{m}$ has a delta kernel in the affine parameter and, generator by generator, its algebra is $\CAR(L^{2}(\R, du)\otimes\Lambda(m)S)$, a continuum mode space in the sense of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.continuum-mode-space}. The same multiplication gives $\Lambda(k)m\gamma_{0}\Lambda(k) = 0$ and $\Lambda(k)k\gamma_{0}\Lambda(k) = 2\Lambda(k)$, so $\Lambda(k)\psi$ keeps only $m\cdot\nabla$, the derivative off the plane: it is not an independent canonical datum on $N_{k}$.

\emph{(ii)} With $r = e^{\lambda/2}$: $B_{\lambda}kB_{\lambda}^{\sim} = r^{2}k$, $B_{\lambda}mB_{\lambda}^{\sim} = r^{-2}m$, $B_{\lambda}^{\sim}kB_{\lambda} = r^{-2}k$, $B_{\lambda}^{*} = B_{\lambda}$, $B_{\lambda}\Lambda(m) = r^{-1}\Lambda(m)$ (because $e_{1}e_{0}\Lambda(m) = -\Lambda(m)$), and so $B_{\lambda}\Lambda(m)B_{\lambda}^{*} = r^{-2}\Lambda(m)$. Under the covariant action $\psi(x) \mapsto B_{\lambda}\psi(B_{\lambda}^{\sim}xB_{\lambda})$ the canonical component along a generator becomes $\psi'_{m}(u) = r^{-1}\psi_{m}(r^{-2}u)$. Its anticommutator is the product of the rotor factor $r^{-2}$ (from $B_{\lambda}\Lambda(m)B_{\lambda}^{*}$) and the transport factor: $\delta(r^{-2}(u - v)) = r^{2}\delta(u - v)$, equivalently the ratio of a cell's length to that of its image under $u \mapsto r^{-2}u$. The product is $1$ and each factor alone is $r^{\mp 2} \neq 1$ for $\lambda \neq 0$; a map that rescales the anticommutator by a constant other than $1$ is not a $*$-automorphism. For a weight $r^{-2w}$ the product is $r^{2 - 4w}$, which is $1$ for all $r$ only at $w = \tfrac12$. In smeared form $\alpha_{\lambda}(a(f)) = a(D_{\lambda}f)$ with $(D_{\lambda}f)(u) = e^{-\lambda/2}f(e^{-\lambda}u)$; $\|D_{\lambda}f\|^{2} = e^{-\lambda}\int|f(e^{-\lambda}u)|^{2}du = \|f\|^{2}$, $D_{\lambda}$ is onto with inverse $D_{-\lambda}$, and $D_{\lambda}D_{\mu} = D_{\lambda + \mu}$; a unitary one-particle group defines a group of quasi-free $*$-automorphisms of the CAR algebra (Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.continuum-mode-space}). The same weight-1/2 dilation, as the scaling about the edge, is clause (i) of the edge-scaling result filed alongside (synthesis.stationary-horizon-state-is-its-own-edge-scaling-limit); here it is obtained with its two factors. This is the flow $D_{t}$ of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.affine-cell-boost-spreads-without-scrambling} at $\lambda = \kappa t$, up to the direction of the time parameter. On $\Lambda(k)$, $e_{1}e_{0}\Lambda(k) = \Lambda(k)$ gives $B_{\lambda}\Lambda(k) = r\Lambda(k)$ and $B_{\lambda}\Lambda(k)B_{\lambda}^{*} = r^{2}\Lambda(k)$; with the transport factor $r^{2}$ the kernel gains $r^{4}$.

\emph{(iii)} For $u > 0$ and $s \in \mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$ (numbers are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, $I_{4}$ the field's operator unit on the even values), $D_{\lambda}u^{s} = e^{-\lambda/2}e^{-\lambda s}u^{s} = e^{-\lambda(1/2 + s)}u^{s}$; the exponent $-(1/2 + s)$ is purely imaginary exactly when $\mathrm{Re}\,s = -\tfrac12$, and then equals $-I_{4}w$ for $s = -\tfrac12 + I_{4}w$. The same holds on $u < 0$ with $|u|$. By the Mellin-Plancherel theorem (cited from memory) the transform $f \mapsto \int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-1/2 - I_{4}w}f(u)\,du/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is unitary from $L^{2}(\R_{+}, du)$ onto $L^{2}(\R, dw)$, and it carries $D_{\lambda}$ to multiplication by $e^{-I_{4}w\lambda}$. So $\alpha_{\kappa t}$ is, in this basis, $a(f_{w}) \mapsto e^{-I_{4}\kappa wt}a(f_{w})$: the quasi-free dynamics of the one-particle Hamiltonian $\kappa w$, self-adjoint with spectrum $\R$, the boost dynamics $a_{j} \mapsto e^{-J\omega_{j}t}a_{j}$ of the windows with the frequencies $\omega_{j}$ refined to a continuum (the windows' modes carry the transverse unit $J = e_{2}e_{3}$ of the radial atom, \claimref{synthesis.transverse-bivector-is-the-complex-unit-of-the-radial-atom}, the generator's continuum the unit $I_{4}$; the comparison is of the frequencies, and the two units are not identified), which Proposition \claimref{synthesis.phase-flow-is-time-rotor-flow-kms-continuation} identifies as the windows' time evolution.

\emph{(iv)} Put $\lambda = I_{4}\theta$. The value factor is $B_{I_{4}\theta} = \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2} + I_{4}e_{1}e_{0}\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}$, the continuation of Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies}(1) (there written with $\sigma = e_{0}e_{1} = -e_{1}e_{0}$, so the sign of the sine flips with the orientation, and with the formal unit of the atom's complexification, which on the even values here acts as $I_{4}$, as that proposition's note on values records), and $e_{1}e_{0}\Lambda(m) = -\Lambda(m)$ gives $B_{I_{4}\theta}\Lambda(m) = e^{-I_{4}\theta/2}\Lambda(m)$, $I_{4}$ commuting with the even element $\Lambda(m)$; the check verifies this at rational points of the unit circle. The transport factor becomes $u \mapsto e^{-I_{4}\theta}u$, a rotation of the affine parameter into the plane $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, the contour rotation that the stored ground-state theorem uses. At $\theta = \pi$ the factor is $-I_{4}$ and $e^{-I_{4}\pi}u = -u$: the half-generator $u > 0$ is carried to $u < 0$. At $\theta = 2\pi$ the factor is $-1$, the spinor sign, and at $4\pi$ it is $+1$, the rotor period $4\pi$ with conjugation period $2\pi$ of the stored Euclidean rotor. On a Mellin mode the continued multiplier is $e^{-I_{4}w(I_{4}\theta)} = e^{w\theta}$, real: the continuation rescales the modes, which is the defining property of the rotor flow (the series' note on its two boost flows), so the rotor flow is the imaginary-rapidity continuation of $\alpha$. Under (N$_{+}$) Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(ii) shows the restriction of the ground state to each half-generator is the continuum I-state for this boost at rapidity period $2\pi$, so the strip of this continuation closes at $\theta = 2\pi$ and nowhere before.

\emph{(v)} $\{a(D_{\lambda}f), a^{*}(g)\} = \ip{g}{D_{\lambda}f}$ by the CAR, and Cauchy-Schwarz with unitarity gives $|\ip{g}{D_{\lambda}f}| \le \|f\|\,\|g\|$ for every real $\lambda$. For normalised indicators of cells $[a, b)$ and $[c, d)$ in $u > 0$ the overlap is $e^{-\lambda/2}|[e^{\lambda}a, e^{\lambda}b)\cap[c, d)|/\sqrt{(b - a)(d - c)}$; the check verifies the bound at every tested pair and rapidity and, for two fixed unit cells at the edge, the exact value $e^{-\lambda/2}$, which falls to $0$. For linear elements the anticommutator is a scalar, so for $A = a(f)$, $B = a^{*}(g)$ and any state $\omega$, $\omega(\{A(\lambda), B\}^{*}\{A(\lambda), B\}) = |\ip{g}{D_{\lambda}f}|^{2} \le \|f\|^{2}\|g\|^{2}$: no out-of-time-order growth, real-time exponent $\limsup_{t\to\infty} t^{-1}\ln|\{A(\kappa t), B\}| \le 0$, and it is $0$ for pairs with $\ip{g}{D_{\lambda}f}$ not decaying exponentially, for instance a Mellin packet whose density in $w$ is the indicator of an interval, whose overlap with itself is the Fourier transform of that indicator and falls only as $1/\lambda$. The general quasi-free statement is Proposition \claimref{synthesis.number-diagonal-dynamics-spreads-nothing}'s conclusion transferred from the windows. What does grow at rate $\kappa$ is support: at $e^{\lambda} = N$ the unit cell at the edge covers exactly $N$ unit cells (checked for several $N$), Proposition \claimref{synthesis.affine-cell-boost-spreads-without-scrambling}(i).

\emph{(vi)} The setup of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises} (the boost $\beta_{t}$, with the Killing field $\kappa u\,\partial_{u}$ on the generators) shows that the Schwarzschild Killing flow and the de Sitter static flow act on the affine parameter of their horizon generators as $u \mapsto e^{\pm\kappa t}u$. A quasi-free automorphism of $\CAR(L^{2}(\R, du)\otimes K)$ that acts on the argument by this dilation and on values by a constant factor is unitary only with the weight $e^{\mp\kappa t/2}$, by the computation of (ii); so the time evolution there is $\alpha_{\pm\kappa t}$ and (iii) to (v) hold as stated. That the constant factor is the boost of a frame carried along the generator is the reading of (ii) in that frame; for every Killing horizon with nonzero surface gravity, off its bifurcation surface and whatever the field strength, it is derived in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.killing-horizon-value-factor-is-the-generator-boost}, which rests on (i) and (ii) here and is cited, not used as a premise.

The conclusion. By (ii) and (iii) the time evolution of the continuum is the single group $\alpha_{\kappa t}$, and by (iv) the rotor flow is its continuation in the rapidity. The statement of Assumption \claimref{scrambling.spreading-law-chaos-bound-saturated} that ``every intrinsic clock runs at the modular rate and the expansion rate is exactly $2\pi T$'' becomes: the one group $\alpha$ has real-time support-dilation rate $\kappa$ by (v), and its KMS period in Killing time is $\beta = 2\pi/\kappa$ under (N$_{+}$) by (iv), so $\kappa = 2\pi/\beta$ is one identity of one group (for Schwarzschild $\kappa = 1/4M$, $\beta = 8\pi M$; the check also confirms that the doubled period of the stored independence model breaks it). On the windows the same identity is premise (R), as the stored phase-flow result shows. The real-time Lyapunov exponent of this quasi-free dynamics on linear elements is $0 < 2\pi T$, so the chaos bound is met with room, not saturated, contrary to the earlier sentence of that assumption, since corrected, and $t_{*} = \kappa^{-1}\ln N = (\beta/2\pi)\ln N$ counts $\ln N$ units of rapidity: the time for one affine cell to spread over $N$. Nothing in the comparison needs a second evolution. The rate in (v) is fixed by $\alpha$ alone, whatever the state; the period is fixed by the state. By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}(ii) and the continuum I-state of Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state-continuum}, the quasi-free state of symbol $(1 + e^{2\pi c\,w})^{-1}$ on the Mellin modes of a half-generator is KMS for $\alpha$ at rapidity period $2\pi c$ for every $c > 0$, so the same group with the same rate $\kappa$ has KMS period $2\pi c/\kappa$ in that state, and $\kappa\beta = 2\pi$ holds exactly when $c = 1$, the value (N$_{+}$) forces. $\square$
