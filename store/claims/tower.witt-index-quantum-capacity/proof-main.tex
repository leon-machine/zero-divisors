\emph{Upper bound.} Let $(a_i, b_i)_{i \leq m}$ be vectors satisfying the CAR. Reading the relations through the Clifford relation on vectors (\claimref{std-algebra.vector-squares}): $\langle a_i, a_j \rangle = 0$ for all $i, j$ (including $i = j$), and $\langle a_i, b_j \rangle = \tfrac12 \delta_{ij}$. The span of the $a_i$ is therefore totally isotropic, and the $a_i$ are independent: a vanishing combination $\sum c_i a_i = 0$ pairs against $b_j$ to give $c_j/2 = 0$. So $m \leq$ the maximal totally isotropic dimension, which for a nondegenerate real form of signature $(p,q)$ is $\min(p,q)$: diagonalise, $V = P \perp N$ with $P$ positive definite of dimension $p$ and $N$ negative definite of dimension $q$; a totally isotropic $U$ meets $P$ trivially (a nonzero $v \in U \cap P$ would have $Q(v) > 0$ and $Q(v) = 0$ at once), so the projection $U \to N$ along $P$ is injective and $\dim U \leq q$; symmetrically $\dim U \leq p$; and $\min(p,q)$ is attained by the null spans of $\min(p,q)$ hyperbolic pairs (the classical bound, cf.\ \cite[Ch.~I]{Lam2005}).

\emph{Attainment.} Split off $\min(p,q)$ orthogonal hyperbolic planes from $(V, Q)$ (Witt decomposition, \claimref{std-algebra.witt-decomposition}) and take their normalised null pairs, as in Proposition \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors}. Along the tower, the form of signature $(p{+}k, q{+}k)$ has Witt index $\min(p,q) + k$, and each rung contributes its own plane to the attaining family.
