\emph{Setting.} Realise the two rung planes in $\Cl(2,2)$ with orthonormal $e_{a}$, $f_{a}$ ($e_{a}^{2} = 1$, $f_{a}^{2} = -1$) and $v_{a} = \tfrac12(e_{a} + f_{a})$, $w_{a} = \tfrac12(e_{a} - f_{a})$; then $v_{a}^{2} = w_{a}^{2} = 0$ and $\{v_{a}, w_{b}\} = \delta_{ab}$, the tower's CAR on these planes (Fact \claimref{field-theory.imported-tower-limit}), and $\mathcal{A} = \Cl(2,2)$, sixteen-dimensional, with parity the grade parity. The bilinears $t_{j} = \sum v_{a}(\tfrac12\sigma_{j})_{ab}w_{b}$ satisfy $[t_{j}, v_{c}] = \sum_{a}(\tfrac12\sigma_{j})_{ac}v_{a}$ (from $[v_{a}w_{b}, v_{c}] = \delta_{bc}v_{a}$), so their commutators realise the slot mixing on the $v_{a}$ as the doublet and on the $w_{a}$ as the conjugate doublet of \claimref{standard-model.ten-transported-ambient-space-doublets-are}. The check uses the real elements $t_{1}$, $t_{3}$ and $t_{2}' = [t_{3}, t_{1}] = I_{4}t_{2}$ (coefficients read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, as below), verifies $[t_{3}, t_{2}'] = t_{1}$, and forms the Casimir $C(x) = [t_{1},[t_{1},x]] + [t_{3},[t_{3},x]] - [t_{2}',[t_{2}',x]]$, which is $\sum_{j}\mathrm{ad}_{t_{j}}^{2}$ because $\mathrm{ad}_{t_{2}}^{2} = -\mathrm{ad}_{t_{2}'}^{2}$. The coefficients of $c$ lie in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, which commutes with the rung vectors (four sign changes past $\gamma_{0}, \ldots, \gamma_{3}$), so every statement below holds verbatim with $I_{4}$-complex coefficients; the check uses real coefficients.

\emph{(1).} $c$ is a combination of vectors, so odd. If $\omega\circ\theta = \omega$ for the grading automorphism $\theta$, then $\omega(c) = \omega(\theta(c)) = -\omega(c) = 0$. The Fock state annihilated by $w_{4}, w_{5}$ is grading invariant (it is $x \mapsto \langle PxP\rangle_{0}/\langle P\rangle_{0}$ with the even projector $P = w_{4}v_{4}w_{5}v_{5}$; the check confirms it vanishes on every odd blade and is normalised). $c^{2} = \sum\alpha_{a}\beta_{b}\{v_{a}, w_{b}\} = \alpha^{\mathsf T}\beta = Q(c)$ and $\{c^{*}, c\} = |\alpha|^{2} + |\beta|^{2} = 2N(c)$ by the CAR. With $w_{a}\Omega = 0$ the only nonzero two-point values are $\omega(w_{a}v_{b}) = \delta_{ab}$, so $\omega(c^{*}c) = \sum|\alpha_{a}|^{2}\omega(w_{a}v_{a}) = |\alpha|^{2}$ and $\omega(cc^{*}) = |\beta|^{2}$; hence $m = \tfrac12(|\alpha|^{2} - |\beta|^{2}) = \tfrac12\omega(c^{*}c - cc^{*})$. The check evaluates all of these exactly at two points.

\emph{(2).} The even part of $\mathcal{A}$ is spanned by the products of an even number of the $v_{a}, w_{a}$, which as $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ modules are summands of tensor products of an even number of doublets, so they carry integer isospin; the odd part, of an odd number, carries half-integer isospin, and since the Casimir has eigenvalue $j(j+1)$, the possible values are $0, 2$ on the even part and half-integer values on the odd part; which values occur is settled by the computation. The check computes the Casimir on all sixteen basis blades and finds, exactly by rank, $0$ on a five-dimensional subspace (the scalars $1$, the total mode number, $v_{4}v_{5}$, $w_{4}w_{5}$ and $v_{4}w_{4}v_{5}w_{5}$ span it), $2$ on a three-dimensional one (the traceless bilinears $t_{j}$), and $\tfrac34$ on the whole odd part. A charge-preserving vacuum of the rung field has a doublet order parameter (\claimref{synthesis.rung-field-on-the-lift-locus}(1): the stabiliser of $c_{\lambda}$ in $U(2)$ is one circle, so the vacuum value is a doublet direction, not a singlet or triplet); an expectation value of an even element of $\mathcal{A}$ transforms in the even module, isospin $0$ or $1$, so it is never that order parameter. (An isospin-one condensate $\omega(t_{j}) \ne 0$ would be a triplet vacuum, a different breaking pattern.)

\emph{(3).} Let $g$ anticommute with the four rung vectors, so it commutes with every even element, in particular with the $t_{j}$, and $g^{2} = 1$. Then $x_{a} = v_{a}g$ is even and $[t_{j}, x_{a}] = [t_{j}, v_{a}]g$, a doublet with Casimir $\tfrac34$. With $x_{a}^{*} = g w_{a}$: $x_{a}x_{b}^{*} = v_{a}g^{2}w_{b} = v_{a}w_{b}$ and $x_{b}^{*}x_{a} = gw_{b}v_{a}g = w_{b}v_{a}$, so $\{x_{a}, x_{b}^{*}\} = \delta_{ab}$; $x_{a}x_{b} = -v_{a}v_{b}$, so $\{x_{a}, x_{b}\} = -\{v_{a}, v_{b}\} = 0$ and $x_{a}^{2} = 0$; and $[x_{a}, x_{a}^{*}] = v_{a}w_{a} - w_{a}v_{a} = 2v_{a}w_{a} - 1$. These are the relations of \claimref{synthesis.even-slots-need-a-spectator-generator}(iv) for slot operators, here read for the rung modes. The hypotheses on $g$ are met by $\gamma_{0}$: it squares to $1$ and anticommutes with the rung vectors, which are orthogonal to spacetime. But $\gamma_{0}$ anticommutes with $\gamma_{1}$, so $\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}$ commutes with $v_{a}$ and anticommutes with $\gamma_{0}$, giving $[\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}, v_{a}\gamma_{0}] = 2\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}v_{a}\gamma_{0} \ne 0$: $v_{a}\gamma_{0}$ is moved by the boost rotors, not a Lorentz scalar. A Lorentz-scalar even doublet therefore needs $g$ orthogonal to spacetime as well as to the rung planes (the check verifies the $\gamma_{0}$ case in $\Cl(3,5)$).

\emph{Reading (argued).} A bosonic doublet with $[b, b^{*}] \to 1$ therefore needs many modes: with $n$ momentum modes of a doublet fermion field paired with $n$ modes of a singlet one, $b_{a} = n^{-1/2}\sum_{j}v_{a,j}u_{j}$ is the doublet analogue of the quasi-spin of \claimref{field-theory.how-close-tower-comes-ccr}, reaching the canonical commutation relations only as $n \to \infty$ on bounded occupation. The two rung planes are fixed directions of the transported ambient space, one null pair each, not such a field. So within the tower the remaining composite route is a doublet-singlet fermion condensate of the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio kind (\claimref{field-theory.composite-scalars-mean-field-level}, at mean field), not a composite of the rung modes; whether a light composite of several fermion species exists is not decided here. The conclusion that the rung field is an elementary (palatial) field is argued, not proved: a separate locality argument for the attached free fields (\claimref{synthesis.locality-of-the-attached-free-fields-fixes-their-statistics}) reaches it for a local elementary rung scalar. Transport-convenient: the identification of the rung field's values with tower modes is the series'; the representation theory is standard.
