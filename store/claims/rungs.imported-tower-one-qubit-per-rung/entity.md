## formal
The transport principle completes $\Cl(p,q)$ to $\Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ by adjoining one hyperbolic pair, and the completed algebra carries a null cone of its own, so the completion iterates: the $k$-th rung is $T_{k} = \Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k)$, with adjoined null pairs $v_{j}, w_{j}$ satisfying, as vectors, $v_{i}v_{j} + v_{j}v_{i} = 0$, $w_{i}w_{j} + w_{j}w_{i} = 0$ and $v_{i}w_{j} + w_{j}v_{i} = \delta_{ij}$. Inside $T_{k}$ the subalgebra $B_{k}$ generated by the $2k$ plane generators is $\Cl(k,k) \cong \mathrm{M}_{2^{k}}(\R)$, the twisted ground generators generate a commuting copy $A_{k} \cong \Cl(p,q)$, and multiplication $A_{k} \otimes B_{k} \to T_{k}$ is an isomorphism, $T_{k} \cong \Cl(p,q) \otimes \mathrm{M}_{2^{k}}(\R)$. With $W_{k} = \operatorname{span}\{v_{1}, \ldots, v_{k}\}$ the Chevalley action makes $\Lambda^{\bullet}W_{k}$, of dimension $2^{k}$, an irreducible $B_{k}$-module, the vacuum $1$ annihilated by every $w_{j}$ and cyclic for the $v_{j}$, and each rung doubles it: $\Lambda^{\bullet}W_{k+1} = \Lambda^{\bullet}W_{k} \otimes \Lambda^{\bullet}(\R v_{k+1})$, one qubit per rung. The Witt index rises by exactly one per rung, and the Witt index of a real quadratic space is the maximal number of anticommuting mode pairs realisable by vectors. On each complexified floor there is a unique conjugate-linear antiautomorphism $*$ fixing the positive frame generators and negating the negative ones, under which the ladder pairs are mutually adjoint, $v_{j}^{*} = w_{j}$. \emph{Provenance:} \cite[Def.~2.2, Thm.~3.2, Prop.~4.1, Thms.~4.2, 4.3, Lem.~5.4]{ButlerT3}.

## technical
The transport completes $\Cl(p,q)$ to $\Cl(p+1,q+1)$ by adjoining a hyperbolic pair and iterates, so the $k$-th rung is $T_k = \Cl(p+k,q+k)$, with adjoined null pairs obeying the CAR as vectors: $v_iv_j + v_jv_i = 0$, $w_iw_j + w_jw_i = 0$, $v_iw_j + w_jv_i = \delta_{ij}$. The plane generators give $B_k \cong \Cl(k,k) \cong \Mat_{2^k}(\R)$, the twisted ground generators a commuting copy $A_k \cong \Cl(p,q)$, and $T_k \cong \Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^k}(\R)$.

The Chevalley action makes $\Lambda^\bullet W_k$, with $W_k = \operatorname{span}\{v_1, \ldots, v_k\}$, an irreducible $B_k$-module of dimension $2^k$ with vacuum $1$, doubling at each rung: one qubit per rung. The Witt index rises by one per rung and equals the maximal number of anticommuting mode pairs realisable by vectors, and on each complexified floor a unique conjugate-linear antiautomorphism makes the ladder pairs mutually adjoint, $v_j^* = w_j$.

## lay
Each completion step adds one pair of directions that behaves exactly like the creation and destruction operators of one fermion. After k steps the algebra splits into the original algebra times a matrix algebra describing k such fermion modes, and the space of states doubles with each step, so each step adds one qubit. The number of independent fermion modes that ordinary directions can carry grows by exactly one per step.

## one-liner
The $k$-th rung $\Cl(p+k,q+k)$ is $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^k}(\R)$; its adjoined null pairs satisfy the CAR as vectors, are mutually adjoint, and act on a Fock module that doubles at each rung, one qubit per rung.
