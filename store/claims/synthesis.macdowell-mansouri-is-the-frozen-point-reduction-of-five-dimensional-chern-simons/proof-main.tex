\emph{Language.} The proof is in geometric algebra throughout: the values of every form are multivectors of $\Cl(2,4)$, and the forms on the five-dimensional base are multivector fields of a flat five-dimensional background (the $d$-dimensional clause of Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}): $dx^{\mu}$, $dy$ are the background vectors $e^{0}, \dots, e^{4}$, the exterior product of the base parts is their outer product, $d$ is $\nabla \wedge$, and an exact form is a $\nabla \wedge$-derivative. The imported formulas below are read in this language; their content is unchanged.

\emph{Imported input.} Five-dimensional Chern-Simons gravity (Chamseddine 1989 and 1990, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-five-dimensional-chern-simons-gravity}), with the transgression and variation formulas of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-chern-weil-and-chern-simons-forms}(b), (c): for a connection $A$ with values in the bivectors of $\Cl(2,4)$ the Chern-Simons five-form of the cubic invariant $P$ satisfies $d\,\mathrm{CS}_{5}(A) = P(F, F, F)$ and $\delta\,\mathrm{CS}_{5} = 3P(\delta A; F, F)$ up to exact forms, and, with $A = \omega + E$ split by the Stelle-West vector $V$ ($\omega$ in the stabiliser of $V$), the transgression from $\omega$ gives $\mathrm{CS}_{5}(A) - \mathrm{CS}_{5}(\omega) = 3\int_{0}^{1}P(E; F_{t}, F_{t})\,dt$ modulo exact forms, with $F_{t} = R + t\,DE + t^{2}K$. The invariant $P$ needs the vector $V$ exactly once among its six legs, $E$ carries $V$, and $\omega$, $R$ and $K$ do not while $DE$ does; so $\mathrm{CS}_{5}(\omega)$ and every term with $DE$ vanish and $L_{5} = 3\int_{0}^{1}P(E; (R + t^{2}K)^{2})\,dt$. Expanding $(R + t^{2}K)^{2} = R^{2} + 2t^{2}RK + t^{4}K^{2}$ (two-forms commute, and the grade-six part of a product of three bivectors is their outer product, symmetric in them) and integrating, $3\int_{0}^{1}(1, 2t^{2}, t^{4})\,dt = (3, 2, \tfrac35)$; the check computes these three integrals with the series' polynomial routines. In particular torsion does not enter $L_{5}$.

\emph{Orientation and standard input.} The chart is that of the stored check of Theorem \claimref{dark-energy.frozen-point-makes-stelle-west-vector}: $\nO = \tfrac12(e_{4} + e_{5})$, $\nInf = e_{4} - e_{5}$, $I_{6} = e_{0}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}e_{5}$, and the base parts of two-forms multiply by the outer product of background vectors (a sum over ordered planes; the check recomputes (i) with the outer product of $\Cl(1,4)$ in place of the Grassmann product and finds the same five-form); the opposite orientation of the null pair flips the sign of every $\epsilon(I, V, \cdot, \cdot)$ value below. The multiplicities $1$, $3$, $5$ in (i) and the passage from five-dimensional Chern-Simons gravity to a four-dimensional curvature-squared density are the standard reduction of Lovelock densities along a line (the note of Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-five-dimensional-chern-simons-gravity}); the content specific to the series is the choice of the frozen point as the reduced direction and of the Stelle-West vector as the parent's vector.

\emph{The translational square.} For $u, w \in V^{\perp}$, $(u\wedge V)\times(w\wedge V) = -Q(V)\,u\wedge w$ (commutator product; the term in $\ip{u}{w}$ cancels). With $E = \theta\wedge V/Q(V)$ this gives $K = E\times E = -Q(V)^{-1}\theta\wedge\theta = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\theta\wedge\theta$, the shift of the Lorentz block in Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-macdowell-mansouri-theorem-stelle-west} and Theorem \claimref{dark-energy.frozen-point-makes-stelle-west-vector}(ii); the check confirms $K_{4} = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\theta_{4}\wedge\theta_{4}$ on samples, with $Q(I) = \Lambda/3$, $Q(V) = -3/\Lambda$ and $\ip{I}{V} = 0$.

\emph{(i)} On the ansatz, $E = E_{4} + \lambda\,dy\,(I\wedge V)/Q(V)$ and $K = K_{4} + K_{\times}$, where $K_{\times} = \lambda\sum_{\mu}(E_{4,\mu}\times(I\wedge V)/Q(V))\,dx^{\mu}\wedge dy$ is linear in $dy$ and carries $I$ once. The top form of $L_{5}$ contains $dy$ exactly once, and its value needs $I$ and $V$ exactly once each, so the surviving terms are: $dy$ from $E$, with $R$ and $K_{4}$ in the other slots; or $dy$ from one factor $K_{\times}$, with $E_{4}$ in the first slot. By multilinearity and the reordering of the four vector legs $\theta_{\mu}, V, \theta_{\nu}, I$ (the form sign and the Clifford sign compensate) the second kind reproduces the first with multiplicity $2$ per occurrence of $K$: $P(E; RR)$, $P(E; RK)$, $P(E; KK)$ reduce to $(\lambda/Q(V))$ times $\epsilon(I, V, R, R)$, $3\,\epsilon(I, V, R, K_{4})$, $5\,\epsilon(I, V, K_{4}, K_{4})$, wedged with $dy$. With the coefficients $(3, 2, \tfrac35)$ the sum is $(3\lambda/Q(V))\big(\epsilon(I, V, R, R) + 2\epsilon(I, V, R, K_{4}) + \epsilon(I, V, K_{4}, K_{4})\big) = (3\lambda/Q(V))\,\epsilon(I, V, F, F)$, with $F = R + K_{4}$. The check verifies the multiplicities and the identity exactly on pseudo-random rational frames and curvatures at $\Lambda \in \{3, -\tfrac32, 12\}$ and $\lambda \in \{1, \tfrac23\}$, with all three pieces nonzero.

\emph{(ii)} By (i), $aP(E; RR) + bP(E; RK) + cP(E; KK)$ reduces to $(\lambda/Q(V))(a\,r + 3b\,k + 5c\,q)$ with $r, k, q$ the three four-dimensional densities. This is $a$ times $\epsilon(I, V, R + sK_{4}, R + sK_{4}) = r + 2s\,k + s^{2}q$ exactly when $3b = 2as$ and $5c = as^{2}$, that is $s = 3b/(2a)$ and $9b^{2} = 20ac$. At $s = 1$ (the Lorentz block of the frozen-point functional) $(a, b, c) = a(1, \tfrac23, \tfrac15)$, a multiple of $(3, 2, \tfrac35)$. Other five-dimensional Lovelock combinations (for example Einstein-Hilbert with volume, $a = 0$) do not reduce to the square.

\emph{(iii)} By the imported variation formula the equations are $P(J; F_{5}, F_{5}) = 0$ for every bivector $J$. On the torsion-free ansatz $F_{5} = R + K_{4} + K_{\times}$ has no leg along $V$, so for $J \in \Lambda^{2}(V^{\perp})$ the six legs cannot contain $V$ and the equation is empty. For $J = u\wedge V$ with $u \in W$, the legs must contain $I$ once, which only $K_{\times}$ supplies: the equation is $2P(u\wedge V; F, K_{\times})$, linear in $F$ and in the frame, the frame equation of the four-dimensional functional wedged with $dy$; on an Einstein field with the matching $\Lambda$, $F$ is (minus) the Weyl curvature (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.gtg-tractor-gauge-field-reproduces-tractor-connection}) and the frame equation vanishes (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.macdowell-mansouri-principle-fixes-the-trace-free-part}). For $J = I\wedge V$, no further $I$ or $V$ may appear, so only $F\wedge F$ contributes and the equation is $\epsilon(I, V, F, F) = 0$; since $L_{5}$ is linear in $\lambda$ by (i), this is $(Q(V)/3)\,\partial L_{5}/\partial\lambda$, and it remains the $\lambda$ equation when $\lambda$ depends on $x$, because $d\lambda\wedge dy$ enters only the translational block, which $L_{5}$ does not contain. The check evaluates all fifteen equations at a point with the frame the identity, for $W(B) = m(B + 3\sigma B\sigma)$, $\sigma = e_{1}e_{0}$ (the Schwarzschild Weyl curvature at radius $r$ along $e_{1}$, $m = -M/(2r^{3})$; trace-free and satisfying the first Bianchi identity, both checked) at $m \in \{1, -\tfrac23\}$ and $\Lambda \in \{3, -\tfrac32\}$: the frame and $V$-free equations vanish, and $\epsilon(I, V, F, F) = 48m^{2}$, a quarter of the Kretschmann invariant $192m^{2} = C_{abcd}C^{abcd}$, so $12M^{2}/r^{6}$, independent of $\Lambda$; this agrees with the general identity $\epsilon(I, V, F, F) = \tfrac14 C_{abcd}C^{abcd}$ times the volume form of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.frozen-point-integrand-on-tractor-transport-is-weyl-squared}(iii) (same orientation), and the scalar equation is the one a released Stelle-West norm obeys (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.stelle-west-norm-is-no-local-output}(iv)), the Lagrangian being of degree one in the scale in both cases. For the type N field $W(B) = \langle BN_{1}\rangle_{0}N_{1} - \langle BN_{2}\rangle_{0}N_{2}$, $N_{i} = (e_{0} + e_{3})\wedge e_{i}$ (also checked Weyl), the value is $0$. On generic sample curvatures the check confirms that the $I\wedge V$ equation equals the four-dimensional density and $(Q(V)/3)\,\partial L_{5}/\partial\lambda$, and that the frame equations are not identically zero. (In components, a labelled reading: $\epsilon(I, V, F, F) \propto \epsilon_{abcd}\epsilon^{efgh}F^{ab}{}_{ef}F^{cd}{}_{gh}$, which for a Weyl tensor is proportional to $C_{abcd}C^{abcd}$ since its double dual is minus itself.)

\emph{(iv)} $V^{\perp}$ is spanned by $e_{0}, \ldots, e_{3}$ and $I$, on which the Gram form is $\mathrm{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1) \oplus (\Lambda/3)$; its signature is $(2,3)$ for $\Lambda > 0$ and $(1,4)$ for $\Lambda < 0$, and $I^{\perp}$ has signature $(1,4)$, $(2,3)$ respectively (computed by congruence at $\Lambda \in \{3, \tfrac34, -3, -\tfrac97\}$). For $\Lambda > 0$ the four-dimensional group is the stabiliser of the reduced direction, $SO(1,4)$, as the frozen-point functional requires, but the five-dimensional frame space contains the timelike $I$. In $\Cl(1,5)$, two orthogonal unit vectors of square $-1$ give a Lorentzian $(1,4)$ frame space and a de Sitter $(1,4)$ complement of the reduced direction (checked). $\Cl(1,5)$ is the repair with $\nu^{2} = -1$ of Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iv).

\emph{(v) (Argued.)} If the five-dimensional action is $\kappa_{5}\int L_{5}$, then by (i) the four-dimensional coefficient of $\int\epsilon(I, V, F, F)$ is $3\kappa_{5}Q(V)^{-1}\int\lambda\,dy = -\kappa_{5}\Lambda\int\lambda\,dy$; with the proper length $P = |Q(I)|^{1/2}\int\lambda\,dy$ of the reduced direction this is $\mp 3\kappa_{5}P/\ell$. The four-dimensional coefficient is proportional to $1/(G\Lambda)$, so $N^{2} = 3/(G\Lambda)$ equals a fixed number times $\kappa_{5}P/\ell$. A quantised level fixes $\kappa_{5}$ up to an integer (for a compact form of the group; for $SU(2,2)$ the standard argument does not apply), and $P/\ell$ is free, so the level does not fix the coupling. No value of the level is compared with $N$: the relation re-expresses $N$ through $\kappa_{5}$ and $P/\ell$ and predicts nothing.
