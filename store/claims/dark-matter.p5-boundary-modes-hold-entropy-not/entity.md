## formal
The ascended boundary mode space stores and returns information \cite[Thm.~11.5, Cor.~11.7]{ButlerT5}, quantified by its deficit theorem \cite[Thm.~9.3]{ButlerT5}, not global charge: Gauss-law charges are returned per tower by discharge (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-schwinger-discharge-charged-hole}), baryon number is returned by no hole in any tower. This is what the imported flux \cite[Assembly~5.4]{ButlerT7} already implies here: a hole emits baryons only once $T_{\mathrm{H}} > m_{p}$, below $M = M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}/8\pi m_{p} \approx 6 \times 10^{36}$ GeV, returning below $10^{-20}$ of a solar-mass progenitor's baryons. Declared premise, decisive for Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.parallel-boundary-mode-spaces-one-wall}.

## technical
The ascended boundary mode space at a black hole's singular locus stores and returns information, quantified by its deficit theorem, but not global charge. Gauss-law charges are returned tower by tower through discharge, while baryon number is returned by no hole in any tower. The imported Hawking flux already implies this here: a hole emits baryons only once $T_{\mathrm{H}} > m_{p}$, that is below $M = M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}/8\pi m_{p} \approx 6 \times 10^{36}$ GeV, returning less than $10^{-20}$ of a solar-mass progenitor's baryons. It is a declared premise.

## lay
When matter falls into a black hole, the information it carried can come back out in the hole's radiation, but this assumption says the hole does not give back its count of protons and neutrons. A black hole could only emit protons once it was tiny and very hot, by which point almost all its mass is already gone. The paper adopts this as a premise.

## one-liner
Premise: black-hole boundary modes return information and Gauss-law charges but not global charges such as baryon number.
