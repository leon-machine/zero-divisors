Everything is computed in Gauge Theory Gravity (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(ii), (iv) for the equivalence with Einstein's equations of the derived metric). On a constant orthonormal physical frame $\{e_{a}\}$ the field is given by the background vector fields $f^{a}$ with $\hb^{T}(f^{a}) = e^{a}$: $f^{0} = N\,\nabla T$ and $f^{l} = a_{l}\,\sigma_{l}$, where $\sigma_{l}$ is the background vector field whose components are the left-invariant form of the statement (in Euler labels $(\psi, \theta, \phi)$, $\sigma_{1} = \sin\psi\,\nabla\theta - \cos\psi\sin\theta\,\nabla\phi$, $\sigma_{2} = \cos\psi\,\nabla\theta + \sin\psi\sin\theta\,\nabla\phi$, $\sigma_{3} = \nabla\psi + \cos\theta\,\nabla\phi$), so that $\nabla\wedge\sigma_{k} = -\sigma_{i}\wedge\sigma_{j}$ for $(i, j, k)$ cyclic (the structure relation $d\sigma_{1} = -\sigma_{2}\wedge\sigma_{3}$ read with $d = \nabla\wedge$). Here $\hb^{T}(\nabla T) = e^{0}/N$ and $\hb^{T}(\sigma_{l}) = e^{l}/a_{l}$. With $D_{a}e_{b} = \Omega(a)\cdot e_{b}$ for a bivector $\Omega(a)$, the identity $e^{b}\wedge(\Omega\cdot e_{b}) = -2\Omega$ for bivectors gives $\Omega(a) = -\tfrac12 e^{b}\wedge D_{a}e_{b}$, the sign of the Definition, in which a Kasner field has $\Omega(e_{i}) = -(p_{i}/\tau)B_{i}$.

(i) \emph{The gauge field from the torsion equation.} The torsion equation of the definition, read on the constant frame, is $\hb^{T}(\nabla\wedge f^{a}) = \sum_{b}e^{b}\wedge(\Omega(e_{b})\cdot e^{a})$, linear and injective in $\Omega$ (twenty-four equations for twenty-four components), so it has one solution. Its left side is $\hb^{T}(\nabla N\wedge\nabla T) = 0$ for $a = 0$ ($N$ depends on $T$ alone) and, for $a = k$,
\[
\hb^{T}\bigl(\nabla a_{k}\wedge\sigma_{k} + a_{k}\nabla\wedge\sigma_{k}\bigr) = H_{k}\,e^{0}\wedge e^{k} - \mu_{k}\,e^{i}\wedge e^{j}, \qquad H_{k} = \alpha_{k}'/N,\ \mu_{k} = a_{k}/(a_{i}a_{j}),
\]
and the solution is $\Omega(e_{0}) = 0$, $\Omega(e_{k}) = -H_{k}B_{k} + \tfrac12(\mu_{i} + \mu_{j} - \mu_{k})\,e_{i}e_{j}$, the display of the statement. The script builds the field on exact second-order jets in the labels $(T, \psi, \theta, \phi)$ at the point $\psi = 0$, $\theta = \pi/2$, where every trigonometric jet is rational (the field is invariant under the left translations, which act transitively on each slice, so one point suffices), treating $a_{l}$, $\alpha_{l}'$, $\alpha_{l}''$ as independent rationals (two generic choices); it solves the torsion equation exactly from the coframe alone with the shared engine, finds the linear map of rank $24$, and finds these bivectors.

\emph{The field equations.} The field strength of the solved gauge field, $R(a\wedge b) = a\cdot\nabla\Omega(b) - b\cdot\nabla\Omega(a) + \Omega(a)\times\Omega(b)$, gives the store's curvature $\mathcal{R}(B) = -R(\hb(B))$ on the physical frame planes, and its Ricci map $\mathcal{R}(b) = \partial_{a}\cdot\mathcal{R}(a\wedge b)$. Written with $\Ric^{\circ}(u, v) = -u\cdot\mathcal{R}(v)$ (dictionary C11), the script finds, exactly at both jets, $N^{2}\Ric^{\circ}(e_{l}, e_{l}) = \alpha_{l}'' + (G^{-1}dV)_{l}$, $N^{2}\Ric^{\circ}(e_{0}, e_{0}) = -\sum\alpha'' + 2C$ and vanishing off-diagonal values. Since $V$ is a sum of exponentials $e^{2\omega(\alpha)}$ with $\omega(1, 1, 1) = 2$, $\sum_{l}(G^{-1}dV)_{l} = -\tfrac12 dV(1, 1, 1) = -2V$; so $\mathcal{R}(a) = 0$ is $\alpha'' = -G^{-1}dV$ together with $-\sum\alpha'' + 2C = 2(C - V) = 0$. This is the Bianchi IX system of Misner's minisuperspace (cited from memory; the check, not the citation, is the proof); its one-wall truncation $V = \tfrac14e^{4\alpha_{1}}$ is the Bianchi II system, and the same harmonic-time form for matter potentials is \claimref{synthesis.kasner-wall-matter-maxwell-bounces-stiff-moves-residue}.

(ii) With $q = t^{2} + l^{2}$ and $' = d/dT = 2lUq\,d/dt$: $\alpha_{1}' = \alpha_{2}' = 2ltU$, $\alpha_{3}' = lqU_{t}$, and $\alpha''$ follows by applying $2lUq\,d/dt$ once more; $e^{2\alpha_{1}} = q$ and $e^{2\alpha_{3}} = 4l^{2}U$. All of these are rational in $t$, and the script checks $\alpha'' + G^{-1}dV = 0$ and $C = V$ exactly at four rational $t$ with $U > 0$ for each of $(m, l) = (4, 3), (0, 1), (12, 5)$ (chosen so that $\sqrt{m^{2} + l^{2}}$ is rational), and that the equations fail when the mass in $a_{3}$ is changed by $\tfrac13$; with the shared engine it also builds the gauge field of (i) on the Taub-NUT data at five rational points where $a_{1}$ and $a_{3}$ are rational ($(m, l, t) = (2, 3, 4), (3, 4, 3), (6, 5, 12), (0, 1, 0), (5, 12, 5)$), solves the torsion equation and finds the Ricci map of the field strength zero, and nonzero when $\alpha_{3}''$ is changed by $\tfrac13$. The metric is the derived metric of a diagonal synchronous field with $N\,dT = dt/\sqrt U$. (That this is Taub's solution, written as in the Taub-NUT literature, is cited from memory; the field equations are verified, so it is not a premise.)

(iii) $U(t_{+}) = 0$ and $U_{t}(t_{+}) = -2\sqrt{m^{2} + l^{2}}/(t_{+}^{2} + l^{2}) < 0$, so near $t_{+}$, $U = |U_{t}(t_{+})|(t_{+} - t)(1 + O(t_{+} - t))$, the proper time to the locus $\tau = \int_{t}^{t_{+}}ds/\sqrt{U(s)}$ is finite, $\tau = 2\sqrt{(t_{+} - t)/|U_{t}(t_{+})|}\,(1 + O(t_{+} - t))$, and $a_{3} = 2l\sqrt U = l|U_{t}(t_{+})|\,\tau\,(1 + O(\tau^{2}))$, while $a_{1} = a_{2} \to \sqrt{t_{+}^{2} + l^{2}}$. With $\tau$ increasing away from the locus, $H_{3} = d\log a_{3}/d\tau$ satisfies $\tau H_{3} \to 1$, so the weights tend to $(0, 0, 1)$ and $\widehat\Omega_{\tau}(e_{k}) = (\tau H_{3})\cdot\Omega(e_{k})/H_{3}$ has the limit of $\Omega(e_{k})/H_{3}$. In $\Omega(e_{k})/H_{3}$ every ratio is rational: by (i), in harmonic time $H_{k}/H_{3} = \alpha_{k}'/\alpha_{3}'$, and $N$ times the rotation coefficient $\tfrac12(\mu_{i} + \mu_{j} - \mu_{k})$ of $\Omega(e_{k})$ is $\tfrac12(a_{i}^{2} + a_{j}^{2} - a_{k}^{2})$, so the ratio of that coefficient to $H_{3}$ is $\tfrac12(a_{i}^{2} + a_{j}^{2} - a_{k}^{2})/\alpha_{3}'$, with $\alpha_{3}' = lqU_{t}$ from (ii). The script evaluates exactly these expressions of (i) on the Taub-NUT data of (ii) at $t = t_{+}$. By hand: $H_{1}/H_{3} = 2tU/(qU_{t})$; for $\Omega(e_{1})$ and $\Omega(e_{2})$ the rotation ratio is $\pm\tfrac12 a_{3}^{2}/\alpha_{3}' = \pm 2lU/(qU_{t})$; for $\Omega(e_{3})$ it is $(q - 2l^{2}U)/(lqU_{t})$, in the orientation of $t$. At $t = t_{+}$ the first two vanish and the third is $1/(lU_{t}(t_{+})) = -(t_{+}^{2} + l^{2})/(2l\sqrt{m^{2} + l^{2}}) = -t_{+}/l$, using $t_{+}^{2} + l^{2} = 2\sqrt{m^{2} + l^{2}}\,t_{+}$; reversing the orientation of time to that of the Definition changes the sign of $H_{3}$ and not that of $\mu$, so $\kappa = t_{+}/l$. With $r = \sqrt{m^{2} + l^{2}}$, $\cos\varphi = m/r$, $\sin\varphi = l/r$, $\cot(\varphi/2) = (1 + \cos\varphi)/\sin\varphi = (r + m)/l = t_{+}/l$. The script checks these values exactly at $(m, l) = (4, 3), (0, 1), (12, 5)$ ($\kappa = 3, 1, 5$). In $\Cl(1,3)$, $IB_{3} = e_{1}e_{2}$, so the limit $-B_{3} + \kappa e_{1}e_{2}$ is $-(1 - \kappa I)B_{3}$; its square is $1 - \kappa^{2} - 2\kappa I$ (computed). The values on unit tangential directions are linear combinations of the three limits, so the Lie algebra they generate is spanned by the single element $-(1 - \kappa I)B_{3}$: the script closes the set $\{0, 0, X\}$ under commutators and finds dimension one, against dimension six for the Kasner values $-p_{i}B_{i}$ at $p = (-\tfrac13, \tfrac23, \tfrac23)$. The difference from the Taub Kasner model is $\kappa IB_{3}$ on $e_{3}$, nonzero, so the field is not admissible for that model (its weights also fail $p_{i} \neq 0$).

(iv) Along the whole approach the weights $\tau H_{l}$ tend to $(0, 0, 1)$, so there are no times $\tau_{k} \to 0$ at which the field is close to a Kasner model with $1 \le u \le 2$ (on that arc $\min|p_{i}| \ge \tfrac27$), and the hypothesis of clause (iii) of \claimref{synthesis.bkl-bounce-invariant-holonomy-and-bound} fails; its conclusion fails too, by (iii). The flat Kasner field $(1, 0, 0)$ is already such a field (its values $(-B_{1}, 0, 0)$ generate $\R B_{1}$, \claimref{synthesis.bkl-bounces-no-framewise-invariant}(ii)); Taub-NUT is a non-flat one, and its limit differs from the flat one by the duality term $\kappa IB_{3}$, the slices' rotation, which vanishes identically for the flat field. That the locus $t = t_{+}$ is a Cauchy horizon across which the field extends (into the NUT region) is the literature (Misner 1963, cited from memory) and is used only in the reading, not in (i) to (iv).
