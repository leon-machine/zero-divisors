\emph{Setting.} Every statement is about linear maps and quadratic forms on the real vector space $\Cl^{+}(1,3)$ with basis $1$, $\gamma_{0}\gamma_{i}$, $\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}$ ($i < j$), $I$. The script builds the $8 \times 8$ rational matrices of $L_{B}$, $L_{J}$, $L_{I}$, $L_{m}$ (into the odd part), $i_{H} = R_{I\sigma_{3}}$, $\Gamma$, of the projectors $\Lambda(k) = \tfrac12(1 + L_{B})$, $\Lambda(m) = \tfrac12(1 - L_{B})$, and the Gram matrices of the flux forms $\psi \mapsto \langle\psi\gamma_{0}\tilde{\psi}a\rangle_{0}$ for $a = k, m$. It decides each identity and each dimension by exact rank computation. A rotor carrying $\gamma_{0} \pm \gamma_{3}$ to another pair of null directions conjugates every map below to the corresponding map and preserves ranks, so the choice of $k$ and $m$ is no loss.

\emph{(i).} The algebra of this clause is Theorem \claimref{synthesis.canonical-part-of-a-spinor-on-a-null-plane}, clauses (i) and (ii), where its argument (formerly given here) now stands: $k\gamma_{0} = 1 + B$ and $m\gamma_{0} = 1 - B$, so $\Lambda(k) = \tfrac12(1 + B)$ and $\Lambda(m) = \tfrac12(1 - B)$ (Theorem \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation}(1) at $a_{0} = 1$); the Gram matrix of $\psi \mapsto J_{\psi}\cdot k$ is $2\Lambda(m)^{T}\Lambda(m)$ exactly, so $J_{\psi}\cdot k = 2|\Lambda(m)\psi|^{2}$, positive definite on the image of $\Lambda(m)$ (rank $4$) and zero on that of $\Lambda(k)$, and the flux through $N_{m}$ is $2|\Lambda(k)\psi|^{2}$; $\ker L_{m}$ and the $(-1)$-eigenspace of $L_{B}$ coincide, of dimension $4$. The script of this result still recomputes these identities. The canonical anticommutator of a spinor field on $N_{k}$ is the one of the components that carry the flux through $N_{k}$; this is the content of clause (i) of the result filed alongside, synthesis.boost-evolution-on-generators-is-one-unitary-group, which compresses the stored anticommutator $\chi(\nabla D)\gamma_{0}$ (Theorem \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation}(2)) to $\Lambda(m)$ and finds only the derivative along the generators, so $\Lambda(m)\psi$ is the canonical component with kernel a multiple of $\delta(u - v)\delta^{2}(x_{\perp} - y_{\perp})\Lambda(m)$. The spinor $\Lambda(k)$ of a plane wave of momentum $k$ (Theorem \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation}(1)) is constant along the generators of $N_{k}$, and its canonical data live on $N_{m}$. (This is the good component of light-front quantisation, Kogut and Soper, cited from memory as orientation.)

\emph{(ii).} With $B_{\lambda} = c + sB$, $c = \cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}$, $s = \sinh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}$, $B_{\lambda}\psi = (c \mp s)\psi$ on the $\mp1$ eigenspaces of $L_{B}$, that is $e^{-\lambda/2}$ on the image of $\Lambda(m)$ and $e^{\lambda/2}$ on that of $\Lambda(k)$. The script takes $c = \tfrac54$, $s = \tfrac34$ ($e^{\lambda/2} = 2$), checks $B_{\lambda}\tilde{B}_{\lambda} = 1$ and $B_{\lambda}k\tilde{B}_{\lambda} = 4k = e^{\lambda}k$, and checks $L_{B_{\lambda}}\Lambda(m) = \tfrac12\Lambda(m)$ and $L_{B_{\lambda}}\Lambda(k) = 2\Lambda(k)$ as matrix identities. The covariant action $\psi(x) \mapsto B_{\lambda}\psi(\tilde{B}_{\lambda}xB_{\lambda})$ transports the generator's affine parameter by $u \mapsto e^{-\lambda}u$, which multiplies $\delta(u - v)$ by $e^{\lambda}$. The anticommutator of the transformed field is therefore multiplied by the square of the value factor times $e^{\lambda}$: $(c - s)^{2}e^{\lambda} = 1$ on $\Lambda(m)$ and $(c + s)^{2}e^{\lambda} = e^{2\lambda}$ on $\Lambda(k)$ (the script: $1$ and $16$). On the canonical part the action is the unitary dilation $\psi_{m}(u) \mapsto e^{-\lambda/2}\psi_{m}(e^{-\lambda}u)$, and at $\lambda = -\kappa t$, with the inverse rotor acting on values by $e^{+\kappa t/2}$ (checked), it is the carrier's boost $\beta_{t}(\psi(u)) = e^{\kappa t/2}\psi(e^{\kappa t}u)$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.null-vacuum-on-a-horizon-generator}(i), whose weight $\tfrac12$ is the one that preserves the anticommutator. The same factorisation is clause (ii) of the result filed alongside.

\emph{(iii).} $\Gamma^{2}\psi = I(I\psi I\sigma_{3})I\sigma_{3} = I^{2}\psi(I\sigma_{3})^{2} = \psi$, since $I$ is central in $\Cl^{+}(1,3)$ (Theorem \claimref{field-theory.where-each-lives}(1)) and $I^{2} = (I\sigma_{3})^{2} = -1$. Left and right multiplications commute, and $I$ is central in the even subalgebra, so $\Gamma$ commutes with every $L_{X}$, $X$ even (the script checks all eight basis elements), with $R_{I\sigma_{3}}$ and with $L_{I}$. Both units square to $-1$ (checked). By exact rank, $\ker(\Gamma \mp 1)$ has real dimension $4$ and its intersection with the $(-1)$-eigenspace of $L_{B}$ has real dimension $2$ for each sign. Each intersection is invariant under both units, so it is one complex dimension under either.

\emph{(iv).} $IB = \gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{0} = -\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{0} = -\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}$, so $J = -IB$ (checked). On all of $\Cl^{+}(1,3)$, $\Gamma i_{H}\psi = I(\psi I\sigma_{3})I\sigma_{3} = -I\psi$, so $\Gamma i_{H} = -L_{I}$. On the $(-1)$-eigenspace of $L_{B}$, $L_{J}\psi = -IB\psi = I\psi$, so $L_{J} = L_{I} = -\Gamma i_{H}$ there; on the $(+1)$-eigenspace $L_{J} = -L_{I} = \Gamma i_{H}$. The script checks $(L_{J} + \Gamma i_{H})\Lambda(m) = 0$, $(L_{J} - L_{I})\Lambda(m) = 0$, $(L_{J} - \Gamma i_{H})\Lambda(k) = 0$, that $(L_{J} + \Gamma i_{H})\Lambda(k) \neq 0$, and $L_{J} \neq \pm L_{I}$. On the canonical channel of chirality $\epsilon$ the rotor $e^{-J\phi/2} = \cos\tfrac{\phi}{2} - J\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2}$ acts as $\cos\tfrac{\phi}{2} + \epsilon\,i_{H}\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2} = e^{\epsilon i_{H}\phi/2}$: helicity $-\epsilon/2$ about $k$, which is helicity $\epsilon/2$ about $m$. With the central unit both canonical channels are complex-linear for $L_{J} = L_{I}$, that is, both carry the same sign relative to that unit; the opposite helicity of the second chirality appears in the conjugate, which is Theorem \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation}(4) (with the unit $I_{4}$ one chirality enters complex-linearly, the other conjugated). The channel count does not depend on the unit.

\emph{(v).} A Weyl spinor field takes values in one $W_{\pm}$, and by (iii) its canonical part on $N_{k}$ is one complex channel; a Hestenes spinor field (all of $\Cl^{+}(1,3)$, a Dirac field) has both, of opposite chirality and so, by (iv), of opposite helicity. The script also counts channels from the flux form itself: the rank of the flux Gram matrix is $4$ (two complex channels), and its restriction to one Weyl half has rank $2$ (one).

\emph{(vi).} With $R = \tfrac54 + \tfrac34 B$: $R\gamma_{1}\tilde{R} = \gamma_{1}$ and $R\gamma_{2}\tilde{R} = \gamma_{2}$ since $B$ commutes with them; $Rk\tilde{R} = 4k$, $Rm\tilde{R} = \tfrac14 m$, and $R(k\wedge\gamma_{j})\tilde{R} = 4\,k\wedge\gamma_{j}$ for $j = 1, 2$, the transverse null components of a field strength (checked). The one-sided action on the canonical spinor $\tfrac12(1 - B)$ gives $\tfrac12 = e^{-\lambda/2}$. $J^{2} = -1$ is the rotor of a full turn in the transverse plane ($e^{J\pi/2} = J$), which acts as $-1$ on spinors and as the identity by conjugation on $\gamma_{1}$. So integer weights belong to conjugation-acted fields and give the offset $0$ of the stored classification (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.half-integer-weight-array-carries-half-ln2}, the sentence after (iii)); weight $\tfrac12$, hence version 3's offset, belongs to spinors.

\emph{(vii).} Read version 3 of Premise (Q) (\claimref{singularity.q-joint-quasi-freeness-s-wave}) with one array per complex canonical channel of a spinor field on a light ray, the array being the discretised modes of that channel. By (v) a Dirac species has two arrays and a Weyl field one; by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.half-integer-weight-array-carries-half-ln2}(i) each array carries $\tfrac12\ln 2$ at the Hawking temperature, so a Dirac species on one light ray carries $\ln 2$ and a Weyl field $\tfrac12\ln 2$. Counting per light ray, with the counted unit a bookkeeping total over all horizon fields, the arrays on a light ray number $2n_{D} + n_{W}$ and carry $(n_{D} + \tfrac12 n_{W})\ln 2$, so the registered unit of $\ln 2$ is two arrays of the total (the script evaluates this for several $(n_{D}, n_{W})$). Under the hypothesis of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.residue-from-mode-entropy} the residue per counted unit is $1/(4\langle s\rangle)$ (its (i)), which for units of two arrays is $1/(4\ln 2)$; a unit of one array would give $1/(2\ln 2)$. The script recomputes the channel counts $2$ and $1$ from the ranks and both residues in units of $1/(4\ln 2)$.

\emph{What is and is not derived.} (i) to (vi) are exact identities in $\Cl(1,3)$. They show that the arrays are the chirality channels of the spinor fields' canonical parts on the horizon's null plane, that two of them are the light-ray content of one massless Dirac species, and that on them helicity about the generators is minus the chirality. They do not fix the horizon's field content: the number of registered units per light ray is half the number of canonical chirality channels summed over all horizon fields, which is what the bookkeeping count records.
