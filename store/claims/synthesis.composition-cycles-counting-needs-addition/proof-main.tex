\emph{(i)} In $\mathbb{F}_{2}$, $1 + 1 = 0$ (Premise (F1)), so by induction $k \cdot 1 = 0$ for $k$ even and $k \cdot 1 = 1$ for $k$ odd.

\emph{(ii)} Every element of $G$ is $g = c^{a}e_{S}$ with $a \in \{0, 1\}$ and $S \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}$ (clause (i) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra}). Since $c$ is central and $c^{2} = 1$, $g^{2} = c^{2a}e_{S}^{2} = e_{S}^{2}$. The image of $e_{S}^{2}$ in $G/\langle c\rangle$ is the configuration $S \triangle S = \emptyset$, so $e_{S}^{2} \in \langle c\rangle = \{1, c\}$ (Premise (F2), clause (F2b)); this is also clause (ii) of the same theorem, $e_{S}^{2} = c^{q(S)}$. Hence $g^{2} \in \{1, c\}$ and $g^{4} = (g^{2})^{2} \in \{1, c^{2}\} = \{1\}$. The powers of $g$ therefore form a cyclic group whose order divides $4$: it is $1$ when $g = 1$, $2$ when $g \ne 1$ and $g^{2} = 1$, and $4$ when $g^{2} = c$. For a direction, $e_{i}^{2} = c^{s_{i}}$, so $e_{i}^{k}$ depends only on $k \bmod 4$, and only on $k \bmod 2$ when $s_{i} = 0$. In every case $e_{i}^{5} = e_{i}^{4}e_{i} = e_{i}$.

\emph{(iii)} The map $k \mapsto k \cdot 1$ from $\Z$ to any unital ring is a ring homomorphism. By clause (v) of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.signed-directions-and-the-integer-clifford-algebra}, $A$ is a free $\Z$-module with basis the blades $e_{S}$, and $1 = e_{\emptyset}$ is one of them. So $k \cdot 1 = k\,e_{\emptyset}$ is zero only for $k = 0$: the map is injective. Its image is $\Z\,e_{\emptyset}$, the grade-$0$ part of $A$.

\emph{(iv)} In $A$ the bit $c$ maps to $-1$, so $c^{a}e_{S}$ maps to $(-1)^{a}e_{S}$ and the image of $G$ is $\{\pm e_{S}\}$; it is closed under multiplication because $G$ is a group and the quotient map is multiplicative (explicitly $e_{S}e_{T} = (-1)^{f(S,T)}e_{S \triangle T}$, clause (v)). If $k \cdot 1 = \pm e_{S}$ then, comparing coefficients in the blade basis, $S = \emptyset$ and $k = \pm 1$. So for $|k| \ge 2$, $k \cdot 1$ is not the image of any element of $G$, in particular not a product of signed configurations; it is reached only as a sum. $\square$

The check builds $G$ as the signed blades of $\Cl(p,q)$ for every signature with $n \le 4$, verifies $g^{2} \in \{\pm 1\}$ and $g^{4} = 1$ for every element, tabulates the orders, verifies $e_{i}^{k} = e_{i}^{k \bmod 4}$ for $k \le 40$, verifies that $k \cdot 1$ for $|k| \le 60$ are distinct grade-$0$ elements, and verifies that products of signed blades are again signed blades, never $k \cdot 1$ with $|k| \ge 2$: every word of length up to five in the three signatures with $n = 2$, and random words of length up to six in five signatures with $n = 4$.
