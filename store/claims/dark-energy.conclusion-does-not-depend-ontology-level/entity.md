## formal
The gravitational-engineering conclusion, that the metric-forming structure admits exactly one coupling sector (stress-energy), with no selective coupling, no enhanced source and no enhanced detector at first order, holds on either reading of the graviton's ontology, with a different level on each. If the graviton is the framework's collective mode: by derivation, Propositions \claimref{dark-energy.acoustic-no-go-one-way-grip} and \claimref{dark-energy.point-infinity-has-no-physical-fluctuation} at proof level within the s-wave sector and Assembly \claimref{dark-energy.lift-inherits-no-go-steps-itemised} for the lift. If the graviton is fundamental: by Weinberg's soft theorem \cite{Weinberg1964}, the leading low-energy coupling of any massless spin-two particle is universal, which is the equivalence principle and rules out a selective coupling at leading order; non-minimal couplings, subleading in energy, are not excluded by the theorem, so the fundamental branch delivers the conclusion at leading order only. The conclusion is therefore common ground between the ontologies at leading order; what is distinctive to the composite reading is the ultraviolet behaviour (quantisation as output; no graviton loops to renormalise), the derivation of weakness as dimensional suppression (Remark \ref{rem:weakness}), and the $\ell_P^2/A$ corrections carried by the spectrum paper; the discriminating experiments, if any, lie in those corrections, and the asymmetry (hard to refute, hard to confirm below the Planck scale) is stated rather than hidden. The steps: the composite branch is the cited chain with its declared levels; the fundamental branch is the imported theorem read within its scope; the structural consequence recorded is that were the composite reading wrong, the leading-order conclusion would survive it.

## technical
The engineering conclusion is that the metric-forming structure admits exactly one coupling sector, stress-energy, with no selective coupling, no enhanced source and no enhanced detector at first order. On the composite reading of the graviton it follows from the acoustic no-go and the pure-gauge character of the $(I,V)$-sector, proved within the s-wave sector, together with the itemised lift to four dimensions, at the levels those steps carry. On the fundamental reading it follows from Weinberg's soft theorem: the leading low-energy coupling of any massless spin-two particle is universal. Non-minimal couplings, subleading in energy, are not excluded by that theorem, so this branch gives the conclusion at leading order only.

The conclusion is therefore common ground between the two ontologies at leading order. What distinguishes the composite reading lies elsewhere: its ultraviolet behaviour, weakness as dimensional suppression, and the $\ell_P^2/A$ corrections, which is where any discriminating experiment would have to look.

## lay
Can anything couple to gravity more strongly or more selectively than ordinary mass and energy do? This result says no, at first order, whether the graviton is a fundamental particle or a collective mode of deeper structure. Each reading reaches the verdict by a different route and at a different level: the composite route is proved within the simplest (s-wave) sector and carried to four dimensions by assembly, while the fundamental route covers only the leading effect at low energy. So the verdict on gravitational engineering, at leading order, would survive even if the composite picture turned out to be wrong.

## one-liner
The conclusion that stress-energy is the only coupling sector at first order holds on both the composite and the fundamental reading of the graviton, at the levels each branch carries: on the composite reading proved in the s-wave sector with an assembled lift, on the fundamental reading at leading order only.
