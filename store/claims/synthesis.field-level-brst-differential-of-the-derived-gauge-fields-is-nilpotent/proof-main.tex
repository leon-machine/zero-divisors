\emph{Setting.} The jet algebra is the free graded-commutative algebra over $\Q[g, \xi]$ on the symbols $A^{a}_{\mu, J}$, $B^{a}_{J}$ (even) and $c^{a}_{J}$, $\bar c^{a}_{J}$ (odd), $J$ a multi-index of derivatives; its odd part is the Clifford algebra of the zero form on the odd symbols, which is how the check multiplies them (GA's blade product with every square $0$). The total derivative $\partial_\mu$ is the even derivation $x_{J} \mapsto x_{J + \mu}$ with $\partial_\mu g = \partial_\mu \xi = 0$. An odd derivation acting from the left is fixed by its values on the symbols, with $s(xy) = (sx)y + (-1)^{|x|}x(sy)$; $s$ is defined on underived symbols by the displayed formulas and on derived ones by $s(x_{J}) = \partial_{J}(sx)$.

\emph{(i).} Bivectors of $\Cl(9,0)$ on disjoint sets of generators commute, so the $\Cl(3,0)$ block, the block on $e_3, \dots, e_8$ and $J$ commute with one another wherever $J$ commutes with the second block. In the $e_3, \dots, e_8$ block the fifteen bivectors span $\mathfrak{so}(6)$; the check computes the kernel of $B \mapsto [B, J]$ on them (nine-dimensional, $\mathfrak{u}(3)$) and projects out $J$ with the scalar product $\langle XY\rangle_0$, leaving eight independent traceless elements. The check then computes all structure constants exactly ($58$ nonzero), confirms the two blocks are ideals with no mixed brackets, that the centre (the common kernel of $\mathrm{ad}$) is one-dimensional and is $T_{12}$, that $[\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}]$ has dimension $11$, that $\kappa_{ab} = -\langle T_aT_b\rangle_0$ has signature $(12, 0, 0)$ and satisfies $\kappa([T_a, T_b], T_c) + \kappa(T_b, [T_a, T_c]) = 0$, and that the matrices commuting with the adjoint action of the eight-dimensional block form a one-dimensional space. A compact Lie algebra (positive invariant form) whose adjoint representation has scalar commutant is simple; the only compact simple Lie algebra of dimension $8$ is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, and the only one of dimension $3$ is $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. So the realisation is isomorphic to, not identical with, the algebra of Theorem \claimref{standard-model.gauge-algebra-minimal-massive-composite} on $\C^{2} \otimes \C^{3}$; it does not fix the hypercharge embedding on matter, and the pure gauge differential sees only the isomorphism class.

\emph{(ii).} First, $s$ commutes with $\partial_\mu$: $[s, \partial_\mu]$ is an odd derivation, and on a symbol $s(\partial_\mu x_{J}) = s(x_{J + \mu}) = \partial_{J + \mu}(sx) = \partial_\mu s(x_{J})$. Second, $s^2 = \tfrac12[s, s]$ is an even derivation, so it vanishes once it vanishes on the symbols, and on a derived symbol $s^2(x_{J}) = \partial_{J}(s^2 x)$; so it suffices to check the underived symbols. On $\bar c$ and $B$, $s^2\bar c^{a} = sB^{a} = 0$. On the ghost, written as the bivector-valued field $c = T_ac^{a}$ (the $T_a$ bivectors of $\Cl(9,0)$), $sc = -\tfrac{g}{2}[c, c] = -gc^2$ and $s^2c = -g\,(sc)c + g\,c\,(sc) = g^2(c^3 - c^3) = 0$; in components this is the Jacobi identity for $f$. On the vector potential, $sA = \nabla c + g[A, c]$ with $[A, c] = Ac - cA$, and $s(Ac - cA) = (sA)c + A(sc) - (sc)A + c(sA)$ (the odd $s$ passes the odd $c$ with a sign), so
\[
s^2A = \nabla(sc) + g\{sA, c\} + g[A, sc] = -g\nabla(c^2) + g\{\nabla c, c\} + g^2\{[A, c], c\} - g^2[A, c^2],
\]
with $\{X, c\} = Xc + cX$ for odd $X$. Here $\{\nabla c, c\} = \nabla(c^2)$, cancelling the first term, and $\{[A, c], c\} = Ac^2 - cAc + cAc - c^2A = [A, c^2]$, cancelling the last. Each cancellation uses the factor $\tfrac12$ (through $sc = -gc^2$) and the covariant term, which is the content of (iii). The check confirms $s^2 = 0$ on all $300$ generators $A^{a}_{\mu, J}$, $c^{a}_{J}$ with $|J| \le 1$ as polynomials in $g$, and $s\bar c^{a}_{J} = B^{a}_{J}$.

\emph{(iii).} With $sc = -hg[c, c] = -2hgc^2$ the computation of (ii) gives $s^2c = 0$ for every $h$ and $s^2A = (1 - 2h)g\big(\nabla(c^2) + g[A, c^2]\big) = (2h - 1)DY$ with $Y = -gc^2$ the ghost variation at $h = \tfrac12$; the check confirms $s^2A^{a}_{\mu} = 3(D_\mu Y)^{a}$ exactly for $h = 2$ and all $a$, $\mu$. Without the covariant term $s^2A^{a}_{\mu} = \partial_\mu(sc^{a})$, confirmed exactly, nonzero for the $44$ components with $sc^{a} \neq 0$ (the $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ ghost has $sc = 0$). With the pair $f_{34}{}^{5} = -f_{43}{}^{5}$ of the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ block shifted by $\pm1$ the Jacobi identity fails and $30$ underived generators have $s^2 \neq 0$.

\emph{(iv).} With $A = T_aA^{a}$ and $F = T_aF^{a}$ as bivector-valued fields, $F = \nabla\wedge A + \tfrac{g}{2}[A\wedge A]$ where $[A\wedge A] = T_cf_{ab}{}^{c}A^{a}\wedge A^{b}$. Then $s(\nabla\wedge A) = \nabla\wedge(\nabla c + g[A, c]) = g\nabla\wedge[A, c]$ since $\nabla\wedge\nabla = 0$ on functions, and $s\tfrac{g}{2}[A\wedge A] = g[A\wedge sA]$ by the symmetry of $[A\wedge B]$ in $A$, $B$ for vectors; collecting, the terms in $\nabla c$ cancel between $\nabla\wedge[A, c]$ and $[A\wedge\nabla c]$, the terms in $g^2$ combine by the Jacobi identity, and $sF = g[F, c]$, that is $sF^{a} = gf_{bc}{}^{a}F^{b}c^{c}$. The check confirms this exactly for all twelve components, each a bivector field. For an invariant form, $s(\kappa_{ab}F^{a}F^{b}) = g\kappa_{ab}(f_{cd}{}^{a}F^{c}c^{d}F^{b} + F^{a}f_{cd}{}^{b}F^{c}c^{d})$, and moving the odd scalar $c^{d}$ past the even bivector $F^{b}$ costs no sign, so this is $g\,c^{d}F^{c}F^{e}(\kappa_{ae}f_{cd}{}^{a} + \kappa_{ca}f_{ed}{}^{a})$ up to relabelling, which vanishes by ad-invariance; this holds before taking the scalar or the pseudoscalar part. The check confirms the invariance of $\kappa$ for the full algebra and its failure when one diagonal entry is changed, and computes $s\langle\kappa FF\rangle_0 = s\langle\kappa FF\rangle_4 = 0$ directly on the $\mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)$ block (with the perturbed $\kappa$, $s\langle FF\rangle_0 \neq 0$). For the full algebra the invariance follows from the covariance (checked for all components) and the ad-invariance of $\kappa$ (checked), by the computation above.

\emph{(v).} $s\Psi = \kappa_{ab}(s\bar c^{a})(\nabla\cdot A^{b} + \tfrac{\xi}{2}B^{b}) - \kappa_{ab}\bar c^{a}\,s(\nabla\cdot A^{b} + \tfrac{\xi}{2}B^{b})$, and $s(\nabla\cdot A^{b}) = \nabla\cdot sA^{b} = \nabla\cdot(Dc)^{b}$, $sB = 0$, which is the displayed expansion; the check compares it term by term with $s\Psi$ for the full algebra and confirms $s(s\Psi) = 0$, as (ii) requires. So $\kappa_{ab}\langle F^{a}F^{b}\rangle_0 + s\Psi$ is invariant by (iv) and (ii).

\emph{What is standard and what is not.} The differential, its nilpotency and the gauge-fixed density are the standard BRST construction (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-brst-kugo-ojima-strocchi-and-gribov}(i)). New here are only the realisation of $\mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3) \oplus \mathfrak{u}(1)$ by bivectors, the bivector form of the field strength, and the exact computations. The differential acts on local fields; the operator charge of the interacting theory on a state space and its positivity, and the Goldstone and doublet terms, are not addressed. Nothing here comes from transport.
