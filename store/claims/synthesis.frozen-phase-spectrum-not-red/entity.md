## formal
Let $\Lambda = 3H^{2} > 0$ and work in the conformal model of Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point} ($\ip{\nO}{\nInf} = 1$, $X(x) = \nO + x - \tfrac12 Q(x)\,\nInf$).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The slicing's symmetry is in the frozen point's stabiliser.)} The vector $I_{\mathrm{fs}} := He_{0}$ has $Q(I_{\mathrm{fs}}) = \Lambda/3$, is carried to the chart frozen point $I_{\Lambda} = \nInf + \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\nO$ by an ambient rotation, and has scale function $\ip{X(x)}{I_{\mathrm{fs}}} = Hx_{0}$, so $g = \eta/(Hx_{0})^{2}$ is de Sitter space in its flat slicing with conformal time $x_{0}$. Its stabiliser $SO(1,4)$ (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-frozen-point-de-sitter-radius}) contains the seven-dimensional subgroup preserving the null ray of $\nInf$: spatial rotations, spatial translations $X(x) \mapsto X(x + a)$, and the dilatation generated by $\nO \wedge \nInf$, which maps $X(x)$ to $\lambda X(x/\lambda)$ and leaves $g$ invariant. Time translation is not in it.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(The index is a weight.)} Let the descent leave a late-time scalar perturbation $\zeta_{\partial}(\vec x)$ on the slice $x_{0} \to 0$ whose two-point function is invariant under the slicing's rotations and translations and transforms under its dilatation as the boundary value of a density of weight $w$, $\langle\zeta_{\partial}(\vec x/\lambda)\zeta_{\partial}(\vec y/\lambda)\rangle = \lambda^{-2w}\langle\zeta_{\partial}(\vec x)\zeta_{\partial}(\vec y)\rangle$ (modulo constants, which matter only at $w = 0$, where the two-point function is logarithmic and the constant is a $\delta^{3}(\vec k)$ term). Then its dimensionless spectrum is a power law, $\Delta^{2}(k) \propto k^{-2w}$, so $n_{s} - 1 = -2w$. A conserved perturbation ($w = 0$) is exactly scale invariant.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(No series weight is red.)} Suppose further that $\zeta_{\partial}$ is the boundary value of a single field of the principal series, the complementary series or the massless point (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group}), or a local polynomial in such fields, so that the map from the fields to $\zeta$ is weight-preserving. By the dictionary $m^{2} = -w(w+3)H^{2}$ (Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-frozen-point-dictionary}, Corollary \claimref{mass.weight-polynomial-two-series}), whose roots are the late-time falloff exponents $\Delta = -w$ of the Klein-Gordon equation, $\Delta^{2} - 3\Delta + m^{2}/H^{2} = 0$, the leading weight of such a field satisfies $-\tfrac32 \le \operatorname{Re} w_{\mathrm{lead}} \le 0$, and weights add under products, so $n_{s} \ge 1$: $n_{s} - 1 = -2w_{\mathrm{lead}} \in [0, 3)$ on the complementary series and the massless point, while on the principal series both weights $-\tfrac32 \pm I_4\nu$ (numbers are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_4\}$) are present, $P$ carries oscillating factors $k^{\pm 2I_4\nu}$, and $n_{s} - 1 = 3$ is the index of the envelope. The exceptional series' members at $\Delta = 3 + k$ have leading weight $k$ and give $n_{s} - 1 = -2k \le -2$. A red tilt $0 < 1 - n_{s} < 2$ from a single field needs a real weight $0 < w < 1$, hence $m^{2} < 0$, outside every series. The measured $n_{s} = 0.9649 \pm 0.0042$ \cite{Planck2018} needs $w = 351/20000$, $m^{2}/H^{2} = -21183201/400000000$, and the dilatation-invariant floor $n_{s} = 1$ lies between $8.3$ and $8.4$ standard deviations from it.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(The frozen point does not fluctuate locally.)} Under the Henneaux-Teitelboim promotion (Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-henneaux-teitelboim-promotion}) $d\Lambda = 0$ is an equation of motion, so on every solution $\Lambda$ is constant and its spatial Fourier transform is supported at $k = 0$: the promoted frozen point supplies no scale-dependent source.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently a descent-sourced spectrum with the measured red tilt requires one of three things: the descent's state breaks the dilatation of (i), the source amplitude then changing by the fraction $(1 - n_{s})/2 \approx 0.018$ per e-fold of scale; or the map from the fields to $\zeta$ shifts the weight while staying dilatation covariant (for example $\zeta = |\nabla|^{-\alpha}\phi$ of a massless $\phi$ gives $n_{s} - 1 = -2\alpha$ without breaking the dilatation, so the measured value would need $\alpha \approx 0.018$, a nonlocal map the series does not supply); or the source lies outside the series' fields, such as composites involving the exceptional series. No fluctuation of the frozen point can supply it.

## technical
In the conformal model the frozen point can be placed at $He_{0}$, a timelike unit direction scaled by $H$; it has the same norm $\Lambda/3$ as the chart frozen point $\nInf + (\Lambda/6)\nO$ and is carried to it by two reflections. Its scale function is $Hx_{0}$, so the metric $\eta/(Hx_{0})^{2}$ is de Sitter space in flat slicing, the late-time boundary being $x_{0} \to 0$. The stabiliser contains the slicing's rotations, spatial translations and the dilatation $X(x) \mapsto \lambda X(x/\lambda)$ generated by $\nO \wedge \nInf$, which leaves the metric invariant. All of this is checked exactly with rotors at rapidity $\ln 4$.

If the late-time perturbation the descent leaves is invariant under those symmetries and scales as a density of weight $w$ (up to a constant at $w = 0$), a two-line scaling argument gives $P(\lambda k) = \lambda^{-3-2w}P(k)$, so $\Delta^{2}(k) \propto k^{-2w}$ and $n_{s} - 1 = -2w$. If moreover the perturbation is the boundary value of a field of the series the store uses, or a local polynomial in such fields, then by the dictionary $m^{2} = -w(w+3)H^{2}$, whose roots are the late-time falloff exponents, its leading weight has real part in $[-3/2, 0]$ and $n_{s} \ge 1$ (on the principal series $n_{s} - 1 = 3$ is the index of an oscillating envelope); the exceptional series gives $n_{s} - 1 \le -2$. The measured $n_{s} = 0.9649$ needs $w \approx 0.018$, a slightly tachyonic $m^{2} \approx -0.053H^{2}$ outside every series, and the scale-invariant floor is about $8.4$ standard deviations away.

Under the Henneaux-Teitelboim promotion the frozen point's norm becomes a field whose constancy is an equation of motion, so it has no fluctuation at nonzero wavenumber. The red tilt must therefore come from the descent's state breaking the slicing dilatation (about $0.018$ per e-fold of scale), from a weight-shifting map to the curvature perturbation such as a fractional inverse gradient, or from a source outside the series' fields.

## lay
The measured pattern of the earliest ripples in the universe is almost, but not quite, the same at every size: slightly stronger on large scales. In this framework the universe after the descent sits at a frozen point whose symmetry includes stretching all distances by a common factor, and that symmetry forces any ripple pattern it leaves to follow a simple power law whose slope is fixed by a weight, the same number the series already uses to describe mass. For every kind of field the series uses, that slope is either exactly flat or tilted the wrong way; the observed tilt would need a field with negative mass squared. The frozen point itself cannot ripple from place to place either. So if the descent is the source and the ripple is read off one of the series' fields, the descent itself must break the stretching symmetry, slightly, by about two percent per factor of e in scale; the alternatives are an unusual nonlocal way of turning the field into the ripple, or a source of a kind the series does not contain.

## one-liner
The frozen point's stabiliser contains the flat slicing's dilatation, so a curvature perturbation left by an invariant descent as the boundary value of a field of the series has $n_{s} - 1 = -2w_{\mathrm{lead}} \ge 0$; the measured $n_{s} = 0.9649$ needs $m^{2} \approx -0.053H^{2}$, so the red tilt needs the descent to break that dilatation, a weight-shifting map to $\zeta$, or a source outside the series.
