## formal
\emph{Premise (common ceiling coupling).} The two towers of the reversal orbit, this one ($r = 6$) and the mirror ($r = 2$), share one ceiling $M$, and the parameters of their deposited dynamics at that ceiling are equal: the gauge coupling of each factor of the copied gauge algebra, $\alpha_{2}(M) = \alpha_{6}(M)$, and every other coupling of the copied theory at $M$. With the copy of content (Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-tower-copy-one}(i)), which gives the two towers the same $\beta$-functions in every scheme, the two theories at $M$ are then isomorphic, so $\Lambda_{2} = \Lambda_{6}$ and $c_{2} = c_{6}$ (Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-tower-copy-one}(iii)).

\emph{Scope.} The premise equates parameters of the dynamics, which are neither the sector labels Premise \claimref{dark-matter.p2-indifference} equates at its version 2 (labels, not states) nor states: P2 so scoped reaches the stored charge and not the ceiling coupling (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.tower-charge-sector-label-temperature-not}, Reading). It equates no state and no deposited energy, so it leaves the mirror temperature ratio $\xi$ free and does not give the $\xi = 1$ of Proposition \claimref{dark-tower-matter.mirrors-temperature-p2}. It acts along the reversal orbit $2 \leftrightarrow 6$ only: it says nothing of the odd towers or of the two-time fork, for which no coupling rule follows (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.no-coupling-rule-odd-towers-follows}).

\emph{What it costs.} One equality between two continuous parameters of two sectors (one per coupling of the copied theory), taken as given and derived by no result of the series; since no coupling value is derived either (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.no-coupling-rule-odd-towers-follows}(b)), it ties two undetermined numbers together without fixing either. On it rest the mass factor $m_{2}/m_{6} = 1$ up to the two sectors' composition, and with it the mirror's share $f_{2} = 1$, $\Omega_{\mathrm{mirror}} = \Omega_{b}$, one of the observed $5.364$ units. Without it the share is a free function of the coupling difference: with identical content a difference $\delta = \alpha_{2}(M)^{-1} - \alpha_{6}(M)^{-1}$ moves $\Lambda_{2}/\Lambda_{6}$ by $e^{-2\pi\delta/b}$, and the share stays within ten percent of one only for $|\delta| < 0.106$ (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-share-one-how-fragile}(ii)). It adds one premise to the register: the share of one holds given P1, P2 (for the asymmetry ratio), this premise (for the mass ratio) and the copy.

\emph{Refutation condition.} The premise is false if the series' dynamics, or another premise of the register, gives the two towers different ceilings or different couplings at the ceiling. It becomes a theorem, and is retired as a premise, if the equality is derived, for example from a symmetry of the descent that acts on the parameters of the dynamics along the reversal orbit.

## technical
The mirror tower copies this tower's content, so the two have the same beta-functions; that alone does not make their coupling values equal. The share of one needs more: a common ceiling $M$ and equal couplings there, which with equal running give equal confinement scales and composite spectra, $\Lambda_{2} = \Lambda_{6}$ and $c_{2} = c_{6}$. Indifference, scoped to sector labels, does not supply that equality, since a coupling is neither a label nor a state. This premise supplies it: the two members of the reversal orbit share one ceiling and every coupling of the copied theory at it.

It equates no state, so the mirror's temperature ratio stays free, and it acts along the reversal orbit only, saying nothing about the odd towers or the two-time fork. What it costs is one posited equality between undetermined parameters of two sectors; it fixes no coupling value. On it rests the mass factor of the mirror share and so the share of one; without it a coupling difference $\delta$ in $\alpha^{-1}$ at the ceiling moves the share by $e^{-2\pi\delta/b}$, within ten percent of one only for $|\delta| < 0.106$. It is refuted if the series gives the two towers different ceilings or couplings, and it becomes a theorem if a symmetry of the descent acting on parameters of the dynamics derives it.

## lay
The mirror world has exactly the same kinds of particles and forces as ours, but that does not by itself make its forces equally strong. For the mirror's matter to weigh exactly as much as ours, its forces must start out with the same strengths as ours at the highest energy the theory reaches. The series' indifference assumption, as now worded, only matches fixed labels such as a stored charge, not force strengths, so this assumption is added to say the two worlds start with equal strengths. It says nothing about how hot the mirror is, and nothing about the other hidden worlds. Its cost is plain: one of the 5.4 units of dark matter rests on it, and a small difference in starting strengths would change the mirror's share a great deal.

## one-liner
Premise: the two towers of the reversal orbit share one ceiling $M$ and equal couplings there, so the copied theories are isomorphic and $\Lambda_{2} = \Lambda_{6}$, $c_{2} = c_{6}$; it equates no state (the temperature ratio stays free), acts along that orbit only, and carries the mass factor of the mirror's share of one.
