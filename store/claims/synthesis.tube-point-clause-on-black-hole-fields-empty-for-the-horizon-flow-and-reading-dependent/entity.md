## formal
Use the labels of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.wick-rotated-boost-selects-observer-tube-point}: $\Cl(2,4)$ with $e_{1}$ (square $+1$, time), $e_{2}, e_{3}, e_{4}$ (square $-1$), $e_{+}$ ($+1$), $e_{-}$ ($-1$), $\nO = \tfrac12(e_{+} + e_{-})$, $\ninf = e_{+} - e_{-}$, $X(x) = \nO + x - \tfrac12 Q(x)\ninf$; as in Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-field-fixes-the-observer-tube-point}, $K = e_{1}e_{2}$ is the boost of the $(e_{1}, e_{2})$ plane normalised to unit rapidity, with bifurcation plane $\operatorname{span}(e_{3}, e_{4})$; the compatible tube points are $m = x_{\perp} + I_{7}\rho u$ ($I_{7}$ the central pseudoscalar of $\Cl(2,4)_{\C} = \Cl(3,4)$, which commutes with every vector; Theorem \claimref{std-algebra.complexification-is-the-odd-extension}), $u = \cosh\eta\,e_{1} + \sinh\eta\,e_{2}$, $\rho > 0$, with observers $x(m) = x_{\perp} + \rho(\sinh\eta\,e_{1} + \cosh\eta\,e_{2})$ in the right wedge. Let $T = \operatorname{span}(e_{3}, e_{4}, e_{+}, e_{-})$, of signature $(1,3)$, and for a bivector $B$ and a vector $a$ let $B \cdot a = \tfrac12(Ba - aB)$. A bivector $B$ \emph{fixes} a point (real or in the tube) with null vector $X$ when $B \cdot X$ is a multiple of $X$. The boost itself fixes no compatible tube point ($K \cdot X(m) = I_{7}\rho\,K \cdot u$ is not a multiple of $X(m)$), in any field, and neither does $aK + B$ for $a \neq 0$ and $B$ a bivector of $T$. So, as in clause (ii) of the de Sitter theorem (``the three transverse symmetries''), \emph{the condition of the clause} \claimref{synthesis.tube-point-clause-invariant-under-boost-commuting-symmetries} is read throughout as: $m$ is fixed by the rotations among the symmetries of the field commuting with $K$, that is, by those generating a compact group (the $\so(3)$ in (iv), the group of $J$ in (vii)). Read instead as every symmetry commuting with $K$ that is not a multiple of $K$, the condition is met in no field that has such a symmetry, the de Sitter field included, since $K + B$ fixes no compatible tube point that $B$ fixes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The centraliser of the boost.)} The bivectors commuting with $K$ are spanned by $K$ and the six bivectors of $T$. Every subalgebra of them isomorphic to $\so(3)$ is the algebra $\so(3)_{I}$ of the bivectors of $T$ annihilating a vector $I \in T$ with $Q(I) > 0$, unique up to scale. Writing $I = \alpha\nO + c + \beta\ninf$ with $c \in \operatorname{span}(e_{3}, e_{4})$, one has $\alpha \neq 0$, and $I$ is a multiple of the de Sitter tractor $\ninf + \tfrac{\Lambda_{I}}{6}\nO$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-field-fixes-the-observer-tube-point} translated along the bifurcation plane to the centre $c/\alpha$, with $\Lambda_{I} = 12\alpha^{2}/Q(I) > 0$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Fixed tube points are fixed observers.)} For every bivector $B$ of $T$ and every compatible $m$, $B \cdot X(m) = B \cdot \operatorname{Re}X(m) = B \cdot X(x(m))$; so $B$ fixes $m$ if and only if it fixes the observer $x(m)$, if and only if $B \cdot \operatorname{Re}X(m) = 0$. The compatible tube points fixed by $\so(3)_{I}$ are exactly those with $x_{\perp} = c/\alpha$ and $\rho^{2} = Q(I)/\alpha^{2} = 12/\Lambda_{I}$, one orbit of the boost. For $I \in T$, $I \neq 0$, with $Q(I) \le 0$ the bivectors of $T$ annihilating $I$ fix no compatible tube point.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(The rotations on the wedge.)} Let $Q(I) > 0$, $\hat I = I/Q(I)^{1/2}$ with the sign of $I$ chosen so that $\alpha > 0$, and let $S_{I}$ be the unit sphere $\{n \in T : \ip{n}{I} = 0,\ Q(n) = -1\}$. Every point of the right wedge is the null line of exactly one
\[
X(\eta, \sigma, n) = \hat I + (1 - \sigma^{2})^{1/2}(\sinh\eta\,e_{1} + \cosh\eta\,e_{2}) + \sigma n, \qquad \eta \in \R,\ 0 \le \sigma < 1,\ n \in S_{I}
\]
($n$ immaterial at $\sigma = 0$), and every such vector is the null vector of a point of the wedge. The boost shifts $\eta$, $\so(3)_{I}$ rotates $n$, and a point of the wedge is fixed by $\so(3)_{I}$ if and only if $\sigma = 0$; the points with $\sigma = 0$ are the observers of the fixed tube points of (ii), and for every $\sigma > 0$ the orbit of $\so(3)_{I}$ is a two-sphere.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Gauges adapted to the horizon's flow.)} Let $(\hb, \Omega)$ be a gravitational field (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}) on a region $U$ with a Killing field $\xi$ (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(vi)), a Killing horizon of $\xi$ on the boundary of $U$, and an $\so(3)$ of symmetries commuting with $\xi$ whose orbits in $U$ are two-spheres. Call a gauge of the field, obtained by a displacement (clause (e) of the Definition), \emph{adapted to the flow of $\xi$} if in it $U$ lies in the right wedge, the boosts of the $(e_{1}, e_{2})$ plane (the flow of $K$) are the flow of a positive multiple of $\xi$, and the $\so(3)$ acts by bivectors of $\Cl(2,4)$. This is the clause's hypothesis (that $K$ generates a symmetry of the field and the symmetries commuting with $K$ act on tube points complex-linearly) with one addition: the symmetry that $K$ generates is the horizon's own Killing flow. In every gauge adapted to the flow of $\xi$ the $\so(3)$ is $\so(3)_{I}$ for one $I$ as in (i), $U$ lies in $\sigma > 0$, and no compatible tube point whose observer lies in $U$ is fixed by the $\so(3)$: the condition of the clause is met by no observer of $U$. The one orbit of fixed tube points, at chart scale $\rho^{2} = 12/\Lambda_{I}$, belongs to no observer of $U$. (The chart value $\Lambda_{I}$ is moved to $s^{2}\Lambda_{I}$, any $s > 0$, by the dilation generated by $e_{+}e_{-}$, which commutes with $K$ and carries an adapted gauge to an adapted gauge; the same is true of the de Sitter field, clause (viii) of the de Sitter theorem. The difference from de Sitter is not this but that there the fixed orbit lies in the region.) Gauges adapted to the flow of $\xi$ exist for every static field with coframe $\sqrt f\,dt$, $dr/\sqrt f$, $r\,d\vartheta$, $r\sin\vartheta\,d\varphi$ on an interval of $r$ with $f > 0$ ending at a simple zero $r_{h}$ of $f$, with $\xi = \partial_{t}$ and $\kappa = \tfrac12|f'(r_{h})|$: send the point $(t, r, n)$ to the point $(\eta, \sigma, n) = (\kappa t, s(r), n)$ of (iii), for any diffeomorphism $s$ of the interval onto an interval $(\sigma_{1}, 1)$, $\sigma_{1} \ge 0$, with $s(r) \to 1$ as $r \to r_{h}$.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Schwarzschild.)} For the Schwarzschild field of Fact \claimref{pairing.imported-schwarzschild-connection-curvature} on $r > 2M$, $f = 1 - 2M/r$, $\kappa = 1/(4M)$: in every gauge adapted to the flow of $\partial_{t}$ no observer of the exterior has a tube point fixed by the rotations. The static observers have the normalised Killing norm $N(r) = \sqrt{f}/\kappa$, strictly increasing with supremum $1/\kappa = 4M$, not attained, and acceleration $(v \cdot D)v$ of magnitude $f'/(2\sqrt f) = M/(r^{2}\sqrt f) > 0$. So neither the condition of the clause, read with $K$ the horizon's flow, nor the norm and free-fall candidates of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-field-fixes-the-observer-tube-point}(iii), (iv) select a finite scale: each is met only in the limit $r \to \infty$.
\item[(vi)] \emph{(Schwarzschild-de Sitter.)} For the static field with the coframe of (iv) and $f = 1 - 2M/r - \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}$, $0 < 9\Lambda M^{2} < 1$, on the static region $r_{b} < r < r_{c}$ between its two positive zeros, with $\kappa_{b} = \tfrac12 f'(r_{b})$, $\kappa_{c} = -\tfrac12 f'(r_{c})$: in every gauge adapted to the flow of $\partial_{t}$ at either horizon no observer of the static region has a tube point fixed by the rotations. The normalised norms $N_{h}(r) = \sqrt f/\kappa_{h}$, $h = b, c$, and the static observers' acceleration $f'/(2\sqrt f)$ are stationary, respectively zero, at one and the same radius $r_{0} = (3M/\Lambda)^{1/3}$ of the static region, where $f(r_{0}) = 1 - (9\Lambda M^{2})^{1/3}$; so $\kappa_{h}N_{h}(r_{0}) = \bigl(1 - (9\Lambda M^{2})^{1/3}\bigr)^{1/2} < 1$ for both horizons and $N_{c}(r_{0})/N_{b}(r_{0}) = \kappa_{b}/\kappa_{c}$. At $\Lambda = M = \tfrac37$ ($r_{b} = 1$, $r_{c} = 2$, $\kappa_{b} = \tfrac27$, $\kappa_{c} = \tfrac{5}{28}$): $r_{0}^{3} = 3$, $(1 - f(r_{0}))^{3} = \tfrac{243}{343}$, $\tfrac{3}{28} < f(r_{0}) < \tfrac{10}{91}$ and the ratio is $\tfrac85$.
\item[(vii)] \emph{(The outcome depends on which symmetry $K$ generates: a twisted gauge.)} In the setting of (iv) take a gauge adapted to the flow of $\xi$, in which $K$ generates $a\xi$, $a > 0$, with the chart $(\eta, \sigma, n)$ of (iii); let $J \in \so(3)_{I}$ generate the rotations $R_{J}(\theta)$ about an axis $\pm n_{J} \in S_{I}$, and let $c \neq 0$. The displacement $\psi(\eta, \sigma, n) = (\eta, \sigma, R_{J}(-c\eta)n)$ of the wedge gives a gauge of the same field in which: the boosts of the $(e_{1}, e_{2})$ plane are symmetries, namely the flow of $a\xi + cJ$; $J$ acts by the same bivector as before; among the symmetries generated by $\xi$ and the $\so(3)$, those commuting with $K$ are spanned by $K$ and $J$, and both act by bivectors. So the hypotheses of the clause as worded hold in this gauge when the symmetries of the field commuting with $a\xi + cJ$ are spanned by $\xi$ and $J$ (true if the field has no symmetries beyond $\xi$ and the $\so(3)$, which is standard for the Schwarzschild and Schwarzschild-de Sitter fields and is cited from memory; true also for the de Sitter field, where the bivectors commuting with $I_{\Lambda}$ and with $K + cJ$ are spanned by $K$ and $J$), although the gauge is not adapted to the flow of $\xi$. In it the rotations among the symmetries commuting with $K$ are the group of $J$, and the compatible tube points they fix are exactly those whose observers lie on the axis, the points $X(\eta, \sigma, \pm n_{J})$: for every $\eta$ and every $\sigma$ in the range of $U$, two observers of $U$ on each rotation sphere. When $J$ is the rotation of the bifurcation plane about the centre, their feet are the centre and their scales are $\rho = (12/\Lambda_{I})^{1/2}(1 - \sigma^{2})^{1/2}/(1 \mp \sigma)$, which vary with $\sigma$ (for another axis the feet lie on a line through the centre). So with this $K$ the condition of the clause is met by the observers on a rotation axis and by no others: it selects a direction and leaves the scale free. On the static fields of (v) and (vi) the Killing field $a\partial_{t} + cJ$ has squared norm $a^{2}f - c^{2}r^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta$ about the axis $\vartheta = 0$, negative near the horizon off the axis, so the horizon is not a Killing horizon of the symmetry that $K$ generates in this gauge.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently what the clause selects on the Schwarzschild exterior and on the static region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter depends on a reading its wording does not fix. (a) If the symmetry generated by the wedge's boost is the horizon's Killing flow, and the rotations among the symmetries commuting with it act on tube points: the condition is met by no observer, for Schwarzschild and for either horizon of Schwarzschild-de Sitter; no scale is selected, and the question whether a tube point is assigned per observer or per horizon is not reached ((iv) to (vi), proved). (b) If $K$ may generate any symmetry of the field, as the clause is worded: the outcome depends on the gauge, and in the twisted gauge of (vii) the observers on one axis meet the condition at every scale ((vii), proved, given the stated condition on the symmetries). The de Sitter field meets that condition too, so it has twisted gauges of its own, in which the observers on an axis through the centre meet the condition at every scale: the stored selection $\rho^{2} = 12/\Lambda$ in de Sitter is a computation in the gauge $\hb = \sigma$ with $K$ the horizon's flow, true as stated, and that the clause fixes $\rho$ there rests on reading (a), as the black-hole statement does. (c) If the words ``acting on tube points complex-linearly'' restrict which symmetries count, a gauge in which $K$ generates the horizon's flow and no rotation acts by a bivector (displace $n$ by a rotation depending on $\sigma$ that does not stay in one one-parameter group) makes the condition empty, so every observer meets it (argued, not computed). The three characterisations that coincide in the de Sitter field separate under reading (a): the fixed-point condition selects no observer; the maximal normalised norm and free fall select no observer on Schwarzschild and one observer, $r_{0}$, in Schwarzschild-de Sitter, the same for both horizons, with one scale $N_{h}(r_{0})$ per horizon, neither equal to $1/\kappa_{h}$. Which reading is the series', and whether an unmet condition leaves $\rho$ free (as the stored reading for $\Lambda \le 0$) or excludes the state, is not decided here.

## technical
A state's tube point is fixed by its wedge's thermal data up to one scale, and the adopted clause fixes the scale by asking that the tube point be invariant under the symmetries of the gravitational field that commute with the wedge's boost. In the de Sitter field, with the boost as the horizon's flow, this picks one observer, the one at the centre of the static patch. This result computes what the same condition gives when a black hole is present, and finds that the answer depends on how the clause is read. Read with the wedge's boost as the horizon's own Killing flow, and in a gauge in which the rotations act on tube points (by conformal maps of the flat background), the rotations are forced to be the rotation algebra of some de Sitter tractor, because every copy of the rotation algebra that commutes with a boost in the conformal group is of that kind; and a tube point is invariant exactly when its observer is a fixed point of the rotations, because the quarter turn that carries a tube point to its observer commutes with everything that commutes with the boost. Around a black hole no observer is a fixed point of the rotations: the orbits are spheres of positive area. So under this reading the condition is met by nobody, for the Schwarzschild exterior and for both horizons of the static region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter. The clause as worded asks less: only that the boost generate some symmetry. Twisting the gauge by a rotation about one axis that grows with the rapidity gives a gauge in which the boost generates the horizon's flow plus a rotation, everything that commutes with it still acts by conformal maps, and the observers on the axis meet the condition, at every distance from the horizon. The same twist can be applied to the de Sitter field, so the stored selection there also rests on taking the boost to be the horizon's flow. So as worded the clause is not independent of the gauge, and on these fields it selects an axis, nothing, or (under a third reading, in which only conformally acting symmetries count) everybody. Throughout, the symmetries that must fix the tube point are the rotations among those commuting with the boost; if every symmetry commuting with the boost other than its multiples were meant, nothing would be fixed in any field. The other candidates of the de Sitter theorem, the observer on whom the boost generator has its largest normalised norm and the static observer in free fall, are a single condition, a stationary lapse. On Schwarzschild the lapse has no stationary point and the norm only approaches $1/\kappa$ at infinity, which is what the store had argued. Between the two horizons of Schwarzschild-de Sitter there is exactly one such observer, the same whichever horizon normalises the generator, and the two horizons give that observer two scales in the ratio of their surface gravities, $8/5$ in the stored sample, neither equal to the inverse surface gravity. By the Tolman relation (cited from memory, used only in this remark) that observer is also the coldest for each horizon. So the adopted clause does not decide the two open cases under any of its readings; a rule built on the norm would assign the foot per observer and the scale per horizon.

## lay
In this framework each observer near a horizon is tied to a reference point in a complexified copy of spacetime, known up to one length, and an adopted rule fixes the length by symmetry: the point must not move under the symmetries that leave the horizon in place. In an empty universe with a positive cosmological constant the rule works and picks the observer at the centre. This result asks what the rule does when a black hole is present. If the rule is read in the natural way, with the horizon's own time flow as the reference symmetry, it picks nobody: the reference point stays put only if its observer stays put under all rotations, and around a black hole every observer is carried around a sphere. That holds outside a lone black hole and between the two horizons of a black hole in an expanding universe, for either horizon. But the rule as written allows another choice of reference symmetry, the time flow combined with a steady rotation, and then it picks the observers on the rotation axis, at any distance. So the written rule does not give one answer here, and none of its readings fixes the length. Two other ways of picking an observer, the one who feels no force and the one for whom the horizon's clock runs fastest, agree with each other: outside a lone black hole they pick nobody at any finite distance, and between two horizons they pick one observer, balanced between the pull of the hole and the expansion, but give that observer a different length for each horizon. A further assumption is needed.

## one-liner
In a gauge where the boost $K$ generates the horizon's Killing flow and the rotations act by bivectors, the rotations are the $\so(3)$ of a de Sitter tractor and a compatible tube point is fixed exactly when its observer is a fixed point of them; so on the Schwarzschild exterior and in the static region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter, for either horizon, no observer meets the tube-point clause's condition under that reading, while in a gauge twisted by a rapidity-dependent rotation, where $K$ generates the horizon flow plus a rotation as the clause's wording allows, the observers on the axis meet it at every scale; the norm and free-fall candidates are one condition $f' = 0$: none on Schwarzschild (supremum $1/\kappa$ at infinity), one radius $r_{0}^{3} = 3M/\Lambda$ for both horizons of Schwarzschild-de Sitter with scales in the ratio $\kappa_{b}/\kappa_{c}$ and $\kappa_{h}N_{h}(r_{0}) = (1 - (9\Lambda M^{2})^{1/3})^{1/2} < 1$.
