## formal
Let $T_{k} = \Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k)$, $n = p + q$, $r = p - q$, and let $B_{\infty} \subset \varinjlim T_{k}$ be the subalgebra generated by every plane generator $e_{j}, f_{j}$, $j \ge 1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The relative commutant of $B_{\infty}$ in $\varinjlim T_{k}$ is exactly the even ground algebra $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \subset T_{0}$. On a single floor the commutant of $B_{k}$ is the twisted copy $A_{k} \cong \Cl(p,q)$ of Theorem \claimref{tower.commuting-copies}, of dimension $2^{n}$; the next plane cuts it to its even half, because the twisted generators $x^{(k)} = x\,\Omega_{k}$ anticommute with $e_{k+1}$ and $f_{k+1}$.
\item[(ii)] So the pair $(\varinjlim T_{k}, B_{\infty})$ determines $\Cl^{0}(p,q)$ up to isomorphism and, for $n \ge 1$, hence $n$; the parity of $r$ (the centre of $\Cl^{0}(p,q)$ is $\R$ for $n$ odd and $\mathrm{span}\{1, \omega\}$ for $n$ even); for $n$ even, whether $r \equiv 0$ or $2 \pmod 4$ ($\omega^{2} = (-1)^{r/2}$); and, with the types of Theorem \claimref{tower.rigidity-clock-frozen}, the class $\{r, -r\}$ (types $\R \oplus \R$, $\HH \oplus \HH$, $\C$, $\R$, $\HH$ for $r = 0$, $4$, $\pm 2$, $\pm 1$, $\pm 3$).
\item[(iii)] The commutant sees no more than that class: $\Cl^{0}(p,q) \cong \Cl^{0}(q,p)$, the generators $u_{i} = e_{i}e_{n}$ on one frame with every square reversed obeying the same relations.
\end{enumerate}
For $n \ge 1$, at the top of the tower the residue is therefore kept, through the planes' commutant, exactly up to the reversal $r \mapsto -r$, the one symmetry along which Premise \claimref{dark-matter.p2-indifference} equates the towers; whether the pair $(\varinjlim T_{k}, B_{\infty})$ also distinguishes $r$ from $-r$ is not settled here.

## technical
In the infinite tower, the elements that commute with every adjoined plane are precisely the even elements of the ground algebra. On any finite floor more survives: the whole ground algebra appears, twisted by the product of the planes' volume elements, as the commutant of those planes. The next plane removes the twisted odd part, and in the limit, where there is no last plane to supply the twist, only the even ground algebra remains. For $n \ge 1$ the even ground algebra records the dimension, the parity of the charge and, through its type, the residue up to sign; it cannot record the sign, because the even algebras of a signature and of its reverse are isomorphic. This is the residue information that the limit algebra itself, being the same for every tower, cannot carry.

## lay
Each tower starts from a different algebra, but at the top all towers look alike. Some memory of the start survives in how the top is put together: the part of it that is untouched by all the added blocks is the even half of the starting algebra. As long as the starting algebra has at least one generator, that half remembers almost everything about where the tower began, except one mirror-image choice: a tower and its reflected partner leave the same trace. That is exactly the pairing of towers that the dark-matter paper treats as alike.

## one-liner
In the tower's limit the commutant of all the planes is $\Cl^{0}(p,q)$, which for $n \ge 1$ keeps the residue $p - q \bmod 8$ exactly up to the reversal $r \mapsto -r$.
