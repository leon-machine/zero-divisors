## formal
At each point of $E$ the fibre of $\shA$ carries the two-sided ideal $J$ generated by the radical $k$, with
\[
J = A k = k A, \qquad J^2 = 0, \qquad \dim_\R J = 8, \qquad A/J \;\cong\; \Cl(1,2) \cong \Mat_2(\mathbb{C}).
\]

## technical
At each point of the blown-up singularity $E$, the fibre $A$ of the Clifford sheaf $\shA$ carries the two-sided ideal $J$ generated by the radical covector $k$. It satisfies $J = Ak = kA$ and $J^2 = 0$, has real dimension $8$, and the quotient is $A/J \cong \Cl(1,2) \cong \Mat_2(\mathbb{C})$.

So on $E$ the fibre has a square-zero ideal of half its dimension, and the reduced algebra left after removing it is the simple algebra $\Mat_2(\mathbb{C})$.

## lay
At the centre of a non-rotating (Schwarzschild) black hole, blown up into a surface, the algebra that describes directions in spacetime degenerates. The part generated by the lost direction squares to zero, and what is left once that part is set aside is a smaller algebra of the kind that describes one time direction and two space directions.

## one-liner
On the blown-up Schwarzschild singularity each fibre has a square-zero ideal $J = Ak$ of dimension $8$ with quotient $\Cl(1,2) \cong \Mat_2(\mathbb{C})$.
