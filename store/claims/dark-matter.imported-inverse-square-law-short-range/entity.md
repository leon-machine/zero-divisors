## formal
Newton's inverse-square law holds down to separations of about $50$ micrometres, and a single extra spatial dimension of size larger than about $40$ micrometres is excluded. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{Lee2020}.

## technical
Laboratory tests of gravity confirm Newton's $1/r^{2}$ law down to separations of about $50$ micrometres. A single extra spatial dimension changes the force law at separations below its size, so the same data exclude one extra dimension larger than about $40$ micrometres.

## lay
Gravity has been measured in the laboratory between objects only a twentieth of a millimetre apart, and it still follows Newton's inverse-square law. That rules out a single hidden extra dimension of space bigger than about four hundredths of a millimetre.
