(i) is the algebra's own inventory: a one-parameter group of inner automorphisms of a Clifford algebra preserving the vector grade is generated by an even element whose commutator preserves grade one, i.e.\ by a bivector (the commutator of a vector with a grade-$2k$ element has grade $2k - 1$, so only $k = 1$ preserves the vector space; a mixed even generator $A_2 + A_4 + \cdots$ reduces to its bivector part, since grade preservation forces $[A_{2k}, v] = 0$ for every vector $v$ when $k \geq 2$, making each higher $A_{2k}$ central, and an even central element is a scalar; scalars generate nothing). The single-pair clause of (ii) is the import, proved in \cite[Prop.~2.1]{ButlerT7} (\claimref{hawking.modular-structure-completed-pair}), unique up to reparametrisation and orientation: the algebra fixes the orbits, not the speed, and the geometric normalisation of \cite[App.~A]{ButlerT5} is what fixes the speed, converting rapidity, and only rapidity, to Schwarzschild time at $\kappa = 1/4M$, so a rescaled generator traverses the same orbits and defines the same clock, not a faster one. For (iii): the flow a bivector $X$ generates acts on the mode operators by $\operatorname{ad}_X$, which is insensitive to a central shift ($\operatorname{ad}_{E_{ii}} = \operatorname{ad}_{E_{ii} - 1/2}$), so it is the adjoint spectrum and not the one-particle spectrum that the classification must read. From the canonical anticommutation relations of the mode operators (Proposition \claimref{scrambling.boundary-mode-space-matrix}), $[E_{ij}, \psi_k^\dagger] = \delta_{jk}\psi_i^\dagger$ and $[E_{ij}, \psi_k] = -\delta_{ik}\psi_j$. Hence $\operatorname{ad}_{E_{ii}}$ has eigenvalues $\pm 1$ on $(\psi_i^\dagger, \psi_i)$ and $0$ elsewhere, and the atom's boost bivector $\sigma_i = 2(\hat n_i - \tfrac12)$ (Fact \claimref{scrambling.imported-modes-modular-inventory}) generates that same flow in the rotor convention $e^{\sigma_i t/2}(\cdot)\,e^{-\sigma_i t/2}$: unit rapidity. The equal-coefficient sum $\sum_i E_{ii}$ gives $\pm 1$ on every mode, the simultaneous boost of all $n$ atoms and the statement's common clock; what is compact here is the phase circle $e^{i\theta \hat n}$, whose generator is $i$ times a bivector, which the real Clifford algebra of the boundary modes excludes, its centre being scalar, so the phase circle is not an intrinsic flow in the sense of (i); the single-atom instance of the exclusion is published as \cite[Prop.~7.21(3)]{ButlerT5} (\claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies}). For $i \neq j$, $\operatorname{ad}_{E_{ij} + E_{ji}}$ has eigenvectors $\psi_i^\dagger \pm \psi_j^\dagger$ with eigenvalues $\pm 1$, and $E_{ij} + E_{ji} = \hat n_+ - \hat n_-$ exactly in the rotated modes $(\psi_i \pm \psi_j)/\sqrt{2}$: again unit rapidity. The antisymmetric $E_{ij} - E_{ji}$ has adjoint eigenvalues $\pm i$ and is compact; the single $E_{ij}$ with $i \neq j$ is nilpotent, generating a unipotent shear; neither carries a rapidity. A diagonal $\sum_i c_i E_{ii}$ has adjoint eigenvalues $\pm c_i$, so unequal $|c_i|$ leave no common rapidity and admit no single conversion. The pairing sector is classified by one product: from the CAR, $\operatorname{ad}$ of $c\,\psi_i^\dagger\psi_j^\dagger + d\,\psi_j\psi_i$ maps $\psi_i^\dagger \mapsto d\,\psi_j$, $\psi_j \mapsto c\,\psi_i^\dagger$, $\psi_j^\dagger \mapsto -d\,\psi_i$, $\psi_i \mapsto -c\,\psi_j^\dagger$, two $2 \times 2$ blocks each with eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{cd}$: hyperbolic for $cd > 0$, where unequal coefficients rescale the common rapidity rather than remove it ($2\psi_i^\dagger\psi_j^\dagger + \tfrac12\psi_j\psi_i$ is a clock at unit rapidity outright); compact for $cd < 0$, eigenvalues $\pm i\sqrt{|cd|}$; nilpotent for $cd = 0$. Every clock in the inventory is therefore hyperbolic with unit rapidity after normalisation, hence by the Witt-extension clause of (iii), in the split space of the $n$ hyperbolic pairs of \claimref{tower.witt-index-quantum-capacity}, $O(n,n)$-conjugate to a sum of standard atom boosts, and the geometric normalisation converts them all at the one $\kappa$. What is unique is the rate, not the flow. Normalisation sets the nonzero adjoint eigenvalues to $\pm 1$, so the continuation of any clock to imaginary rapidity acts on the mode operators by $e^{\pm i\theta}$ and has conjugation period exactly $2\pi$, the atom's period of Fact \claimref{scrambling.imported-modes-modular-inventory} (\cite[Prop.~7.21(1)]{ButlerT5}); at the geometric rate $\kappa$ that period is the Schwarzschild span $2\pi/\kappa = \beta$.
