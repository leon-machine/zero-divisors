The Pauli matrices square to $1$ and anticommute pairwise, so by the universal property (\claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) $e_k \mapsto \sigma_k$ extends to a homomorphism $\Cl(3,0) \to \Mat_2(\C)$. The images of the eight blades are linearly independent over $\R$ (the check computes the rank), and both algebras have real dimension $8$ (\claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}), so it is an isomorphism. $\sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_3 = I_4\,1$ by direct multiplication. $I$ is central by \claimref{primer.centre} and squares to $-1$ by \claimref{primer.sign}, consistent with its image. Inside the algebra: $\tfrac12(1 \pm e_3)$ are complementary idempotents, $e_1$ anticommutes with $e_3$ so $e_1\epsilon_{22} = \epsilon_{11}e_1$ and $e_1\epsilon_{11} = \epsilon_{22}e_1$, and $e_1^{2} = 1$; these give the matrix-unit relations, and $Ie_2 = -e_1e_3$ gives $\epsilon_{12} = \tfrac12(e_1 + Ie_2)$ and the displayed expansions of $e_1, e_2, e_3$ (each checked by direct multiplication). Since $I$ is central, $x = \sum_{ij} x_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}$ with $x_{ij} \in \mathrm{span}\{1, I\}$, and $x \mapsto (x_{ij})$, with $I$ read as the matrix entries' unit, is the isomorphism above.
