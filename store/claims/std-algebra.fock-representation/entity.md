## formal
Let $V = W' \oplus W$ with Witt basis $v_i \in W'$, $w_i \in W$ ($1 \le i \le k$) as in \claimref{std-algebra.witt-basis-car}, so $V \cong \R^{k,k}$. On $\Lambda W$ let $w \in W$ act by $w \wedge{}$ and $v \in W'$ by the contraction $\iota_{2B(v, \cdot)}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] this extends to an algebra isomorphism $\Cl(V, Q) \cong \End(\Lambda W) \cong \Mat_{2^{k}}(\R)$;
\item[(ii)] $f = v_1 w_1 v_2 w_2 \cdots v_k w_k$ is a primitive idempotent with $v_i f = 0$ for all $i$; the left ideal $\Cl(V, Q)\, f$ is a spinor space of dimension $2^{k}$ with basis $\{w_I f\}$, and $w_I f \mapsto w_{i_1} \wedge \dots \wedge w_{i_m}$ is an isomorphism of $\Cl(V, Q)$-modules $\Cl(V, Q)\, f \cong \Lambda W$.
\end{enumerate}
So the Fock space is the minimal left ideal generated by the vacuum idempotent $f$, and the $w_I f$ are its occupation-number basis.

## technical
A split space $\R^{k,k}$ is a sum of two totally null halves paired by the form. Letting one half act by wedging and the other by contraction on the exterior algebra of the first half realises the whole Clifford algebra as all linear maps of that exterior algebra, a matrix algebra of size $2^{k}$. Inside the algebra itself, the product of the $k$ pairs $v_i w_i$ is the vacuum idempotent; multiplying it from the left by the algebra gives a minimal left ideal, the Fock space, whose basis is the creators applied to the vacuum.

## lay
The state space of $k$ fermion modes, with its creation and annihilation operators, already sits inside the Clifford algebra of $k$ space and $k$ time directions. The "empty" state is an element of the algebra, and the other states are obtained by multiplying it by the creation arrows.

## one-liner
$\Cl(\R^{k,k}) \cong \End(\Lambda W)$ by wedge and contraction, and the Fock space is the minimal left ideal $\Cl\, f$ of the vacuum idempotent $f = \prod_i v_i w_i$.
