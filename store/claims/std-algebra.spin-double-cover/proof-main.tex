Homomorphism: $\Ad_{gh}(x) = \widehat{gh}\, x\, (gh)^{-1} = \hat{g}(\hat{h} x h^{-1}) g^{-1} = \Ad_g \Ad_h (x)$. For a unit vector $u$, $\Ad_u = s_u$ is a reflection (\claimref{primer.twisted-adjoint-action}, \claimref{std-algebra.reflection}) with determinant $-1$, so a versor of $k$ factors acts by an isometry of determinant $(-1)^{k}$.

Surjectivity: by \claimref{std-algebra.cartan-dieudonne} every isometry is $s_{u_1} \cdots s_{u_k}$ with $Q(u_i) \ne 0$; rescale $u_i$ to $Q(u_i) = \pm 1$ (allowed since $s_{\lambda u} = s_u$), so it is $\Ad_g$ with $g = u_1 \cdots u_k \in \Pin$. If it has determinant $1$ then $k$ is even and $g \in \Spin$. If moreover it lies in $\mathrm{SO}^{+}$, it has such a factorisation with $\prod Q(u_i) > 0$, so $g \tilde{g} = \prod Q(u_i) = 1$ (\claimref{std-algebra.inverse-of-vector}) and $g \in \Spin^{+}$.

Image of $\Spin^{+}$: if $R = u_1 \cdots u_k \in \Spin^{+}$, then $k$ is even, because a product of $k$ vectors lies in $\Cl^{k \bmod 2}$ and a nonzero element is not both even and odd (\claimref{std-algebra.z2-grading}); and $\prod Q(u_i) = R \tilde{R} = 1 > 0$. So $\Ad_R = s_{u_1} \cdots s_{u_k}$ is a product of evenly many reflections with $\prod Q(u_i) > 0$, which lies in $\mathrm{SO}^{+}$ by its definition.

Kernel: suppose $\Ad_g = 1$ on $V$ with $g \in \Pin$. By \claimref{std-algebra.z2-grading} a versor is even or odd. If $g$ is even, $g x = x g$ for all $x \in V$, so $g$ is central; by \claimref{primer.centre} the centre is $\R$ for $n$ even and $\R \oplus \R\omega$ for $n$ odd, and $\omega$ is then odd, so the even part of the centre is $\R$ and $g = \lambda$. If $g$ is odd, $g x = -x g$ for all $x \in V$; expand $g$ in an orthogonal basis of unit vectors. An odd blade $e_I$ commutes with $e_i$ for $i \in I$ (it passes $|I| - 1$ factors that anticommute and one that is $e_i$ itself) and anticommutes for $i \notin I$ (\claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}); conjugation by the invertible $e_i$ maps the blade basis to itself up to sign, so every blade of $g$ must anticommute with every $e_i$, forcing $I = \emptyset$, which is not odd. So $g = \lambda$, and $g\tilde{g} = \lambda^{2} = \pm 1$ gives $\lambda = \pm 1$. Conversely $\pm 1$ act trivially.
