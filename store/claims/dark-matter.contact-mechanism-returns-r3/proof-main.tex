(i) Take coordinates on the five-dimensional tower's space $\R^{4}$ with our space the hyperplane $x_{4} = 0$ (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.nesting}(ii)). By Premise E (\claimref{dark-matter.e-equal-mass-per-unit-ball}) the tower's mass density is $\mu/(V_{4}d^{4})$ per unit four-volume. The ball of radius $d$ about a mass element at height $x_{4} = h$ meets the hyperplane exactly when $|h| \leq d$ (C); each such element counts in full (I); and each sources on one side only (S), the side its sign of $\omega$ names through Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.direction-nesting-adds-algebra}(i). Which side is immaterial to the number: the matter of one sign sources our world from the slab $0 \leq h \leq d$ and that of the other from $-d \leq h \leq 0$, and whether the tower holds both signs at half the density each or one sign at the whole density, the mass sourcing our world per unit of our three-volume is
\[
\int_{0}^{d}\frac{\mu\,dh}{V_{4}d^{4}} \;=\; \frac{\mu}{V_{4}d^{3}},
\]
one length $d$ of the extra direction, against ours, $\mu/(V_{3}d^{3})$ by E.

(ii) The contact count runs downward only. A ball of the containing tower is a solid region meeting the nested world's space in a region of positive measure, whereas the balls of a tower nested in this world are discs in a sheet, and no mass of sheets per unit of our three-volume is defined until the sheets' spacing is. So count on the three-dimensional tower's own space, the plane $x_{3} = 0$ in ours (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.nesting}(ii)), where our matter is the containing tower's: its density is $\mu/(V_{3}d^{3})$ per unit three-volume, the ball about an element meets the plane when $|x_{3}| \leq d$, it counts in full, and each sheet is sourced from one side, the side named by the sheet's own $\omega$ across the direction it lacks (Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.direction-nesting-adds-algebra}(iii) or (iv); at residue $1$ each module names a side, and S takes each sheet to hold one module, so a sheet of either kind is sourced from one side; a sheet holding both would be sourced from both, at twice the count). Per unit area of the plane our matter is $\mu/(V_{3}d^{2})$ and the tower's own, by E, $\mu/(V_{2}d^{2})$, so on that world
\[
\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{us}}}{\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}} \;=\; \frac{V_{2}}{V_{3}} .
\]
By O the same ratio holds read on our space, $\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}/\rho_{\mathrm{us}} = V_{3}/V_{2}$. A family of sheets $s$ apart, each of density $\mu/(V_{2}d^{2})$ per unit area, has density $\mu/(V_{2}d^{2}s)$ per unit three-volume, so $s = d$: the sheets are one length $d$ apart, which is the sheet clause of Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} with its unit named. The nestings over the mirror (residues $3$ and $1$) are not reached by the count, since the residue-$3$ tower's space contains the mirror's and not ours; $f_{3} = f_{5}$ and $f_{1} = f_{7}$ are P2's audit along the reversal orbit, as in \eqref{eq:sum}.

(iii) (i) and (ii) are R3's two clauses with the value one length $d$. The unit is E's: with any other contact region the slab has another width (a cube of the ball's volume has side $V_{3}^{1/3}d = 1.61\,d$, and cubic cells give the $5.000$ of Table \ref{tab:costs}). Two-sided contact gives twice the count, so S is the factor one half. In (ii) the downward count fixes $\rho_{\mathrm{us}}/\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}$ on the sheet, and for sheets $s$ apart the ratio read on our space is $V_{3}d/(sV_{2})$; O sets the two equal, which is $s = d$, so O is the equality of the sheet spacing and the extra length. Every count is a mass per region of radius $d$, so $d$ cancels in each ratio as $\mu$ does. No step uses the mass of a quantum or the number of quanta. The script recomputes the slab count as an integral, the downward count, the total, the alternatives of Table \ref{tab:excludes}, and the non-composition of Remark \ref{rem:scope}.

(iv) Let a five-dimensional tower keep E but hold quanta of mass $k\mu$, one per $k$ balls of radius $d$, and drop R2. Its own cell radius $a$ is given by one quantum per ball of radius $a$, $V_{4}a^{4} = kV_{4}d^{4}$, so $a = k^{1/4}d$. Read on the quantum's own cell, C counts the quanta within $a$ of the hyperplane on one side, a slab of width $a$, and the mass per unit of our three-volume is $\mu a/(V_{4}d^{4})$, a share $(V_{3}/V_{4})(a/d)$; a three-dimensional tower's sheets come out $a$ apart by the same steps, a share $(V_{3}/V_{2})(d/a)$. With $k = 16$, $a = 2d$ and the five-dimensional share is $2V_{3}/V_{4}$. E does not fix $a$, so on that reading it does not fix the shares.
