(i) Take coordinates on the five-dimensional tower's space $\R^{4}$ with our space the hyperplane $x_{4} = 0$ (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.nesting}(ii)). By R2 the quanta's centres have density $1/V_{4}$ per unit four-volume. A cell, the unit ball about a centre at height $x_{4} = h$, meets the hyperplane exactly when $|h| \leq 1$ (C); each such quantum counts whole (I); and each sources on one side only (S), the side its sign of $\omega$ names through Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.direction-nesting-adds-algebra}(i). Which side is immaterial to the number: the quanta of one sign source our world from the slab $0 \leq h \leq 1$ and those of the other from $-1 \leq h \leq 0$, and whether the tower holds both signs at half the density each or one sign at the whole density, the quanta sourcing our world per unit of our three-volume number
\[
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dh}{V_{4}} \;=\; \frac{1}{V_{4}},
\]
one cell radius of the extra direction; with R1 the density is $m/V_{4}$ against ours, $m/V_{3}$.

(ii) The contact count runs downward only. A cell of the containing tower is a solid region meeting the nested world's space in a region of positive measure, whereas the cells of a tower nested in this world are discs in a sheet, and no count of discs per unit of our three-volume is defined until the sheets' spacing is. So count on the three-dimensional tower's own space, the plane $x_{3} = 0$ in ours (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.nesting}(ii)), where our quanta are the containing tower's: their centres have density $1/V_{3}$ per unit three-volume, a cell meets the plane when $|x_{3}| \leq 1$, it counts whole, and each sheet is sourced from one side, the side named by the sheet's own $\omega$ across the direction it lacks (Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.direction-nesting-adds-algebra}(iii) or (iv); at residue $1$ each module names a side, and S takes each sheet to hold one module, so a sheet of either kind is sourced from one side; a sheet holding both would be sourced from both, at $2/V_{3}$). Per unit area of the plane our quanta number $1/V_{3}$ and the tower's own $1/V_{2}$, so on that world
\[
\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{us}}}{\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}} \;=\; \frac{V_{2}}{V_{3}} .
\]
By O the same ratio holds read on our space, $\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}/\rho_{\mathrm{us}} = V_{3}/V_{2}$. A family of sheets $s$ apart, each of density $m/V_{2}$ per unit area, has density $m/(V_{2}s)$ per unit three-volume, so $s = 1$: the sheets are one cell radius apart, which is the sheet clause of Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} with its unit named. The nestings over the mirror (residues $3$ and $1$) are not reached by the count, since the residue-$3$ tower's space contains the mirror's and not ours; $f_{3} = f_{5}$ and $f_{1} = f_{7}$ are P2's audit along the reversal orbit, as in \eqref{eq:sum}.

(iii) (i) and (ii) are R3's two clauses with the value one cell radius. The unit is R2's: with any other cell convention the contact slab has another width (the mean spacing is $n^{-1/3} = 1.61\,d_{b}$, and cubic cells give the $5.000$ of Table \ref{tab:costs}). Two-sided contact gives $2/V_{4}$, so S is the factor one half. In (ii) the downward count fixes $\rho_{\mathrm{us}}/\rho_{3\mathrm{D}}$ on the sheet, and for sheets $s$ apart the ratio read on our space is $V_{3}/(sV_{2})$; O sets the two equal, which is $s = 1$, so O is the equality of the sheet spacing and the extra length. Every count is a number of quanta per unit region of radius $d_{b}$, so $d_{b}$ cancels in each ratio as $m$ does by R1.
