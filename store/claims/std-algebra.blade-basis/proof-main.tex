Spanning. The algebra is generated by $V$, so by the $e_i$; it is spanned by words $e_{j_1} \cdots e_{j_m}$. By \claimref{std-algebra.vector-squares}, $e_j e_i = -e_i e_j + 2B(e_i, e_j)$ and $e_i e_i = Q(e_i)$. Induction on the length of the word and on the number of inversions writes any word as a combination of increasing words $e_I$.

Independence. By \claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}, the operators $c(v) = v \wedge {} + \iota_{B(v, \cdot)}$ on $\Lambda V$ satisfy $c(v)^{2} = Q(v)$, so the universal property gives a homomorphism $\Cl(V, Q) \to \operatorname{End} \Lambda V$, $v \mapsto c(v)$. Compose with evaluation at $1 \in \Lambda V$ (the symbol map $\sigma$). For $I = \{i_1 < \dots < i_k\}$, $\sigma(e_I) = c(e_{i_1}) \cdots c(e_{i_k}) 1 = e_{i_1} \wedge \dots \wedge e_{i_k} + (\text{terms of degree} < k)$, by induction on $k$, since each contraction lowers degree. These images are triangular against the basis $e_{i_1} \wedge \dots \wedge e_{i_k}$ of $\Lambda V$, hence independent, and so are the $e_I$.
