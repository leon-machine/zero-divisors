## formal
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The relation was found by solving \eqref{eq:sum} for the observed value, not predicted.
\item[(ii)] Among all expressions $1 + 2a + 2b$ with $a$ and $b$ ratios of the eight measures $V_{2}$ to $V_{5}$ and $|\Sph^{1}|$ to $|\Sph^{4}|$ ($3136$ expressions), $54$ lie within one standard deviation of $5.364$, taking $14$ distinct values, and $85$ within two; neighbouring targets give comparable counts ($43$ at $5.322$, $33$ at $5.24$). The closed form is one of fourteen equally simple candidates in the error bar (script). The mechanism of Section \ref{subsec:free} adds a discrete look-elsewhere of the same kind: of the clause combinations Table \ref{tab:excludes} lists, only the chosen one reaches the observed value, and the clauses were chosen with it known.
\item[(iii)] The ball and sphere forms of Remark \ref{rem:forms} are one relation. The exchanged assignment and the clock reflection give the same total as the other root of $T(c) = 5.364$, with the three- and five-dimensional shares exchanged; none of them is independent evidence for the total, and the exchanged assignment is not physically consistent and differs from the rule in composition (Remark \ref{rem:forms}).
\item[(iv)] The relation rests on R1, R2 and R3 (since 19 November 2026 on Premise E and R3, see (viii)), none supplied by the series; the contact mechanism explains R3's value and does not derive it, and the de Sitter reading, which replaces R2 by democracy and fixes the unit, gives thickness $\tfrac{3\pi}{8}$ or $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ and totals of $5.000$, $5.667$ or $4.333$, none within four standard deviations.
\item[(v)] The comparison is with one Planck column; the others give $5.322$ to $5.376$, and the ACT DR6 combination gives about $5.22$, below the floor of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(ii). If that lower value holds, no thickness in the family fits.
\item[(vi)] The relation needs residues $0$ and $4$ empty: with residue $4$ populated the ratio is about $6.16$ (\claimref{synthesis.lowest-causal-residues-zero-four-content}), and with both chiral ideals admitted at residues $0$ and $4$ the total is about $8.254$. With one spinor ideal the emptiness of residues $0$ and $4$ is equivalent to the pairing reading of mass in dimensions $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, where the reversion pairing on one ideal vanishes identically (\claimref{synthesis.reversion-pairing-vanishes-residues-zero-four}); in the lowest-causal window it is also what anomaly freedom of a single ideal gives at $(1,1)$, and at $(1,5)$, $(5,1)$ under the reading (G6) that a tower's own gauge fields live on its full six-dimensional space and act on its constituents (\claimref{synthesis.residues-zero-four-real-chirality-anomaly}). The series adopts the single chiral ideal at residues $0$ and $4$ with the pairing reading of mass as a costed clause of the constituent definition, chosen knowing that the unit-ball fit needs it, and records (G6) as the general reading of where a tower's own gauge fields live, not as a postulate. The clause is part of what the relation rests on, beside R1 to R3. \emph{Note.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.invariant-forms-on-spinor-ideal-factor-through-reversion}, every spin-invariant bilinear form on a single spinor ideal is a linear functional of the reversion pairing, so at residues $0$ and $4$ no other pairing exists that could carry a mass term: the clause chooses between the pairing reading and the momentum reading of mass, not among pairings. It remains a clause chosen with the fit known. \emph{Note (register, 2026-11-10).} The clause is now a registered premise, \claimref{dark-matter.single-ideal-at-residues-zero-and-four}, and so is the population of the odd towers, \claimref{dark-matter.odd-towers-populated}, which is inferred from the number the relation fits; the stored statements of the total assume both.
\item[(vii)] \emph{(Note, 2026-11-10: the two readings are alternatives.)} The de Sitter reading of (iv) and the unit-ball reading cannot be held together: Premise D with R1 and R2 forces one quantum per de Sitter ball (Premise \claimref{dark-matter.d-democracy-total-mass}, note; argued). D is the premise of the de Sitter reading only and is not counted with R1 to R3. R3's unit is each tower's own cell radius, so R2 also supplies the one length of the relation. \emph{(Superseded on 19 November 2026 by (viii).)}
\item[(viii)] \emph{(Note, 2026-11-19: the premises of the relation.)} By the decision of 19 November 2026 the relation rests on Premise E, equal mass per unit ball (\claimref{dark-matter.e-equal-mass-per-unit-ball}), and on R3 with E's one common length as its unit, in place of R1, R2 and R3; R1 and R2 are one model of E and are not needed (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}(ii)). Three things follow, each stated there. E with R3, for some length, holds exactly when the shares hold tower by tower, $\tfrac{4}{3}$ for each three-dimensional tower, $\tfrac{8}{3\pi}$ for each five-dimensional one and $1$ for the mirror, with the two thicknesses equal; so the premises restate the closed form's shares and add only that equality, which the total does not see, and a three-dimensional tower's share holds by the definition of the length (the same, (iii)). E holds in one length only (the same, (iv)); the democracy premise D is E at the de Sitter radius, given the stored reading of the sphere form (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.dark-to-baryon-total-uses-r1-r2-and-d-only-as-mass-per-unit-ball}(ii)), so D with the thickness one de Sitter radius is the same law at that length and not a second law, and it cannot be held with E at another length. And E does not say what its length is: it is tied to this world's baryon spacing only in the particle model, through R1 and R2 together.
\end{enumerate}
The relation is fitted: a closed form, a consistent mechanism inside the sourcing rule, and one length, that of Premise E, which R3 takes as the thickness and a contact mechanism on four clauses of its own returns. Its tier is observation level on Premise E and R3 (on R1 to R3 until 19 November 2026; (viii)) and on the costed clause (vi), and it supersedes nothing in Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent}: the mirror's share stays derived and the odd towers' shares stay underived, now with one candidate value each and its price stated.

## technical
The unit-ball relation $\Omega_{\mathrm{dark}}/\Omega_{b} = 1 + 2 \cdot \frac{4}{3} + 2 \cdot \frac{8}{3\pi}$ was found by solving the sum for the observed value, not predicted. Among the $3136$ expressions $1 + 2a + 2b$ with $a$, $b$ ratios of ball volumes and sphere measures, $54$ lie within one standard deviation of $5.364$, taking $14$ distinct values, and the mechanism's clause choices add a further look-elsewhere of the same kind. The ball and sphere forms are one relation, and the exchanged assignment and the clock reflection are not independent evidence.

The relation rests on Premise E (equal mass per unit ball) and R3, neither supplied by the series (on R1, R2 and R3 until 19 November 2026), and on residues $0$ and $4$ being empty, which the series obtains from a single chiral spinor ideal there with the pairing reading of mass, a costed clause adopted knowing that the fit needs it (with residue $4$ populated the ratio would be about $6.16$); the de Sitter reading misses by more than four standard deviations; and an ACT DR6 combination near $5.22$ lies below the family's floor, so if it holds no thickness fits. The relation is fitted, at observation level on E and R3, and leaves the derived mirror share untouched. E with R3, for some length, is equivalent to the closed form's shares holding tower by tower with equal thicknesses, so the premises restate the shares and give the fit no independent support. The single-ideal clause and the population of the odd towers are now registered premises of the total; the democracy premise D is E at the de Sitter radius, given the stored reading of the sphere form, and the de Sitter reading misses because its reductions give the thickness $3\pi\ell/8$ or $\pi\ell/4$ and not $\ell$; E holds in one length only, and D with R1 and R2 forces one quantum per de Sitter ball (argued).

## lay
The paper's formula for the amount of dark matter matches the measured value closely, and this assessment explains why that match should not be over-read. The formula was found by working backwards from the measurement, and many equally simple formulas fit just as well. It rests on two unproved premises (equal mass per unit ball, and the one-unit thickness; until 19 November 2026 it was stated with three, two of them about particles), which together say no more than the formula does, and on a further choice, made knowing the answer it needed, that two of the hidden families hold no matter; the match is to one Planck data set; another survey combination gives a lower value that no version of the formula can reach. So it is recorded as a fitted relation at observation level, not a prediction.

## one-liner
The unit-ball relation for the dark-to-baryon ratio is a fit resting on Premise E and R3 (equivalent together to its two shares) and on the costed single-ideal clause that empties residues 0 and 4, at observation level, one of fourteen equally simple values within the error bar of one Planck column, not a prediction.
