In a division algebra, and in a sum of two, every idempotent is a sum of the componentwise trivial ones, hence central: $(x,y)$ idempotent in $D_1 \oplus D_2$ forces $x \in \{0,1\}$, $y \in \{0,1\}$, all four combinations central. Conversely if $\Cl(p,q) \cong \Mat_k(\mathbb{F})$ (or a sum) with $k \geq 2$, the matrix unit $E_{11}$ is a non-central idempotent. The signature list is Proposition \claimref{transport.exceptional-division-algebras}. For the concrete form: if $u^2 = 1$ then $\tfrac12(1+u)$ is idempotent, and it is central iff $u$ is; a product of two orthogonal invertible vectors squares to $(ab)^2 = -a^2b^2 = +1$ iff $a^2 b^2 = -1$, i.e.\ one timelike and one spacelike factor, available whenever $pq \neq 0$; in definite signature take instead a grade-one unit $u = e_1$ with $e_1^2 = +1$ ($p \geq 1$), or in the negative-definite case a suitable blade, e.g.\ $u = e_1e_2e_3e_4$ with $u^2 = +1$ for $q \geq 4$; the classification guarantees existence in all listed cases, the explicit blade being a convenience, not the proof.
