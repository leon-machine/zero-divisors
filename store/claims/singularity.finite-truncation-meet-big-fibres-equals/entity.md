## formal
For finite $F = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \subseteq E$,
\[
\bigcap_{j} B_j \;=\; \Bigl\langle \bigcup_j \Aa_{x_j}^{+} \Bigr\rangle \;=\; \operatorname{range}(E_F),
\]
the joint-number-diagonal algebra, of dimension $2^N$: the meet of the big fibres and the join of the small fibres coincide, and both are the algebra of compatible evaluations of $\mathfrak{M}$ at the points of $F$. Here $\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle$ denotes the generated subalgebra.

## technical
On a finite truncation $F = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \subseteq E$, three algebras coincide: the meet $\bigcap_j B_j$ of the big fibres, the subalgebra generated by the small fibres $\Aa_{x_j}^{+}$, and the range of the multi-mode descent $E_F$. All three are the joint-number-diagonal algebra, of dimension $2^N$, and this is also the algebra of compatible evaluations of the sheaf assembly $\mathfrak{M}$ at the points of $F$.

The meet is the joint-diagonal algebra because, by the mode-wise parity proposition, an element fixed by every $\operatorname{Ad}\sigma(x_j)$ is diagonal in the joint number basis and conversely. The join reaches the same algebra because the commuting single-site $\sigma(x_j)$ generate the $2^N$ independent diagonal monomials $\prod_{j \in S}\sigma(x_j)$, $S \subseteq F$.

## lay
On finitely many modes there are two natural ways to build the algebra of classical occupation records: keep only what is even in every mode separately, or combine the even parts of the individual modes. This result shows that the two give the same algebra, which is also exactly what the multi-mode descent keeps. It has dimension two to the power of the number of modes, one dimension for each pattern of occupations.

## one-liner
On a finite truncation the meet of the big fibres, the join of the small fibres and the range of $E_F$ are one algebra, the joint-number-diagonal algebra of dimension $2^N$.
