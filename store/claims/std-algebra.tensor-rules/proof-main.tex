In the tensor product $\Cl(V) \otimes \Cl(W)$ the two factors commute. Since $\dim W$ is even, $\omega_W$ anticommutes with every $w \in W$, as distinct orthogonal vectors anticommute (\claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}) (\claimref{std-algebra.pseudoscalar-commutation}; directly, $w$ anticommutes with all but one factor of $\omega_W$ written in an orthogonal basis through $w$, and $\dim W - 1$ is odd). Put $\phi(v) = v \otimes \omega_W$ and $\phi(w) = 1 \otimes w$. Then $\phi(v)^{2} = v^{2} \otimes \omega_W^{2} = \epsilon Q(v)$, $\phi(w)^{2} = Q_W(w)$, and $\phi(v)\phi(w) = v \otimes \omega_W w = -v \otimes w\,\omega_W = -\phi(w)\phi(v)$. So $\phi(v + w)^{2} = \epsilon Q(v) + Q_W(w)$, and the universal property (\claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) gives a homomorphism $\Phi$ from $\Cl(V \perp W, \epsilon Q \oplus Q_W)$. Its image contains $1 \otimes \Cl(W)$, hence $1 \otimes \omega_W^{-1} = \epsilon (1 \otimes \omega_W)$, hence $v \otimes 1 = \epsilon\,\phi(v)(1 \otimes \omega_W)$, so it is everything. Both algebras have dimension $2^{\dim V + \dim W}$ (\claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}), so $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

The signs: $\omega_W^{2} = (-1)^{k(k-1)/2} \prod Q(e_i)$ for an orthonormal basis of $W$ of dimension $k$ (\claimref{primer.sign}), which is $+1$ for $\R^{1,1}$, $-1$ for $\R^{2,0}$ and $\R^{0,2}$, and $+1$ for $\R^{0,8}$ and $\R^{8,0}$. With $\epsilon = -1$ the $p$ positive and $q$ negative vectors of $V$ become $p$ negative and $q$ positive ones. $\Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_2(\R) \cong \Mat_2(\Cl(p,q))$ is the matrix description of a tensor product with a matrix algebra, and $\Cl(1,1) \cong \Mat_2(\R)$ by \claimref{std-algebra.cl11-matrix}.
