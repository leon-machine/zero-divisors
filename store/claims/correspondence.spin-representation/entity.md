## formal
The bivectors of $\Cl(V_\C)$ close under commutator (a copy of $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)_\C := \Lambda^2 V_\C$, acting on vectors by $[\beta, c(w)] = c(\beta w)$), and their action on the conformal spinor space $\CS$ (twistor space) is a Lie algebra isomorphism
\[
\rho: \mathfrak{so}(2,4)_\C \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \mathfrak{sl}(\CS) .
\]
Consequently the image of $\mathrm{Spin}(V_\C)$ in $GL(\CS)$ is all of $SL(\CS)$, which acts transitively on $\CS\setminus\{0\}$.

## technical
The bivectors of $\Cl(V_\C)$ close under commutator, forming a copy of $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)_\C = \Lambda^2 V_\C$ that acts on vectors by $[\beta, c(w)] = c(\beta w)$. Their action on the conformal spinor half $\CS$ is a Lie algebra isomorphism $\rho: \mathfrak{so}(2,4)_\C \to \mathfrak{sl}(\CS)$, both sides having dimension fifteen. Consequently the image of $\Spin(V_\C)$ in $GL(\CS)$ is all of $SL(\CS)$, which acts transitively on $\CS\setminus\{0\}$.

## lay
The conformal symmetries of spacetime, extended to complex numbers, can be realised inside the completed Clifford algebra. Acting on the four-dimensional spinor space of the conformal algebra they give exactly the trace-free linear transformations of that space, no more and no fewer. At the level of groups every volume-preserving linear map of that space is achieved, so any nonzero spinor in it can be moved to any other.

## one-liner
The Clifford bivectors form $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)_\C$ and act on the conformal spinor space as an isomorphism onto $\mathfrak{sl}(\CS)$, so the spin group acts on $\CS$ as all of $SL(\CS)$, transitively on nonzero conformal spinors.
