Read in Gauge Theory Gravity, the Riemann normal coordinates below are the background coordinates of a normal gauge at $p$, with $\hb(p) = 1$ and $\Omega(p) = 0$, the metric is the derived metric and $R_{acbd}$ is read from the field strength (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}, clauses (i), (iii) and (iv)). \emph{(i), the boost plane.} Take Riemann normal coordinates at $p$ adapted to an orthonormal frame with $e_{0} \pm e_{1}$ along $\ell$ and $n$, so that to second order $g_{ab}(x) = \eta_{ab} - \tfrac13 R_{acbd}x^{c}x^{d}$ with signature $(-,+,+,+)$ (the third-order terms are treated at the end of this step). On the plane $x^{2} = x^{3} = 0$ put $x = \rho(\sinh\lambda, \cosh\lambda, 0, 0)$; then $\partial_{\rho}x = x/\rho$ and $u := \partial_{\lambda}x = \rho(\cosh\lambda, \sinh\lambda, 0, 0)$. The flat part gives $\eta(\partial_{\rho}x, \partial_{\rho}x) = 1$, $\eta(\partial_{\rho}x, u) = 0$ and $Q(u) = -\rho^{2}$. The curvature part is $R(V, x, W, x) = R_{acbd}V^{a}x^{c}W^{b}x^{d}$, antisymmetric in its first two and in its last two slots; it vanishes whenever $V$ or $W$ is $\partial_{\rho}x \propto x$, and for $V = W = u$, all four vectors lying in the $(e_{0}, e_{1})$ plane, it equals $R_{0101}(u^{0}x^{1} - u^{1}x^{0})^{2} = R_{0101}\rho^{4}(\cosh^{2}\lambda - \sinh^{2}\lambda)^{2} = R_{0101}\rho^{4}$. So the induced metric is $d\rho^{2} - (\rho^{2} + \tfrac13R_{0101}\rho^{4})\,d\lambda^{2}$ to this order, independent of $\lambda$. Writing $F^{2} = \rho^{2}(1 + k\rho^{2})$ with $k = R_{0101}/3$, $F(0) = 0$ and $F'(0) = 1$ for every $k$. Continuing $\lambda = -i\theta$ gives $d\rho^{2} + F^{2}d\theta^{2}$, which is the form of Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-section-period} with unit rate; that proposition's argument (smoothness at $\rho = 0$ iff the angle period times $F'(0)$ is $2\pi$) uses only $F(0) = 0$ and $F'(0)$, so the section is regular at the bifurcation point exactly for $\theta$-period $2\pi$. For the metric $d\rho^{2} - F^{2}d\lambda^{2}$ the curve of fixed $\rho$ has proper time $F\,d\lambda$ and proper acceleration $F'/F$; at rapidity period $2\pi$ the proper inverse temperature is $2\pi F$, so $T/(a/2\pi) = 1/F'$, and $F'^{2} = (F^{2})'^{2}/(4F^{2}) = (1 + 2k\rho^{2})^{2}/(1 + k\rho^{2}) = 1 + 3k\rho^{2} + O(\rho^{4})$ from the second-order terms. The third-order term $-\tfrac16\nabla_{e}R_{acbd}x^{c}x^{d}x^{e}$ of the normal-coordinate expansion contributes $-\tfrac16\nabla_{e}R(u, x, u, x)\,x^{e} = O(\rho^{5})$ to $F^{2}$, rapidity dependent through $x^{e}$, hence $O(\rho^{3})$ to $F'^{2}$; so $T = (a/2\pi)(1 - \tfrac32k\rho^{2} + O(\rho^{3}))$, the stated $T$. With a transverse offset $y = (x^{2}, x^{3})$ the term $R(u, x, u, x)$ acquires $2R(u, x_{\parallel}, u, y) + R(u, y, u, y)$, which depend on $\lambda$ through $u$; their size is $|\mathrm{Riem}|\,\rho^{3}|y|$ and $|\mathrm{Riem}|\,\rho^{2}|y|^{2}$, so the conical angle on the edge element at distance $|y|$ from $p$ is $2\pi$ up to relative order $|\mathrm{Riem}|\,|y|^{2}$, the leading-order statement and no more. The script checks the induced metric exactly at rational rapidities (rational $\cosh$, $\sinh$) for algebraic curvature tensors built as sums of Kulkarni-Nomizu products, the rational-function identity for $F'^{2}$, and, as a control, that the offset metric depends on $\lambda$.

\emph{(i), the diamond.} For $\zeta^{t} = (L^{2} - t^{2} - r^{2})/2L$, $\zeta^{i} = -t\,x^{i}/L$: $\partial_{t}\zeta^{t} = -t/L$, $\partial_{i}\zeta^{t} = -x^{i}/L$, $\partial_{t}\zeta^{i} = -x^{i}/L$, $\partial_{j}\zeta^{i} = -t\,\delta^{i}_{j}/L$. \emph{(ii).} Fix a finite window $F$. The flow $\tau$ preserves $\mathfrak{A}_{F} \cong \End(\Lambda^{\bullet}\C^{F})$, a matrix factor, and acts there as $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{i\lambda K})$, $K = \sum_{j \in F}w_{j}N_{j}$. If $\omega$ is $(\tau, \beta)$-KMS on $\mathfrak{A}$, its restriction to the invariant subalgebra $\mathfrak{A}_{F}$ is KMS (Theorem \claimref{hawking.kms-restricts-invariant-subalgebras}), hence equal to the Gibbs state $\varrho = e^{-\beta K}/\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-\beta K}$ (Theorem \claimref{singularity.kms-uniqueness-factor}, Theorem \claimref{hawking.gibbs-states-are-kms-conversely-finite}), which is faithful since $\varrho$ is invertible; its modular flow is $\sigma_{s}(x) = \varrho^{is}x\varrho^{-is}$ (Theorem \claimref{singularity.modular-theory-matrix-factor-proved-outright}), which is $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{-i\beta Ks})$, the flow at $-\beta s$. Conversely let $\omega|_{\mathfrak{A}_{F}} = \mathrm{tr}(\varrho'\,\cdot\,)$ be faithful with $\operatorname{Ad}(\varrho'^{is}) = \operatorname{Ad}(e^{-i\beta Ks})$ for all real $s$. Differentiating at $s = 0$, $[\ln\varrho' + \beta K, x] = 0$ for every $x \in \mathfrak{A}_{F}$, so $\ln\varrho' + \beta K$ is central; the centre of a matrix factor is the scalars, so $\varrho' = c\,e^{-\beta K}$, and normalisation gives $\varrho' = \varrho$, which is KMS (Theorem \claimref{hawking.gibbs-states-are-kms-conversely-finite}). If this holds on every window, $\omega$ is KMS on the dense union of windows and hence on $\mathfrak{A}$ (Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-kms-passes-inductive-limits}). On the von Neumann algebra $\pi_{\omega}(\mathfrak{A})''$ the analogous equivalence is Takesaki's theorem (cited from memory, not used); the statement above is the window form. The script verifies, on a window of three modes in $\Cl(3,3)$ with rational Boltzmann factors $q_{k}$ standing for $e^{-2\pi w_{k}}$, that the Gibbs element $G$ implements the imaginary-time flow on the generators, $G v_{k} G^{-1} = q_{k}^{-1}v_{k}$ and $G v_{k}^{*}G^{-1} = q_{k}v_{k}^{*}$, which is $\tau_{i\beta}$ (the content of the KMS identity, which by cyclicity of the trace holds for $\operatorname{Ad}(\varrho')$ with any invertible $\varrho'$), the Fermi-Dirac occupations $q_{k}/(1 + q_{k})$, that the centre is the scalars (the commutator map with the six generators has rank $63$), and that two faithful non-Gibbs states (one Boltzmann factor changed; a hop term added) fail $\operatorname{Ad}(\varrho') = \tau_{i\beta}$ on the generators and the KMS identity with $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$, while $c\varrho$ passes both; and a correlated state with the same single-mode marginals ($G$ plus a small multiple of $\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}$, $\sigma_{k} = 2N_{k} - 1$) keeps every single-mode expectation and fails the joint condition.

\emph{Remark (a survey, not part of the theorem).} The store offers two other ways to obtain the temperature, and neither applies to the store's mode spaces. The Bisognano-Wichmann and Borchers mechanism delivers $2\pi$ from positivity of energy and covariance (Fact \claimref{angle.imported-theorems-deliver-their-hypotheses}), but on a mode space where the boost acts by phases it dilates no nontrivial translation group (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.point-spectrum-boost-admits-no-half-sided-translations}), so that route needs the continuum mode space and a local net that the store does not construct. The incidence vacua read boost-constantly have inverse temperature $-\ln|Q(x)|$, a function of the interval rather than a constant rapidity period (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.boost-stationary-incidence-vacua-read-interval}(v)), so they do not give $a/2\pi$. Within the store, then, (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) is not derived from weaker premises.
