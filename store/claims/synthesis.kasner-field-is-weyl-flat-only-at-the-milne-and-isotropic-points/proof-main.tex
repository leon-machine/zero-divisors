\emph{(i)} For a diagonal metric depending on $t$ only, the check computes the Christoffel symbols, the Riemann tensor $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\sigma}$, the Ricci tensor and $R$, and the Weyl components $C_{abab} = R_{abab} - \tfrac12(g_{aa}R_{bb} + g_{bb}R_{aa}) + \tfrac{R}{6}g_{aa}g_{bb}$ ($a \neq b$), divided by $g_{aa}g_{bb}$, which gives the mixed components $C_{ab}{}^{ab}$, all exactly as monomials $c\,t^{e}$ with rational $e$. By hand: with $\sum p = 1$ every spatial Ricci component vanishes, $R_{ii}/g_{ii} = p_{i}(\sum p - 1)/t^{2} = 0$, while $R_{00} = (1 - S)/t^{2}$ and $R = (S - 1)/t^{2}$. The orthonormal Riemann components are $R_{0i0i} = -p_{i}(p_{i} - 1)/t^{2}$, and the Weyl formula gives the mixed component $t^{2}C_{0i}{}^{0i} = p_{i}^{2} - p_{i} + \tfrac{1 - S}{3}$ (the orthonormal component $C_{0i0i}$ is its negative, since $g_{00} = -1$). With $p_{3} = 1 - p_{1} - p_{2}$ this is $E_{1}/3$ for $i = 1$ and $-E_{2}/3$ for $i = 2$. The engine agrees on nine rational points of the plane, for the general formula at each $i$ and for $E_{1}$, $E_{2}$, and confirms $C_{23}{}^{23} = C_{01}{}^{01}$, $C_{13}{}^{13} = C_{02}{}^{02}$, $C_{12}{}^{12} = C_{03}{}^{03}$ and the vanishing trace $\sum_{i}C_{0i}{}^{0i} = 0$, with every component a pure $t^{-2}$ term. For a diagonal field the Weyl curvature is purely electric: the Weyl map on bivectors, $W(B) = \tfrac14 C_{ab}{}^{cd}B^{ab}e_{c}\wedge e_{d}$ for $B = \tfrac12 B^{ab}e_{a}\wedge e_{b}$, is diagonal on the frame bivectors, with $W(e_{0}\wedge e_{i}) = C_{0i}{}^{0i}\,e_{0}\wedge e_{i}$ and the same eigenvalue on the dual bivector $e_{j}\wedge e_{k}$, which is $\pm I_{4}\,e_{0}\wedge e_{i}$. So the Weyl map vanishes exactly when every $\lambda_{i}$ vanishes, that is (by the trace) when $E_{1} = E_{2} = 0$; otherwise a nonzero eigenvalue is a constant times $t^{-2}$, nonzero on all of $t > 0$. The check also computes the Einstein tensor: $G_{00} = G_{ii}/g_{ii} = (1 - S)/(2t^{2})$ at every sample, so under Einstein's equations the source has $P = \rho$, with $\rho \ge 0$ exactly when $S \le 1$.

\emph{(ii)} By (i), $\lambda_{1} = \lambda_{2} = \lambda_{3} = 0$ exactly when $p_{i}^{2} - p_{i}$ takes the same value for all $i$, and a value $c$ of $p^{2} - p$ is taken at the two roots $r$ and $1 - r$. So each $p_{i} \in \{r, 1 - r\}$ for one $r$. If $n$ of the exponents equal $r$ and $3 - n$ equal $1 - r$, the sum condition is $nr + (3 - n)(1 - r) = 1$, that is $(2n - 3)r = n - 2$: $n = 3$ gives $r = \tfrac13$, the isotropic point; $n = 2$ gives $r = 0$, the exponents $(0, 0, 1)$; $n = 1$ gives $r = 1$, again $(1, 0, 0)$ up to order; $n = 0$ is $n = 3$ with $r$ and $1 - r$ exchanged. Hence the Weyl-flat points are exactly the three permutations of $(0, 0, 1)$ and $(\tfrac13, \tfrac13, \tfrac13)$; the check enumerates this. As an independent confirmation the check also eliminates $p_{2}$ from $E_{1} = E_{2} = 0$ over the rational functions of $p_{1}$: the Sylvester determinant is $-9p_{1}^{2}(p_{1} - 1)(3p_{1} - 1)$, and the fibres over $p_{1} = 0, 1, \tfrac13$ give exactly $(0, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 0)$ and $(\tfrac13, \tfrac13)$ in $(p_{1}, p_{2})$. At the Milne points the engine returns vanishing Ricci and Weyl tensors: the field is flat. At the isotropic point it returns $R = -\tfrac23 t^{-2}$, the value stored for the isotropic disk point.

\emph{(iii)} Theorem \claimref{synthesis.attachment-transport-is-flat-only-where-weyl-vanishes} gives that the conformal spinors of the attachment can be parallel for the tractor gauge field only where the Weyl curvature vanishes, and that off Weyl-flat regions the transport supplies no state. A purely electric Weyl map with real eigenvalues is of Petrov type I or D, so the type N case of that theorem does not arise. By (i) and (ii), off the four points the Weyl curvature is nonzero at every $t > 0$, in particular on every neighbourhood of the wall. The rest is the reading of (P1) and (P2): (P1) supplies the contracting Kasner stage that Theorem \claimref{synthesis.kasner-exit-needs-a-contracting-metric-stage-and-a-wall-matching} requires, and (P2) a rule carrying its state across; the rule's form is not fixed there, and by this clause it cannot be the attachment's transport.

\emph{(iv)} At $p_{i} = \tfrac13$ the check divides $g_{p}$ by $t^{2/3}$ and finds a flat bracket $-t^{-2/3}dt^{2} + \delta$ (vanishing Riemann tensor); its cometric entry $-t^{2/3}$ vanishes at order $\tfrac23$, not one, so in the smooth structure of $t$ condition (T3) of tameness fails (and the cometric is not smooth in $t$). With $\eta = \tfrac32 t^{2/3}$, $d\eta/dt = t^{-1/3}$ and the bracket is $-d\eta^{2} + \delta$; with $t' = \eta^{2}/4$, $dt'/d\eta = \eta/2$, so $-d\eta^{2} = -(4/\eta^{2})dt'^{2} = -t'^{-1}dt'^{2}$: the radical-normal tame wall in the coordinate $t'$. Tameness is therefore a property of the smooth structure; the general power-law statement, in cosmic and in conformal time, is clause (iv) of the result (\claimref{synthesis.radical-normal-tame-wall-is-a-regular-initial-surface-whose-smoothness-selects-no-state}). At the Milne point $(0, 0, 1)$ the engine finds the field flat; its metric entry $t^{2}$ vanishes at order two, so the metric degenerates and the cometric entry $t^{-2}$ has a pole: the opposite of a tame wall, at which the cometric degenerates at order one and the metric has a simple pole (Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-walls-sphere-theorem}).

\emph{Reading (argued, not part of the statement).} Under (P1) and (P2), with four-dimensional Kasner exponents, the deposit chain's first step cannot start from the attachment's conformal vacuum at a generic Kasner point; the reference state must come with the (P2) rule. At the isotropic point a conformal vacuum exists for conformally covariant fields, and in conformal time its wall is the regular radical-normal wall; in cosmic time it is not tame. At the Milne points the field is flat and the restriction of the Minkowski vacuum to the Milne wedge is available, but those walls are null cones of flat space, not degenerate cometrics. So in the cosmic-time smooth structure the two routes to a state at the descent that the series has, the attachment where the Weyl curvature vanishes and the regular radical-normal wall, do not meet on the four-dimensional Kasner plane. Kasner approaches with hidden directions, such as the five-dimensional end of (P2), are not covered. Transport-absent.
