By Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.two-families-are-two-chiralities}(iii) the Witt pair lies in opposite families exactly when $\dim V_{\C} = 2m$ with $m$ odd, and a choice between $W$ and $W'$ that the connected group cannot undo is exactly a difference of family, since the connected group is transitive within a family (Witt together with Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.two-families-are-two-chiralities}(ii)): so the bit exists if and only if $m$ is odd, and it is the chirality $\epsilon(-1)^{m}$ against $\epsilon$ of the two pure spinors, the Fock-grade parity of the top state $v_{1}\cdots v_{m}f$.
