The descent plants one Witt flag on the three-pair structure, a choice of half of each pair, whose single orientation bit is at once the arrow of time and the particle-antiparticle orientation. That this bit is initial data is a CPT-covariance argument the dark-matter paper states under three premises; the corrected argument (\claimref{dark-matter.orientation-arrow-seventeenth-refutation}) rests on two of them and a third premise in place of the last: that CPT conjugation, an antilinear conjugation, is an isomorphism of every kinematic structure and leaves every action functional invariant, the gauge couplings included (premised for the derived gauge sector; \claimref{dark-matter.premise-cpt-covariance}, whose action clause is stated at the strength at which \claimref{dark-matter.orientation-arrow-seventeenth-refutation} uses it); Hypothesis (S) (\claimref{standard-model.hyp-s-slot-statistics}), the Standard-Model paper's open premise that the composite's slots carry fermionic statistics; and that the flag is a datum of each solution, planted by the descent, rather than part of the kinematic structure (\claimref{dark-matter.premise-flag-as-datum}). The third premise of the paper's version, the exhaustiveness of its trichotomy, is not used: conjugation maps each solution space onto itself and exchanges the two orientations, so the bit is not constant on any nonempty solution space, which is initial data, and a parameter of the laws, being constant on each solution space, is excluded directly. The flag is a $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ datum, an orientation, and not a choice of frame vector. That the framework inherits its asymmetry rather than generating it, with two unexplained continuous numbers, is a declared stance there and is carried as one here.
