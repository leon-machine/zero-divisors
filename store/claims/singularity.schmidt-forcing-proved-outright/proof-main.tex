Invariance up to phase under a one-parameter unitary group means $\Omega$ is an eigenvector of its generator. $G$ is diagonal in the product number basis with $G|jk\rangle = (j - k)|jk\rangle$: eigenvalue $0$ on $\operatorname{span}\{|00\rangle, |11\rangle\}$, eigenvalue $+1$ on $\C|10\rangle$, eigenvalue $-1$ on $\C|01\rangle$. An eigenvector for $\pm 1$ is a product vector, and a product vector is annihilated by some nonzero $a \in M$ (respectively fails cyclicity), contradicting separating; so $\Omega = c_0|00\rangle + c_1|11\rangle$. If either coefficient vanished, $\Omega$ would again be a product vector; so both are nonzero, and phases can be absorbed into the basis vectors, leaving the stated real form.
