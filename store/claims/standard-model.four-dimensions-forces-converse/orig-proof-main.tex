(1) $\su((n+2)/2) = \su(3)$ iff $(n+2)/2 = 3$ iff $n = 4$, and for $n \neq 4$ the dimensions $3 + ((n+2)/2)^{2} - 1 + 1$ and $12$ differ, so the algebras are not isomorphic; the $\su(2)$ factor is dimension-independent because the two-ness of the minimal massive composite is the hypothesis, not the pair count.

(2) The kernel computation of Theorem \claimref{standard-model.gauge-algebra-minimal-massive-composite} used nothing about $m$ and $k$: the pairs $(A, B) \in U(m) \times U(k)$ with $A \otimes B = 1$ are the scalars $(\lambda I_{m}, \lambda^{-1}I_{k})$, so the algebra is $(\uu(m) \oplus \uu(k))/\uu(1) = \su(m) \oplus \su(k) \oplus \uu(1)$, of dimension $(m^{2} - 1) + (k^{2} - 1) + 1 = m^{2} + k^{2} - 1$, for every $m, k \ge 1$ (with $\su(1) = 0$; for $m = k$ the factor swap is excluded by the definition of an automorphism of the hierarchy, as in Section \ref{sec:algebra}'s preamble). Isomorphic Lie algebras have equal dimension, so $m^{2} + k^{2} = 13$, whose only solutions in positive integers are $\{m, k\} = \{2, 3\}$ (since $13 - 1, 13 - 4, 13 - 9$ are $12, 9, 4$, of which only $9$ and $4$ are squares, and $13 - 16 < 0$). For $\{m, k\} = \{2, 3\}$ the algebra is \eqref{eq:algebra} by Theorem \claimref{standard-model.gauge-algebra-minimal-massive-composite}, in either order. The script confirms that $m^{2} + k^{2} = 13$ has exactly the solutions $(2, 3)$ and $(3, 2)$ for $1 \le m, k \le 12$.
