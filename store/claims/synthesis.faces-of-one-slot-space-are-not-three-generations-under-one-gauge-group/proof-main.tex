\emph{Setting.} The face-$F$ copy of Theorem \claimref{families.three-identical-families-mixing-shared-slots} is the composite of the two conformal spinors on the slots $F$ with the construction of Hypothesis (S) applied to it; under (S) (Theorem \claimref{standard-model.hyp-s-slot-statistics}) its state space is $\Lambda^{\bullet}(\C^{2} \oplus W)$, the slots are fermionic modes, $U(2) \times U(3)$ acts as $\Lambda^{\bullet}(A \oplus B)$, and the chirality is the parity of the mode number. The slot mixing $U(2)$ of a face is the weak group with hypercharge: it acts on the slot coefficients by $\alpha \mapsto X\alpha$ with generators $T_{j} = \tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\sigma_{j}$ and $Y = \tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\mathbf{1}$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.rung-field-d-term-action-and-tree-masses-on-the-lift-locus}). The stored theorem says the faces pairwise share exactly one slot; we take that literally, in one state space $V = \Lambda^{\bullet}(S \oplus W)$ with $S = \C^{3}$ spanned by the slot modes $s_{1}, s_{2}, s_{3}$, so that $V_{F} = \Lambda^{\bullet}(S_{F} \oplus W) \subset V$ and two faces share the modes of their common slot. Three generations of a field theory are three copies of the content acted on by one gauge group, with $3 \times 16$ independent one-particle states of each chirality.

\emph{(1).} Let $\mathfrak{g}$ act linearly on $S$ and suppose two distinct face planes $S_{F}$, $S_{G}$ are invariant. They are two distinct planes in a three-dimensional space, so $S_{F} \cap S_{G} = \ell$ is a line (the common slot), and $\ell$ is invariant as an intersection of invariant subspaces. Then $S_{F}$ contains the invariant line $\ell$, so it is not irreducible; likewise $S_{G}$. Hence at most one face plane is an irreducible invariant subspace. If the weak $\su(2)$ acts on the slots, a face whose slots form a weak doublet has an irreducible invariant plane (the doublet), so at most one face does. The script computes, for each pair of faces, the full joint stabiliser $\{X \in \gl(3, \mathbb{Q}) : XS_{F} \subset S_{F}, XS_{G} \subset S_{G}\}$ by exact elimination on the conditions $X(e_{a}) \wedge B_{F} = 0$ ($a \in F$, $B_{F}$ the face blade in $\Cl(3,0)$), finds dimension $5$, and verifies that every basis element preserves both planes and maps the common line into itself; a matrix moving the common line inside $S_{F}$ off $S_{G}$ is outside it, and the stabiliser of one plane has dimension $7$ and contains $E_{ab}$, $E_{ba}$, which have no common eigenline in $S_{F}$ (an irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$). The rational computation decides the complex case, since the conditions are linear with rational coefficients. With the weak group of the face $\{1,2\}$, $T_{3} = \tfrac12(n_{1} - n_{2})$ on occupation numbers, the even half of $V_{\{1,2\}}$ has the symmetric multiset $\{-\tfrac12: 4, 0: 8, \tfrac12: 4\}$, while those of $V_{\{1,3\}}$ and $V_{\{2,3\}}$ are $\{0: 8, \tfrac12: 8\}$ and $\{-\tfrac12: 8, 0: 8\}$; the weight multiset of a finite-dimensional $\su(2)$ representation is symmetric under $T_{3} \to -T_{3}$, so these halves are not invariant. The face $\{1,3\}$'s would-be quark doublet $\{s_{1}w, s_{3}w\}$ has $T_{3} = \tfrac12$ and $0$: one member of a weak doublet and one singlet.

\emph{(2).} Take $W = \C^{3}$ (the three pair modes of one pair system) and write basis states of $V$ as exterior monomials $s_{A}w_{B}$, $A \subset \{1,2,3\}$, $B \subset \{1,2,3\}$, $64$ in all. $V_{F}$ is spanned by the monomials with $A \subset F$, $32$ of them, $16$ of even mode number. A monomial with $|A| = 0$ lies in all three $V_{F}$, one with $|A| = 1$ in the two faces containing its slot, one with $|A| = 2$ in exactly one face, and $|A| = 3$ in none. By inclusion and exclusion the face modules span $3 \cdot 32 - 3 \cdot 16 + 8 = 56$ states, and the even halves $3 \cdot 16 - 3 \cdot 8 + 4 = 28$ of the $32$ even states: $4$ slot-free states (the colour-neutral vacuum and the three two-pair states, the $\nu^{c}$ and $u^{c}$ of every face in the store's labelling), $12$ one-slot states (each $Q$ and $L$ state shared by the two faces through its slot) and $12$ two-slot states ($e^{c}$ and $d^{c}$, one face each). If the faces are allowed different halves, an even half and an odd half are disjoint and two halves of one parity overlap as above, so the best choice, two faces of one parity and one of the other, gives $16 + 16 - 8 + 16 = 40$. The script builds the $64$ states, computes the spans by exact rank ($56$, $28$, and $40$ as the maximum over all eight choices) and the sharing pattern $\{(0, 3): 4, (1, 2): 12, (2, 1): 12\}$ of (slot number, number of faces).

\emph{(2), separate pair modes.} If each face carries its own pair modes $W_{F} \cong \C^{3}$ (nine pair modes in all) while the slots are still shared, a monomial $s_{A}w_{B}$ of face $F$ has $B \subset W_{F}$, so two faces share exactly the monomials with $B = \emptyset$ and $A$ inside both faces: the vacuum (all three faces) and the one-slot states $s_{i}$ (two faces each). The even halves therefore share only the vacuum and span $3 \cdot 16 - 2 = 46$; with halves chosen face by face, two even and one odd give $31 + 16 = 47$, three odd give $48 - 3 = 45$, so the maximum is $47$. The script builds the $2^{12}$ states of this reading and recomputes $46$ and $47$ by exact rank. Either way the count is below $48$.

\emph{(3).} Three generations need $48$ independent one-particle states of one chirality, acted on alike by one gauge group. Whenever the faces share slot modes, (2) gives at most $40$ with one shared pair system ($28$ with the chirality of (S), one $\nu^{c}$ and one $u^{c}$ serving all three faces) and at most $47$ with separate pair modes ($46$ with the chirality of (S), the vacuum shared), so the faces' copies are not independent. And (1) gives at most one face transforming as a generation under any gauge algebra acting on the slots. So in this reading the conjecture \claimref{families.three-generations-faces-maximal-composite} fails as a field-theory statement. What is not excluded is the reading in which each face is a separate two-spinor composite with its own slot modes and pair modes, three copies of $\Lambda^{\bullet}(\C^{2} \oplus W)$ with one gauge group acting diagonally; there "two faces share one slot" is a correspondence of labels between composites, not a shared mode, and the mixing-by-overlap reading of Theorem \claimref{families.three-identical-families-mixing-shared-slots}, stated there at observation level, has no operator content without a further rule relating the composites. The result is transport-absent: it is linear algebra on the slot module.
