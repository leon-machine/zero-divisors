## formal
Inside $T_k$, let $B_k$ be the subalgebra generated by the $2k$ plane generators $e_1, f_1, \dots, e_k, f_k$, and let $A_k$ be the subalgebra generated by the twisted base generators $x^{(k)} := x\,\omega_1 \omega_2 \cdots \omega_k$ for $x$ among the ground generators. Then: $B_k \cong \Cl(k,k) \cong \Mat_{2^k}(\R)$; $A_k \cong \Cl(p,q)$ via $x \mapsto x^{(k)}$ (the twisted generators satisfy the ground Clifford relations); $A_k$ and $B_k$ commute elementwise; and multiplication $A_k \otimes B_k \to T_k$ is an isomorphism of algebras,
\[
\Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k) \;\cong\; \Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^k}(\R) \;=\; \Mat_{2^k}(\Cl(p,q)).
\]
The isomorphism is canonical given the ordered planes, and compatible with the tower embeddings up to the twist tracked in Section \ref{sec:limit}.

## technical
Inside the floor $T_k$, the $2k$ plane generators generate a subalgebra $B_k \cong \Cl(k,k) \cong \Mat_{2^k}(\R)$, and the twisted ground generators $x^{(k)} = x\,\omega_1 \cdots \omega_k$ generate a subalgebra $A_k \cong \Cl(p,q)$, because the twist preserves the ground Clifford relations. The two subalgebras commute elementwise, and multiplication is an algebra isomorphism $A_k \otimes B_k \to T_k$, giving $\Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k) \cong \Cl(p,q) \otimes \Mat_{2^k}(\R) = \Mat_{2^k}(\Cl(p,q))$.

The isomorphism is canonical once the planes are ordered, and is compatible with the tower embeddings up to the grade twist tracked in the limit construction.

## lay
Adding hyperbolic planes to a Clifford algebra creates nothing essentially new. The enlarged algebra splits into two pieces that commute with each other: a slightly twisted copy of the original algebra, and an algebra of ordinary real square matrices. So each floor of the tower is the ground algebra with matrix entries.

## one-liner
Each floor $\Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k)$ of the tower is canonically a twisted copy of $\Cl(p,q)$ tensored with $\Mat_{2^k}(\R)$, that is $\Mat_{2^k}(\Cl(p,q))$.
