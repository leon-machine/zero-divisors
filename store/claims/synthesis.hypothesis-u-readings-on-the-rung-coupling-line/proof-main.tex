\emph{Step 1 (one-family entries).} By Proposition \claimref{standard-model.one-vev-vector-relations-breaking-pattern} the mass form of $v_{4}$ on the sixteen is nonzero exactly on $(L, \nu^{c})$ and $(Q, u^{c})$ with moduli $1$, and that of $w_{4}$ exactly on $(L, e^{c})$ and $(Q, d^{c})$ with moduli $1$. By Proposition \claimref{families.doublet-direction-module-georgi-jarlskog-clebsch}(ii) and (iv), $T_{\mathrm{GJ}}$ has entries $-1$ on $(L, e^{c})$ and $\pm\tfrac13$ on $(Q, d^{c})$, and $T^{u}_{\mathrm{GJ}}$ entries $+1$ on $(L, \nu^{c})$ and $\pm\tfrac13$ on $(Q, u^{c})$, in the normalisation where the $10$ forms have entries $\pm 1$. By Proposition \claimref{standard-model.equal-weight-module}(1) the combination $\tfrac34(v_{4} + T^{u}_{\mathrm{GJ}})$ couples $(Q, u^{c})$ exactly as $v_{4}$ does, so on each $(Q, u^{c})$ entry the two forms carry the same sign; and it has no $(L, \nu^{c})$ entry, so there the signs are opposite. The mass form is linear in the coupling, so $a v_{4} + a' T^{u}_{\mathrm{GJ}}$ has entries $-(a - a')$ on $(L, \nu^{c})$ and $\pm(a + a'/3)$ on $(Q, u^{c})$; the same argument with the down-type pair (the check confirms the signs directly) gives $b - b'$ on $(L, e^{c})$ and $\pm(b + b'/3)$ on $(Q, d^{c})$. The check evaluates the mass form on the exact $32$-state Fock model for a grid of rational weights and confirms every entry, and that no other species pair is coupled.

\emph{Step 2 (any family matrix).} With one family matrix $F$, $M_{\nu} = y_{\nu}F$ and $M_{u} = y_{u}F$ with $y_{\nu}$, $y_{u}$ the entries of Step 1, so $M_{\nu}M_{\nu}^{\dagger} = \kappa^{2}M_{u}M_{u}^{\dagger}$ with $\kappa = |y_{\nu}/y_{u}|$ and the singular values scale by $\kappa$; this is the mechanism of Theorem \claimref{standard-model.family-no-go-vev-cannot-separate}, there for $e$ against $d$. The check verifies $M_{\nu}M_{\nu}^{\mathsf{T}} = \kappa^{2}M_{u}M_{u}^{\mathsf{T}}$ exactly for the democratic $F = \J$ (whose one nonzero eigenvalue of $FF^{\mathsf{T}}$ is $9$) and for a generic rational $F$. This gives (i).

\emph{Step 3 (the distinguished points).} On the real line, $(a - a')^{2} - (a + a'/3)^{2} = a'\bigl(\tfrac89 a' - \tfrac83 a\bigr)$, which vanishes exactly at $a' = 0$ and $a' = 3a$ (for complex weights $\kappa = 1$ is an Apollonius circle through these two points); on that circle the neutrino's rank-one eigenvalue equals the top's, and its distinguished point $a' = 0$ is the pure $10$. $\kappa = 0$ exactly at $a' = a$, and by Proposition \claimref{standard-model.equal-weight-module}(2) this is exactly when $a v_{4} + a' T^{u}_{\mathrm{GJ}}$ commutes with the condensate projector $P$ (the check confirms both on the grid). With one family structure, $(M_{\nu})_{33} = 0$ in the basis where $M_{u}$ is diagonal holds then in every direction, the case Premise (EW)'s statement notes would make every light neutrino massless. This gives (ii). For (iii), at $a' = b' = 0$ the four moduli are $|a|, |a|, |b|, |b|$, equal exactly when $|a| = |b|$, which by Proposition \claimref{standard-model.bidoublet-complex} is the case of a real rung vector (up to a rotation of the rung plane and an overall phase).

\emph{Step 4 (the bottom reading).} Solving $|a - a'| = \kappa |a + a'/3|$ on the real branch with $a'/a < 1$ and $a + a'/3$ of the sign of $a$ gives $a'/a = (1 - \kappa)/(1 + \kappa/3)$. With $m_{b} = 1.3651$ GeV and $m_{t} = 92.45$ GeV, the intermediate-scale targets to which the Standard-Model paper's stored coupling sets are fitted \cite{ButlerT12}, $\kappa \in (0.01476, 0.01477)$ and $a'/a \in (0.9804, 0.9805)$, by exact rational bracketing in the check. Neither $0$ nor $1$ (nor $3$), so the bottom reading is at no point that Step 3 distinguishes; it is a tuned interior point at distance about $\tfrac43\kappa \approx 0.020$ from the condensate-commuting coupling. Using pole masses instead ($m_{b}/m_{t} \approx 0.024$) moves the point to $a'/a \approx 0.968$ and changes nothing in the conclusion; running to the ceiling, where (U) is stated, is not evaluated, so (iv) is a statement at $M_{I}$.

\emph{Scope.} The result assumes one family structure shared by the $10$ and $126$ couplings and no $120$; it assumes neither (U) nor (EW), which it locates. With independent family matrices $Y$ and $f$ for the $10$ and the $126$, (EW) holds only along the heavy direction, $M_{\nu} = M_{u} - m_{t}\hat{f}$ (Proposition \claimref{standard-model.neutrino-dirac-matrix-charged-fermions}), and the neutrino's leading eigenvalue is then set by $\hat{f}$'s entries off the heavy direction; that case is not covered here.
