## formal
\emph{Hypotheses.} (B) Each tower of the reversal orbit carries a boundary-mode algebra in the sense of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.boundary-mode-algebra-windows-boost-dynamics}, $\mathfrak{A}$ for this tower and $\mathfrak{A}'$ for the mirror, with boost dynamics $\tau$, $\tau'$ generated by one-particle Hamiltonians $h$ and $h' = u h u^{*}$, where $u$ is a unitary carrying this tower's modes to the mirror's; that the reversal carries the mode set and its frequencies across in this way is assumed here, not derived. The joint dynamics is $\tau_{t} \otimes \tau'_{t}$ with no cross term, which is what the superselection of the tower charge gives (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.superselected-hence-dark-exactly-stable}, itself holding given P1 to P3 and tameness). Write $\xi$ for the ratio of the two towers' KMS temperatures where it is defined.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(One clock gives one temperature.)} Any state of the joint algebra that is KMS at $\beta$ for $\tau \otimes \tau'$ restricts on every window to the product of the two towers' Gibbs states at the same $\beta$, and is the product of the two I-states at $\beta$ (Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state}); so $\xi = 1$. P2 is not applied to the energy the descent deposited per unit charge.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Two clocks persist.)} For $\beta \neq \beta'$ the product $\omega_{\beta} \otimes \omega'_{\beta'}$ of the I-states is invariant under $\tau \otimes \tau'$, is KMS for $\tau \otimes \tau'$ at no inverse temperature, and is KMS at $\beta$ for the flow $\tau_{t} \otimes \tau'_{(\beta'/\beta)t}$. So $\xi \neq 1$ requires a joint state that is not KMS for the shared flow, and under the decoupled dynamics such a state is stationary.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Symmetric law, asymmetric realisation.)} The mixture $\tfrac12(\omega_{\beta} \otimes \omega'_{\beta'} + \omega_{\beta'} \otimes \omega'_{\beta})$ is invariant under the exchange of the towers and under $\tau \otimes \tau'$, is not quasi-free, and is KMS for $\tau \otimes \tau'$ at no $\beta$; each of its two components has $\xi \neq 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Reading, conditional on a modelling hypothesis.} (M) If the descent's deposited state on the reversal pair is a state on two such algebras and T14's cosmological temperature ratio $\xi = T'/T$ (Proposition \claimref{dark-tower-matter.mirrors-temperature-p2}) is the ratio of their KMS temperatures, then by (i) a descent in equilibrium for the shared clock gives $\xi = 1$ and $\Delta\Neff = 7.45$ without applying P2 to the deposit, and the cold mirror allowed by the $\Neff$ cap $\xi < 0.448$ (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.open-channels-their-costs}) requires, besides a scope of P2 that excludes the deposit, a descent state out of joint equilibrium, which (ii) then keeps. (M) is not proved here; the result proper is (i) to (iii) under (B).

## technical
Suppose each tower of the reversal pair carries an algebra of boundary modes with its boost clock, the mirror's clock being the reversal's copy of ours, and the two evolve independently, as superselection gives (which itself holds given the dark-matter premises P1 to P3). If the joint state is in equilibrium for the shared clock, uniqueness of KMS states on windows puts both towers at one temperature, without applying P2 to the deposited energy. A product of the towers' equilibrium states at two temperatures is stationary under the decoupled dynamics but is an equilibrium only for a flow that runs the mirror's modes at another rate. A half-and-half mixture of the two assignments is invariant under the exchange of the towers and stationary but is neither quasi-free nor in equilibrium. If T14's mirror temperature ratio is read as this ratio of KMS temperatures, a modelling step not proved here, the cold mirror of the no-dark-radiation stance needs a descent that leaves the pair out of joint equilibrium, in addition to a narrowed P2.

## lay
The framework's prediction of no extra dark radiation needs the mirror copy of our matter to be much colder than ours. This result works in a simplified model in which each copy carries the boundary modes the framework uses for black holes, each with its own copy of one shared clock. In that model, if the two copies are in thermal equilibrium for the shared clock, they have the same temperature, whatever is assumed about how the birth of the universe shared out its energy. A temperature difference is possible only if the two copies started out of equilibrium, and since they never interact afterwards except through gravity, it would then last. Whether the model's temperatures are the cosmological ones is an assumption, not a result.

## one-liner
For two decoupled boundary-mode towers with reversal-copied boost clocks, a state KMS for the shared clock gives both one temperature without applying P2 to the deposit, and any ratio other than one needs a non-equilibrium state, which then persists; reading this as T14's $\xi$ is a modelling hypothesis.
