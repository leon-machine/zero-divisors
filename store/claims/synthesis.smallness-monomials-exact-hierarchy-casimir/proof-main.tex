By Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-frozen-point-dictionary} the frozen point has $Q(I_{\Lambda}) = \Lambda/3 = H^{2}$, and a density of weight $w$ coupled to it has $m^{2} = -w(w+3)\,\Lambda/3 = c(w)\,H^{2}$.

(i) $G\Lambda = 3H^{2}/M_{P}^{2} = 3N^{-2}$ and $G\,Q(I_{\Lambda}) = H^{2}/M_{P}^{2} = N^{-2}$, by $N = M_{P}/H$.

(ii) $c(w(m)) = m^{2}/H^{2} = (m/M_{P})^{2}(M_{P}/H)^{2} = (m/M_{P})^{2}N^{2}$, for any weight the dictionary assigns to $m$, real or complex.

(iii) $c(w) = -w(w+3) = \tfrac94 - (w + \tfrac32)^{2}$. For $w = -\tfrac32 \pm i\nu$ with $\nu$ real, $c = \tfrac94 + \nu^{2} \ge \tfrac94$; for real $w$, $c \le \tfrac94$ with equality only at $w = -\tfrac32$, and $c \ge 0$ exactly on $[-3, 0]$. So $m > 3H/2$ forces a complex weight with $\nu^{2} = x^{2} - \tfrac94$, $x = m/H$, and $m \le 3H/2$ a real weight in $[-3, 0]$ (both weights coincide at $m = 3H/2$), with $\operatorname{Im} w = 0$. For $x > 3/2$, $x - \nu = (x^{2} - \nu^{2})/(x + \nu) = \tfrac94/(x + \nu)$, and $x \le x + \nu \le 2x$ gives $9/(8x) \le x - \nu \le 9/(4x)$; dividing by $x$ gives the relative bound, and $\nu < x$ strictly, so equality never holds. With $x = (m/M_{P})N$ this is the stated form.

(iv) Apply (iii) at $m = M_{P}$: $N_{w}^{2} = N^{2} - \tfrac94$, and $N - N_{w} = \tfrac94/(N + N_{w}) \le 9/(4N)$.

The last sentence collects (i) to (iii). The check builds $I_{\Lambda} = n_{\infty} + (\Lambda/6)n_{0}$ in $\Cl(2,4)$ in the dictionary's chart ($n_{0}\cdot n_{\infty} = 1$), reads $Q(I_{\Lambda}) = H^{2}$, and verifies (i) to (iv) in exact rationals: the Casimir on complex pairs, the range of $c$ on real weights, and the bounds of (iii) as squared inequalities so that no square root is taken.

Numerically (not part of the proof): with $H_{0} = 67.4$ km/s/Mpc and $\Omega_{\Lambda} = 0.685$, $H = 1.19 \times 10^{-42}$ GeV and $N = 1.03 \times 10^{61}$; for the electron $x = 4.3 \times 10^{38}$ and the relative gap of (iii) is below $1.3 \times 10^{-77}$; for a neutrino of $1$ meV it is below $4 \times 10^{-60}$; the threshold $3H/2$ is $1.8 \times 10^{-33}$ eV, so only a strictly massless or ultralight field has a real weight. The conventions of the Observation are unchanged: comparing gravity with a dimension-one coupling at the Hubble scale, rather than at another scale, is a choice, and reading the three numbers as one problem is the Observation's, not this result's.
