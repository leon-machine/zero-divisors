Work with Weyl symbols and the Moyal product, which is exact on products of quadratics, as in the first route of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.operator-join-ordering-constants}, with the grip built from the chart matrices $A(U \wedge I_{\Lambda})$ of that proposition (Lemma \claimref{mass.de-sitter-scale-frozen-point}, Proposition \claimref{mass.chart-form-spin-isomorphism}). Write $h = \bar{Z}\cdot Z$ for the helicity symbol, so that $\hat{h}$ is its Weyl quantisation, and $e_{ab} = Z^{a}\bar{Z}_{b}$, $\sum_{a} e_{aa} = h$.

(i) For $N = 1$ the grip's components are the moments $M_{U \wedge I_{\Lambda}}$ of a single spinor, and the classical join is $m^{2} = \tfrac{\Lambda}{12}h^{2} = 2\lambda \cdot \tfrac14 h^{2}$, $\lambda = \Lambda/6$; the check verifies this as a polynomial identity from the grip's definition (the $\hbar^{0}$ part below). The Moyal square $\sum_{U,V}G^{UV}P_{U} \star P_{V}$ has no $\hbar^{1}$ part and its $\hbar^{2}$ part equals $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}$ times that of $h \star h$ minus $2\lambda$; hence $\hat{m}^{2} = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\hat{h}^{2} - 2\lambda\hbar^{2} = \tfrac{\Lambda}{12}(\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2})$, the $N = 1$ instance of the constant $-\tfrac{N\Lambda}{3}\hbar^{2}$ of that proposition. For the conformal Casimir, $\tr(h^{2}) - \tfrac14(\tr h)^{2}$ is, as a function of one spinor, $\sum_{a,b}e_{ab}e_{ba} - \tfrac14 h^{2} = \tfrac34 h^{2}$; its Jordan-symmetrised quantisation $\sum_{a,b}\hat{e}_{ab}\hat{e}_{ba} - \tfrac14\hat{h}^{2}$ is the Casimir element of the trace form on $\mathfrak{gl}(4)$ restricted to its traceless part, which is the quantisation of $C_{2}(\Lambda^{2}\CS)$ since the two agree classically (Proposition \claimref{mass.casimir-form-join}(2)) and the invariant symmetric quadratic in the generators is unique up to scale. The Moyal computation gives $\sum_{a,b}e_{ab} \star e_{ba} - \tfrac14 h \star h = \tfrac34 h \star h - 3$ at order $\hbar^{2}$, so $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{conf}} = \tfrac34(\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2})$. The split of Proposition \claimref{mass.casimir-form-join}(1) holds term by term in the quantum theory, since it decomposes an orthogonal basis of bivectors into those orthogonal to $I_{\Lambda}$ and those containing it, so $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{dS}} = \tfrac34(\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2}) - \tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2}) = \tfrac12(\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2})$. Independently, in the holomorphic realisation $\bar{Z} = -\hbar\,\partial/\partial Z$, the check applies the grip operator and the Casimir to every monomial of degree $d \le 3$ (for $\lambda = 1, -\tfrac23$ and $\hbar = 1, 3$) and finds the scalars $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}d(d+4)\hbar^{2}$ and $\tfrac34 d(d+4)\hbar^{2}$, with $\hat{h} = -(d+2)\hbar$, so $\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2} = d(d+4)\hbar^{2}$; and it confirms both operator identities on a mixed-degree test function. Both operators are scalar on each $\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\C^{4}$ for every $d$ because that space is irreducible for $\mathfrak{sl}(4)$ and for the stabiliser of $I_{\Lambda}$, a real form of $\mathfrak{sp}(4)$.

(ii) The identities of (i) hold in the oscillator algebra, so they hold on every module of it; on functions of homogeneity $n$ the palatial helicity acts as $-(n + 2)\hbar$ (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-palatial-quadratics-homogeneity-charge}), which is $2s\hbar$ for Penrose's $n = -2s - 2$, and $\hat{h}^{2} - 4\hbar^{2} = 4(s^{2} - 1)\hbar^{2}$. Substituting gives the three values; the classical ones are $h = 2s$ in (i).

(iii) At $s = 0$, $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{dS}} = -2\hbar^{2}$. Homogeneity $-2$ corresponds under the Penrose transform to the scalar field with kernel $1/Q(x)$ (Fact \claimref{field-theory.imported-standard-field-theory}), the conformally invariant scalar of weight $-1$. With the Casimir normalised to act as $-m^{2}/H^{2}$ on a scalar module (Fact \claimref{mass.imported-unitary-representations-de-sitter-group}), $-2$ is $m^{2} = 2H^{2}$, and $\Delta(3 - \Delta) = 2$ at $\Delta = 1, 2$; Theorem \claimref{mass.mass-weight-dictionary} with Corollary \claimref{mass.weight-polynomial-two-series}(2) assigns the same $2H^{2}$ to $w = -1$. The grip is $\hat{m}^{2} = 2\lambda(0 - 1)\hbar^{2} = -H^{2}\hbar^{2}$, and the classical $C_{\mathrm{dS}} = \tfrac12 h^{2}$ vanishes at $s = 0$. The general statement is the split itself: $-H^{2}\hat{C}_{\mathrm{dS}} = \hat{m}^{2} - H^{2}\hat{C}_{\mathrm{conf}}$ for any composite, and for one spinor $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{conf}} = 3(s^{2} - 1)\hbar^{2}$ vanishes only at $|s| = 1$.

The normalisation hypothesis in (iii) is not proved here. An argument for it: $C_{2}(W) = \tfrac12 g g M M$ is the standard bivector Casimir of the rotations of $W$, whose overall scale is fixed for $W = \Lambda^{2}\CS$ by (i), and a change of normalisation is an overall scale, not an additive shift by another Casimir; the restriction to $I_{\Lambda}^{\perp}$ then carries the same scale. Independently, $\tfrac12 d(d+4)$ is the canonical Casimir of the stabiliser algebra (a real form of $\mathfrak{sp}(4)$) on $\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\C^{4}$ in the restricted trace form, so the split definition of $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{dS}}$ reproduces it. Two facts support it, both cited from memory and not used: the computed $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{conf}} = 3(s^{2} - 1)$ is the standard conformal Casimir $\Delta(\Delta - 4) + 2j_{1}(j_{1} + 1) + 2j_{2}(j_{2} + 1)$ of the massless representation, $\Delta = |s| + 1$, $(j_{1}, j_{2}) = (|s|, 0)$; and the computed $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{dS}} = 2(s^{2} - 1)$ is the de Sitter Casimir $\Delta(\Delta - 3) + s(s + 1)$ of the representation with the same $\Delta$ and spin. Under the opposite reading, that the grip is the field mass and the normalisations differ by $C_{\mathrm{conf}}$ (the remark in Proposition \claimref{mass.casimir-form-join}), the conformally invariant scalar would have $m^{2} = -H^{2}$, in conflict with the weight dictionary's $2H^{2}$ for the same field.
