The proof is in Gauge Theory Gravity on flat spacetime (Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}); on a general conformal manifold the same computation runs in a local trivialisation, where the tractor connection has the same form (Fact \claimref{dark-energy.imported-frozen-point-geometry}). Tractors are vectors $V = \sigma\nInf + \mu + \rho\nO$ of $\Cl(2,4)$ ($\nO\cdot\nInf = 1$, both null, orthogonal to spacetime), and in the scale $g$ of $I$, the derived metric of the field $(\hb, \Omega)$, the tractor connection is the gauge field
\[
\Omega_{T}(a) = \Omega(a) - \nInf\wedge\hb^{-1}(a) + \nO\wedge\Sch(\hb^{-1}(a)) = \Omega(a) + u\wedge\hb^{-1}(a), \qquad u := \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\nO - \nInf,
\]
since on the Einstein region $\mathcal{R}(a) = \Lambda a$ and the Schouten map is $\Sch(a) = \tfrac12(\mathcal{R}(a) - \tfrac16\mathcal{R}\,a) = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}a$; $u$ is a constant vector of the added plane, with $Q(u) = -\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}$. (In the slots of the Fact this is $\nabla_{a}(\sigma, \mu_{b}, \rho) = (\nabla_{a}\sigma - \mu_{a}, \nabla_{a}\mu_{b} + g_{ab}\rho - P_{ab}\sigma, \nabla_{a}\rho + P_{a}{}^{b}\mu_{b})$ with $P_{ab} = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}g_{ab}$.) $I = \nInf + \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\nO$ is parallel for every $\Lambda$ (including $\Lambda = 0$, where $I$ is null): it is constant and $\Omega_{T}(a)\times I = (u\wedge A)\cdot I = u\,(A\cdot I) - A\,(u\cdot I) = 0$, as $A\cdot I = 0$ and $u\cdot I = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6} - \tfrac{\Lambda}{6} = 0$.

\emph{The field strength on the Einstein region.} With $A = \hb^{-1}(a)$, $B = \hb^{-1}(b)$,
\[
F_{T}(a\wedge b) = a\cdot\nabla\,\Omega_{T}(b) - b\cdot\nabla\,\Omega_{T}(a) + \Omega_{T}(a)\times\Omega_{T}(b)
= R(a\wedge b) + u\wedge\bigl(D_{a}B - D_{b}A\bigr) + (u\wedge A)\times(u\wedge B),
\]
because $\Omega(a)\times(u\wedge B) = u\wedge(\Omega(a)\cdot B)$ ($u$ is orthogonal to spacetime and constant). The middle term is the torsion and vanishes; the last is $-Q(u)\,A\wedge B = \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}A\wedge B$. On a physical bivector $X$, $F_{T}(\hb(X)) = -\mathcal{R}(X) + \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}X$, and on an Einstein field $\mathcal{R}(X) = \mathcal{W}(X) + \tfrac{\Lambda}{3}X$ (the Weyl decomposition with $\Sch = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\,\mathrm{id}$, Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(v)), so
\[
F_{T}(\hb(X)) = -\mathcal{W}(X):
\]
a spacetime bivector, with no $\nO$ (Cotton) or $\nInf$ block; it acts on a tractor only through its $\mu$ slot, $F_{T}(\hb(X))\times V = -\mathcal{W}(X)\cdot\mu$.

\emph{Kernel.} If $I' = \sigma'\nInf + \mu' + \rho'\nO$ is parallel, then $[D^{T}_{a}, D^{T}_{b}]I' = F_{T}(a\wedge b)\times I' = 0$ for all $a, b$, so $\mathcal{W}(X)\cdot\mu' = 0$ for every bivector $X$. The hypothesis that the Weyl part has no kernel vector is this: $\mathcal{W}(X)\cdot v = 0$ for all $X$ implies $v = 0$ (in components $W_{abcd}v^{d} = 0$); it says that the linear map $v \mapsto (X \mapsto \mathcal{W}(X)\cdot v)$ has rank four, which is an open condition (rank is lower semicontinuous), so it holds on a neighbourhood $U$ of $x_{0}$, taken connected and inside the Einstein region, and there $\mu' \equiv 0$. Then $D^{T}_{a}I' = (a\cdot\nabla\sigma')\nInf + (a\cdot\nabla\rho')\nO - (u\cdot I')\,A$ with $u\cdot I' = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\sigma' - \rho'$, so $\sigma' = c$ is constant and $\rho' = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}c$, that is $I' = cI$ on $U$. The difference $I' - cI$ is parallel and vanishes on $U$; a parallel section on a connected region is determined by its value at one point (the transport is a linear ordinary differential equation along curves), so $I' = cI$ on $M$.

\emph{Consequences.} The Einstein scale of a parallel tractor is its top slot in any scale (the almost-Einstein correspondence recorded in the cited Fact), so $\sigma' = c\sigma$, the metrics are $g' = \sigma'^{-2}g_{\text{ref}} = c^{-2} g$, their zero sets coincide, and $Q(I') = c^2Q(I) = c^2\Lambda/3$. For the reversed signature the argument applies verbatim: the class $[-g]$ has the same Levi-Civita connection and the same Ricci tensor, its Weyl tensor with lowered indices is the negative of that of $g$ (so it has the same kernel vectors), and in an Einstein scale its tractor connection has the same form with the same conclusion. For the towers, apply the statement to each $I_r$ with $I = I_{r_0}$ for one tower; $c_r = \pm 1$ is equivalent to $Q(I_r) = Q(I)$, and then all towers' metrics coincide (the sign of $c_r$ drops out of $\sigma^{-2}$).

\emph{Examples.} At a point of a Schwarzschild or Schwarzschild-de Sitter exterior the Weyl part of the field strength is $\mathcal{W}(X) = \tfrac{M}{2r^{3}}(X + 3\sigma_{r}X\sigma_{r})$ in the store's sign, $\sigma_{r}$ the radial boost plane (the store-sign spectrum $2M/r^{3}$ on $\sigma_{r}$ and $I\sigma_{r}$, $-M/r^{3}$ on the four mixed planes; the cosmological constant enters only the Ricci part). The check computes the tractor field strength of the static Schwarzschild-de Sitter field with the library's engine on exact jets and finds $F_{T}(e_{\mu}\wedge e_{\nu}) = -\mathcal{W}(\hb^{-1}(e_{\mu})\wedge\hb^{-1}(e_{\nu}))$ with no $\nO$ or $\nInf$ component (at $\Lambda = 3/7$ and $\Lambda = 0$), and that $v \mapsto (X \mapsto \mathcal{W}(X)\cdot v)$ has rank four there: no kernel vector (check\_gtg\_tractor\_field\_strength\_is\_minus\_weyl). For a type N Weyl map, $\mathcal{W}(X) = (X\cdot N)\,N - (X\cdot IN)\,IN$ with $N = k\wedge e_{2}$, $k = e_{0} + e_{1}$ null, $k$ is a kernel vector ($N\cdot k = 0 = (IN)\cdot k$); for $\mathcal{W} = 0$ every vector is, the case of vanishing Weyl part (conformally flat derived metric), in which the tractor connection is flat and parallel tractors are not unique. The algebraic step on $U$ is checked at $\Lambda = 3/7$ and $\Lambda = 0$: $D^{T}_{a}(\sigma'\nInf + \rho'\nO)$ has algebraic part $-(\tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\sigma' - \rho')A$, zero exactly on the line of $I$, and $Q(cI) = c^{2}\Lambda/3$.

\emph{In components (the earlier route).} The source applies $\nabla_{c}$ to the slot form of the connection and antisymmetrises: the top and bottom slots of the curvature vanish (the Cotton terms vanish since $P \propto g$), and the middle slot is the Riemann tensor corrected by the Kulkarni-Nomizu product of $P$ and $g$, which on an Einstein space is $\pm W_{cab}{}^{d}\mu_{d}$; the earlier checks assemble $W$ from its electric part $E = (M/r^{3})\operatorname{diag}(-2,1,1)$ and verify the common kernel of the six curvature components on the tractor fibre (two-dimensional for Schwarzschild, three for type N, six for $W = 0$).
