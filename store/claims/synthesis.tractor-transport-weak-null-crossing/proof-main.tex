The parallel frame, the geodesic and the Schouten tensor below are those of the derived metric of the gravitational field, that is a $D$-parallel physical frame along a worldline of $D$-parallel velocity and the Schouten map of the field strength (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}(ii), (iii) and (v)). \emph{The transport equation.} In the parallel frame along $\gamma$ the Levi-Civita terms of the tractor connection of Fact \claimref{interior.imported-tractor-connection} drop out, and parallel transport $\nabla_{\gamma'}(\sigma,\mu,\rho) = 0$ reads $\sigma' = \mu(\gamma')$, $\mu' = -\gamma'^{\flat}\rho + \Sch(\gamma',\cdot)\,\sigma$, $\rho' = -\ip{\Sch(\gamma',\cdot)}{\mu}$, that is $x' = Ax$ with $A = E_{\gamma'} + N_{\Sch(\gamma',\cdot)}$ as in the statement. Since $v \mapsto N_v$ is linear, $A = E_{\gamma'} + S\nu N_n + N_R$. Three algebraic facts are used, each a direct evaluation of the maps on $(\sigma,\mu,\rho)$: (a) $N_v N_w(\sigma,\mu,\rho) = (0,0,-\sigma\ip{v}{w})$, so the maps $N_v$ commute, any product of three of them vanishes, and $N_n^2 = 0$ because $n$ is null; (b) $N_n E_{\gamma'} N_n = \nu N_n$, since $E_{\gamma'}$ maps the $\mu$-component $\sigma n$ to the $\sigma$-component $\sigma\, n(\gamma') = \nu\sigma$ and the $\rho$-component $-\ip{n}{\mu}$ to the $\mu$-component $\ip{n}{\mu}\gamma'^{\flat}$, which $N_n$ sends to $-\nu\ip{n}{\mu}$; (c) $N_n N_R N_n = 0$ by (a). The check verifies (a) to (c), and that $A$ preserves the tractor metric, on fixed rational data.

\emph{(1) The Jacobi-type equation.} Write the equation as the Volterra equation $y(s) = 1 + \int_{s_0}^{s} (s - t)\,\nu^2 S(t)\,y(t)\,dt$ on $[s_0, s_c)$. Its kernel satisfies $|(s - t) S(t)| \le |s_c - t|\,|S(t)|$, which is integrable by hypothesis, so Picard iteration converges and gives a unique continuous solution on $[s_0, s_c]$ with $|y - 1| \le \exp(\nu^2 \int_{s_0}^{s_c}|s_c - t||S|\,dt) - 1$; shrinking $[s_0, s_c]$ so that this integral is small makes $y \ge \tfrac12$. Its derivative $y'(s) = \int_{s_0}^{s}\nu^2 S y$ satisfies $\int_{s_0}^{s_c}|y'| \le \nu^2 \int_{s_0}^{s_c}\int_{s_0}^{s}|S(t)| y(t)\,dt\,ds = \nu^2\int_{s_0}^{s_c}(s_c - t)|S(t)|y(t)\,dt < \infty$. The same construction from $s_1$ backwards on $(s_c, s_1]$, scaled to match $y(s_c)$, gives the right half; so continuous positive solutions with integrable derivative exist, and $f = y'/(\nu y)$ is integrable. Differentiating, $f' = y''/(\nu y) - y'^2/(\nu y^2) = \nu(S - f^2)$ on each side.

Now $x = (1 + fN_n)\hat x$, and $(1 + fN_n)^{-1} = 1 - fN_n$ by (a). Then
\[
\hat x' = \bigl[(1 - fN_n)A(1 + fN_n) - f' N_n\bigr]\hat x
= \bigl[A + f[A, N_n] - f^2 N_n A N_n - f' N_n\bigr]\hat x .
\]
With $A = E_{\gamma'} + S\nu N_n + N_R$: $[N_n, N_n] = [N_R, N_n] = 0$ by (a); $N_n A N_n = \nu N_n$ by (b), (c) and (a). So the bracket is $E_{\gamma'} + S\nu N_n + N_R + f[E_{\gamma'}, N_n] - \nu f^2 N_n - f' N_n$, and the Riccati equation $f' = \nu(S - f^2)$ cancels the $N_n$ terms, leaving $E_{\gamma'} + N_R + f[E_{\gamma'}, N_n]$. Its entries are constants, components of $R$, and multiples of $f$, all integrable. A linear equation with integrable coefficient has, for each initial value, a unique absolutely continuous solution on $[s_0, s_1]$ (Picard iteration in the integral form, with the Gronwall bound $\|\hat x(s)\| \le \|\hat x(s_0)\|\exp\int\|\cdot\|$); so the transport across $s_c$ exists, as $x(s_1) = (1 + f(s_1)N_n)\hat T (1 - f(s_0)N_n)x(s_0)$, with $\hat T$ the transport of $\hat x$.

\emph{(2) Comparing two scales.} (Each $f = y'/(\nu y)$ is a change of splitting along $\gamma$ only: it is the one induced by a scale $e^{\omega}$ with $\omega = \log y$ along $\gamma$, since $f n(\gamma') = (\log y)'$.) Let $y_1, y_2$ be admissible and $r := y_2/y_1$. On each side the Wronskian $W = y_1 y_2' - y_1' y_2$ is constant (the equation has no first-order term), so $r' = W/y_1^2$ is continuous up to $s_c$ from each side, with one-sided values $W_\pm/y_1(s_c)^2$, and $r'' = -2Wy_1'/y_1^3$ is integrable. Hence $\delta := f_2 - f_1 = r'/(\nu r)$ is absolutely continuous on each closed side, with a jump $c := \delta(s_c^+) - \delta(s_c^-)$. Since $N_n^2 = 0$, $(1 + aN_n)(1 + bN_n) = 1 + (a + b)N_n$, so on each side $\hat x_2 = (1 - \delta N_n)\hat x_1$ for the solutions continued within that side. Following a solution from $s_0$: the two gauged solutions agree up to the factor $(1 - \delta N_n)$ on the left, both continue continuously across $s_c$ in their own gauges, and on the right the gauge-$2$ continuation equals $(1 - \delta N_n)$ applied to the gauge-$1$ solution with initial value $\exp(c N_n)\hat x_1(s_c)$ at $s_c$. So the two transports differ exactly by the insertion of $\exp(cN_n)$ at the crossing, in the gauge of $y_1$. Every $c$ occurs: keep $y_2 = y_1$ on the left and put $y_2 = y_1 + c'v$ on the right, on an interval $(s_c, s_1']$ shrunk, depending on $c'$, so that $y_2 > 0$ there (harmless, since transport away from $s_c$ is regular), with $v$ the solution with $v(s_c) = 0$, $v'(s_c^+) = 1$ (continuous derivative up to $s_c$, since $|v(t)| \le C|t - s_c|$ makes $Sv$ integrable). Then $W_+ = c'\,(y_1 v' - y_1' v)(s_c^+) = c'\,y_1(s_c)$, because $|y_1'(t)\,v(t)| \le C\,|t - s_c|\,(C' + \int_t^{s_1}|S|)$ tends to zero by dominated convergence against the integrable $|t' - s_c||S(t')|$; so $c = c'/(\nu y_1(s_c))$ ranges over $\R$. Distinct $c$ give distinct transports, since $\exp(cN_n)$ moves every tractor with $\sigma \neq 0$.

\emph{(3) The metric's scale.} If $S$ has a fixed sign near $s_c$ and $\int|S| = \infty$ on one side, then $y' = y'(s_0) + \nu^2\int S y$ with $y \ge \tfrac12$ is monotone and unbounded there, so $|f| \to \infty$. In the splitting of $g$, $x = (1 + fN_n)\hat x$ has components $\sigma = \hat\sigma$, $\mu = \hat\mu + f\hat\sigma\, n$, $\rho = \hat\rho - f\ip{n}{\hat\mu}$, with $\hat x$ continuous; they stay bounded only if $\hat\sigma(s_c) = 0$ and $\ip{n}{\hat\mu}(s_c) = 0$, and $\sigma = \hat\sigma$, $\ip{n}{\mu} = \ip{n}{\hat\mu}$ because $n$ is null. A change of splitting with bounded parameters and bounded inverse preserves boundedness. If $S$ is integrable, $A$ is integrable, the transport in the splitting of $g$ exists and is unique, and $f = y'/(\nu y)$ is then continuous for exactly the solutions $y$ with continuous derivative, for which $\delta$ has no jump.

\emph{(4)} If $\Ric = \Lambda g$, then $R = 4\Lambda$ and the four-dimensional Schouten formula of Fact \claimref{interior.imported-tractor-connection} gives $\Sch = \tfrac12(\Lambda - \tfrac{4\Lambda}{6})g = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}g$, so $\Sch(\gamma',\cdot) = \tfrac{\Lambda}{6}\gamma'^{\flat}$ is bounded and $S = 0$; then $A$ is bounded wherever the parallel frame is defined, and the only input is the continuity of that frame, which is the existence of Levi-Civita transport across $s_c$.

\emph{The no-surface-layer hypothesis.} All integrals above are of the function $\Sch(\gamma',\cdot)$ off $s_c$. A part of the Schouten tensor concentrated at $s_c$ would add a distributional term to $A$: a pure null shell $c_0\, n \otimes n$ contributes $c_0 \nu N_n \delta_{s_c}$, whose effect is a further factor $\exp(c_0\nu N_n)$ already contained in the ambiguity of (2), but a shell with a trace part contributes a multiple of $N_{\gamma'^{\flat}}\,\delta_{s_c}$, a jump outside $\exp(\R N_n)$; the hypothesis excludes both, and the uniqueness statements of (3) and (4) hold only under it.
