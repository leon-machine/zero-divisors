Locality and gluing are inherited from the sheaf of sections of $\Aa$, because membership in $\mathfrak{M}(U)$ is a fibrewise condition: a family of sections $s_i \in \mathfrak{M}(U_i)$ agreeing on overlaps glues to a unique section $s$ of $\Aa$, and $s(x) \in \Aa_x^{+}$ holds at each $x$ because it holds for whichever $s_i$ is defined there. The restriction maps exist because the ladder bundle is a bundle of algebras with smooth ($C^\infty$-linear) structure maps, Fact \claimref{singularity.p-reduced-metric-level-reading-many}(ii): nothing in the construction differentiates, so restriction is restriction of coefficient functions. The equivalence with $w_1(\shL) = 0$ is the chain stated: without a global $k$ the parity $\sigma$ is defined only up to the sign of $k$, the even condition is still well defined ($\alpha_x$ is insensitive to the sign, $\sigma \mapsto -\sigma$ giving the same automorphism), but the ladder bundle itself, and with it $\Aa^{+}$, exists only in the $\shL$-twisted sense of \cite[\S 4]{ButlerT4}; the untwisted statement used by the chain is exactly the collar-data case.
