The composite $V \xrightarrow{f} W \to \Cl(W, Q')$ is linear and squares to $Q'(f v) = Q(v)$, so the universal property of $\Cl(V, Q)$ (\claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) gives a unique homomorphism $\Cl(f)$ extending it. Uniqueness gives $\Cl(g f) = \Cl(g)\Cl(f)$ and $\Cl(1) = 1$. If $f$ is injective, extend a basis $e_1, \dots, e_m$ of $V$ by the images: $f(e_1), \dots, f(e_m), w_{m+1}, \dots, w_n$ is a basis of $W$. $\Cl(f)$ maps the blade basis $e_I$ of $\Cl(V, Q)$ to the blades $f(e)_I$, which are part of a blade basis of $\Cl(W, Q')$ by \claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}, hence independent; so $\Cl(f)$ is injective.
