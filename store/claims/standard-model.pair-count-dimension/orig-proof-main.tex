Over $\C$ a nondegenerate symmetric form on a space of even dimension $2r$ is equivalent to the split form, since every nondegenerate quadratic form over an algebraically closed field is equivalent to $x_{1}x_{2} + x_{3}x_{4} + \cdots$ (diagonalise, then pair the squares as $(y_{1} + iy_{2})(y_{1} - iy_{2})$). The split form on $\C^{2r}$ has the totally isotropic subspace spanned by $x_{1}, x_{3}, \ldots, x_{2r-1}$, of dimension $r$; and no totally isotropic subspace can exceed dimension $r$, because such a subspace $U$ satisfies $U \subseteq U^{\perp}$ and $\dim U + \dim U^{\perp} = 2r$. With $2r = n + 2$ the maximal isotropic dimension is $(n+2)/2$. A twistor is the Fock vacuum of the pair system built on a maximal isotropic subspace (Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-pair-systems-two-chirality-halves}), one pair per dimension, which is the count. In dimension four, $(4+2)/2 = 3$, the three pairs of Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-pair-systems-two-chirality-halves}.
