Inside $\Cl(H)$: $u^{2} = w^{2} = 0$ and $uw + wu = 1$ give $(uw)^{2} = u(wu)w = u(1 - uw)w = uw - u^{2}w^{2} = uw$, $uwu = u(1 - uw) = u - u^{2}w = u$, $wuw = (1 - uw)w = w - uw^{2} = w$, $u\,uw = 0$ and $wu\,u = 0$, and the rest of the table $\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{kl} = \delta_{jk}\epsilon_{il}$ follows the same way; $\epsilon_{11} + \epsilon_{22} = uw + wu = 1$, and $1, u, w, uw$ span $\Cl(H)$ (\claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}), so the $\epsilon_{ij}$ do too, and $x \mapsto (x_{ij})$ is an isomorphism onto $\Mat_2(\R)$. The same isomorphism through the universal property: write $E_{12}, E_{21}$ for the two matrices. They are square-zero and $E_{12}E_{21} + E_{21}E_{12} = 1$. Put $e = u + w$, $f = u - w$, an orthogonal basis of $H$ with $Q(e) = 1$, $Q(f) = -1$ (\claimref{std-algebra.witt-decomposition}). The linear map $\phi(e) = E_{12} + E_{21}$, $\phi(f) = E_{12} - E_{21}$ satisfies $\phi(e)^{2} = 1$, $\phi(f)^{2} = -1$ and $\phi(e)\phi(f) + \phi(f)\phi(e) = 0$, so $\phi(x)^{2} = Q(x)$ for all $x \in H$, and the universal property (\claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) gives a homomorphism $\Cl(H) \to \Mat_2(\R)$ with $u \mapsto E_{12}$, $w \mapsto E_{21}$. The images of $1, u, w, uw$ are $1, E_{12}, E_{21}, E_{11}$, which span $\Mat_2(\R)$; both algebras have dimension $4$ (\claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}), so the homomorphism is an isomorphism, and $wu = 1 - uw \mapsto E_{22}$. For $\Cl(2,0)$ the two given matrices square to $1$ and anticommute, and $1, e_1, e_2, e_1e_2$ map to four independent matrices; the displayed elements $\tfrac12(1 \pm e_1)$, $\tfrac12(e_2 \pm e_1e_2)$ are the preimages of the matrix units (by direct multiplication they satisfy $\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{kl} = \delta_{jk}\epsilon_{il}$).
