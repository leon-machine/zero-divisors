(4) is the two cited computations side by side; in words, the conformal algebra is the invariant core of both storeys. The homogeneity dictionary is an identity rather than a convention match: in the realisation $\bar Z_\beta = -\hbar\,\partial_\beta$, the one that satisfies the palatial sign $[Z^\alpha, \bar Z_\beta] = +\hbar\delta^\alpha_\beta$, the generator $q(\mathrm{id})$ acts on homogeneity-$h$ functions as $-(h+2)\hbar$, so eigenfunctions of the helicity operator $s = \tfrac12 q(\mathrm{id})$ with eigenvalue $s\hbar$ have homogeneity exactly $h = -2s - 2$ (the script checks this as an operator identity on all monomials of degree $\le 3$): the $-2$ of Penrose's homogeneity \cite{Penrose1967, PenroseRindler86} \emph{is} the ordering shift of Lemma \claimref{correspondence.symmetrised-quadratics-palatial-algebra}(3). This computation is classical: the helicity operator as the symmetrised quadratic $\tfrac14(Z^\alpha\bar Z_\alpha + \bar Z_\alpha Z^\alpha)$ and the resulting $-2s-2$ are Penrose's \cite[Vol.~2, Ch.~6]{PenroseRindler86}; what the series adds is the pairing with the Clifford ordering shift of Proposition \claimref{correspondence.clifford-side-ordering-shift}. The residual freedom (which chirality is called helicity $+s$) is conventional; the magnitude, one half per dimension at each storey, is not.
