$\omega_j^2 = e_j f_j e_j f_j = -e_j^2 f_j^2 = -(1)(-1) = 1$, using $f_j e_j = -e_j f_j$ (\claimref{std-algebra.orthogonal-anticommute}). $\omega_j e_j = e_j f_j e_j = -e_j^2 f_j = -f_j$ while $e_j \omega_j = e_j e_j f_j = f_j$: they anticommute, and likewise for $f_j$. A ground generator or a generator of another plane anticommutes with both letters of $\omega_j$, hence commutes with their product. Pairwise commutation of the $\omega_j$ is the same parity count. The $\Omega_k$ statements follow: against $e_j$, exactly one factor ($\omega_j$) anticommutes.
