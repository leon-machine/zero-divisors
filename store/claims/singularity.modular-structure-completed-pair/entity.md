## formal
Let $(v, v^*)$ satisfy \eqref{eq:car} and define $\sigma := v^* v - v v^*$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sigma^2 = 1$; and in the hyperbolic atom of Lemma \claimref{singularity.pseudo-inverse-identities-hyperbolic-atom}, $\sigma = e_0 e_1$ exactly, the unit boost bivector of the plane;
\item the rotor flow $R_\lambda = \exp(\lambda\sigma/2)$ acts by $v \mapsto e^{-\lambda} v$ and $v^* \mapsto e^{+\lambda} v^*$: the pair diagonalises the boost with opposite weights, the structure of outgoing and ingoing horizon modes;
\item the spectral projectors $\tfrac12(1 \pm \sigma)$ of the boost are identically the occupation projectors $v^* v$ and $v v^*$: the descent projectors of Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases};
\item in the fermionic representation $\sigma = 2N - 1$: the generator of the geometric boost is, up to normalisation, the number operator, $K := \tfrac{\sigma}{2} = N - \tfrac12$;
\item $\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,1)$ is one-dimensional, so the boost flow is the \emph{only} one-parameter flow implemented by real rotors of the atom, up to reparametrisation of the flow parameter and orientation. The scope matters and is stated: the mode algebra $\Mat_2(\C)$ admits a three-parameter family of one-parameter unitary groups, and the uniqueness claimed is within the class the geometry supplies, real rotor flows of $\Cl(1,1)$; Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies} later leaves that class deliberately, its Euclidean rotor being complex.
\end{enumerate}
One object, three readings: boost generator, number operator, generator of the descent projectors.

## technical
For a pair $(v, v^*)$ obeying the canonical anticommutation relations, set $\sigma := v^*v - vv^*$. Then $\sigma^2 = 1$, and in the hyperbolic atom $v = \tfrac12(e_0 + e_1)$, $v^* = \tfrac12(e_0 - e_1)$ inside $\Cl(1,1)$ one has $\sigma = e_0e_1$, the unit boost bivector of the plane. The rotor flow $R_\lambda = \exp(\lambda\sigma/2)$ acts by $v \mapsto e^{-\lambda}v$ and $v^* \mapsto e^{\lambda}v^*$, so the pair diagonalises the boost with opposite weights, the structure of outgoing and ingoing horizon modes.

The spectral projectors $\tfrac12(1 \pm \sigma)$ of the boost are exactly the occupation projectors $v^*v$ and $vv^*$, which are the descent projectors, and in the fermionic representation $\sigma = 2N - 1$, so $K := \sigma/2 = N - \tfrac12$. Since $\mathrm{Spin}^+(1,1)$ is one-dimensional, the boost flow is the only one-parameter flow implemented by real rotors of the atom, up to reparametrisation and orientation; this uniqueness holds within real rotor flows of $\Cl(1,1)$, not among all one-parameter unitary groups of $\Mat_2(\C)$.

## lay
A pair of mutually adjoint elements that each square to zero, like the creation and annihilation operators of a fermion, combines into a single element that behaves as a boost, the geometric operation relating observers in relative motion. This one element plays three roles at once: it generates the boost, it counts whether the mode is occupied, and it produces the two projections later used to discard coherences. Among flows built from real rotations of this small two-dimensional algebra, the boost is the only one, up to its speed and direction.

## one-liner
The element $\sigma = v^*v - vv^*$ of the completed pair is at once the boost generator, the shifted number operator and the generator of the descent projectors, and its boost flow is, up to reparametrisation, the only real rotor flow of the atom.
