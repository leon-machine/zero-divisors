The Christoffel symbols, free-fall worldlines and Schouten tensor below are those of the derived metric of the gravitational field, read as connection components, geodesics and the Schouten map of the field strength by Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-gtg-dictionary}; the tractor transport is that of Fact \claimref{interior.imported-tractor-connection} in the splitting of $g$, as in Proposition \claimref{synthesis.tractor-transport-weak-null-crossing}. By hypothesis, for every $v \ge v_{0}$ the function $f(v, \cdot)$ has simple roots $r_{-}(v) < r_{+}(v)$ with $f(v, \cdot) < 0$ between them and $r_{\pm}(v) \to r_{\pm}$; for $\Lambda = 0$ this follows from $m(v) \ge m(v_{0}) > |e|$.

\emph{(1) Field strength.} The metric components are polynomial in $m(v)$, $1/r$, $r$ and $x, y$, so the curvature at a point depends only on the second-order jet of $g$ there, that is on $m, m', m''$, $e^{2}$, $\Lambda$ and the point. With $\Gamma^{a}{}_{bc} = \frac12 g^{ad}(\partial_{b}g_{dc} + \partial_{c}g_{db} - \partial_{d}g_{bc})$, $R^{a}{}_{bcd} = \partial_{c}\Gamma^{a}{}_{db} - \partial_{d}\Gamma^{a}{}_{cb} + \Gamma^{a}{}_{ce}\Gamma^{e}{}_{db} - \Gamma^{a}{}_{de}\Gamma^{e}{}_{cb}$ and $\Ric_{bd} = R^{a}{}_{bad}$, the de Sitter member ($m = e = 0$) gives $\Ric = -\Lambda g$, so the store's Ricci tensor (for which $\Ric = \Lambda g$ on de Sitter) is minus this one, as in the calibration of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.plane-wave-weak-null-crossing-null-shell-ambiguity}. The standard computation for the Vaidya form (only $g_{vv} = f$ depends on $v$; $g^{vv} = 0$, $g^{vr} = -1$, $g^{rr} = -f$) gives, in the store's sign, $\Ric = \Lambda g - (2m'/r^{2})\,dv\otimes dv + (e^{2}/r^{4})(-h_{2} + h_{S})$: the $m'$ term comes from $\partial_{v}g_{vv} = -2m'/r$ entering $R_{vv}$ alone, and the $e^{2}$ term is the trace-free Maxwell stress of a radial electric field, $-h_{2} + h_{S}$ having trace $-2 + 2 = 0$. Its trace is $4\Lambda$, so $\Sch = \frac12(\Ric - \frac{R}{6}g)$ is the stated expression. The check verifies the full Schouten tensor exactly at four rational points with rational jets, charges and both signs of $\Lambda$. Since $m' \ge 0$ the dust coefficient $-m'/r^{2}$ is $\le 0$, the focusing sign in the store's convention (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.crossing-transport-tipler-threshold-sharp}).

\emph{(2) The worldline.} \emph{Null tangent and geodesic equation.} $\xi = \partial_{v} + \frac12 f\partial_{r}$ has $g(\xi, \xi) = f - 2\cdot\frac12 f = 0$. For $\gamma' = w\xi$ with $w = dv/ds$, the $v$-component of the geodesic equation is $dw/ds + w^{2}\Gamma^{v}(\xi, \xi) = 0$; since $\Gamma^{v}{}_{bc} = -\Gamma_{r,bc}$ and $\Gamma_{r,vv} = -\frac12\partial_{r}g_{vv}$, $\Gamma_{r,vr} = \Gamma_{r,rr} = 0$ for this metric, $\Gamma^{v}(\xi,\xi) = \frac12\partial_{r}f$, so $(\log w)' = -\frac12\partial_{r}f$ along $\gamma$ (prime is $d/dv$). The $r$-component is then implied by the null condition, and the angular components vanish; the check verifies all four components at the sample points.

\emph{Convergence to $r_{-}$.} Put $\varphi(v) = r(v) - r_{-}(v)$. Implicit differentiation at a simple root with $\partial_{r}f < 0$ gives $r_{-}'(v) = (2/r_{-})\,m'(v)/\partial_{r}f(v, r_{-}(v)) \le 0$, and similarly $r_{+}' \ge 0$. Since $f(v, r_{-}(v)) = 0$, $f(v, r) = a(v)\varphi$ with $a$ continuous, so $\varphi' = \frac12 a\varphi - r_{-}' \ge \frac12 a\varphi$ and $\varphi(v) \ge \varphi(v_{0})\exp\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\frac12 a > 0$; in the same way $r < r_{+}(v)$ throughout. So $\gamma$ stays in region II, where $r' = f/2 < 0$: $r$ decreases to a limit $r_{*} \ge r_{-}$. If $r_{*} > r_{-}$ then $f(v, r(v)) \to f_{\infty}(r_{*}) < 0$ and $r' \le -c < 0$ eventually, which is impossible for a bounded decreasing function; so $r \to r_{-}$. Write $\delta = r - r_{-} \ge 0$ and $f(v, r) = f_{\infty}(r) + 2(m_{\infty} - m(v))/r$. As $r_{-}$ is a simple root with $f_{\infty}'(r_{-}) = -2\kappa_{-}$, there is $\delta_{1} > 0$ with $f_{\infty}(r_{-} + \delta) \le -\kappa_{-}\delta$ for $0 \le \delta \le \delta_{1}$; for $v \ge v_{1}$ (where $\delta \le \delta_{1}$), $\delta' \le -\frac12\kappa_{-}\delta + (m_{\infty} - m)/r_{-}$, so
\[
\delta(v) \le e^{-\kappa_{-}(v - v_{1})/2}\delta(v_{1}) + \frac{1}{r_{-}}\int_{v_{1}}^{v}e^{-\kappa_{-}(v - u)/2}(m_{\infty} - m(u))\,du ,
\]
and integrating in $v$ (Fubini) $\int_{v_{1}}^{\infty}\delta \le 2\delta(v_{1})/\kappa_{-} + (2/(\kappa_{-}r_{-}))\int_{v_{1}}^{\infty}(m_{\infty} - m) < \infty$.

\emph{Blue-shift factor and affine length.} $-\frac12\partial_{r}f(v, r) = -\frac12 f_{\infty}'(r_{-} + \delta) + (m_{\infty} - m)/r^{2} = \kappa_{-} + \epsilon(v)$ with $|\epsilon| \le K(\delta + (m_{\infty} - m))$ for $v \ge v_{1}$, which is integrable. Hence $\log w - \kappa_{-}v$ converges, $w = Ce^{\kappa_{-}v}(1 + o(1))$ with $C > 0$, and $s_{c} - s(v) = \int_{v}^{\infty}du/w(u) = e^{-\kappa_{-}v}(1 + o(1))/(\kappa_{-}C)$ is finite; in particular $(s_{c} - s)\,w \to 1/\kappa_{-}$, and being continuous and positive on $[v_{0}, \infty)$, $(s_{c} - s)w$ is bounded above and below by positive constants.

\emph{Parallel conormal.} Along $\gamma$, $(\nabla_{\gamma'}n)_{a} = \gamma'(1/w)\,\delta^{v}_{a} - w^{-1}\Gamma^{v}{}_{ba}\gamma'^{b}$. Here $\gamma'(1/w) = -w'/w = \frac12\partial_{r}f$ and $\Gamma^{v}{}_{ba}\xi^{b}$ is $\frac12\partial_{r}f$ for $a = v$ and $0$ otherwise, so $\nabla_{\gamma'}n = 0$. $n$ is null because $g^{vv} = 0$, and $n(\gamma') = w^{-1}w = 1$. The covector $\gamma'^{\flat}$ is parallel because $\gamma$ is a geodesic (the check also verifies it directly).

\emph{Decomposition.} By (1), for radial $\gamma'$: $(\Lambda/6)g(\gamma', \cdot) = (\Lambda/6)\gamma'^{\flat}$; $h_{2}(\gamma', \cdot) = \gamma'^{\flat}$ and $h_{S}(\gamma', \cdot) = 0$; $(dv\otimes dv)(\gamma', \cdot) = w\,dv = w^{2}n$. So $\Sch(\gamma', \cdot) = -(m'/r^{2})w^{2}\,n + (\Lambda/6 - e^{2}/(2r^{4}))\gamma'^{\flat}$, which is the stated $S\nu n + R$ with $\nu = 1$. In a $D$-parallel frame the components of $\gamma'^{\flat}$ are constant and $r \in [r_{-}, r(v_{0})]$, so $R$ has bounded components on the finite interval $[s_{0}, s_{c})$, hence integrable. Finally $|S|\,ds = (m'/r^{2})w^{2}\,ds = (m'/r^{2})w\,dv$, so
\[
\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{c}}(s_{c} - s)|S|\,ds = \int_{v_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(s_{c} - s)w}{r^{2}}\,m'\,dv ,
\]
which lies between positive multiples of $\int_{v_{0}}^{\infty}m' = m_{\infty} - m(v_{0})$, the accreted mass.

\emph{(3) The threshold.} By the last display without the factor $s_{c} - s$, $\int|S|\,ds = \int (m'/r^{2})\,w\,dv$, and since $w/e^{\kappa_{-}v}$ and $r$ are bounded above and below by positive constants on $[v_{0}, \infty)$, this is finite exactly when $F < \infty$. All hypotheses of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.tractor-transport-weak-null-crossing} that concern the approach side hold by (2): a $D$-parallel frame along $\gamma$ on $[s_{0}, s_{c})$, $\Sch(\gamma',\cdot) = S\nu n + R$ with $n$ parallel and null, $\nu = 1$, $R$ integrable, $S \le 0$ of fixed sign, and the Tipler-type integral finite. If $F < \infty$, $A = E_{\gamma'} + N_{\Sch(\gamma',\cdot)}$ has integrable components in that frame ($E_{\gamma'}$ is constant, $N$ is linear in $S\nu n + R$), so solutions of $x' = Ax$ have limits at $s_{c}$. If $F = \infty$, clause (3) of the stored proposition, a statement on one side of $s_{c}$, gives that bounded parallel tractors in the splitting of $g$ have $\sigma(s_{c}) = 0$ and $\ip{n}{\mu(s_{c})} = 0$. Case (c) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.crossing-transport-tipler-threshold-sharp} requires an infinite Tipler-type integral, excluded by (2). The last sentence of (3) is clauses (1)-(3) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.tractor-transport-weak-null-crossing}, whose remaining hypotheses (continuation of the field across $s_{c}$ with continuous derived metric, continuous $D$-transport, no surface layer) are exactly the ones assumed there.

\emph{(4) Tails.} If $m' = \mu e^{-2\alpha v}(1 + o(1))$, then $m_{\infty} - m = O(e^{-2\alpha v})$ is integrable, so (2) applies; $m'e^{\kappa_{-}v}$ is comparable to $e^{(\kappa_{-} - 2\alpha)v}$, integrable exactly when $2\alpha > \kappa_{-}$, that is $\beta > \frac12$. Moreover $|S| = (m'/r^{2})w^{2}$ is comparable to $e^{-2\alpha v}e^{2\kappa_{-}v} = (e^{-\kappa_{-}v})^{2\beta - 2}$, and $e^{-\kappa_{-}v}$ is comparable to $s_{c} - s$ by (2), so $|S|$ is comparable to $(s_{c} - s)^{2\beta - 2}$; the integrability classes of the stored power profiles (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.crossing-transport-tipler-threshold-sharp}(4)) depend only on integrals and so transfer to comparable profiles. If $m' = \mu v^{-p}(1 + o(1))$ with $p > 2$, then $m_{\infty} - m = O(v^{1 - p})$ is integrable, $m'e^{\kappa_{-}v} \to \infty$ so $F = \infty$, and with $v = \kappa_{-}^{-1}\log\frac{1}{s_{c} - s} + O(1)$ from (2), $|S|$ is comparable to $(s_{c} - s)^{-2}(\log\frac{1}{s_{c} - s})^{-p}$, the stored logarithmic profile with $q = p > 1$, case (b). The check verifies the exact exponent bookkeeping in the model $w = e^{\kappa v}$ (the $L^{1}$ test, the Tipler integrand $m'/\kappa$ and the profile exponent) and the inner surface gravity and attracting ray flow on a Reissner-Nordstrom and a Reissner-Nordstrom-de Sitter example with rational roots. $\square$

\emph{Remark (semiclassical flux; literature from memory, not part of the proof).} For a quantum field in the Unruh state on Reissner-Nordstrom-de Sitter, Hollands, Wald and Zahn (2020) find (literature, cited from memory, not checked) that the renormalised flux $T_{VV}$ against $V_{-} = -e^{-\kappa_{-}v}$ behaves like $C/V_{-}^{2}$, that is $T_{vv}$ tends to a constant, generically nonzero and of either sign. This lies outside the hypotheses above, and not inside case (b) as a reading $\beta = 0 < \tfrac12$ would suggest. A null-dust rate $m' \to c \neq 0$ violates $m \to m_{\infty}$, the accreted mass is infinite, and formally $|S| = |m'|w^{2}/r^{2}$ is comparable to $(s_{c} - s)^{-2}$. That is the inverse-square edge profile of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.crossing-transport-tipler-threshold-sharp}(3), where the Tipler-type integral diverges: case (c), not (b). If the flux is negative ($m' < 0$, as the cited result allows), then $S \ge 0$, the defocusing sign, and by that clause $\ip{n}{\mu}$ is unbounded except on a line of data. So, read literally, the semiclassical flux removes even the transport modulo $\exp(\R N_{n})$. The reading is heuristic on three counts: the flux is that of a test field with no backreaction, it is not null dust, and with infinite accreted mass the convergence $r \to r_{-}$ of (2) is not available.
