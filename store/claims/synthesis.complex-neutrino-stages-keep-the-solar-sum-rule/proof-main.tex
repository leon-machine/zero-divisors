\emph{Setting.} By Proposition \claimref{synthesis.solar-angle-light-plane-sum-rule}(i), at the order of Lemma \claimref{neutrino.breaking-relation} the light neutrino mass matrix on the light plane is $-(d\delta)^{2}/\alpha$ times $(\Pi A_{\nu}\Pi)(\Pi A_{\nu}\Pi)^{\mathsf T}$ with $A_{\nu} = E + X_{\nu}$; the identity uses transposes only, so it holds for complex $X_{\nu}$ as Proposition \claimref{synthesis.series-family-structures-give-no-lopsided-second-stage} uses it. In the real orthonormal basis $(\hat{b}_{e}, \hat{b}_{o})$ this is a complex multiple of $N = BB^{\mathsf T}$, $B = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} k & x\\ y & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, $k = 2/3$. A complex symmetric matrix has a Takagi factorisation $N = V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$ with $V$ unitary and $\Sigma \ge 0$ diagonal (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-takagi-factorisation}, \cite[Cor.~4.4.4]{HornJohnson}); the columns of $V$ are eigenvectors of $NN^{\dagger} = V\Sigma^{2}V^{\dagger}$, the physical light neutrino states, and the entries of $\Sigma$ the masses. An overall complex factor changes neither.

\emph{(i)} $N = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} k^{2} + x^{2} & ky\\ ky & y^{2}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$. With $d = x/y$, $|d| = 1$ and $D = \mathrm{diag}(1, d)$, $(DND)_{11} = k^{2} + x^{2}$, $(DND)_{12} = dky = kx$, $(DND)_{22} = d^{2}y^{2} = x^{2}$, which is $N_{s}$. If $N_{s} = V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$ then $N = \bar{D}N_{s}\bar{D} = (\bar{D}V)\Sigma(\bar{D}V)^{\mathsf T}$, and $\bar{D}V$ is unitary: the same masses, and Takagi vectors that differ from those of $N_{s}$ by phases on their components. Symmetric, antisymmetric and Hermitian stages and multiples of the sign line all have $|x| = |y|$ (loc. cit. (iii)). For real textures the antisymmetric stage and its symmetric twin agree at all orders (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.one-type-second-stages-are-twins}); the reduction here is the complex analogue at the Lemma's order. The check verifies $DND = N_{s}$ and $D(NN^{\dagger})D^{\dagger} = N_{s}N_{s}^{\dagger}$ exactly at four complex samples.

\emph{(ii)} Write $N_{s} = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} a & b\\ b & c\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$ with $a = k^{2} + x^{2}$, $b = kx$, $c = x^{2}$, so $N_{s}N_{s}^{\dagger} = \bigl[\begin{smallmatrix} |a|^{2} + |b|^{2} & q\\ \bar{q} & |b|^{2} + |c|^{2}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$, $q = a\bar{b} + b\bar{c}$. For a Hermitian $2 \times 2$ matrix with diagonal difference $p > 0$ and off-diagonal $q$ the eigenvector of the larger eigenvalue makes the angle $\theta$ with the first axis, $\tan 2\theta = 2|q|/p$, $0 \le 2\theta < \pi/2$, and the other eigenvector makes the same angle with the second axis. Put $x = te^{I_{4}\varphi}$ (numbers are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$). Then $q = k\bar{x}(k^{2} + x^{2} + |x|^{2}) = k\bar{x}(k^{2} + 2t^{2}\cos\varphi\,e^{I_{4}\varphi})$, so $|q|^{2} = k^{2}t^{2}(k^{4} + 4k^{2}t^{2}\cos^{2}\varphi + 4t^{4}\cos^{2}\varphi) = k^{8}s(1 + 4su + 4s^{2}u)$, and $p = |a|^{2} - |c|^{2} = k^{4} + 2k^{2}t^{2}\cos 2\varphi = k^{4}(1 + 2s(2u - 1))$. That gives the tilt. For the masses, $\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2} = |\det N_{s}| = |ac - b^{2}| = |x|^{4} = k^{4}s^{2}$ and $\sigma_{1}^{2} + \sigma_{2}^{2} = \tr N_{s}N_{s}^{\dagger} = |a|^{2} + 2|b|^{2} + |c|^{2} = k^{4}(1 + 4su + 2s^{2})$, whose ratio is $\rho + 1/\rho$. The check verifies the four moduli identities exactly at six complex samples.

\emph{(iii)} Let $A = (1 - \rho)^{2}/\rho$, so $\rho + 1/\rho = A + 2$; $A$ is decreasing in $\rho \in (0, 1)$, so $\rho \le \rho_{0}$ is $A \ge A_{0} = (1 - \rho_{0})^{2}/\rho_{0}$, and $\rho_{0} < 3 - 2\sqrt2$ is $\sqrt{A_{0}} > 2$. On the level set of $A$, (ii) gives $4su = As^{2} - 1$, and $u \in [0, 1]$ forces $s \ge 1/\sqrt{A}$, with $s = 1/\sqrt{A}$ exactly when $u = 0$. Substituting, $1 + 4su + 4s^{2}u = 1 + (As^{2} - 1)(1 + s) = s(As^{2} + As - 1)$ and $1 - 2s + 4su = s(As - 2)$, so on the level set
\[
f(s) := \tan^{2}2\theta_{\nu} = \frac{4(As^{2} + As - 1)}{(As - 2)^{2}}, \qquad f'(s) = \frac{4\bigl[(2As + A)(As - 2) - 2A(As^{2} + As - 1)\bigr]}{(As - 2)^{3}} = \frac{-4As(A + 4)}{(As - 2)^{3}} .
\]
Since $As \ge \sqrt{A} > 2$ the denominators are positive (so also $1 + 2s(2u - 1) > 0$ and (ii) applies) and $f$ is strictly decreasing: on the level set $f \le f(1/\sqrt{A}) = 4\sqrt{A}/(\sqrt{A} - 2)^{2}$, with equality only at $u = 0$. The function $y \mapsto 4y/(y - 2)^{2}$ has derivative $-4(y + 2)/(y - 2)^{3} < 0$ for $y > 2$, so over $A \ge A_{0}$ the largest value is at $A = A_{0}$. With $p = \sqrt{\rho_{0}}$, $y = \sqrt{A_{0}} = (1 - p^{2})/p$, and $t = p(1 + p)/(1 - p)$ satisfies $4t/(1 - t)^{2} = 4p(1 - p^{2})/(1 - 2p - p^{2})^{2} = 4y/(y - 2)^{2}$, with $t < 1$ for $p < \sqrt2 - 1$; since $\tan^{2}2\theta = 4\tan^{2}\theta/(1 - \tan^{2}\theta)^{2}$ is increasing in $\tan^{2}\theta \in [0, 1)$, $\tan^{2}\theta_{\nu} \le t$. At $u = 1$, (ii) gives $\tan^{2}2\theta_{\nu} = 4s$ and $\rho + 1/\rho = (1 + 2s)^{2}/s^{2} - 2$, that is $s = \sqrt{\rho}/(1 - \sqrt{\rho})^{2}$ and $\tan^{2}\theta_{\nu} = \sqrt{\rho}$, the stored real value. At $p = 1/10$: $y = 99/10$, $4y/(y - 2)^{2} = 3960/6241$, $t = 11/90$; at $s = 10/99$, $u = 0$, $\rho + 1/\rho = (1 + 2s^{2})/s^{2} = 100 + 1/100$, so $\rho = 1/100$ and the bound is attained. The check verifies the substitution at sixteen rational points, the derivative numerator as a polynomial identity in $s$ (its coefficients have degree at most two in $A$, and it is verified at four values of $A$, which proves it for all $A$), the endpoint and the closed form at three points each, the numbers at $p = 1/10$, and, as a sampled control, the bound at every grid point $(s, u)$, $s = i/300$, $u = j/10$, with $\rho \le 1/100$.

\emph{(iv)} For the charged sector, Proposition \claimref{synthesis.series-family-structures-give-no-lopsided-second-stage}(iv) gives the light left singular vector at angle $\theta_{e}$ from $\hat{b}_{o}$ with $\tan^{2}\theta_{e} = \sigma_{2}/\sigma_{1} = m_{e}/m_{\mu}$ for any complex stage with $|x_{e}| = |y_{e}|$. Up to phases, the lightest charged and neutrino states are $(e^{I_{4}\alpha}\sin\theta_{e}, \cos\theta_{e})$ and $(e^{I_{4}\beta}\sin\theta_{\nu}, \cos\theta_{\nu})$, so $|U_{e1}| = |\cos\theta_{e}\cos\theta_{\nu} + e^{I_{4}(\beta - \alpha)}\sin\theta_{e}\sin\theta_{\nu}| \ge \cos(\theta_{e} + \theta_{\nu})$, which is the bound on $\Delta$. Numbers (as in the stored sum rule, PDG pole masses): $\sqrt{m_{e}/m_{\mu}} \le 0.06955$, $\sqrt{11/90} \le 0.34961$, so $\tan\Delta \le (0.06955 + 0.34961)/(1 - 0.06955 \times 0.34961) < 0.4297$, $\Delta < 23.3^{\circ}$, $\cos^{2}\Delta > 0.8441$, while $(1 - 0.275)(1 - 0.02023) = 0.71033 < 0.711$ bounds $|U_{e1}|^{2}$ at the three-sigma edges (NuFIT 6.0, union of its four columns; Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-nufit-6-oscillation-parameter-ranges}).

\emph{(v)} The $P_{12}$-odd part $\tfrac12(M - P_{12}MP_{12})$ of a family-symmetric matrix is symmetric, and a symmetric odd stage compresses to $y = x$ (loc. cit. (iii), as $\hat{b}_{e}$, $\hat{b}_{o}$ are real). By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.rung-field-coupling-to-its-conjugate-is-the-second-family-matrix} the couplings $Y$ to $c$ and $\tilde{Y}$ to $c^{*}$ of a $10$ are complex symmetric, and by Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-clebsch-structure-seesaw} they enter the charged-lepton and Dirac-neutrino matrices as $M_{e} = \beta Y + \bar{\alpha}\tilde{Y} - 3w_{d}f$ and $\mD = \alpha Y + \bar{\beta}\tilde{Y} - 3w_{u}f$ (the $10$/$126$ symmetric and $120$ antisymmetric family types as in Proposition \claimref{synthesis.solar-angle-light-plane-sum-rule}(iii)). The $126$ also gives $M_{R} = v_{R}f$, so a $P_{12}$-odd part of $f$ would change $M_{R}$ away from $\alpha\Pi + M_{3}\J/3$, outside the setting; with $f$ $P_{12}$-even its odd part is zero and it adds nothing to the odd stage. So for all complex coefficients and vevs each sector's odd stage is the odd part of a complex symmetric matrix and has $y_{s} = x_{s}$. Clause (iv) then excludes the measured solar angle if $m_{1}/m_{2} \le 0.01$. By clause (iv) of Proposition \claimref{synthesis.solar-angle-light-plane-sum-rule}, $|y_{\nu}| \ne |x_{\nu}|$ (or $|y_{e}| \ne |x_{e}|$) needs a symmetric and an antisymmetric odd part in one sector, and among the couplings of the Fact only the $120$ is antisymmetric. The check builds $M_{e}$ and $\mD$ from sample complex symmetric $Y$, $\tilde{Y}$, a $P_{12}$-even symmetric $f$ and complex vevs, finds the odd part of $f$ zero and $y = x \ne 0$ in both sectors, and finds $|y| \ne |x|$ once an antisymmetric $h$ is added to $\mD$. $\square$
