\emph{(i)} Fact \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors} gives $v_iv_j + v_jv_i = 0$, $w_iw_j + w_jw_i = 0$, $v_iw_j + w_jv_i = \delta_{ij}$ for the $k$ adjoined null pairs of $T_k$, so the pairs are $k$ fermionic modes and their algebra is a window algebra of $k$ modes, of dimension $4^{k}$; by Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-algebra-boundary-modes-car-algebra} the limit is the CAR algebra over a countable set with these pairs as modes. Corollary \claimref{tower.answers-iteration-question}(1) states that on the physical closure one ascent completes the description and that the further rungs are carried by the shadow-wall sequence, displaced away from the physical region. Hence the rungs beyond the first are not physical modes, and the countable index set of the physical boundary modes is not supplied by them; what the tower supplies is a realisation of the CAR algebra, which by uniqueness of the CAR relations on each window (a finite set of modes satisfying the CAR generates a full matrix algebra, unique up to isomorphism) is the same algebra whatever indexes the modes. The script checks the CAR for the null pairs $v_j = \tfrac12(e_j + f_j)$, $w_j = \tfrac12(e_j - f_j)$ in $\Cl(n, n)$ for $n = 1, \dots, 4$, and that $v_jw_j$ and $w_jv_j$ are complementary projections.

\emph{(ii)} The array and its weights are those of Theorem \claimref{singularity.bosonisation-bridge-s-wave-sector}, whose marginal occupancies are $n_k = (e^{\beta\kappa k} + 1)^{-1}$. Conjugation $\psi \mapsto \psi^{\dagger}$ on a subset of modes preserves the CAR (it exchanges the roles of $\psi_k$ and $\psi_k^{\dagger}$ in $\{\psi_k, \psi_k^{\dagger}\} = 1$ and leaves the other anticommutators zero), so the $b_k$ are a CAR array. For $k < 0$, $\tau_t(\psi_k) = e^{-i\kappa kt}\psi_k$ gives $\tau_t(b_k) = \tau_t(\psi_k)^{\dagger} = e^{i\kappa kt}b_k = e^{-i\kappa|k|t}b_k$. With $N'_k = b_k^{\dagger}b_k = \psi_k\psi_k^{\dagger} = 1 - N_k$ for $k < 0$, $\kappa kN_k = \kappa|k|N'_k + \kappa k$, which sums to the displayed identity of Hamiltonians on any finite window; two Hamiltonians differing by a constant have the same Gibbs state, and $\langle N'_k\rangle = 1 - n_k = (e^{\beta\kappa|k|} + 1)^{-1}$. The frequency count is the observation that $k \mapsto |k|$ is two-to-one on $\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$. The script builds Jordan-Wigner windows $k = -K, \dots, K$ ($K = 1, 2$) with real matrices, verifies the CAR for $\psi$ and for $b$, the Hamiltonian identity, the multiplicities, and the marginals of the Gibbs state at three rational values of $e^{-\beta\kappa}$, exactly.

\emph{(iii)} For one mode with $\tau_t(a) = e^{-i\omega t}a$, the KMS condition at $\beta$ applied to $a^{\dagger}$ and $a$ gives $\omega(a^{\dagger}a) = e^{-\beta\omega}\omega(aa^{\dagger})$, that is $n = e^{-\beta\omega}(1 - n)$, linear in $n$ with coefficient $1 + e^{-\beta\omega} \neq 0$, so $n = (e^{\beta\omega} + 1)^{-1}$ uniquely; this is the one-mode case of Lemma \claimref{hawking.gibbs-states-are-kms-conversely-finite}. At $\omega = 0$ the flow is trivial on the mode, a KMS state of a trivial flow is a trace, and the trace on the mode's $2 \times 2$ matrix algebra is unique: the state is $\tfrac12\,\mathrm{tr}$, with $n = \tfrac12$. The script checks the relation and its solution for $\omega = 0, \kappa, 2\kappa, 3\kappa$ at $e^{-\beta\kappa} = \tfrac12$.

For the closing paragraph: by (ii) the $b_j$, $j \in \mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}$, form a countable mode set with positive frequencies $\kappa|j|$, which is what Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.boundary-mode-algebra-windows-boost-dynamics} requires; its boost dynamics is the restriction of the array's. A quasi-free state on that mode set that is KMS at $\beta$ is, by Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state}, the state with symbol $(1 + e^{\beta h})^{-1}$, $h = \mathrm{diag}(\kappa|j|)$, whose marginals are those of (ii); so, under (Q) (Premise \claimref{singularity.q-joint-quasi-freeness-s-wave}, which the bosonisation theorem holds given and which makes the joint state quasi-free), the array's state with the bosonisation theorem's marginals restricts to the I-state there; parts (i) to (iii) do not use (Q), and by (iii) it is the trace on the zero mode. The provenance statement collects (i) and the definition of the array: the modes are the ladder pairs of the blown-up singular boundary aggregated over s-wave channels, an identification the singularity paper makes and flags, and the tower is the model of their algebra. Transport content: (i) uses the series' tower; (ii) and (iii) are standard fermion bookkeeping and transport-absent.
