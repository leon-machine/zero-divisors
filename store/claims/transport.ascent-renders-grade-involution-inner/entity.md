## formal
Let $\gamma = \pm 1$. Conjugation by $\nu$ in $B_\gamma$ restricts on $A$ to the grade involution:
\[
\nu\, a\, \nu^{-1} \;=\; \hat{a} \qquad (a \in A).
\]
In particular $\nu \omega \nu^{-1} = -\omega$ and $\nu \varepsilon_\pm \nu^{-1} = \varepsilon_\mp$: the two formerly central projectors become an inner-conjugate pair. Consequently the two-sided ideal of $B_\gamma$ generated by either projector is all of $B_\gamma$, by the one-line computation
\[
\varepsilon_+ \;+\; \nu\,\varepsilon_+\,\nu^{-1} \;=\; \varepsilon_+ + \varepsilon_- \;=\; 1 .
\]
The obstruction of Remark \ref{rem:typeC-depth} is thereby resolved in the exact sense there identified: the symmetry exchanging the two ideals, outer on the split algebra, is inner in the ascent, and its availability is what fuses them.

## technical
In the one-generator ascent $B_\gamma$ of $A$, with adjoined generator $\nu$ of $Q(\nu) = \gamma = \pm 1$, conjugation by $\nu$ restricts on $A$ to the grade involution: $\nu\, a\, \nu^{-1} = \hat a$. Since the volume element $\omega$ has odd grade, $\nu\omega\nu^{-1} = -\omega$, so the formerly central projectors are exchanged, $\nu \varepsilon_\pm \nu^{-1} = \varepsilon_\mp$, and become an inner-conjugate pair.

Consequently the two-sided ideal of $B_\gamma$ generated by either projector is all of $B_\gamma$, because $\varepsilon_+ + \nu\varepsilon_+\nu^{-1} = \varepsilon_+ + \varepsilon_- = 1$. The symmetry exchanging the two ideals, outer on the split algebra, is inner in the ascent, and its availability is what fuses them.

## lay
Some Clifford algebras split into two independent halves, and the symmetry that swaps the halves cannot be produced from inside the algebra. Adding one new direction changes that: conjugating by the new direction performs the swap. Once the swap is available inside the algebra, the two halves can no longer be kept apart, and they merge into one.

## one-liner
In the one-generator ascent, conjugation by the adjoined generator $\nu$ is the grade involution on $A$, exchanging the projectors $\varepsilon_\pm$, so either one generates the whole algebra.
