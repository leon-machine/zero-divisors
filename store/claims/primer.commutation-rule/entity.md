## formal
If the element $x$ lies in the subalgebra generated by vectors orthogonal to a vector $v$, then
\begin{equation}
v\,x = \hat{x}\,v, \qquad x\,v = v\,\hat{x} .
\end{equation}

## technical
Let $v$ be a vector and let $x$ lie in the subalgebra generated by vectors orthogonal to $v$. Then $v\,x = \hat{x}\,v$ and $x\,v = v\,\hat{x}$, where $\hat{\cdot}$ is the grade involution. For a single orthogonal vector this is the anticommutation $vu = -uv$, and the general case follows because both sides are linear in $x$ and multiplicative, $\hat{\cdot}$ being an automorphism.

## lay
In a Clifford algebra two perpendicular directions anticommute: swapping their order flips the sign. This rule extends that to anything built from directions perpendicular to a given one. Moving the given direction past such an element flips the sign of its odd part and leaves its even part alone.

## one-liner
If $x$ lies in the subalgebra generated by vectors orthogonal to $v$, then $v x = \hat{x} v$ and $x v = v \hat{x}$.
