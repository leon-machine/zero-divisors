\emph{(i).} For $x, y \in F_{k}$ and $\phi, \psi \in F_{k}^{*}$ the form $Q(x + \phi) = \phi(x)$ gives $x^{2} = 0$, $\phi^{2} = 0$ and $x\phi + \phi x = Q(x + \phi) - Q(x) - Q(\phi) = \phi(x)$ in the Clifford algebra, so the basis $V_{S}$ of $F_{k}$ and the dual basis $W_{S}$ are a CAR family of $2^{k}$ vectors; they span $2^{k}$ orthogonal hyperbolic planes, so the algebra is $\Cl(2^{k}, 2^{k}) = B_{2^{k}}$ and by Theorem \claimref{tower.fock-module-one-qubit-per-rung} its Fock module is the exterior algebra of the span of the $V_{S}$, which is $\Lambda^{\bullet}F_{k}$, of dimension $2^{2^{k}}$. The recursion $c_{n+1} = 2^{c_{n}}$ from $c_{0} = 3$ is $3, 2^{3} = 8, 2^{8} = 256, 2^{256}$. The script computes the dimension $8$ as the rank of the left ideal $B_{3}P$ on the vacuum idempotent $P = \prod_{j}w_{j}v_{j}$, the relations of the eight state-indexed pairs in $\Cl(8,8)$, and the dimension $256$ twice: the vacuum idempotent of $\Cl(8,8)$ has scalar part $2^{-8}$, the normalised trace of an idempotent of rank one in $\Mat_{256}(\R)$ (Theorem \claimref{tower.commuting-copies} with $p = q = 0$), so its left ideal has dimension $4^{8} \cdot 2^{-8} = 256$; and on the exterior algebra of eight letters the contractions have a common kernel of dimension one and the creation monomials reach $256$ states.

\emph{(ii).} Theorem \claimref{tower.witt-index-quantum-capacity}: along the tower the maximal number of pairs of a CAR family of vectors is the Witt index, which rises by exactly one per rung. For $k \ge 2$, $2^{k} > k + 1$ (induction: $2^{k+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{k} > 2k + 2 > k + 2$), and $2^{0} = 1$, $2^{1} = 2$. From $B_{3}$ the ascent gives $B_{4}$, with four pairs and a module of dimension $2^{4} = 16$ (computed as a rank). For the complex base, Theorem \claimref{one-relation.two-capacities-three-consequences} gives complex capacity $\lfloor (p+q)/2 \rfloor$, three for $(2,4)$ and rising by one per ascent. The floor $2^{k}$ is $2^{k} - k$ ascents above the floor $k$.

\emph{(iii).} Let $(a_{i}, b_{i})_{i \le m}$ be a CAR family in $A$ and put $x_{i} = a_{i} + b_{i}$, $y_{i} = a_{i} - b_{i}$. From $a_{i}^{2} = b_{i}^{2} = 0$ and the relations, $x_{i}^{2} = 1$, $y_{i}^{2} = -1$, and the $2m$ elements anticommute pairwise: $x_{i}y_{j} + y_{j}x_{i} = -(a_{i}b_{j} + b_{j}a_{i}) + (b_{i}a_{j} + a_{j}b_{i}) = 0$, and $x_{i}x_{j} + x_{j}x_{i} = 2\delta_{ij} = -(y_{i}y_{j} + y_{j}y_{i})$. By the universal property (Theorem \claimref{std-algebra.existence-uniqueness}) there is a homomorphism $\Cl(m,m) \to A$ sending the generators to the $x_{i}, y_{i}$ and $1$ to $1$. $\Cl(m,m) \cong \Mat_{2^{m}}(\R)$ (Theorem \claimref{tower.commuting-copies}) is simple (Proposition \claimref{tower.matrix-amplification-structural-properties}), and the kernel is a two-sided ideal not containing $1$, so the map is injective and $4^{m} = \dim\Cl(m,m) \le \dim A$. With $\dim B_{j} = 4^{j}$ this gives $m \le j$, and with $\dim\Cl(p + j, q + j) = 2^{p+q}4^{j}$ it gives $m \le j + (p+q)/2$. Since $2^{k} > k$ for all $k \ge 0$, the $2^{k}$ states of the level $B_{k}$ are not the pairs of a CAR family in $B_{j}$ for any $j < 2^{k}$, and for $j = 2^{k}$ the ladder vectors of $B_{2^{k}}$ are such a family, identified with the states by any bijection of bases. The floors strictly between the levels $c_{n}$ and $c_{n+1}$ number $c_{n+1} - c_{n} - 1$: $8 - 3 - 1 = 4$ and $256 - 8 - 1 = 247$. The stored results on the tower (the Fock module, the capacity theorem, the commuting copies, the rigidity and limit theorems, the iteration theorem) are statements for every floor $j$ or for the limit; none distinguishes a floor of the form $2^{k}$; this is a reading of those statements, not an audit of the tower region, and the selection sentence is argued on it. The bound $j + (p+q)/2$ is the complex capacity of Theorem \claimref{one-relation.two-capacities-three-consequences} when $p + q$ is even; it need not be attained by real families. On a floor that is not split a family of elements that are not vectors can exceed the Witt index: in $\Cl(2,0)$, with $e_{1}^{2} = e_{2}^{2} = 1$ and $(e_{1}e_{2})^{2} = -1$, the elements $a = \tfrac12(e_{1} + e_{1}e_{2})$ and $b = \tfrac12(e_{1} - e_{1}e_{2})$ have $a^{2} = b^{2} = 0$ and $ab + ba = 1$ (computed), while the Witt index is zero. The script checks the dimension count on families that are not vectors: the ladder of $B_{2}$ and of $B_{3}$ conjugated by the unit $1 + v_{1}e_{2}f_{2}$ (the trivector is nilpotent) is a CAR family whose ordered monomials have rank $16$ and $64$, the whole algebra.

\emph{(iv).} Using $W_{S}V_{T'} = \delta_{ST'} - V_{T'}W_{S}$,
\[
V_{T}W_{S}V_{T'}W_{S'} = \delta_{ST'}V_{T}W_{S'} - V_{T}V_{T'}W_{S}W_{S'} ,
\]
and the last term is symmetric under exchange of the two factors $(T, S) \leftrightarrow (T', S')$ (two sign changes), so $[V_{T}W_{S}, V_{T'}W_{S'}] = \delta_{ST'}V_{T}W_{S'} - \delta_{S'T}V_{T'}W_{S}$ and $[L(X), L(Y)] = L([X, Y])$ by linearity. Each $W_{S}$ annihilates the vacuum $\Omega$ of $\Lambda^{\bullet}F_{k}$, so $L(X)\Omega = 0$; on a state $x = \sum_{S}x_{S}V_{S}\Omega$ of one letter $L(X)x = Xx$; and $[L(X), V_{T}] = \sum_{T'}X_{T'T}V_{T'}$, so $L(X)$ is the derivation of the exterior algebra extending $X$ (the map that Theorem \claimref{field-theory.second-quantisation} names $d\Gamma$ for the field algebra). Hence $\bigl(L(v_{1})L(w_{1}) + L(w_{1})L(v_{1})\bigr)\Omega = 0 \neq \Omega$. For $k \ge 2$ take the states $x = v_{\emptyset}$ and $y = v_{\{2\}}$: since $v_{1}^{2} = 0$ on $F_{k}$, $L(v_{1})^{2}(x \wedge y) = 2\,v_{1}x \wedge v_{1}y = 2\,v_{\{1\}} \wedge v_{\{1,2\}} \neq 0$, and the module is faithful. The script computes the commutator of the lifted pair, the nonzero square ($16$ and $96$ blade terms) and two failing anticommutators in $\Cl(4,4)$ and $\Cl(8,8)$. That the inclusions $B_{j} \subset B_{j+1}$ keep the relations is Proposition \claimref{tower.ladder-pairs-satisfy-car-vectors}.

\emph{(v).} $|J| = \pi N^{2}/\ln 2$ is Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-mode-count}(ii) in the capacity convention. The bracket $2^{407} < |J| < 2^{408}$ is quoted: the text of Theorem \claimref{standard-model.trichotomy-what-kind-number} says "about $407$" for the base-two logarithm, its check asserts that the integer part of the logarithm is $407$ for counts between $4.75$ and $4.85 \times 10^{122}$, and the technical level of the refuted entry on largeness states the bracket. Given it, $256 < 407$ and $408 < 2^{256}$ give $2^{256} < 2^{407} < |J| < 2^{408} < 2^{2^{256}}$; the same holds for any number with base-two logarithm between $256$ and $2^{256}$. On the proved tower the module of the floor $j$ has $2^{j}$ states, at least $|J|$ exactly when $j \ge 408$. That the count is of registered units averaging two arrays, with no single ladder pair qualifying, is Theorem \claimref{synthesis.the-residue-requires-two-arrays-per-unit-on-average-and-fixes-no-grouping}; no identification of units with pairs is made.
