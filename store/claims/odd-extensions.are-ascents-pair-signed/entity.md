## formal
In the tractor bundle of signature $(2,4)$ let $I$ be the frozen point, $Q(I) = \Lambda/3$, and for $\Lambda \neq 0$ let $V$ be the Stelle-West vector. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $I$ and $V$ span a hyperbolic plane: $\ip{I}{V} = 0$, $Q(I)$ and $Q(V)$ have opposite signs, and the unit directions $e_{I}$, $e_{V}$ anticommute with each other and with the Lorentz frame of $\{I,V\}^{\perp}$;
\item[(ii)] $I^{\perp}$ is spanned by the Lorentz frame and $e_{V}$, and $\Cl(I^{\perp})$ is the subalgebra of the rung generated by these five: the space extension $\Cl(1,4)$ for $\Lambda > 0$ ($e_{V}^{2} = -1$) and the time extension $\Cl(2,3)$ for $\Lambda < 0$ ($e_{V}^{2} = +1$); adjoining $e_{I}$ returns the whole rung, so the ascent factors as
\[
\Cl(1,3) \;\subset\; \Cl(I^{\perp}) \;\subset\; \Cl(2,4),
\]
with the sign of $\Lambda$ deciding which of the pair's two signed directions is adjoined first;
\item[(iii)] at $\Lambda = 0$ the frozen point, written $I_{0}$ from here on, is null and lies in its own complement, $I^{\perp}$ is degenerate of signature $(1,3;1)$, and $\Cl(I^{\perp}) \cong \Cl(1,3;1)$ is the degenerate algebra with $I$ itself the nilpotent generator: $I^{2} = 0$, $I$ anticommutes with the Lorentz frame, and $I\,\Cl(1,3;1)$ is a sixteen-dimensional ideal of square zero;
\item[(iv)] the Lorentz frame $\{I,V\}^{\perp}$ has signature $(1,3)$ for every value of $\Lambda$, including zero.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
In the tractor bundle of signature $(2,4)$, with frozen point $I$, $Q(I) = \Lambda/3$, and for $\Lambda\neq 0$ the Stelle-West vector $V$, the pair spans a hyperbolic plane: $\langle I,V\rangle = 0$, $Q(I)$ and $Q(V)$ have opposite signs, and the unit directions $e_I$, $e_V$ anticommute with each other and with the Lorentz frame of $\{I,V\}^\perp$. The algebra $\Cl(I^\perp)$ is the space extension $\Cl(1,4)$ for $\Lambda > 0$ and the time extension $\Cl(2,3)$ for $\Lambda < 0$, and adjoining $e_I$ returns the rung, so $\Cl(1,3)\subset\Cl(I^\perp)\subset\Cl(2,4)$, with the sign of $\Lambda$ deciding which signed direction is adjoined first.

At $\Lambda = 0$ the frozen point $I_0$ is null and lies in its own complement, so $I^\perp$ has signature $(1,3;1)$ and $\Cl(I^\perp)\cong\Cl(1,3;1)$, with $I$ the nilpotent generator, $Q(I) = 0$, and $I\,\Cl(1,3;1)$ a sixteen-dimensional ideal of square zero. The Lorentz frame $\{I,V\}^\perp$ has signature $(1,3)$ for every $\Lambda$, zero included.

## lay
When the cosmological constant is not zero, the frozen point and the Stelle-West vector together make up the two extra directions by which the spacetime algebra climbs to the larger conformal algebra. The sign of the cosmological constant decides which of the two, a space-like or a time-like extra direction, comes first. When the cosmological constant is zero the frozen point has zero length and the intermediate algebra becomes degenerate. In every case the ordinary spacetime frame is unchanged.

## one-liner
For $\Lambda \neq 0$ the frozen point and the Stelle-West vector are the ascent's hyperbolic pair, so $\Cl(1,3)\subset\Cl(I^\perp)\subset\Cl(2,4)$ with $\Cl(I^\perp)$ equal to $\Cl(1,4)$ or $\Cl(2,3)$ according to the sign of $\Lambda$, and degenerate, $\Cl(1,3;1)$, at $\Lambda = 0$.
