$A$ is finite-dimensional by \claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}. Left multiplication $L_x$ is injective iff surjective. If $L_x$ is surjective there is $y$ with $xy = 1$; then $L_x L_y = 1$, so $L_y$ is injective, hence bijective, so $L_y L_x = 1$ and $yx = 1$: $x$ is invertible. Thus (a) $\Leftrightarrow$ $L_x$ not injective $\Leftrightarrow$ (b), and likewise with right multiplication for (c).

For nondegenerate $(V, Q)$, \claimref{primer.classification-real-clifford-algebras} gives $A \cong \Mat_k(D)$ or $\Mat_k(D) \oplus \Mat_k(D)$ with $D \in \{\R, \C, \HH\}$, a semisimple algebra. Under (b) the right annihilator $r(x) = \{y : xy = 0\}$ is a nonzero right ideal. In a semisimple algebra every right ideal has the form $eA$ with $e$ idempotent (Wedderburn-Artin: in $\Mat_k(D)$, a right ideal is the set of matrices whose columns lie in a subspace $U \subseteq D^{k}$, and $e$ is a projection onto $U$ along a complement; in a sum, componentwise). Then $e \in eA = r(x)$, $e \ne 0$, and $xe = 0$, giving (d); (d) implies (b). The left annihilator gives (e) the same way.

For $\Cl(0,0,1) = \R 1 \oplus \R e_1$ with $e_1^{2} = 0$: $(a + b e_1)^{2} = a^{2} + 2ab\, e_1$ equals $a + b e_1$ only for $(a, b) = (0, 0)$ or $(1, 0)$, and $e_1 e_1 = 0$.
