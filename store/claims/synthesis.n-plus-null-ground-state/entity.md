## formal
\emph{Premise (N$_{+}$).} Units $\hbar = c = 1$. Let $\mathcal{H}$ be one of: (a) the future event horizon of the eternal (Kruskal-extended) Schwarzschild solution; (b) the future cosmological horizon of the static patch of de Sitter space; (c) a local Rindler horizon $\mathcal{H}_{p,\ell}$ of Premise \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-local-horizon-premise} in Minkowski space, flat spacetime with vanishing gravitational field ($\hb = 1$, $\Omega = 0$; Definition \claimref{obj.synthesis.gtg-flat-spacetime-gauge-fields}). On each generator of $\mathcal{H}$ let $u$ be the affine parameter, with $u = 0$ on the bifurcation (edge) surface and $u \in \R$ the whole generator of the extended solution. The modes of $\mathcal{H}$ are the continuum mode space of Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.continuum-mode-space}, one carrier per light ray: $\CAR(L^{2}(\R, du)\otimes K)$, $K$ the transverse and internal labels, generated by fields $\psi(u)$ with $\{\psi(u), \psi^{*}(v)\} = \delta(u - v)\otimes\id_{K}$, carrying the affine null translations $\nu_{a}(\psi(u)) = \psi(u + a)$, which act on the fields by parallel transport along the generator, the zero flat connection on $K$: no internal rotation accompanies the translation. Then the state $\omega$ of these modes is gauge-invariant and quasi-free, it is invariant under $\nu$, and the generator of $\nu$ in its GNS representation is positive: $\omega$ is a ground state for the affine null translations along the generators. It contains no temperature, period or Euclidean section.

\emph{The two data the premise supplies.} (1) The lift. The clause names the lift of the affine translations to the fields, by parallel transport. This excludes the translation-invariant Fermi seas $\chi_{(-\infty, 0)}(P - \kappa)$, $\kappa \ne 0$ a constant Hermitian operator on $K$: they are local gauge transforms of the null vacuum, with its local algebras and modular objects, locally normal to it and generator-Hadamard, and they are ground states only of the lift with flat connection $\kappa\,du$, not of $\nu$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.modular-data-on-a-generator-fix-neither-the-translation-lift-nor-the-sign-of-i}(ii), (iv)). (2) The orientation of $i$. The positivity clause refers to the imaginary unit of the field algebra: the filled positive-energy sea $\chi_{(0, \infty)}(P)$, the time reverse of the null vacuum and the image of it under complex conjugation of the Majorana fields, has the same local algebras and the same net-level modular data and is a ground state only for the reversed translations (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.modular-data-on-a-generator-fix-neither-the-translation-lift-nor-the-sign-of-i}(iii), (iv)). Which of the two is the state of the light rays, the orientation of the imaginary unit relative to the time orientation, is a datum that (N$_{+}$) assumes; the series does not fix it.

\emph{Local form.} On a local Rindler horizon $\mathcal{H}_{p,\ell}$ of a gravitational field $(\hb, \Omega)$ on flat spacetime with nonvanishing field strength, the premise is read at leading order in the size of the patch: in a gauge with $\hb(x_0) = 1$ and $\Omega(x_0) = 0$ at the point $x_0$ of $p$ (the reading of a normal neighbourhood), the state of the modes on the generators is a ground state for the null translations of the background along $\ell$, a physically null direction at $x_0$. That this is the leading-order form of (c) is argued, not derived.

\emph{Scope.} In case (a) the premise is a Hartle-Hawking-type regularity condition on the whole Kruskal generator: it describes the eternal hole in equilibrium. A hole formed by collapse is not covered by case (a): its horizon generators have no $u < 0$ half, and its physical state is not a ground state for $\nu$ on the horizon. It is covered by the premise's local form read on the other sheet, given the reduction of the outgoing sector to one chiral CAR field per light ray (the postulate J3, Premise \claimref{hawking.joint-j3-s-wave-reduction}): posed in its short-distance form (on a generator, the ground-state singularity plus a remainder bounded near the point, a class the same in every regular parameter for the weight-$\tfrac12$ kernel) on the ingoing sheet through each point of the future horizon, whose generator has both halves, it gives the late-time state of the near-horizon exterior outgoing modes as the continuum I-state at $\beta = 2\pi/\kappa$; the condition is sufficient, and states outside the class can have the same limit (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collapsing-hole-flux-from-ground-state-on-the-ingoing-sheet}). In the collapse of a null shell the vacuum before the collapse is in that class, and among states KMS for the Killing translations at late times finite null energy across the future horizon holds only at $\kappa/2\pi$ (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.collapse-in-vacuum-regular-at-future-horizon-selects-hawking-temperature}). Hawking's flux at infinity follows from that late-time state under Premise \claimref{hawking.premise-i-interface-identification} with the greybody passage; the greybody factors themselves and collapse geometries other than the spherical null shell are not covered. Not part of this premise: the weight clause of Premise \claimref{singularity.q-joint-quasi-freeness-s-wave}, $\omega_{k} = \kappa(k + \tfrac12)$ in its version 3, which stays a separate hypothesis (the half-integer offset is read from the modes being spinor fields of boost weight $\tfrac12$ in the state (N$_{+}$) selects, a reading and not a consequence). \emph{Scope note (local normality).} Within stationary gauge-invariant quasi-free states, (N$_{+}$) is equivalent to local normality of the state to the null vacuum on the algebra of an interval of each generator containing the bifurcation surface (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.local-normality-at-the-edge-gives-the-null-ground-state}(iv)), the analogue for that generator algebra of the local normality principle of algebraic quantum field theory, which is stated for the algebras of spacetime regions (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-haag-local-normality}). (N$_{+}$) remains the premise; local normality at the edge is its equivalent form within that class, and adopting it would not derive (N$_{+}$) from the series. \emph{Scope note (stationarity-free forms).} Without stationarity two forms are known, neither of which is (N$_{+}$). The folium form: local normality to the null vacuum on every interval of the generator closed by its Moebius covariance, the arcs through the point at infinity included, puts a pure gauge-invariant quasi-free state in the null vacuum's folium, where the affine translations are positive, and excludes the affine-KMS states (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.normality-on-the-closed-generator-gives-the-vacuum-folium-not-the-ground-state}). The local form at every point: the generator analogue of the microlocal (Hadamard) condition, on both orderings of the two-point kernel, holds exactly when the kernel differs from the null vacuum's by a smooth kernel, and gives the local form of (N$_{+}$) at every point of the interval, with no stationarity and no temperature; the affine-KMS states satisfy it (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.microlocal-condition-on-a-generator-gives-the-local-null-ground-state}). Neither condition, nor both together, gives the ground-state clause without stationarity: a normal excitation of the vacuum by a one-particle vector that is a Schwartz function satisfies both and is not a ground state. \emph{Scope note (the eternal hole when $\Lambda > 0$).} Case (a) is an idealisation in a universe with a positive cosmological constant. On a spacetime with a bifurcate Killing horizon the stationary quasi-free Hadamard state is unique, but on the extended Schwarzschild-de Sitter field, whose two horizons have different temperatures, Kay and Wald give a no-go result: no such state exists (a gap in their proofs was corrected by Kay and Lupo 2016, App. B; on Kerr the corresponding result is argued, from superradiance) (Fact \claimref{synthesis.imported-kay-wald-uniqueness-and-no-go}). The series' universe has $\Lambda > 0$, carried by its frozen point ($Q(I_{\Lambda}) = \Lambda/3$, the square of the cosmological horizon's surface gravity, Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-static-boost-frozen-point}), so its holes are Schwarzschild-de Sitter holes and the equilibrium state of case (a) has no stationary counterpart there; the physics is carried by the local reading (the local form at points of the generators, and for holes formed by collapse its short-distance form on the ingoing sheet). The passage from the bulk no-go to the state of the generators is argued, since no theorem restricting a four-dimensional Hadamard state to the algebra of one generator is imported. \emph{Scope note (the open premise).} That the series' states satisfy the Hadamard (microlocal) condition on horizon generators is recorded as an open premise, not adopted as a postulate: it would give the local form of (N$_{+}$) at every point of every generator, and with the bulk theorems imported as Facts (Facts \claimref{synthesis.imported-microlocal-hadamard-condition}, \claimref{synthesis.imported-local-quasi-equivalence-of-hadamard-states}, \claimref{synthesis.imported-propagation-of-hadamard-form}) it would follow from the bulk Hadamard condition only given a restriction of bulk Hadamard states to the generator algebra, which is not in the store (for the Dirac field it needs the s-wave reduction J3). \emph{Scope note (the reduction of the open premise; added 1 November).} The restriction named above as missing is computed on the flat field for the Dirac field on every null plane, with the transverse labels as multiplicity space and no s-wave reduction: every gauge-invariant quasi-free Hadamard state restricts to a state satisfying the generator condition, hence the local form of (N$_{+}$) at every point of every local Rindler horizon of the flat field (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.bulk-hadamard-states-restrict-to-the-generator-condition-on-flat-null-planes}). Where the field strength is nonzero the same local form follows for such states by Theorem \claimref{synthesis.hadamard-scaling-limit-premise}, (HS) (adopted as a premise on 1 November, proved on 2 November). So, for its use here (the local form at every point of every local Rindler horizon), the open premise is reduced to the statement that the series' states are gauge-invariant quasi-free Hadamard states, (HS) being a theorem; given that statement, the local form is a consequence rather than only argued, while the passage from it to the period of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) stays argued at leading order. Neither reaches the ground-state clause. These scope notes record what is known; what the premise asserts is unchanged.

\emph{What it delivers.} By Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-null-energy-gives-r-premises}, (N$_{+}$) fixes $\omega$ as the filled sea $\chi_{(-\infty,0)}(P)$, $P = i\,d/du$, and its restriction to either side of the edge is the continuum I-state at rapidity period $2\pi$ for the boost future-directed on that side. Hence the continuum restatements of (R) (Premise \claimref{singularity.r-regularity-fixed-locus}) for the eternal hole, of (R$_{\Lambda}$) (Premise \claimref{synthesis.r-lambda-regularity-cosmological-horizon}) and of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) (clause of Premise \claimref{synthesis.c-loc-local-horizon-premise}) in Minkowski space, with the joint KMS part of (Q), follow from (N$_{+}$); where the field strength is nonzero the continuum form of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) follows from the local form, argued at leading order. (N$_{+}$) cannot be posed on point-spectrum mode sets with phase-acting flows (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.point-spectrum-boost-admits-no-half-sided-translations}): the stored wordings of (R), (R$_{\Lambda}$) and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) are its discretisations, recovered only for modes of vanishing Mellin width. For case (c) the positivity and the invariance follow, not assumed, from a unitary representation of $\mathrm{Spin}^{+}(2,4)$ with nonnegative conformal energy on the state's GNS space whose GNS vector is translation invariant (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.positive-conformal-energy-gives-positive-null-translations}). For case (b) with conformally invariant fields the positivity follows in the same way from nonnegative conformal energy: the uniform affine translation along a de Sitter cosmological horizon's generators is the background conformal null rotation generated by $\tfrac12 nI = P_{n} + \tfrac{\Lambda}{12}n\nO$, a nonnegative generator, with the identification of the horizon modes argued (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.de-sitter-horizon-translation-is-conformal-null-rotation}). The series' modes carry such a representation: the twistor attachment's conformal energy $|s| + 1 + n$ is the nonnegative generator of $Z = e_{0}\nu$, and the attachment's Fock vacuum satisfies the positivity and invariance clause on the horizons of case (c) and, for conformally invariant fields, of case (b), where it is the conformal vacuum (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.attachment-conformal-energy-is-generated-by-z}; exact at the one-particle level, the Fock level by the standard second-quantisation functor). The state the clause admits is unique, so on those horizons (N$_{+}$) is the choice of that vacuum as the state of the light rays: a restatement of the premise and not a derivation of it, in which positivity is no longer assumed but the state is still chosen. Positivity remains part of the premise only in case (a), the eternal hole (and, in case (b), for fields that are not conformally invariant, which these results do not reach).

\emph{What it costs.} The thermal input is relocated, not removed: at a Killing horizon (N$_{+}$) is the regularity of the state across the bifurcation surface of Kay-Wald and Sewell (Fact \claimref{angle.imported-theorems-deliver-their-hypotheses}), the import every derivation of the Hawking temperature makes. Within the stationary class this relocation is proved rather than cited: on each generator the boost is the anticommutator-preserving scaling about the edge, so a stationary gauge-invariant quasi-free state is its own scaling limit there and satisfies (N$_{+}$) exactly when it is locally the null vacuum at the bifurcation surface, while stationary states thermal on each half at any period, with or without cross-edge correlation, are locally the null vacuum at every other point, so no condition at points off the edge gives (N$_{+}$) or (R) (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.stationary-horizon-state-is-its-own-edge-scaling-limit}). Within the stationary class (N$_{+}$) is therefore stationarity plus its local form at the bifurcation surface, which on the eternal hole lies off the Painlev\'e-Gullstrand chart. It is strictly stronger than (R): it forces the exact joint product form over boost modes, which by Theorem \claimref{singularity.exact-equilibrium-over-determines-boundary-modes} removes the inter-mode correlations the series' later steps need, so those correlations are departures from (N$_{+}$), as they are from (Q).

\emph{Refutation condition.} The premise is false if any of the following is established: (a) a result showing that the series' other premises force, on a horizon in its scope, a state that is not a ground state for the affine null translations, for example one that is singular across the bifurcation surface of the eternal hole or of de Sitter space; (b) a confirmed departure from the temperatures it delivers, $\kappa/2\pi$ at the horizons of (a) and (b) and $a/2\pi$ for uniformly accelerated observers in the Minkowski vacuum; (c) a proof that the horizon modes must be a point-spectrum mode set, so that no continuum carrier exists on which (N$_{+}$) can be posed; (d) a proof that the inter-mode correlations the series' later steps need belong to the state of the horizon modes itself rather than to a departure from equilibrium, which the joint product form forbids.

\emph{Beyond one flat chart.} The whole generator of the eternal horizon (case (a)) and of the de Sitter cosmological horizon (case (b)), through its bifurcation surface, is global and is not carried by a single gravitational field $(\hb, \Omega)$ on flat spacetime; the statements above that hold on the invertible region are in GTG, and the whole generator, through its bifurcation surface, is read on the manifold assembled from the derived metric's charts.

\emph{Note (a modular form of the ground-state clause, 2 November 2026).} For the fermionic (CAR) modes of one generator the ground-state clause has an equivalent form without a temperature: a state is the null vacuum of this premise exactly when, for every point $a$ of a set unbounded below, its restriction to the modes beyond $a$ is KMS, at some period, for the boost about $a$; two points already force the period $2\pi$, and no stationarity, quasi-freeness or gauge invariance is assumed (\claimref{synthesis.thermality-of-every-future-half-generator-fixes-the-period-and-the-null-ground-state}). This is the generator form of geometric modular action. It is available where every point of a generator is the edge of a Killing wedge, on the null planes of Minkowski space and, argued, on de Sitter horizons (given geometric modular action and that the horizon modes generate the wedge algebras), not on the eternal hole, where only the bifurcation surface carries a Killing boost. It is recorded as an equivalent form of the clause, not as a further premise; what (N$_{+}$) asserts is unchanged.

\emph{Note (a field-level modular form of the ground-state clause, 6 November 2026).} Within gauge-invariant quasi-free states on the modes of one generator the ground-state clause has a second equivalent form, free of stationarity, of any modular group and of any temperature: for a set of cuts whose differences generate a dense subgroup of $\R$ (three cuts $0$, $1$, $c$ with $c$ irrational suffice), the modular conjugation of the algebra of the modes beyond each cut acts on the fields as the twisted charge-conjugating reflection about the cut, with the sign $\eta = -1$ (field-level geometric modular action; Theorem \claimref{synthesis.geometric-modular-conjugations-on-a-generator-give-the-null-ground-state}(iv), (v), the converse imported). In this form the lift of (1) above is fixed by the reflections, whose products are the field translations, and the orientation of (2) is the sign $\eta$: $\eta = +1$ gives the filled positive-energy sea, so $\eta = -1$ is assumed, as (N$_{+}$) assumes the orientation. It needs only the generator algebra, so it can be posed in every case of the scope, the eternal hole included, where it is posed and not derived. Forms of the same condition on the net of local algebras only cannot give the clause: every local gauge transform of the null vacuum, the Fermi seas included, satisfies them (Theorem \claimref{synthesis.modular-data-on-a-generator-fix-neither-the-translation-lift-nor-the-sign-of-i}(i), (iv)). It is recorded as an equivalent form of the clause within that class, as the KMS form of 2 November was, not as a further premise; (N$_{+}$) remains the premise and what it asserts is unchanged.
## technical
The series has three thermal premises of one shape: (R) at the black-hole horizon, (R$_{\Lambda}$) at the cosmological horizon and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) on every local Rindler horizon, each asserting that boundary modes are thermal for a boost at the period where the boost's Euclidean section is smooth. This premise replaces their thermal content with one statement that has no temperature in it. Place the modes continuously along each light ray of the horizon, with the affine translations along the rays. (N$_{+}$) asks that the state of these modes be gauge-invariant and quasi-free and a ground state for those translations: invariant, with positive generator. The store proves that such a state is the filled sea of every generator and that, seen from either side of the edge, it is exactly thermal at rapidity period $2\pi$; so the continuum forms of (R) for the eternal hole, of (R$_{\Lambda}$) and of (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) in Minkowski space, with the joint thermal part of (Q), follow from it. Where the gravitational field strength is nonzero the local horizons need its leading-order local form, which is argued. The premise supplies two data that the structure of the light rays alone does not: the translations act on the fields by parallel transport along the rays, which excludes the translation-invariant states filled up to a nonzero null momentum (they have the vacuum's local algebras and modular objects), and the orientation of the imaginary unit relative to time, which separates the vacuum from the filled positive-energy sea and which the series does not fix.

The scope is the eternal hole (a Hartle-Hawking-type state), de Sitter space and the local horizons. A hole formed by collapse is reached through the premise's short-distance form read on the ingoing sheet through each point of the future horizon: given the reduction J3 of the outgoing sector to one chiral field per light ray, it gives the late-time outgoing state near the horizon at temperature $\kappa/2\pi$ (a sufficient condition); the vacuum before a null-shell collapse satisfies it, and among late-time thermal states only $\kappa/2\pi$ has finite energy across the future horizon; greybody factors and other collapse geometries are not covered. On local Rindler horizons of Minkowski space, and on de Sitter horizons for conformally invariant fields, the positivity follows from positive conformal energy, and the twistor attachment's vacuum satisfies the positivity and invariance clause there, so on those horizons the premise is the choice of that vacuum, a restatement rather than a derivation; only at the eternal hole (and for fields that are not conformally invariant in de Sitter space) is positivity still assumed. The counted point-spectrum mode sets on which (R), (R$_{\Lambda}$) and (R$_{\mathrm{loc}}$) are worded become discretisations. The thermal input is moved, not removed: it is now the regularity of the state across the bifurcation surface (Kay-Wald, Sewell), and within the stationary class this is proved: a stationary state satisfies the premise exactly when it is locally the null vacuum at the bifurcation surface, and no condition at other points implies it. Equivalently, within stationary gauge-invariant quasi-free states the premise is local normality to the null vacuum at the bifurcation surface, the analogue on one generator of Haag's local normality; the premise stays the premise, with this as its equivalent form there. Without stationarity, local normality on the generator closed at infinity gives only the vacuum's folium, and the generator analogue of the Hadamard condition only the local form at every point; neither, nor both, gives the ground state. The eternal hole of case (a) is an idealisation when $\Lambda > 0$, as in the series' universe: by Kay and Wald no stationary Hadamard state exists on a Schwarzschild-de Sitter hole, so the local reading carries the physics. Whether the series' states satisfy the Hadamard condition on horizon generators is an open premise, not a postulate; for the local form at every point of every local Rindler horizon it now reduces to the series' states being Hadamard, by the flat-field restriction result and, where the field strength is nonzero, the scaling-limit theorem (HS). The premise forces the exact joint product form, stronger than (R) on single modes, so the correlations the chain needs are departures from it. It is a Tier C premise, taken as given, refuted if the other premises force a horizon state in its scope that is not such a ground state, by a confirmed departure from the temperatures it gives, by a proof that the modes cannot live on a continuum, or by a proof that the needed correlations belong to the state itself. The whole generator of the eternal and de Sitter horizons, through the bifurcation surface, is global and is not carried by the store's one-chart fields, so it is read on several charts of flat spacetime, each carrying its own gravitational field, glued on overlaps by displacement and rotation gauge transformations.

## lay
Black holes, the horizon of an expanding universe and the horizon an accelerating observer sees all have a temperature. The series used to assume each temperature with its own premise. This premise replaces them with one assumption that mentions no temperature: the quantum modes running along each horizon's light rays are in their lowest-energy state for sliding along the rays. The store proves that this state, seen from one side of the horizon, is exactly thermal at the expected temperature. The assumption describes a black hole that has always existed in equilibrium; read on the light rays falling into the horizon, its short-distance form also covers a hole formed by collapse, given a simplification of the radiating fields to one direction per light ray. For the horizon an accelerating observer sees, and for the cosmological horizon with fields that keep their form under rescalings of lengths, it amounts to choosing the standard vacuum of the field theory. It still carries an input: that the state is smooth where the two halves of the horizon meet, the condition every derivation of black-hole temperature uses in some form. A black hole that has always existed in equilibrium is an idealisation in a universe with a positive cosmological constant, where no such equilibrium state exists, so the local form of the assumption is the one that matters there; whether the series' states are smooth enough at short distances to give that local form everywhere is still open, though it now comes down to the usual short-distance requirement on states: how such states look at very short distances where gravity is present, assumed before, is now proved. The assumption also supplies two things the light rays alone do not: that sliding along a ray carries the fields along with no internal rotation, which rules out states filled up to some nonzero momentum, and which way round the square root of minus one points relative to the direction of time, which the series does not otherwise fix. For the simplest class of states it can be restated as a mirror condition at a few points of each ray, with no reference to time evolution or temperature.
