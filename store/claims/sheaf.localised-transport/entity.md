## formal
The subalgebra of $\shC$ generated by $T^*M$ is canonically isomorphic to $\shA$, globally, including over $\Sig$, because $h$ restricted to $T^*M$ equals $G$ identically by construction. Over $\Mo \cap \Mp$ the inclusion $\shA \subset \shC$ is fibrewise the conformal embedding $\Cl(p,q) \subset \Cl(p{+}1,q{+}1)$ of Rule 1 \cite{ButlerT1}. Over $\Sig$ the very same elements change ideal-theoretic character: a radical covector $k$ satisfies $k^2 = 0$ in both algebras, but the two-sided ideal it generates is square-zero in $A(x)$ (Theorem \claimref{sheaf.degeneration-sheaf}) and is the whole algebra in $C(x)$ (Proposition \claimref{sheaf.resolution-ideal-theoretically}), where $k$ acquires a partner $k^*$ built from the coupling, with $kk^* + k^*k = 1$ and $(k^*)^2 = 0$. The completion does not modify the degenerate algebra; it re-embeds it where its infinitesimals cease to be infinitesimal. In particular the localised ladder pair $(k, k^*)$ exists exactly over $\Sig$: the CAR structure of the series' operator-algebraic reading is supported on the singular locus.

## technical
The subalgebra of the ambient sheaf $\shC = \Cl(T, h)$ generated by $T^*M$ is canonically isomorphic to $\shA$ globally, the wall included, because $h$ restricts to $G$ on $T^*M$ identically. Over $\Mo \cap \Mp$ the inclusion $\shA \subset \shC$ is fibrewise the conformal embedding $\Cl(p,q) \subset \Cl(p{+}1, q{+}1)$. Over $\Sig$ a radical covector $k$ has $Q(k) = 0$ in both algebras, yet the two-sided ideal it generates is square-zero in $A(x)$ and is the whole of $C(x)$, where $k^* = \nO/(2\nu)$ satisfies $Q(k^*) = 0$ and $k k^* + k^* k = 1$.

So the completion does not modify the degenerate algebra; it re-embeds it where its infinitesimals cease to be infinitesimal. The ladder pair $(k, k^*)$ exists exactly over $\Sig$, so the canonical anticommutation structure of the series' operator-algebraic reading is supported on the singular locus.

## lay
When the degenerate algebra is placed inside the larger completed algebra, it sits there unchanged. What changes is its surroundings: on the wall, a direction of zero length that was a dead end in the small algebra finds a partner in the large one, and together they behave like the creation and annihilation operators of a single fermion. This pairing happens only on the wall, so the fermion-like structure lives exactly where the geometry is singular.

## one-liner
The degenerate Clifford sheaf embeds canonically in the completed sheaf, and on the wall the radical covector acquires a partner with which it forms a ladder pair that exists exactly over the wall.
