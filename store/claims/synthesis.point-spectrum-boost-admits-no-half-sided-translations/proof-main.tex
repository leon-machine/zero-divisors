\emph{(i)} By Definition \claimref{obj.hawking.boundary-mode-algebra-windows-boost-dynamics} the dynamics multiplies each generator $a_{j}$ by a phase, so it maps each window algebra $\mathfrak{A}_{F}$, the algebra generated by $\{a_{j}\}_{j \in F}$, onto itself; on $\mathfrak{A}_{F} \cong \End(\Lambda^{\bullet}\C^{F})$ ($\C = \mathrm{span}_{\R}\{1, I_{4}\}$) it is $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{I_{4}tK})$ with $K = \sum_{j \in F}\omega_{j}N_{j}$ (in the Fock basis of the window each monomial in the $a_{j}, a_{j}^{\dagger}$ picks up the phase of its net weight, the same as under $\operatorname{Ad}(e^{I_{4}tK})$). The orbit $\{\tau_{t}(x)\}_{t}$ of $x \in \mathfrak{A}_{F}$ is a bounded subset of the finite-dimensional space $\mathfrak{A}_{F}$, since each $\tau_{t}$ is isometric, so its closure $K_{x}$ is compact. Since $K$ is self-adjoint, $\operatorname{ad}K$ is diagonalisable on $\mathfrak{A}_{F}$ with real eigenvalues, the differences of eigenvalues of $K$; so $[K, P] = I_{4}cP$ with $c$ real and nonzero has only $P = 0$. This is the finite-window obstruction (an element scaled by a real exponential cannot be an eigenvector of a unitary flow) in exact form; Proposition \claimref{hawking.modular-structure-completed-pair}(2) shows the real scaling $v \mapsto e^{-\lambda}v$ is carried by the rotor flow, which is not a $*$-automorphism. The script verifies it on a three-mode window for the weights $(-1, 0, 1)$ and $(1, 2, 3)$: the real form of the equation, $[K, X] = -cY$ and $[K, Y] = cX$ for $P = X + I_{4}Y$, has full rank for $c = 1, 2, 7$, while at $c = 0$ its solution space has dimension twice the dimension of the commutant of $K$ ($24$ and $10$, the sums of squared multiplicities of the two spectra).

\emph{(ii)} Fix $a \in \R$ and $x \in \mathfrak{A}_{F}$. The covariance gives $\gamma_{a} = \tau_{t}\circ\gamma_{e^{-ct}a}\circ\tau_{-t}$ for every $t$, so, with $s = e^{-ct}a$ and $y_{t} = \tau_{-t}(x) \in K_{x}$,
\[
\|\gamma_{a}(x) - x\| \;=\; \bigl\|\tau_{t}\bigl(\gamma_{s}(y_{t}) - y_{t}\bigr)\bigr\| \;=\; \|\gamma_{s}(y_{t}) - y_{t}\| .
\]
Let $\varepsilon > 0$ and cover the compact set $K_{x}$ by finitely many balls $B(y_{i}, \varepsilon)$. By strong continuity there is $\delta > 0$ with $\|\gamma_{s}(y_{i}) - y_{i}\| < \varepsilon$ for all $i$ whenever $|s| < \delta$, and since each $\gamma_{s}$ is isometric, $\|\gamma_{s}(y) - y\| \le \|\gamma_{s}(y - y_{i})\| + \|\gamma_{s}(y_{i}) - y_{i}\| + \|y_{i} - y\| < 3\varepsilon$ for every $y \in K_{x}$ and $|s| < \delta$. Choosing $t$ with $ct$ so large that $e^{-ct}|a| < \delta$ gives $\|\gamma_{a}(x) - x\| < 3\varepsilon$. Hence $\gamma_{a}(x) = x$ on the dense subalgebra $\bigcup_{F}\mathfrak{A}_{F}$ (Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-algebra-boundary-modes-car-algebra}: the CAR algebra is the closure of the union of the windows), and $\gamma_{a} = \id$ by continuity.

\emph{(iii)} Invariance of $\omega$ makes $V_{t}$ well defined and unitary. For $x \in \mathfrak{A}_{F}$ the orbit $V_{t}\pi(x)\Omega = \pi(\tau_{t}(x))\Omega$ is the image of the orbit of $x$ under the bounded map $y \mapsto \pi(y)\Omega$, so its closure is compact. The argument of (ii), with $U(a)$ unitary in place of $\gamma_{a}$ and vectors in place of elements, gives $U(a)\pi(x)\Omega = \pi(x)\Omega$ for every $x$ in the union of the windows; these vectors are dense because $\Omega$ is cyclic, so $U(a) = 1$. If $\Delta^{I_{4}s} = V_{-\beta s}$, the conclusion of the abstract theorem of Fact \claimref{angle.imported-theorems-deliver-their-hypotheses}, $\Delta^{I_{4}s}U(a)\Delta^{-I_{4}s} = U(e^{-2\pi s}a)$, reads $V_{t}U(a)V_{-t} = U(e^{2\pi t/\beta}a)$ with $t = -\beta s$, the hypothesis of (iii) with $c = 2\pi/\beta \neq 0$, so any $U$ satisfying that theorem's hypotheses is trivial. For a faithful $(\tau, \beta)$-KMS state the modular group is $\sigma_{s} = \tau_{-\beta s}$ by the correspondence between the KMS condition and modular theory (Takesaki, cited from memory; on a matrix factor it is the explicit computation of Proposition \claimref{singularity.modular-theory-matrix-factor-proved-outright}, $\sigma_{s}(a) = \rho^{I_{4}s}a\rho^{-I_{4}s}$ with $\rho \propto e^{-\beta K}$), and $\Delta^{I_{4}s}\pi(x)\Omega = \pi(\sigma_{s}(x))\Omega = V_{-\beta s}\pi(x)\Omega$ on a dense set, which is the case named in the statement.

\emph{(iv)} For $\omega_{k} = \kappa k$, $\tau_{2\pi/\kappa}(a_{k}) = e^{-2\pi I_{4}k}a_{k} = a_{k}$; for $\omega_{k} = \kappa(k + \tfrac12)$, $\tau_{2\pi/\kappa}(a_{k}) = e^{-I_{4}\pi(2k+1)}a_{k} = -a_{k}$. The script checks the integrality behind both on a three-mode window: for the weights $(-1, 0, 1)$ every eigenvalue of $\operatorname{ad}K$ is an integer (the set is $\{-2, \ldots, 2\}$); for $(\tfrac12, \tfrac32, \tfrac52)$ the eigenvalues lie in $\tfrac12\Z$, are integers exactly on even matrix units, and reach $\tfrac92$.

For the consequence: the theorem of Fact \claimref{angle.imported-theorems-deliver-their-hypotheses} produces $2\pi$ only from a nontrivial $U$; by (ii) and (iii) none exists on the spaces named, for any $\beta$ and any real weights. On the continuum mode space with a Hamiltonian of continuous spectrum the compactness used in (ii) fails, and the imported free-field examples realise the mechanism there; whether the boundary modes of the series satisfy the remaining hypotheses on that space is not addressed.
