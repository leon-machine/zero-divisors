## formal
Let $(V, Q)$ be a real quadratic space of dimension $n$ whose radical $\operatorname{rad} V$ (\claimref{obj.std-algebra.radical}) has dimension $r$, let $J = \Cl(V, Q)\, \operatorname{rad} V\, \Cl(V, Q)$ be the two-sided ideal it generates, and let $\bar V = V / \operatorname{rad} V$ with $\bar Q(v + \operatorname{rad} V) = Q(v)$. Let $U$ be any complement of $\operatorname{rad} V$, with basis $e_1, \dots, e_m$ ($m = n - r$), and $f_1, \dots, f_r$ a basis of $\operatorname{rad} V$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $f a = \hat{a} f$ for every $f \in \operatorname{rad} V$ and $a \in \Cl(V, Q)$; hence $J = \Cl(V, Q)\operatorname{rad} V = \operatorname{rad} V\, \Cl(V, Q)$, and $J$ is spanned by the blades $e_I f_K$ with $K \ne \emptyset$, so $\dim J = 2^{n} - 2^{n-r}$.
\item[(ii)] For $k \ge 1$, $J^{k}$ is spanned by the blades $e_I f_K$ with $\lvert K \rvert \ge k$. So $J^{r+1} = 0$, and $J^{r} = \Cl(U) f_1 \cdots f_r \ne 0$ when $r \ge 1$: the nilpotency index of $J$ is exactly $r + 1$.
\item[(iii)] $\bar Q$ is well defined and nondegenerate, the map $V \to \bar V$ extends to a surjective algebra homomorphism $\pi : \Cl(V, Q) \to \Cl(\bar V, \bar Q)$ with kernel exactly $J$, and $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism $\Cl(U, Q|_U) \to \Cl(\bar V, \bar Q)$. So
\begin{equation}
\Cl(V, Q) / J \cong \Cl(\bar V, \bar Q), \qquad \Cl(V, Q) = \Cl(U, Q|_U) \oplus J ,
\end{equation}
the second a direct sum of a subalgebra and an ideal.
\item[(iv)] $J$ is the largest nilpotent left (or right, or two-sided) ideal of $\Cl(V, Q)$, and it is the radical of the bilinear form $(x, y) \mapsto \langle x y \rangle_0$. In particular $J$ does not depend on choices, and the nondegenerate algebra $\Cl(\bar V, \bar Q)$ has no nonzero nilpotent one-sided ideal.
\end{enumerate}
For $r = 1$: $J^{2} = 0$ and $\dim J = 2^{n-1}$. Example: for a null vector $k$ of $\R^{1,3}$ the null hyperplane $k^{\perp}$ has radical $\R k$; its Clifford algebra is the subalgebra of $\Cl(1,3)$ generated by $k^{\perp}$, $J = k\,\Cl(k^{\perp})$ squares to zero, the generators $N = k a$ ($a \in k^{\perp}$) of the null rotations $1 + N$ about $k$ are the bivectors of $J$, so they commute, multiply to zero and have $e^{N} = 1 + N$, and $\Cl(k^{\perp})/J \cong \Cl(0,2)$.

## technical
In a Clifford algebra of a degenerate form the null directions orthogonal to everything, the radical, generate an ideal that is the same whether taken from the left, the right or both sides, because a radical vector anticommutes with every vector. In a basis split into a complement and the radical, the ideal is spanned by the blades with at least one radical factor; its $k$-th power by the blades with at least $k$ radical factors, so the $(r+1)$-th power vanishes and the $r$-th does not. Dividing out the ideal leaves the Clifford algebra of the nondegenerate quotient space, and the Clifford algebra of any complement is a subalgebra mapping onto it. The ideal is also the largest nilpotent ideal, identified intrinsically as the radical of the scalar-part form.

## lay
When some directions of a space have zero length and are perpendicular to everything, their algebra picks up a layer of elements that vanish when multiplied by themselves enough times. Removing that layer leaves exactly the ordinary algebra of the remaining, healthy directions, and that layer is the largest part of the algebra that both vanishes on repeated multiplication and is closed under multiplication by the whole algebra.

## one-liner
The radical generates the ideal $J = \Cl\operatorname{rad}V$, spanned by the blades with a radical factor, with $J^{r} \ne 0 = J^{r+1}$; $\Cl(V)/J \cong \Cl(V/\operatorname{rad}V)$, and $J$ is the largest nilpotent ideal.
