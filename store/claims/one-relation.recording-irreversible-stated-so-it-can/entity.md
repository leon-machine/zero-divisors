Everything in this section and the next that speaks of irreversibility rests on this premise.

## technical
A laboratory measurement couples a superposed ideal to recording degrees of freedom, and the records written within the modular period of the boundary modes are never unwritten: recording is irreversible. This is a flagged premise, not a theorem.

Its nearest relative in the series is the theorem that the descent is a one-way conditional expectation; the step from that to irreversible laboratory recording is the emergent-versus-exact gap, which no paper of the series proves. Everything in the measurement and experience analysis that speaks of irreversibility rests on it.

## lay
When an instrument records a measurement result, that record is assumed to be permanent: it cannot be undone. The series proves something similar for a more abstract process but not for real laboratory records, so this is stated openly as an assumption that could be refuted.

## one-liner
Premise: records written by a laboratory measurement within the modular period of the boundary modes are never unwritten.
