(1) $\leftrightarrow$ (2): a line of $\PCS$ is a $2$-plane of $\CS$; two lines are skew exactly when the planes meet in $0$, i.e.\ split $\CS$; volume elements of the planes are fixed up to scale, and $\varepsilon(N_0\wedge N_\infty) \neq 0$ because the four spanning vectors form a basis, so the joint normalisation is available. (2) $\leftrightarrow$ (3): $N_\infty$ and $N_0$ are simple, hence null vectors of $V_\C$ (Theorem \claimref{correspondence.spinor-identification}(2)), with $\ip{\nO}{\nInf} = \varepsilon(N_0\wedge N_\infty) = 1$; conversely a null pair with pairing $1$ gives simple bivectors whose planes are complementary (if the planes met, the wedge would vanish). Orthogonality of the mixed slot: a wedge of the form $N_0 \wedge (s\wedge p)$ with $s \in \Som$, $p \in \Spi$ contains three factors from the $2$-plane $\Spi$, hence vanishes; likewise for $N_\infty$. The mixed slot has dimension $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ and the complement has dimension $6 - 2 = 4$; since $\Lambda^2\CS = \Lambda^2\Som \oplus (\Som\wedge\Spi) \oplus \Lambda^2\Spi$ and the two extreme summands span a nondegenerate (hyperbolic) plane, the middle summand is the complement exactly.
