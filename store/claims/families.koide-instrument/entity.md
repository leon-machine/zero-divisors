## formal
Whatever the mechanism, the relation's observed exactness is a measurement of the frozen vacuum's face sector. By $Q_{v} = 1/(3\cos^{2}\theta)$, $\delta Q/Q = 2\tan\theta\,\delta\theta$, so at $\theta = 45^{\circ}$ the relative deviation $3.3 \times 10^{-6}$ of Proposition \claimref{families.linear-conjecture-refuted-data} puts the angle of the $\sqrt{m}$ vector to the democratic axis at $45^{\circ}$ minus $1.7 \times 10^{-6}$ radians on the central masses, an exact-arithmetic consequence the script checks. The $\pm 0.09$ MeV on $m_{\tau}$ moves the angle by about $3.8 \times 10^{-6}$ radians, so the offset the instrument reads is below its resolution: the data are consistent with exactly $45^{\circ}$. Any mechanism that moves the singlet and doublet norms together leaves $\theta$ fixed and is invisible to the relation; what it measures is the differential effect on the two components. Whatever else it is, the relation is a precision instrument reading the frozen vacuum's face sector.

## technical
Since $Q_{v} = 1/(3\cos^{2}\theta)$ for the angle $\theta$ between the $\sqrt{m}$ vector and the democratic axis, $\delta Q/Q = 2\tan\theta\,\delta\theta$, and at $\theta = 45^{\circ}$ the relative deviation $3.3 \times 10^{-6}$ of the charged-lepton Koide ratio puts the angle at $45^{\circ}$ minus $1.7 \times 10^{-6}$ radians on the central masses. The $\pm 0.09$ MeV uncertainty on $m_{\tau}$ moves the angle by about $3.8 \times 10^{-6}$ radians, so the offset is below the instrument's resolution and the data are consistent with exactly $45^{\circ}$.

A mechanism that moves the singlet and doublet norms together leaves $\theta$ fixed and is invisible to the relation, which measures only their differential effect. The observation reads the relation, whatever its mechanism, as a precision instrument on the frozen vacuum's face sector.

## lay
Whatever eventually explains the Koide relation, its remarkable precision already says something. Read geometrically, it states that an angle in the space of family masses is forty-five degrees to within about two millionths of a radian, which is finer than current measurements of the tau mass can resolve. The paper treats the relation as a sensitive instrument for probing the vacuum's family structure, not only as a puzzle.
