## formal
The flavour problem, in this framework and at the displaced-Gaussian state class, is not an undiscovered operator: it is the numbers layer, three times over. The split ($m_{\mu}/m_{e} \approx 207$), the angle ($45^{\circ}$, present only through the depth), and the depth ($x^{*} \approx 769$) are one open problem in three forms, and the Gaussian vacuum treats all three as inputs. The registered open problem is the non-Gaussian completion: structure beyond the class (a non-Gaussian frozen vacuum with higher cumulants, or a larger algebra) that produces the $207$ from caused structure, constrained by the relabelling-signature refutation condition of Proposition \claimref{families.classification-displaced-gaussian-class-solved-level}, and required to preserve the skeleton of Theorem \claimref{families.skeleton-rank-one-plus-ordering-shift} and to introduce no second free number. Until it is solved, the Koide relation stands within the framework as what the chain of refutations has made it: a $10^{-5}$-precision measurement of a frozen vacuum whose structure is theorems and whose numbers remain to be derived.

## technical
At the displaced-Gaussian state class the flavour problem is the numbers layer three times over: the split $m_{\mu}/m_{e} \approx 207$, the angle $45^{\circ}$ (present only through the depth) and the depth $x^{*} \approx 769$ are one open problem in three forms, all of them inputs to the Gaussian vacuum. The registered open problem is a non-Gaussian completion, a frozen vacuum with higher cumulants or a larger algebra, that produces the $207$ from caused structure.

The completion is constrained by the relabelling-signature refutation condition of the classification, must preserve the rank-one-plus-shift skeleton, and must introduce no second free number. Until it is found the Koide relation stands in the framework as a $10^{-5}$-precision measurement of a frozen vacuum whose structure is proved and whose numbers remain to be derived.

## lay
Three seemingly separate puzzles, the electron-muon mass ratio, the Koide angle and the depth parameter, turn out to be one unanswered question in this framework. Its simplest vacuum states take all three as inputs instead of predicting them. The paper registers the missing ingredient as its main open problem and sets conditions that any solution must satisfy.
