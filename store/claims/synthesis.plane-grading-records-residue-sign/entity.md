## formal
Let $T_{k} = \Cl(p{+}k, q{+}k)$ be the floors of the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$, $n = p + q \ge 1$, $r = p - q$, with ground generators $x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}$ and planes $(e_{j}, f_{j})$, $e_{j}^{2} = 1$, $f_{j}^{2} = -1$, $j \ge 1$; let $L_{p,q} = \varinjlim T_{k}$ (the real algebraic limit), $B_{\infty} \subset L_{p,q}$ the subalgebra generated by the plane generators, $P_{j} \subset B_{\infty}$ the subalgebra generated by $e_{j}, f_{j}$, and $\alpha_{B}$ the grading of $B_{\infty}$ alone: the automorphism of $B_{\infty}$ that negates every plane generator. Isomorphisms are of real algebras unless stated.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Twisted commutant.)} The set $Z^{1} = \{x \in L_{p,q} : xb = \alpha_{B}(b)x \text{ for all } b \in B_{\infty}\}$ is exactly the odd ground part $\Cl^{1}(p,q) \subset T_{0}$. With the relative commutant $Z^{0} = \Cl^{0}(p,q)$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.plane-commutant-even-ground}, $Z^{0} \oplus Z^{1} = \Cl(p,q)$, a subalgebra graded by this decomposition. So the triple $(L_{p,q}, B_{\infty}, \alpha_{B})$ determines the ground algebra $\Cl(p,q)$ with its grading.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Classification with the plane grading.)} For $n, n' \ge 1$ there is an isomorphism $\Psi \colon L_{p,q} \to L_{p',q'}$ with $\Psi(B_{\infty}) = B_{\infty}$ and $\Psi \circ \alpha_{B} = \alpha'_{B} \circ \Psi$ on $B_{\infty}$ if and only if $n' = n$ and $r' \equiv r \pmod 8$. In particular for $r \not\equiv 0, 4 \pmod 8$ no isomorphism of pairs from the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$ to the tower over $\Cl(q,p)$ respects the planes' grading; the reversal of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.tower-top-pair-forgets-reversal}(i) does not, since it sends the odd generator $e_{2}$ to the even element $e_{1}f_{1}$.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Framing.)} For $j \ne k$ the intersection of $P_{j}$ with the commutant of $P_{k}$ is $\mathrm{span}\{1, e_{j}f_{j}\}$, and $\alpha_{B}$ restricted to $P_{j}$ is $\operatorname{Ad}(e_{j}f_{j})$. Hence an isomorphism of pairs that carries each plane $P_{j}$ onto a plane $P'_{\pi(j)}$, with $\pi$ injective, respects the planes' grading, and the framed tower tops obey the classification of (ii): the framing by the individual planes records the sign of $r$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(Clocks with no zero mode.)} On the complexification let $K_{j} = \tfrac12 e_{j}f_{j}$ and, for real weights $\omega_{j}$, let $\tau_{t} = \operatorname{Ad}\bigl(e^{it\sum_{j}\omega_{j}K_{j}}\bigr)$, defined floor by floor (the phase flow of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.phase-flow-is-time-rotor-flow-kms-continuation}(iii); every such clock fixes the ground algebra). Let $\tau'$ be such a clock on the planes of the tower over $\Cl(p',q')$ with \emph{every} weight $\omega'_{j} \ne 0$, for instance the common-rate boost $\kappa K_{\mathrm{tot}}$ or the weights $\omega'_{k} = \kappa k$. If an isomorphism $\Psi \colon L_{p,q} \to L_{p',q'}$ with $\Psi(B_{\infty}) = B_{\infty}$ maps $\Cl(p,q)$ into the fixed points of $\tau'$, which holds in particular when the complexification of $\Psi$ intertwines a clock of the source with $\tau'$, then $\Psi \circ \alpha_{B} \circ \Psi^{-1} = \operatorname{Ad}(c) \circ \alpha'_{B}$ on $B_{\infty}$ for a $\tau'$-invariant invertible $c \in B_{\infty}$ with $c\,\alpha'_{B}(c)$ a positive real number, and $\Psi$ induces a grading-preserving isomorphism $\Cl(p,q) \cong \Cl(p',q')$. Hence $n' = n$ and $r' \equiv r \pmod 8$: for $r \not\equiv 0, 4 \pmod 8$ no isomorphism of pairs from the tower over $\Cl(p,q)$ to the tower over $\Cl(q,p)$ is compatible with a clock that has no zero mode. The hypothesis is sharp: if $\omega'_{1} = 0$, the reversal $\Phi$ of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.tower-top-pair-forgets-reversal}(i) maps $\Cl(p,q)$ into the fixed points of $\tau'$. When every weight is an odd multiple of one frequency $\omega_{0}$ with $\gcd$ of the multiples $1$, $\tau$ at half its least period $2\pi/\omega_{0}$ is $\alpha_{B}$ itself.
\item[(v)] \emph{(Over $\C$ none of this records the sign.)} The $\C$-linear assignment $x_{i} \mapsto i\,y_{i}$, every plane generator fixed, is an isomorphism of the complexified limits over $\Cl(p,q)$ and $\Cl(q,p)$ that fixes $B_{\infty, \C}$ pointwise, so it respects the plane grading, every plane and every clock of (iv). The sign of $r$ is recorded by these data only together with the real structure.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
The top of the tower over a signature and over its reversal are isomorphic as algebras with the plane subalgebra marked, through a re-pairing of the planes that mixes even and odd plane elements. This result asks which further datum stops that. The grading of the plane algebra by itself is enough: the elements that anticommute with every plane generator are exactly the odd elements of the ground algebra, and together with the commuting ones, the even ground elements, they rebuild the whole ground algebra $\Cl(p,q)$ with its grading. Since $\Cl(p,q)$ and $\Cl(q,p)$ are different algebras unless $r \equiv 0, 4 \pmod 8$, an isomorphism that respects the planes' grading cannot carry a residue to its reversal; the four-generator shift shows it can still carry $r$ to $r + 8$. Keeping the planes individually supplies the planes' grading, since each plane's even part is what commutes with another plane, so any isomorphism that sends planes to planes respects it. A clock on the planes does almost as much. If every plane turns at a nonzero rate, the clock has a single lowest state on each floor, and the grading acts on that state by a sign; an isomorphism that sends the ground into the clock's fixed points must then twist the planes' grading by an inner automorphism whose square class is positive, which forces the two ground algebras to be isomorphic as graded algebras. The store's reversal escapes only by moving the ground onto a plane generator, which a clock with no zero mode does not leave fixed. When the rates are odd multiples of one frequency the clock at half its period is the grading itself. Over the complex numbers none of this works, because multiplying ground generators by $i$ reverses their squares while fixing every plane. This covers the I-state's weights $\omega_{k} = \kappa k$ as well as a common rate. A state version (a product Gibbs state of such a clock, whose modular flow is the clock run at $-\beta t$ on each floor) follows if an isomorphism carrying one state to the other intertwines their modular flows on the limit, which standard modular theory gives but which is not proved here.

## lay
A tower of algebras built over a signature and the tower built over its mirror image have the same top, even when one keeps track of which part of the top came from the added blocks. The earlier result showed this with an explicit relabelling. Here the question is what extra bookkeeping breaks the tie. It is enough to remember, for the added blocks alone, which of their elements count as odd, the property that tells fermions from bosons. That information can be kept by remembering each added block separately, or by running a clock on the blocks in which every block turns at some nonzero rate: the relabelling that swaps a tower for its mirror has to borrow one of the added blocks, and a clock that moves every block leaves none to borrow. With either (for the clock, provided the relabelling sends the starting algebra to elements the clock leaves still), the top remembers whether the tower started from a signature or from its mirror, except for the two residues that are their own mirrors. Over the complex numbers even this is lost, so the distinction lives in the real structure.

## one-liner
At the tower top the grading of the plane algebra alone records the ground algebra $\Cl(p,q)$, so isomorphisms that respect it, keep the individual planes, or fix the ground under a phase clock with no zero mode separate $r$ from $-r$ for $r \not\equiv 0, 4 \pmod 8$ over $\R$, though not over $\C$.
