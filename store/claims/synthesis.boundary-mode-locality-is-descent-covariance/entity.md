## formal
Let $v_{1}, \dots, v_{n}$ satisfy the CAR (a finite window of boundary modes), $\sigma_{k} = v_{k}^{*}v_{k} - v_{k}v_{k}^{*} = 2N_{k} - 1$ (Theorem \claimref{singularity.modular-structure-completed-pair}), $\mathcal{D}$ the algebra generated by the $\sigma_{k}$, and $E_{k}(x) = N_{k}xN_{k} + (1 - N_{k})x(1 - N_{k})$ the descent of mode $k$ (Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases}). Put $\mathcal{E} = E_{1}\circ\cdots\circ E_{n}$, the descent of the window. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\mathcal{E}(x) = \sum_{\nu}P_{\nu}xP_{\nu}$, the sum over the $2^{n}$ joint-number projectors $P_{\nu} = \prod_{k}(N_{k}\ \text{or}\ 1 - N_{k})$; it is an idempotent conditional expectation onto $\mathcal{D}$, which is the joint-number-diagonal algebra, of dimension $2^{n}$;
\item[(ii)] for every element $H$ of the window algebra, $\mathcal{E}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{H} = \operatorname{ad}_{H}\circ\mathcal{E}$ if and only if $H \in \mathcal{D}$; hence a one-parameter group $\alpha_{t} = \operatorname{Ad}(e^{JtH})$, written with the boundary modes' complex unit $J = e_{2}e_{3}$, which commutes with every mode (\claimref{synthesis.transverse-bivector-is-the-complex-unit-of-the-radial-atom} for one mode; the further modes lie in planes orthogonal to spacetime), commutes with $\mathcal{E}$ for all $t$ if and only if $H \in \mathcal{D}$;
\item[(iii)] consequently the condition of Premise \claimref{singularity.l-boundary-mode-locality} on a window, that the internal dynamics be generated by elements of the algebra of the $\sigma_{k}$, is equivalent to
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(T)] the internal dynamics of the boundary modes commutes with the descent: $\alpha_{t}\circ\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}\circ\alpha_{t}$ for all $t$;
\end{enumerate}
hopping $v_{j}^{*}v_{k} + v_{k}^{*}v_{j}$, pairing $v_{j}^{*}v_{k}^{*} + v_{k}v_{j}$ and every generator with a component outside $\mathcal{D}$ fail (T), each witnessed by an occupation projector $N_{j}$ with $\mathcal{E}(\operatorname{ad}_{H}N_{j}) = 0 \neq \operatorname{ad}_{H}N_{j}$, while diagonal products such as $\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}$ satisfy it;
\item[(iv)] for channels the same covariance admits greybody filtering: a beam splitter of transmission $c^{2}$ between a mode and a vacuum in-mode gives on the transmitted mode the channel $\Phi\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a & b\\ \bar b & d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a + s^{2}d & cb\\ c\bar b & c^{2}d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $c^{2} + s^{2} = 1$, which satisfies $\Phi\circ E = E\circ\Phi$ and carries the occupation $N \mapsto c^{2}N$.
\end{enumerate}
So on every finite window, (L$_{E}$) is the statement that dynamics and descent commute for all $t$, with one clause for hopping, pairing and unsupplied generators alike; commuting at a single time is weaker, since permutations of the joint-number basis commute with $\mathcal{E}$.

## technical
The series' descent on a window of boundary modes is the pinching onto the joint-number-diagonal algebra: it keeps occupation data and erases every coherence. A generator commutes with this pinching exactly when it is itself number-diagonal: if it commutes, it must commute with every joint-number projector, since the pinching of a commutator with such a projector is zero; and a number-diagonal generator is a combination of the projectors, which the pinching fixes. On three modes the script finds the space of covariant generators to be exactly the eight-dimensional diagonal algebra inside the sixty-four-dimensional window algebra. Covariance at a single time is weaker: a unitary that permutes the joint-number basis (times phases) commutes with the pinching, and the hop flow reaches such a permutation, the full swap, at one time. So (T) is a statement about the continuous flow, for all $t$, and the equivalence holds on finite windows only; continuum windows, and the differential dynamics (L$_{E}$) mentions there, are not covered.

The locality premise (L$_{E}$) says precisely that the boundary-mode dynamics is generated by number-diagonal elements. So (L$_{E}$) is equivalent to the requirement that the dynamics commute with the descent. Hopping and pairing, which earlier work had to exclude by two different arguments (a theorem within the supplied class for hopping, the premise itself for pairing), now fail one condition in the same way.

For channels the same covariance allows partial transmission: a beam splitter against a vacuum in-channel commutes with the descent and carries occupation $N \mapsto \Gamma N$. That is the occupancy content of the interface identification (I), which suggests reading (L$_{E}$) and (I) as one covariance condition on the inside and the outside of the horizon, the label correspondence of (I) remaining an input.

## lay
The series assumes that a black hole's boundary quantum bits evolve without moving anything from one bit to another, a locality assumption it could not derive. It also has a standard operation, the descent, that reads off how full each bit is and forgets everything else. This result shows that, for a finite set of bits, the locality assumption says exactly that the evolution and this reading-off can be done in either order with the same result, at every moment of the evolution. This is a restatement of the assumption in terms of an operation the series already has, not a derivation of it. Two exclusions the series stated separately become one, and the same condition, applied to the escape of radiation from the black hole, allows the familiar partial transmission while forbidding mixing between channels.

## one-liner
On every finite window, the locality premise (L$_{E}$) is equivalent to the boundary-mode dynamics commuting for all times with the descent, the joint-number pinching, which excludes hopping, pairing and unsupplied generators by one computation and admits greybody filtering for channels.
