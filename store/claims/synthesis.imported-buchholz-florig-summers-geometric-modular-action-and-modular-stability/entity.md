## formal
Let $\omega$ be a state on a C$^{*}$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ with GNS representation $(\pi, \mathcal{H}, \Omega)$, let $\mathcal{W}$ be the family of wedges of Minkowski space, and let $W \mapsto \mathcal{R}(W)$ assign to each wedge a von Neumann algebra on $\mathcal{H}$ generated by observables of $\mathcal{A}$, with $\Omega$ cyclic and separating for each $\mathcal{R}(W)$ and modular objects $\Delta_{W}$, $J_{W}$. Numbers are read in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, the unit of $\mathcal{H}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (The Condition of Geometric Modular Action; the source's items (a) to (d), numbered (1) to (4) here.) (1) $W \mapsto \mathcal{R}(W)$ is an order-preserving bijection; (2) if $W_{1}\cap W_{2} \ne \emptyset$, then $\Omega$ is cyclic and separating for $\mathcal{R}(W_{1})\cap\mathcal{R}(W_{2})$, and conversely, if $\Omega$ is cyclic and separating for $\mathcal{R}(W_{1})\cap\mathcal{R}(W_{2})$, then the closures of $W_{1}$ and $W_{2}$ meet; (3) for each $W$, $\mathrm{Ad}\,J_{W}$ leaves the set $\{\mathcal{R}(W')\}_{W' \in \mathcal{W}}$ invariant; (4) the group generated by the $\mathrm{Ad}\,J_{W}$ acts transitively on that set. It is a condition on the wedge algebras as sets, not on how $J_{W}$ acts on individual observables.
\item[(b)] (The Modular Stability Condition.) $\Delta_{W}^{I_{4}t}$ lies in the group generated by the modular involutions $J_{W'}$, $W' \in \mathcal{W}$, for all real $t$ and every $W$.
\item[(c)] (Translations; Proposition 3.5.) Under (a), there is in the GNS representation a continuous unitary representation $U$ of the translations $\R^{4}$ which leaves $\Omega$ invariant and acts covariantly on the wedge algebras, $U(\xi)\mathcal{R}(W)U(\xi)^{-1} = \mathcal{R}(W + \xi)$.
\item[(d)] (Spectrum up to sign; Proposition 3.6.) Under (a) and (b), the spectrum of $U$ lies in the closed forward cone or in its negative.
\item[(e)] (No ground state from (a) alone.) The condition of geometric modular action does not imply that $\Omega$ is a ground state.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Provenance:} \cite{BFS1999}, building on \cite{BS1993}.

## technical
Buchholz and Summers proposed to characterise the vacuum by the modular objects of the wedge algebras alone: the modular conjugation of each wedge's algebra should map the family of wedge algebras onto itself and act transitively on it, as the wedge reflections act on the wedges. Buchholz, Florig and Summers derived from this condition, with no continuity assumed, a continuous unitary representation of the spacetime translations under which the vector is invariant and the wedge algebras are covariant; adding modular stability, that the modular groups are generated by the modular conjugations, they showed the energy-momentum spectrum lies in the closed forward cone or in its negative. They note that the condition of geometric modular action by itself does not make the vector a ground state. The condition is stated on the algebras as sets: it says where the modular conjugation sends each algebra, not how it acts on individual observables.

## lay
In quantum field theory each wedge-shaped region of spacetime carries a mathematical reflection defined by the vacuum. Buchholz, Florig and Summers showed that if these reflections permute the regions as ordinary mirror reflections do, the theory acquires a well-behaved description of shifts in space and time, and with one further condition its energy is either everywhere nonnegative or everywhere nonpositive; the reflections alone do not decide that the state is the lowest-energy one. The result is taken from the literature.

## one-liner
If the wedge algebras' modular conjugations permute the wedge algebras transitively as the wedge reflections do (geometric modular action), a continuous translation representation fixing the vector follows, and with modular stability its spectrum lies in the closed forward cone or its negative; geometric modular action alone does not give a ground state (Buchholz, Florig and Summers 1999).
