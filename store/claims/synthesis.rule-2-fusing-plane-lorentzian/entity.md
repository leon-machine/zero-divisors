## formal
Let $A = \Cl(1,4)$ be generated by $e_{0}$ with $Q(e_{0}) = 1$ and $e_{1}, \ldots, e_{4}$ with $Q(e_{i}) = -1$, put $\omega = e_{0}e_{1}e_{2}e_{3}e_{4}$ (central in $A$, $\omega^{2} = 1$) and $\varepsilon_{\pm} = \tfrac12(1 \pm \omega)$, and let $B_{\gamma}$ be the Rule 2 repair of Proposition \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iv), generated by $A$ and one $\nu$ with $\nu^{2} = \gamma \in \{\pm 1\}$, so $B_{+} = \Cl(2,4)$ and $B_{-} = \Cl(1,5)$. Let $P_{\gamma} \subset B_{\gamma}$ be the set of elements anticommuting with each of $e_{0}, \ldots, e_{4}$, the candidate fused directions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(The fusing plane.)} $P_{\gamma} = \mathrm{span}\{\nu, \Omega\}$ with $\Omega = \omega\nu$ the volume element of $B_{\gamma}$. For $x \in P_{\gamma}$, $x^{2}$ is real, and $x \mapsto x^{2}$ is a quadratic form of signature $(1,1)$ for either $\gamma$: $\nu^{2} = \gamma$, $\Omega^{2} = -\gamma$, $\nu\Omega = -\Omega\nu$. Its two null lines are spanned by $\nu\varepsilon_{+} = \varepsilon_{-}\nu$ and $\nu\varepsilon_{-} = \varepsilon_{+}\nu$, the off-diagonal matrix units of Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten}: nonzero elements of square zero.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Both repairs in one algebra.)} Every $x \in P_{\gamma}$ with $x^{2} = \pm 1$ completes $e_{0}, \ldots, e_{4}$ to a Clifford generating set of all of $B_{\gamma}$, of signature $(2,4)$ if $x^{2} = 1$ and $(1,5)$ if $x^{2} = -1$; its volume element $\omega x$ lies in $P_{\gamma}$, is orthogonal to $x$, and has square $-x^{2}$. In particular $e_{i} \mapsto e_{i}$, $\nu_{+} \mapsto \nu_{-}\omega$ extends to an isomorphism $\Cl(2,4) \to \Cl(1,5)$ fixing $A$ pointwise; it sends the fused vector $\nu_{+}$ to the volume element of $\Cl(1,5)$ up to sign and the volume element of $\Cl(2,4)$ to $-\nu_{-}$.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(Exactly two completions.)} An automorphism of $B_{\gamma}$ fixing $A$ pointwise maps $P_{\gamma}$ to itself and preserves $x^{2}$, so it never exchanges the two signs. Conjugation by $u = a\varepsilon_{+} + b\varepsilon_{-}$, $ab \neq 0$, fixes $A$ pointwise and scales the null lines by $b/a$ and $a/b$; these act transitively on each level set $\{x \in P_{\gamma} : x^{2} = c\}$, $c \neq 0$. Up to automorphisms fixing $A$ there are therefore exactly two completions of $A$'s generators by one unit vector of the fused algebra, one for each sign of its square.
\item[(iv)] \emph{(What decides the sign.)} Hence a principle stated on the pair $(B, A)$ and invariant under its automorphisms decides the Rule 2 sign only through the causal type of the fused vector in the Lorentzian plane $P$, that is, through which axis of $P$ is taken as the new vector and which is then the volume element. By (ii) the fused vector squares to $+1$ exactly when the completion's volume element squares to $-1$; the centre of the completion's even part being $\mathrm{span}\{1, \omega x\}$ (Proposition \claimref{synthesis.plane-commutant-even-ground}(ii)), this holds exactly when that centre is $\C$ with the volume element as its unit, as for $\Cl(2,4)$ in Proposition \claimref{odd-extensions.twistor-algebra}(i). The choice $\Cl(2,4)$ is the choice that puts the positive axis of $P$ in the vector slot and leaves the negative axis as the complex unit of the even algebra; $\Cl(1,5)$ is the same algebra with the two axes exchanged.
\end{enumerate}

## technical
Rule 2 repairs the split extension $\Cl(1,4)$ by one more generator $\nu$ whose square, $+1$ (giving $\Cl(2,4)$) or $-1$ (giving $\Cl(1,5)$), the fused algebra forgets. This result finds where the bit sits. Inside the fused algebra, the elements that anticommute with all five generators of the extension form a plane spanned by $\nu$ and the volume element $\Omega$; the square is a quadratic form on it, and it is Lorentzian for either choice, because $\nu$ and $\Omega$ always square to opposite signs. The plane's two null lines are the Rule 2 matrix units $\nu\varepsilon_{\pm}$, the zero divisors that fuse the two ideals.

Any unit vector of that plane completes the extension's generators to a generating set of the whole algebra, of signature $(2,4)$ or $(1,5)$ by the sign of its square, and the volume element of that completion is the orthogonal axis, with the opposite square. So one algebra contains both repairs, and the isomorphism between them that fixes the extension exchanges the new vector with the volume element. The automorphisms fixing the extension act on the plane as boosts and their negatives, transitively on each sign and never across. A principle that sees only the fused algebra and the extension cannot choose; one that chooses must say which element is a vector. The spectrum condition on the completion's spin group, the result filed alongside, synthesis.positive-energy-selects-rule-2-sign, is a selector of that kind: it is a condition on the group built from the chosen vector slot, not on the abstract algebra. The series' choice $\Cl(2,4)$ is equivalent to the volume element, the other axis, squaring to $-1$, which makes it the complex unit of the even algebra; this is the residue modulo four already in the store, restated, not a dynamical derivation.

## lay
When the series' fourth step glues two halves of an algebra back together, it adds one new direction whose type, time-like or space-like, the resulting algebra cannot remember. This result shows where that missing bit lives. Inside the glued algebra, the directions that could do the gluing form a flat two-dimensional plane that has the geometry of a spacetime with one time and one space axis, whichever choice was made. Its light-like lines are the special elements that do the gluing. Picking the new direction time-like gives the series' conformal algebra; picking it space-like gives the alternative. In both cases the axis not picked becomes the algebra's built-in imaginary unit or its opposite. So nothing inside the glued algebra can make the choice; it has to come from outside.

## one-liner
Relative to the split extension $\Cl(1,4)$, the fused directions of either Rule 2 repair form a plane $\mathrm{span}\{\nu, \Omega\}$ on which the square is a Lorentzian form with the Rule 2 matrix units as null lines; the two repairs are its two causal types of unit vector, one algebra with vector and volume element exchanged, so no principle on the fused algebra and the extension alone fixes $\nu^{2}$.
