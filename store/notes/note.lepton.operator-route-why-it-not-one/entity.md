## formal
The ledger's states contain no $(2,1)$, but its operators do: $\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda^{4}, \Lambda^{1}) = \Lambda^{1} \otimes (\Lambda^{4})^{*} \supseteq (2,3) \otimes (3,\bar{3})$, and $2 \otimes 3 = 2 \oplus 4$, $3 \otimes \bar{3} = 1 \oplus 8$ \cite[\S 11.2, \S 13.2]{FultonHarris1991}, so a $(2,1)$ of weight $1 - 4 = -3$ sits among the operators that annihilate four cells and create one; likewise in $\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda^{5}, \Lambda^{2})$. One might hope to read T12's premise that elementary fermions are generators, not states, as admitting such an operator as the lepton. The note has no argument that closes this route, and does not claim one: what it observes is that the operator is a composite of five cell operators, that $\operatorname{End}(\Lambda^{\bullet})$, of dimension $64^{2}$, contains a great many representations of the group, and that the note knows no principle that would select this one among them and call it elementary. The route inside the ledger is closed at the level of states by Fact \claimref{lepton.imported-tensor-ledger-contains-no-lepton}; at the level of operators it is not closed but unresolved, for want of a selection principle, and the script records the representation-theoretic fact that the $(2,1)_{-1/2}$ is there.
