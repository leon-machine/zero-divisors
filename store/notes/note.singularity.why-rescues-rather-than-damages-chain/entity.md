## formal
Read carelessly, Theorem \claimref{singularity.exact-equilibrium-over-determines-boundary-modes} is an obstruction: the state that exact thermality requires is the state with no information in it. Read carefully, it is the reason the order of argument in this subsection matters. Had per-mode thermality been derived from the exact equilibrium condition, as hypothesis (ii) suggested, then thermality and correlation would have been in direct competition and the chain could not have had both. Theorem \claimref{singularity.ascended-marginals-are-diagonal} shows that per-mode thermality does not rest on equilibrium at all: it rests on parity, which survives an arbitrary non-stationary history. The state class the chain actually needs is
\[
\bigl\{\ \text{even states, per-mode populations at } \beta = 2\pi/\kappa, \ \text{correlations free}\ \bigr\},
\]
in which every single quantum is exactly thermal and the joint state of boundary modes and radiation together is free to carry everything the evaporation writes. Where it is carried is sharpened by the rate model and should be recorded here: under (I) and (L$_E$) the window marginal itself is fixed to be the product member at every stage (Theorem \claimref{singularity.eviction-window-thermal-product-radiation-entropy}(1)), its internal correlation and descent deficit identically zero, and the writing lies across the window/radiation cut and among the quanta of the radiation, not inside the boundary-mode marginal. That this class is where (P) and (R) put the state, and that the dynamics of the boundary modes keeps it there, is Corollary \claimref{singularity.chains-state-class-stability} below; the finite-mode model of Section \ref{sec:model} lies in it, and Lemma \claimref{singularity.parity-protection} is the model's proof that it does. The theorem also settles a question the chain could otherwise have been asked indefinitely: no sharpening of Step 4d will ever supply Step 6b's correlations, because no equilibrium principle can.
