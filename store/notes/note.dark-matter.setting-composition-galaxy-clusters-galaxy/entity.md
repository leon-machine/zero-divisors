## formal
The rule fixes how much matter each tower holds, and with it how much of the dark matter collides: the mirror's $18.6$ percent, a plasma once ionised inside a halo (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what}), and the three-dimensional towers' $49.7$ percent, at composite strength within their sheets. Counted as a bound on what a galaxy cluster loses in a merger, the two add, to $68$ percent; Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(v) counts the three-dimensional towers alone. The companion note T14b \cite{ButlerT14b} tests these amounts where dark matter's collisions show, with a multi-species simulation code, a Bullet-like merger of two galaxy clusters, the assembly of a galaxy cluster from eight progenitors and a semi-analytic model of the Milky Way, deposited with its code, run outputs and films. It takes the amounts as given and runs them in two readings: as this paper writes them, and with the odd towers' matter collisionless, so that only the mirror collides, and it runs the exchanged assignment of Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.equivalent-forms-assignment-not} in the same way, since the two assignments differ in composition and not in the total. Its results are imported here.
