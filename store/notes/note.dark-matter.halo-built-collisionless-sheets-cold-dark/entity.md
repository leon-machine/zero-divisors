## formal
Beyond linear order the question is what the sheets do to a halo, and the answer is an argument, not a simulation. A particle of a three-dimensional tower has two velocities: its motion within its sheet, the tower's own dynamics, and the motion of the sheet, which by Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.reduced-response-gravity-needs-supporting-stress} follows the normal component of gravity pointwise. The one constraint the sheets impose is that at a point of a sheet the normal velocity is single-valued, the sheet being a surface; in-plane velocities are whatever the tower's dynamics allows, and sheets may cross, nothing forbidding it and the sourcing rule already stacking them. Cold dark matter is the same object in all three directions: a single-valued velocity field on a three-dimensional Lagrangian sheet in phase space, multi-streaming only where Lagrangian patches cross. So the sheet family's phase space contains cold dark matter's, one direction treated exactly as cold dark matter treats all three and two directions treated more permissively; with cold initial conditions, which the seeding floor gives (Appendix \ref{app:seeding}), the in-plane motion is itself a cold single-valued flow and the two phase spaces coincide. The Lagrangian map of the sheet matter is then cold dark matter's at every order in collisionless dynamics, not only at the linear order Figure \ref{fig:boltzmann} checks, and a simulation of sheet-labelled particles against plain ones would integrate the same equations. Halo shapes, velocity structure and the pancake, filament and halo sequence are cold dark matter's. What can differ is what the sheets add. First, in-sheet self-interaction: a quantum collides only with quanta of its own sheet, at composite strength on the reading of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what}, so the collision rate is set by the surface density within a sheet and the galaxy-cluster bounds on $\sigma/m$ must be re-expressed for a cross-section per sheet before the fraction test of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(v) is read against them. The companion note T14b does this: per unit of the mass gravity sees, the cross-section is $10^{8}$ to $10^{13}\ \mathrm{cm^{2}\,g^{-1}}$, so at composite strength the sheet matter is a fluid within its sheets, not collisionless, and in simulated galaxy clusters it forms a cored component rounder than cold dark matter (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-composition-simulated}). The conclusion of this remark therefore holds only if the three-dimensional towers' matter is collisionless. Second, on the deposit branch, an in-plane thermal velocity $\sigma_{v}$: free streaming then damps small scales within the plane only, and averaged over orientations the damping is $\langle\exp(-k^{2}\sigma_{v}^{2}\tau^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)\rangle$, milder than isotropic warm dark matter at the same dispersion by the missing normal direction; at the seeding floor $\sigma_{v}$ is of order $10^{-4}$ of our matter's thermal velocity and the cutoff lies far below any observed scale. Structural level: the argument rests on cold initial conditions, on the sheets carrying no stress and on the sheet matter being collisionless.
