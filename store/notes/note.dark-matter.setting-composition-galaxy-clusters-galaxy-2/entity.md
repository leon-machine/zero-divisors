## formal
What follows for this paper is that the amounts survive. With the odd towers' matter collisionless the composition passes the Bullet Cluster, under either assignment, and is not excluded by the shapes of galaxy clusters. Read at composite strength, as Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what} reads it, the composition fails both, and it fails them because of that self-interaction, not because of the amounts. That reading is a premise of T14b, not derived here or there: it is a statement about the three-dimensional towers' couplings and the strength with which their lightest stable state interacts, which the series does not fix (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.no-coupling-rule-odd-towers-follows}). The mirror's exposure is the same in both readings and is refutation item 6's: (iv) turns the dark-disk test into two measurements not yet made. The simulations are idealised, with one merger geometry, $80{,}000$ particles per merger and two seeds per galaxy cluster, and they compare compositions; their absolute numbers are not predictions.

\emph{The density the rule assigns excludes composite strength as well.} On a sheet the number per unit area and the expansion rate both fall as $a^{-2}$ in radiation domination, so for two-dimensional s-wave annihilation, $\sigma_{1}v = 2P/m$ with $P$ the absorption probability per encounter, the ratio of the annihilation rate to the expansion rate does not fall, and the depletion runs on logarithmically to the present. A symmetric relic annihilating at full strength, $P = 1$, into lighter states of its own tower, which a confining tower has (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what}, the Goldstone clause), is left at about $3 \times 10^{-14}$ of the density R2 assigns; a relic whose number is fixed at the descent, interacting too weakly to annihilate, keeps it. This is an order-of-magnitude estimate at the mean density on R1 and R2, script-checked in floating point (Section \ref{sec:verification}, Part N). It does not say why the odd towers' matter is free; it says that the density the rule assigns, like the galaxy-cluster record, needs it to be nearly so.
