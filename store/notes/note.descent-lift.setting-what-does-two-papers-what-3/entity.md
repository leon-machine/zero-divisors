## formal
\textbf{What this settles.} Run on the pairing creator, the transport algorithm returns type P at cost zero and hands back the pair annihilation, an element of the singularity paper's vacuum stabiliser (Fact \claimref{descent-lift.imported-descent-modes-univalence-supplied-class}); read through the type-N refinement instead, it returns the obstruction of the adjoined null vectors, which no equivariant element-level repair meets. Under Step 2 it adjoins nothing; under the refinement Step 3 adjoins a plane whose repair acts on points and returns no element from $n$; on neither reading is a rotation produced. The ascent itself contains the pairing and the hop on equal terms, as rotors of its own Spin group built from its own ladder elements, and it has a symmetry exchanging them (iv); that symmetry exchanges the parity sectors too, so it is a symmetry of the algebra and not an operation the univalence rule admits. So the transport principle neither produces nor excludes the pairing generator: what distinguishes a hop from a pairing is the vacuum, which of $f, f^{*}$ annihilates it, together with the parity grading the vacuum fixes, and the singularity paper's exclusion of pairing terms is a premise about that choice, not a consequence of transport. Clause (vi) says where the pairing generator lives: on the even sector of the pair, the creator and its pseudo-inverse are one effective ladder pair, Anderson's pseudospin \cite{Anderson1958}, and the pairing rotation is the rotation of that plane. The registered claim, that the principle produces the generator, is dead at Step 3 as the registration predicted; the kill conditions K1 to K3 of that registration triggered under Step 2, K4 held, every prediction P1 to P6 held, and clause (vii) is outside the registration.
