## formal
The transport principle sorts the zero divisors of a real Clifford algebra into three types and repairs each by a minimal ascent \cite{ButlerT1}. Type C is the deepest: not a direction without a partner but a splitting of the algebra itself into two ideals, along the central idempotents $\varepsilon_\pm = \tfrac12(1 \pm \omega)$ built from the volume element, which no inner automorphism can exchange. Its repair, Rule 2, adjoins one generator, fuses the ideals into a simple algebra, and renders inner the very symmetry whose absence defined the obstruction. The capstone \cite{ButlerT17} records that every other direction of the programme has an owner among the series' papers, and that this one does not: no paper of the series exhibits a physical system whose natural algebra is split, with Kaluza-Klein extensions the named candidates. This note takes the row.

The claim is modest and the standard is the series'. What is proved here is algebra: where the obstruction sits among the one-dimensional extensions of an even-dimensional algebra, what its ideals are, and what its repair is. What is physical is imported as Facts with provenance: that odd-dimensional gamma matrices come in two inequivalent representations, that a single planar fermion breaks parity through an induced Chern-Simons term while a doubled one does not, that graphene's low-energy theory is that doubled fermion, that a five-dimensional fermion shifts a Chern-Simons level by half the sign of its mass. The note's contribution is the identification: those two representations are the two ideals, the parity that relates them is the outer exchange, the doubling is the fusion, and the fused algebra of the physical lineage is the conformal algebra the series has used since its root paper. Section \ref{sec:boundary} says exactly what has been exercised and what has not.

Notation follows the root paper: $\Cl(p,q)$ has $p$ generators of square $+1$ and $q$ of square $-1$, $n = p + q$, $d = p - q \bmod 8$ is the clock residue, $\omega = e_1 \cdots e_n$ is the volume element, $\Cl^0$ is the even subalgebra, and $\hat{a}$ is the grade involution. The series labels this world's algebra $\Cl(1,3)$ (time of square $+1$), residue $6$; the dark-matter paper explains why the labelling is a convention and what changes hands under its reversal \cite{ButlerT14}, and Corollary \claimref{rule2.odd-lorentzian-dimension-exactly-one-real} below is where that reversal bites. Below, ``spacelike'' and ``timelike'' name generators of square $-1$ and $+1$ respectively, the series' convention; in the mirror's convention the names swap and the mathematics does not.
