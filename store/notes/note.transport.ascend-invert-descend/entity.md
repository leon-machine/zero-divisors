## formal
In plain terms, the classical reciprocal is a composite of three steps. To divide by $x$, first \emph{ascend}: represent the point $x$ in the completion as the null ray $[X(x)]$. Next \emph{invert}: apply the reflection $\iota$, which is total and never fails. Finally \emph{descend}: return the answer to $\mathbb{V}$ with the read-out $\dsc$. Lemma \claimref{transport.descent-read-out-map}(2) states that whenever $Q(x) \neq 0$ this composite gives exactly the classical answer $x/Q(x)$. When $Q(x) = 0$ the first two steps still succeed, and the composite fails only at the last: the answer $\iota[X(x)]$ exists, but it is an ideal point, and ideal points correspond to no point of the original space to which $\dsc$ could return them. The division in the completion succeeds; what fails is the return of its result to $\mathbb{V}$. The classical impossibility of dividing by a null vector is thereby relocated rather than removed: it is not a failure of the operation but a failure of the descent, and the transport explains the obstruction rather than removing it. The answer exists, and it lies permanently outside the original space.
