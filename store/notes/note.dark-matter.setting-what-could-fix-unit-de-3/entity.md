## formal
This reading removes the two costs Remarks \claimref{note.dark-matter.what-rule-has-has-not} and \claimref{note.dark-matter.extra-direction-must-co-expand} record: no conserved count is needed to fill the odd towers at one microscopic spacing, and there is no metre-scale expanding extra dimension. Two things stand against it. First, under the sourcing rule a five-dimensional tower's fifth direction is a direction of its own space, not of spacetime, so the residue-$5$ tower cannot be identified with our own tractor ambient $I^{\perp}$; the reading survives only as ``each tower has its own $\dS_{n}$ at the shared $\Lambda$'', which the series permits and does not make. Second, the thickness is still R3's, now at one de Sitter radius: within the series as written there is no $\dS_{5}$ to slice, because there is no five-dimensional Einstein equation for it to solve, and $c$ is exactly the freedom P1 leaves, which R3 fixes, the contact mechanism of Section \ref{subsec:free} explains, and the geometry does not reach. To compute the thickness at all one must promote the fifth direction to a metric direction, against the sourcing rule. Doing so: $\dS_{5}$ of radius $\ell$ is foliated by $\dS_{4}$ slices,
\begin{equation}
ds_{5}^{2} = \ell^{2}d\theta^{2} + \cos^{2}\theta\; ds_{4}^{2}, \qquad \theta \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2),
\end{equation}
with our world the equator $\theta = 0$. Let the five-dimensional tower's dust fill the bulk at density $\rho_{5}$, comoving with the slices, and let ours sit on the equator with four-dimensional density $\rho_{\mathrm{us}}$. Two standard ways of extracting a four-dimensional Friedmann equation exist, and both are computed here.
