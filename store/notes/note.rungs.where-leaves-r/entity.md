## formal
Three things are now exact that were not. The five-mode ledger is the tower's two-rung module, so (S) is a statement about the tower. The ladder group is the rung-mixing group on the one-constituent sector. And the mass bracket is the frozen point's grip on the slot-antisymmetric bivector, at $\Lambda = 0$, which is a null vector of the ambient space that invites the next rung. What is missing is a single mechanism: why a conformal spinor constituent (a twistor) occupies a rung. The count is the obstacle. Null momenta invite one rung in total (Corollary \claimref{rungs.fact-null-momenta-invite-one-rung}); the bivectors of two conformal spinors invite one more, together (Proposition \claimref{rungs.two-twistor-bivector-null-invites-one}); neither gives one rung per constituent, and the second gives the right vector space with a $U(2)$ that mixes chirality. The precise form of the missing statement is this: the composite's internal algebra is generated by $3 + n$ anticommuting mode pairs, the three pairs of $W$ shared and one new pair per constituent. Fact \claimref{rungs.imported-tower-one-qubit-per-rung} then supplies everything else, the module, the grading and the groups. Why a conformal spinor constituent should contribute exactly one mode to the composite, rather than none (the tensor ledger of Fact \claimref{rungs.imported-ladder-automorphism-group-no-lepton}, where the slots are labels and the pairs are shared) or three (a copy of $W$ per constituent, the product state that Proposition \claimref{rungs.transported-module-holds-tuple-one-vector} excludes), is the question, and the note has no argument for it. It belongs to the tower paper, where the mode count is a theorem about the real Witt index and where a statement about what a conformal spinor constituent contributes to a composite's algebra would have to be made.
