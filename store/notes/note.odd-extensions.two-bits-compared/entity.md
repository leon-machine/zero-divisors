## formal
The root paper's Rule 2 leaves a sign free too, the square of the generator adjoined to a type-C obstruction, and records that the completed algebra forgets it. The sign left free here is of a different kind. The completed algebra, the rung $\Cl(2,4)$, forgets it in the same way; but the intermediate algebra of Theorem \claimref{odd-extensions.are-ascents-pair-signed} remembers it, the doubled $\Cl(1,4)$ against the complexified $\Cl(2,3)$, because the base $\Cl(1,3)$ is even-dimensional and exactly one of the two single extensions changes the parity of $q$ (Lemma \claimref{odd-extensions.volume-element-odd-dimension}). So the sign of $\Lambda$ is a geometric datum with an algebraic witness one level below the rung, and the witness is what Section \ref{sec:rung} used to separate the oriented towers.
