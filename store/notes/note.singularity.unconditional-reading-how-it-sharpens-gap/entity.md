## formal
Theorem \claimref{singularity.descended-form-state-independent} says less than it appears to, and that matters. For a single fermionic mode ``thermal'' is not a discriminating property: after the descent the mode's state space is one-dimensional and every point of it is a Gibbs state. So no part of the physical content of this step lies in the \emph{shape} of the reduced density matrix, and the chain must not claim the Fermi-Dirac form as a prediction: the descent produces that form from any input whatever. All of the content lies in the number $\beta_k$, in the claim that it is the same at every mode, and in the claim that its value is $2\pi/\kappa$. The gap is therefore not ``which class of states''; it is ``which point of a line, and why the same point at every mode''. The rest of this subsection is about that line.
