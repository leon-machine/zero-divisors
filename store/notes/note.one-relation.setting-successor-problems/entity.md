## formal
Five. (1) \emph{Which capacity is physical:} Proposition \claimref{one-relation.two-capacities-three-consequences} keeps the real and complex capacities apart and labels each consequence; the open question is which of the two the selected state of Remark \claimref{note.one-relation.two-residues-named-so-synthesis-can} uses, and whether the boost qubit of clause (1), a real-capacity statement, and the pair count of clause (3), a complex-capacity statement, are read by one state or by two. Half of it is answered (Remark \claimref{note.one-relation.where-exhaustiveness-first-fails-4-quadric}): the incidence-line structure is a complex-capacity statement at $\nu = 3$, where purity is automatic, and the real capacity of $(1,3)$, one, admits no pairing; what remains is which capacity the state reads. (2) \emph{The state-selection residue:} prove that the selected state is compatible with the incidence-line structure of Proposition \claimref{one-relation.spacetime-manifold-vacuum-lines}, or exhibit the incompatibility that would break the vacuum-line reading. (3) \emph{Emergent versus exact superselection:} close the disanalogy of Proposition \claimref{one-relation.record-chain-votes-measurement-without-collapse} by quantifying how far laboratory decoherence approximates algebraic superselection on the modular period. (4) \emph{The additivity fragment:} either derive that weights compose additively from the vacuum's symmetry structure, or accept it as the framework's final probabilistic postulate and say so in the capstone's claim table. (5) \emph{The primordial spectrum:} compute the descent-sourced spectrum against the measured index, the programme item named in Remark \claimref{note.one-relation.two-residues-named-so-synthesis-can} and assigned to the capstone \cite{ButlerT17}.
