## formal
The case against the orientation clause: a flag is defined per rung (Definition \claimref{obj.dark-matter.witt-flag}) and every tower has rungs, so nothing in the algebra forbids each tower its own choice; the clause is a statement about the event, not the algebra. What survives: the choice is initial data of the descent (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.orientation-arrow-seventeenth-refutation}), a per-tower choice would be a second uncaused datum of one event, which P2 refuses, and the clause is used in exactly two places, neither of which fails without it: the internal statement of the fifteenth refutation (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.asymmetry-arrow-counted-fifteenth-refutation}), and the second route to Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-tower-copy-one}(v), whose first route is P2's audit. Withdraw the clause and (v) stands by the audit, P4 reverts to a premise for the mirror, and the count is unchanged. Off the reversal orbit the clause cannot reach: over $\C$ there is no signature, an identification of two towers' ambients is fixed only up to a group whose two components exchange the two families, and ``the one flag'' would be defined only up to the bit at issue; hence the scoping in Premise \claimref{dark-matter.p1-single-frozen-point}.

The case against the rest: nothing in the transport principle forbids multiple division events, and a multi-descent history would give each tower its own $\Lambda$, expansion and asymmetry, dissolving the count, the ratio and the universality at once. What survives: the vacuum theorem derives one frozen point per metric, and there is observationally one metric, a second gravity being excluded by the equivalence-principle tests \cite{ButlerT6}; and the dating Observation locates the descent at the origin, a later descent being a second big bang inside this one's classical era, for which the record shows nothing \cite[Obs.~7.4]{ButlerT12}. P1 is supported, not proved; a pre-descent theory of the freezing (the capstone's declared open problem) would settle it. Its reading as a sourcing rule (Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones}) is a further choice inside P1, the one that makes the count and the audit consistent; a different rule would have to be stated and both re-run.
