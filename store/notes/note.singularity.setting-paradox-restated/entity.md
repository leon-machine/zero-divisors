## formal
Hawking's argument: general relativity drives information into the singularity and destroys it. Quantum mechanics: information cannot be destroyed. The chain's finding: both statements are true, at different levels of description, and the contradiction is a category error.

\begin{center}
\emph{Information is lost in $\Cl(1,3)$. Information is preserved in $\Cl(2,4)$. These are the same physics read at two levels, and the discrepancy between the levels is not destruction: it is the fixed projection of Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases}, extended to the boundary mode space by Definition \claimref{obj.singularity.multi-mode-descent}, and its size is an equation: the relative entropy of Theorem \claimref{singularity.deficit-theorem-cost-descent-exactly}, carried entirely by the correlations of the quanta (Corollary \claimref{singularity.deficit-carried-quanta-correlations}).}
\end{center}

General relativity is right that at the metric level the information is not conserved. Quantum mechanics is right that at the full level it is. The deficit between the two levels is not unaccounted for: traced through Theorem \claimref{singularity.modular-descent}, it is precisely the Hawking flux, thermal at $\TH$, the shadow of the entanglement with the adjoined partner; and its magnitude is not estimated but computed, $\rel{\rho}{\EN\rho} = C(\rho) - C(\EN\rho)$, the correlations the descent cannot carry (Corollary \claimref{singularity.deficit-carried-quanta-correlations}; Section \ref{subsec:descentapplied} evaluates it for the model in exact arithmetic). The paradox does not need resolving; it needs the two levels computed separately. What remains a genuine question is the modern sharp form: whether the exterior radiation is unitary \emph{by itself} and follows the Page curve, i.e.\ whether the projection's deficit is returned to the radiation before the end. That question this section takes as far as the two levels allow.
