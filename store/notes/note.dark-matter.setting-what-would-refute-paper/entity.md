## formal
Seven ways, each with its cost. Item 6 is the standing test, where the derivation meets the record and the record is already pressing; item 7 is its gravitational-wave form, unconditional in class and model-dependent in rate; item 2 is an unconditional running experiment; item 4 is bounded by the deposit interval; item 5 is conditional on an unsolved successor problem; items 1 and 3 are theory-internal risks. That the odd towers are empty, or that no consistent value of their relic densities supplies the remainder, does not refute this paper, which does not derive the remainder (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent}(iii)); the capstone carries both as conditions on the programme \cite{ButlerT17}. The unit-ball rule's own exposures follow the six and refute the rule, not the paper.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exhibit caused, tower-distinguishing structure in the pre-descent state, and P2 fails where most depends on it: the mirror's equivalence to this tower and the family ordering fall together (this paper and the families paper share the weakness).
\item Detect dark matter directly or indirectly at any strength above the gravitational floor, and the superselection is breached: Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.superselected-hence-dark-exactly-stable} is refuted outright, and with it the exact stability, the gravity-only seeding, and both conditional null stances.
\item Break the superselection argument itself: find a frozen-phase local operator that changes signature. The stability guarantee goes, though not the population count.
\item Exhibit one exactly massless dark species at a temperature above the thresholds of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.open-channels-their-costs}, and the radiation audit fails against the measured $\Neff$. Within the interval of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number} an excess reads first as a deposit hotter than the paper's estimate, the deposit energy being a datum the framework does not fix; it refutes item 2 only above the cap's own limit on the deposit, $T'/T_{\gamma} = 0.448$ for the mirror, where a species must have been heated by something other than gravity.
\item Derive the tower dimensions from the descent and find a window other than P3's, and the count of causal residues moves with the census of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.towers-are-residues-single-clock-filter}(iii); the asymmetric share moves only if a new even causal residue appears at a dimension divisible by four (Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.flag-bit-exists-exactly-tower-dimension}): the windows that make $r = 0$ and $r = 4$ causal do so at $(1,1)$ and $(1,5)$, which carry no bit, so on those windows the asymmetric share is unchanged.
\item \emph{The standing test.} Find the mirror's share other than one. Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-tower-copy-one} makes it a theorem, so this refutes P2 for the mirror, the descent's single ceiling, or the charge reading of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number}. With the remainder of the ratio underived, this is the one place the paper's derived content meets the record, and the record is already pressing on it.

\emph{Already in tension:} the share is $18.6$ percent of the dark matter in cold, dissipative mirror baryons, and the record's analysis of mirror dark matter's galactic structure allows at most $0.8\,\Omega_{b}$ at low mirror temperature \cite{RouxCline2020}, a fifth below the prediction, which is a quarter above it. That analysis rescales the mirror's baryon-to-photon ratio as $\eta' = \eta\,\beta/x^{3}$ and includes a helium mirror down to $x = T'/T = 10^{-3}$, which covers the hot end of the framework's interval; it does not include the interval's floor, where the framework's mirror, whose $\eta'$ runs from about $0.008$ to $26$, reaches forty billion times ours, and is helium throughout on the expected branch (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number}), nor the supernova energy of helium stars, which the companion note T14b finds to be $3.6$ to $7.3$ times ours per unit mass formed \cite{ButlerT14b}.

The dark-disk test runs in two phases. In the \emph{diffuse phase} mirror gas accreted into a halo is shock-heated to the virial temperature and ionised, a fluid with a mean free path of order ten parsecs ($\sigma/m \sim 10^{8}\ \mathrm{cm^{2}\,g^{-1}}$); this is what the Bullet Cluster's bound on the subcluster's mass loss, about $23$ percent and inferred from the mass-to-light ratios, tests \cite{Randall2008}; read as a bound on the dark matter, $18.6$ percent passes, and simulated, a merger in which only the mirror collides gives a mass-to-light change of $0.82$, consistent with the observed $0.84 \pm 0.07$, as cold dark matter's is (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-composition-simulated}(ii)). Its radiative cooling time is of order ten gigayears at the virial radius and a gigayear or less inside the cooling radius, where $n \gtrsim 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$, so, as with our baryons, the inner gas radiates its support within a Hubble time and the outer gas does not. In the \emph{settled phase} what cools forms a mirror disk or compact objects of total mass up to $\Omega_{b'} \approx \Omega_{b}$; a thin disk of the visible disk's mass, about $50\ M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$ locally, exceeds the Gaia thin-dark-disk bounds of a few $M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$ \cite{Schutz2018, Widmark2021} by an order of magnitude, so the settled phase must not be a thin disk. A thick or fragmented component, or mirror feedback holding gas in the diffuse phase, avoids the bound; fragmentation into compact objects is closed by the microlensing limits (compact objects at most $1$ percent of the halo from $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$ to $6.3\ M_{\odot}$ and $10$ percent up to $860\ M_{\odot}$ \cite{Mroz2024}) unless the objects form outside that window. The phase split, the star-formation efficiency, the settled gas's fate and whether helium composition delays settling are the model; the record's mirror-galaxy analysis \cite{RouxCline2020} models them with a rescaled $\eta'$ and a helium mirror at the hot end of the interval, not at its floor; Randall et al.'s mass-to-light ratios \cite{Randall2008} are the observation behind the mass-loss bound, and Clowe et al.'s offset \cite{Clowe2006} behind the lensing test. Model-dependent, so on the burning branch a tension and not an exclusion: this is the framework's one theorem-level dark-matter prediction, and it is at the edge of the record already. The companion note T14b models the settled phase in the Milky Way (Fact \claimref{dark-matter.imported-composition-simulated}(iv)): the proton-only branch is excluded at the Sun, and the burning branch's helium mirror, cooling slowly and with helium-star feedback, is open at the Sun under some normalisations of its gas and in the bulge only if the bulge holds over twice the mirror's proportional share of its dark mass, so that either of two measurements not yet made would decide it. Neither measurement can yet be made from published results. The tightest Gaia bound on a thin dark disk, about $5\ M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$ at $95$ percent \cite{Widmark2021}, holds only for scale heights of $50$ pc or less, and at the $100$ to $500$ pc the test needs the bound is weaker; the dynamical models of the bulge \cite{Portail2017} measure its dark mass, about $3.1 \times 10^{9}\ M_{\odot}$, as halo-like, and do not separate a disk-like part from the stars' mass-to-light ratio. The test therefore needs two analyses not yet made: a vertical-dynamics or phase-space-spiral analysis of Gaia data with a dark disk of that thickness, and a bulge model with a disk-like dark component. Until then the Galaxy test is registered, not run.
\item \emph{Dark neutron-star mergers.} The copy theorem makes the mirror sector baryonic, dissipative and at the proton mass, so it forms stars, and its neutron stars have our equation of state: the same maximum mass and the same tidal deformability $\Lambda(M)$. The prediction is a class of compact-binary mergers in the ground-based gravitational-wave band with component masses of one to two solar masses, $\Lambda$ in the range of ordinary neutron stars, and no electromagnetic counterpart at any depth or distance. Nothing in the standard model produces that class, and primordial black holes in the mass range have $\Lambda = 0$. The rate is model-dependent: the mirror sector is helium-dominated on the expected branch (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.stored-asymmetry-charge-not-number}) and helium star formation has no calibrated efficiency, so the natural expectation is within an order of magnitude of the visible binary-neutron-star rate, tens to hundreds per cubic gigaparsec per year, and the framework fixes the class and the deformability, not the number. GW190425, with no counterpart and its deformability unconstrained, is the class's example event. Refutation: a large sample of neutron-star-mass mergers whose counterpart fraction is consistent with an all-visible population bounds $\Omega_{b'}$ below $\Omega_{b}$ and refutes Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mirror-share-assembled} as item 6 does; confirmation is a counterpart fraction well below the visible population's with $\Lambda$ consistent with our equation of state, which third-generation detectors will measure for hundreds of events.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{The unit-ball rule's exposures} (Section \ref{sec:rule}; these cost the rule and Premises R1 to R3, not the paper). (a) A combined measurement of $\Omega_{\mathrm{dark}}/\Omega_{b}$ settling below the floor $5.255$ of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(ii) by more than its error excludes every thickness at once; the ACT DR6 combination's $5.22$ is already there if it holds. (b) A sharper measurement leaving $c = 1$ outside the window of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(iv) excludes R3 and the mechanism behind it; one leaving both windows excludes the exchanged assignment too and leaves a fitted $c$ with no geometric name. (c) The self-interacting fraction (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness}(v)): at composite strength it is about a half at $c = 1$, and simulated, that composition fails the Bullet Cluster and the shapes of galaxy clusters (Section \ref{subsec:simulated}); with the odd towers' matter collisionless the colliding fraction is the mirror's $18.6$ percent, the composition passes the merger and is not excluded by the shapes, and a colliding fraction measured well away from it excludes that reading whatever the total does. Under that reading the fraction no longer separates the rule from the exchanged assignment (Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.equivalent-forms-assignment-not}). (d) The sheets' displacement (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.reduced-response-gravity-needs-supporting-stress}): if the three-dimensional towers' sheets are held fixed by a stress the series does not name, the linear spectra exclude them (Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.sheet-family-linear-spectra}) and no thickness reaches $4.364$ from the five-dimensional towers alone; if they move, their halos are cold dark matter's when their matter is collisionless (Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.halo-built-collisionless-sheets-cold-dark}) and a cored fluid at composite strength (Section \ref{subsec:simulated}), and their exposure is the fraction test of (c) and, on the deposit branch, an in-plane free-streaming cutoff. Against these stands the caveat of Observation \claimref{dark-matter.status-rule}(ii): the closed form is one of fourteen equally simple candidates in the error bar, found by solving for the target, so the value alone is not evidence; the floor is, and the fraction is evidence about the three-dimensional towers' couplings.

Stabilising two-time dynamics opens a fork onto an oriented Kleinian Standard Model (Corollary \claimref{dark-matter.which-towers-can-carry-arrow}) with an asymmetric share of one: on the fork the asymmetric ratio is two on the two equalities Corollary \claimref{dark-matter.which-towers-can-carry-arrow} names as premises there. The fork is a theoretical one (two-time dynamics has no stable formulation on the record) and is carried as such.
