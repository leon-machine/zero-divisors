## formal
It would be a serious overstatement to say the framework derives quantum mechanics here, and this paper does not say it. What Section \ref{sec:lab} does is point at structures the series fixed for other reasons for the laboratory theory's three postulates, of which one is sourced, the mass (the condensate, at the level of the families paper's reading), one is half sourced, the time split (its orientation the flag, identified with the arrow under Hypothesis (S); its frame not sourced), and the third was not a postulate, the complex structure being the rest frame read through the law (Lemma \claimref{one-relation.one-datum-not-two-imaginary-unit}); and add four exact statements of its own, that the first-order operator acting on the left is unique, that the form of the law on even fields is forced by its square given first order, that the imaginary unit and the frame are one datum, and that the arrow and the frequency are one bit. The dynamics of the nonrelativistic descent from Dirac to Schr\"odinger is the textbook one, imported, and first-orderness is the one dynamical import. What remains genuinely postulated in the laboratory is the measure, and Section \ref{sec:born} isolates exactly how much of it the framework does not remove: a fragment smaller than Born's rule and not zero.
