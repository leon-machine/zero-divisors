## formal
Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent} derives one share of the survey ratio, the mirror's, and leaves the four odd towers' shares to a coupling rule the series does not supply (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.no-coupling-rule-odd-towers-follows}); that those towers are populated at all is the inference of Observation \claimref{dark-matter.which-towers-are-populated}. This section states one rule that returns the remainder as a closed form, and says what the rule assumes, what mechanism returns its one length, and what the geometry of the series does and does not fix about it. The rule is a reading of the sourcing rule of Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} with three premises added, at observation level throughout; it is not a coupling rule. \emph{Note (what the total rests on, 10 November 2026).} The three added premises are not all the total rests on. On the stored dependence edges the past cone of the closed form (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}) holds R1, R2 and R3; P1, through the sourcing rule; P2 and P3, through the census; and two conditions now registered as premises, the single-ideal clause that empties residues $0$ and $4$ (Premise \claimref{dark-matter.single-ideal-at-residues-zero-and-four}) and the population of the odd towers (Premise \claimref{dark-matter.odd-towers-populated}), the second inferred from the number it helps fit. R2 enters twice, as the density and as the unit of R3's thickness. The democracy premise D belongs to the de Sitter reading and is not among them. The total remains a fit: the closed form fixes two shares against one measured number, the remainder below; the relation was found by solving for that number, and the contact clauses and the single-ideal clause were chosen with it known (Observation \claimref{dark-matter.status-rule}(i), (ii), (vi)). The standing of the result is Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.unit-ball-rule}, Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness} and Observation \claimref{dark-matter.status-rule}.

\emph{A note on placement.} The unit-ball rule was proposed by the author after the first version of this paper was complete, and it was added as a section of its own, after the audits, rather than worked through the earlier sections, so that no result numbered in Sections 2 to 6 would move and every citation of them from elsewhere in the series would stay valid. It therefore reads as an addition to the paper. It is not a peripheral one: the rule is the paper's central quantitative result, the step that carries the derived mirror share through to the full dark-to-baryon ratio.

\emph{The number to explain.} With the mirror's share one (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent}(i)), the ratio is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{dark}}}{\Omega_{b}} \;=\; 1 + 2 f_{3\mathrm{D}} + 2 f_{5\mathrm{D}}, \qquad f_{3\mathrm{D}} := f_{1} = f_{7}, \quad f_{5\mathrm{D}} := f_{3} = f_{5},
\end{equation}
where in this section $f_{r}$ is the share of the tower of residue $r$, its matter per unit of our volume in units of ours (not the asymmetric tower factor of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.population-count-one-share-half-percent}, which vanishes for odd $r$), and the two equalities are P2's audit along the reversal orbit, which exchanges $1$ with $7$ and $3$ with $5$. The comparison throughout is with the Planck 2018 column that includes temperature, polarisation, low-$\ell$ polarisation and lensing, $0.1200/0.02237 = 5.364 \pm 0.065$ \cite{Planck2018}; the same paper's other columns give $5.376$ without lensing and $5.322$ with baryon acoustic oscillations, and the ACT DR6 combination with Planck, lensing and DESI gives about $5.22$ \cite{ACTDR6}. Agreement to more than two decimals is agreement with one column's rounded central value and carries no weight beyond $\pm 0.065$. The number to explain is $4.36 \pm 0.07$ from the four odd towers.
