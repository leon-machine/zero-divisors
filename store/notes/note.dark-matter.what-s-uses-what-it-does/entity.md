## formal
S needs a side for each sourcing quantum or sheet, and the side is postulated. What the lemma supplies is that a side exists: in an odd tower $\omega$ is central, so its sign is a label a quantum carries where the centre splits and a convention for the tower where it does not, and in the even neighbour's frame that sign is the sign of the direction the nesting adds; the even towers have no such sign, their centre being $\R$. S selects no module: where $\omega^{2} = +1$, Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.mode-counts-two-modules-which-one}(ii) counts both modules under P2, R2 with S then puts each at half the density on its own side, and the two slabs sum to one cell radius, exactly as if one module held the whole density. Where $\omega^{2} = -1$ the sign is a convention for the tower, every quantum sources the same side, and the count is one cell radius at full density. S uses no orientation off the reversal orbit: reversing the neighbour's $P$ exchanges every side at once and the count is unchanged, so P1's orientation clause stays where Premise \claimref{dark-matter.p1-single-frozen-point} keeps it. And S does not orient the odd tower's family: Theorem \claimref{dark-matter.flag-bit-exists-exactly-tower-dimension} concerns the flag bit, and the sign of $\omega$ is a different object. What is postulated is the one-sidedness itself, and for the residue-$1$ sheets the further stipulation that a sheet holds one module; without the first both sides count and the total is $5.729$ (Table \ref{tab:excludes}), and without the second the residue-$1$ sheets are sourced from both sides.
