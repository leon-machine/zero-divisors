## formal
Per the series' practice the meeting point with the cosmological-constant problem is stated where it occurs, with its limits. Theorem \claimref{dark-energy.frozen-point-makes-stelle-west-vector}(2) answers a narrower question than the one usually asked: not what value $\Lambda$ takes, and not why it is nonzero, but what its being nonzero yields. When $\Lambda \neq 0$ the frozen point has length, the ambient structure reduces to a reductive group, and gravity's action is a single curvature-squared invariant seen from the point at infinity. When $\Lambda = 0$ the frozen point is null, the prefactor $1/Q(I) = 3/\Lambda$ poles, and what remains is the Einstein-Cartan functional: an action principle still, but one that no longer reads as a curvature squared and no longer sees the point at infinity. The delimiters: this is a statement about the form of the action, not about its existence; it gives no value and no smallness, and the smallness problem is not addressed and is declared so in Section \ref{sec:verdict}. That the boundary of the curvature-squared form is itself a division by zero (normalisation by a null vector, $1/Q(I)$) is, given this series' founding subject, worth recording; whether it is more than a coincidence is not something this paper can decide.
