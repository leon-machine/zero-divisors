## formal
Define a \emph{boundary-tame corank-one degeneration} as a pair $(\shW, G)$ on a manifold with boundary $E$ satisfying: (T1$\partial$) $E$ is a smooth boundary hypersurface; (T2$\partial$) $G$ extends smoothly to $E$ with corank exactly one and smooth radical line; (T3$\partial$) the dying eigenvalue vanishes to exactly first order in a defining function. Theorem \claimref{sheaf.schwarzschild-degeneration-boundary-tame} says Schwarzschild, blown up and weighted, is boundary-tame. The adaptations of this paper's tame-wall results are minor and are itemised here. The fibrewise results (Theorem \claimref{sheaf.degeneration-sheaf}, Proposition \claimref{sheaf.resolution-ideal-theoretically}, Proposition \claimref{sheaf.exceptional-algebra-deformation-normal-cone}, Theorem \claimref{sheaf.metrics-residue-square-root-twist}) use only the form and its one-sided limit and go through in the bundle-valued sense of Remark \claimref{note.sheaf.setup-generalises-arbitrary-bundles} applied to $(\shW, G)$, with one convention mirrored: here the dying eigenvalue is \emph{negative} on the physical side ($g_k = -r$), so the quotient signature is $(p, q{-}1)$ rather than $(p{-}1, q)$ and the completion's coupling sign is the opposite branch of Remark \claimref{note.sheaf.only-product-coupling-constants-matters}; Proposition \claimref{sheaf.wall-fibre-black-hole} and Theorem \claimref{sheaf.one-sided-completion-singularity} carry the mirrored statements. The completion (Theorem \claimref{sheaf.one-sided-completion}) used three regions, wall, collar physical side, exterior, all of which exist here, and never used the far side to prove nondegeneracy on the physical closure. Only the shadow-wall analysis of Section \ref{sec:wall} genuinely concerns the far side, and Theorem \claimref{sheaf.one-sided-completion-singularity} records what happens to it. The sign-crossing clause of (T3), meaningless at a boundary, is replaced by the first-order clause (T3$\partial$).
