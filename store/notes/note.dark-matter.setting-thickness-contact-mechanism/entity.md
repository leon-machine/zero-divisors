## formal
R3 is a premise: it fixes the thickness at one cell radius, on one side, and nothing in the series does. This subsection gives a mechanism that returns that value. The count is made in the towers' own spaces, by asking which quanta of a tower are in contact with a world whose space is a slice of the tower's, the count being made on the nested world's space and carried to the containing world's by reciprocity. The mechanism has four clauses on how cells make contact. They were chosen with the value known, each has a stated alternative that the observation excludes (Table \ref{tab:excludes}), and the mechanism holds for one nesting at a time and does not compose across two (Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.scope-mechanism}). So what follows explains R3's value and does not derive it, and the tier of the rule is unchanged. The one-parameter family in which the thickness is a parameter is recorded afterwards as the sensitivity analysis. Throughout, $d_{b}$ is the common spacing of R2, the Wigner-Seitz radius, lengths are in units of it, a quantum's \emph{cell} is the ball of radius one about it in its tower's space, and a count is \emph{downward} when a world counts the cells of the tower whose space contains its own.

\emph{The four clauses.} (C) Contact: a quantum sources a world whose space is a slice of its tower's exactly when its cell meets that slice. (I) Indivisibility: a quantum that sources a world counts whole, its mass $m$ and not a fraction of it set by how much of its cell the slice cuts. (S) The side: across the direction a nesting adds or lacks, sourcing runs on one side only, the side named by the sign of the central volume element $\omega$ of the odd member of the nesting, whether that member is the tower counted or the world counting; where the odd member has two modules (residues $1$ and $5$) each module names a side, and a sheet of the three-dimensional tower holds one module. (O) Reciprocity: the ratio of two nested towers' densities found by the downward count on the nested world's space is the ratio read on the containing world's space. S postulates the side; Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.direction-nesting-adds-algebra} only says what a side is. O is the equality of R3's two thicknesses in another form (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.contact-mechanism-returns-r3}(iii)).
