## formal
Notation follows the twistor and mass papers \cite{ButlerT9, ButlerT11}. The completion of the spacetime algebra is $\Cl(1,3) \to \Cl(2,4)$, with ambient space $V = \R^{2,4}$ \cite[\S 2]{ButlerT9}; twistor space $\CS$ is one chirality half of the ascended Dirac module (Fact \claimref{standard-model.imported-pair-systems-two-chirality-halves}); a twistor $Z = (\omega, \pi)$ carries the pair systems of the completion; $\bar{Z} \cdot Z$ is the Hermitian pairing. Four words are used throughout in fixed senses. A \emph{pair} is one fermionic ladder pair (one creation and one annihilation operator) of the completion. A \emph{level} of the hierarchy is a tier of the partition of a composite into twistors and of each twistor into its pairs; the twistor paper's word for the same tiers is \emph{storey}, kept where that paper is quoted. This fixed sense is the tower level; the paper's epistemic phrases, ``record level'', ``observation level'', ``assembly level'' and the levels of the results in Section \ref{sec:ledger}, use the word in the series' other sense, the standard at which a statement is established. The \emph{state space} of a system is the Fock space its pairs generate, in the sense of the tower paper \cite{ButlerT3}; from Section \ref{sec:N} the paper also refers to the boundary mode space of the cosmological horizon, in the sense of the entropy paper \cite{ButlerT8}, and the text says which space is meant. A \emph{rung} is one hyperbolic pair of the tower, and the constituent count of Section \ref{sec:ladder} is called a step of the ladder, not a rung.
