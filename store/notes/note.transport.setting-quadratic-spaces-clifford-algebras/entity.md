## formal
$(\mathbb{V}, Q)$ is a finite-dimensional real quadratic space with polarisation $a \cdot b = \tfrac12(Q(a+b) - Q(a) - Q(b))$, so $a \cdot a = Q(a)$, and is \emph{nondegenerate} when $a \cdot b = 0$ for all $b$ forces $a = 0$. It then has an orthogonal basis $e_1, \dots, e_n$ with $e_i^{\,2} \mathrel{:=} Q(e_i) = +1$ for $p$ vectors and $-1$ for the remaining $q = n-p$; the \emph{signature} $(p,q)$ is an invariant (Sylvester), and is \emph{mixed} when $p, q \geq 1$.

The \emph{Clifford algebra} $\Cl(p,q) = \Cl(\mathbb{V}, Q)$ is generated by $\mathbb{V}$ subject to $v^2 = Q(v)$. Polarising gives $ab + ba = 2\,a \cdot b$, so orthogonal vectors anticommute; $ab = a \cdot b + a \wedge b$ with $a \wedge b \mathrel{:=} \tfrac12(ab-ba)$ the \emph{outer product}, pure grade two. The ordered products $e_{i_1} \cdots e_{i_k}$ over an orthogonal basis, $i_1 < \dots < i_k$, the \emph{basis blades} with $1$ as the empty product, form a linear basis, so $\dim \Cl(\mathbb{V},Q) = 2^n$; this holds for degenerate forms too \cite{Chevalley1997}, which we need for Lemma \claimref{transport.splitting-along-hyperbolic-plane} and for the null-generator ascent (Lemma \claimref{transport.decomposition-commutation}, Theorem \claimref{transport.one-generator-minimal-minimal-completions-classified}(2), (4)). An element has \emph{grade} $k$ if it combines blades of length $k$, basis-independently \cite{Porteous1995}. A vector with $Q(v) \neq 0$ is invertible, $v^{-1} = v/Q(v)$; a nonzero vector with $Q(v) = 0$ is \emph{null}, and null vectors exist exactly in mixed signature, where they form a cone \cite[Sections 2 to 4]{ButlerT0}. A word on the two notations: we write $\Cl(p,q)$ whenever only the signature matters, and $\Cl(\mathbb{V})$ or $\Cl(\mathbb{V}')$ for the algebra of a \emph{specific} quadratic space, chiefly an extension quantified over in a theorem, whose form is not assumed nondegenerate and so may carry no signature label at all.
