## formal
The share is a ratio of densities, not of sizes. One quantum per unit ball is a density of $\tfrac{1}{V_{D}}$, so a tower with a larger cell has its one quantum spread over more volume and is filled more thinly: the three-dimensional towers' disc ($3.14$) is smaller than our ball ($4.19$), so their filling is denser and their share exceeds one; the five-dimensional towers' four-ball ($4.93$) is larger, so their filling is sparser and their share is below one. The opposite reading, share proportional to the cell, would say that a larger world holds more matter, which is right for total mass at equal density but is not what gravity in our world sees (Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones}); it gives $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and $\tfrac{3\pi}{8}$ and a total of $4.856$, excluded at more than seven standard deviations.
