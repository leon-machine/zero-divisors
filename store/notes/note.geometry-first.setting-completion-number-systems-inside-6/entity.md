## formal
Two remarks on what (F4) is needed for. Theorems \ref{thm:bitroots} and \claimref{synthesis.frobenius-and-universality-in-clifford-algebras} use completeness only through square roots of positive numbers and roots of polynomials of odd degree, so they hold over every ordered field with those two properties, for example the real algebraic numbers. Completeness itself is needed for limits, such as the half-angle rotor of \cite[Example 6.1]{ButlerT0}, which needs completeness to be defined for every angle.

One family is not reached by any finite step. The commutation relation of a boson, $ab - ba = 1$, has no solution in a nonzero finite-dimensional real algebra: acting on the algebra by left multiplication, $ab - ba$ has trace $0$ and $1$ has trace the dimension. Whether such relations arise as a limit on the infinite tower of T3 \cite{ButlerT3} lies outside this note.
