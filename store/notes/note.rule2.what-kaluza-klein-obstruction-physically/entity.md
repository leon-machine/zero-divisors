## formal
Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(ii) says that a five-dimensional fermion cannot avoid choosing an ideal: to write its Dirac operator one picks $\Gamma^4 = -\gamma$ or $+\gamma$, and no change of basis undoes the choice. Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iii) says that the five-dimensional parity exchanges the choices; Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-five-dimensional-chern-simons-shift} says that the choice, together with the sign of the mass, is physical, carried by the induced Chern-Simons level. The obstruction is therefore not a formality of the real classification table: it is the statement that a five-dimensional fermion with a mass is parity-violating in a way its four-dimensional restriction is not, and that the violation is labelled by the ideal. Theorem \claimref{rule2.rule-2-kaluza-klein-algebra}(iv)-(v) says what repairs it: a sixth direction, in which the two species become the $\varepsilon_+$ and $\varepsilon_-$ halves of one $\Mat_4(\HH)$-module, exchanged by the inner $\Ad_{\nu e_4}$. That is the algebraic form of the familiar statement that a six-dimensional spinor reduces to a five-dimensional fermion in each of the two representations, and that the pair is parity-invariant. Which sixth direction is Remark \claimref{note.rule2.forgotten-bit-read}.
