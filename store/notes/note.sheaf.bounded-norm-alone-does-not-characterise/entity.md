## formal
It is tempting to define $\shW$ as the module generated by \emph{all} one-forms of bounded $G$-norm. That definition collapses: the form
\[
\alpha \;=\; du + \tfrac12\sqrt{m^2 - u^2}\; d\theta
\]
is $u$-smooth with $G(\alpha, \alpha) = G(du,du) + \tfrac14 (m^2 - u^2)\, G(d\theta,d\theta) = \frac{m^2 - u^2}{4u^4} - \frac{m^2-u^2}{4u^4} = 0$ \emph{exactly}: a null mixing of a divergent radial component against a divergent angular one. Both $\alpha$ and its partner with the opposite angular sign have bounded (indeed zero) norm, so the module they generate contains their difference and sum, hence $d\theta$ and $du$ separately, hence every one-form: the bounded-norm module is the full cotangent module, whose Gram does not extend to $E$. Boundedness must therefore be demanded of \emph{components} against a fixed nondegenerate frame, which is what the rain characterisation of Proposition \claimref{sheaf.basic-properties-operational-characterisation} does, and what the uniqueness theorem of Section \ref{subsec:uniqueness} must and does assume. The same phenomenon reappears there as an explicit second boundary-tame extension (Example \claimref{note.sheaf.rain-boundedness-essential-null-mixing-extension}).
