## formal
The natural conjecture, that coupling to the frozen point explains the observed masses, fails quantitatively, and the failure is the finding. The unit is $H \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-33}\,\mathrm{eV}$, so the fields with real weight in $[-3,0]$, the complementary series with its endpoints, end at $m = 3H/2$, below every known massive particle. Every known massive field is in the principal series, with $\mathrm{Re}\,w = -\tfrac32$ and $\mathrm{Im}\,w = \nu \approx m/H$, about $3 \times 10^{38}$ for the electron. The dictionary does not remove the hierarchy problem; it restates it: the hierarchy is the question of why the imaginary parts of the conformal weights of matter are so large in Hubble units. What the dictionary does yield: a single origin for mass and dark energy, both couplings to $I$; the de Sitter representation theory of matter as its $w$-classification, with the $-\tfrac32$ of the principal series read as the universal real part of every weight; and, together with the area formula of Section \ref{sec:area}, a two-scale structure for the matter programme: the frozen point supplies the unit, the spinor bracket supplies the compositeness, and the internal symmetry of the multi-spinor system (many conformal spinors) is where the forces arise, taken up by the standard-model paper \cite{ButlerT12}.
