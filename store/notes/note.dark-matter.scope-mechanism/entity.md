## formal
Two limits keep the mechanism an explanation of R3's value rather than a derivation of it. First, it does not compose. For the double nesting of the three-dimensional tower's plane in the five-dimensional tower's space, R3 gives $\rho_{5\mathrm{D}}/\rho_{3\mathrm{D}} = V_{2}/V_{4} = 0.637$, one cell radius for each of the two unshared directions; counting the five-dimensional cells that meet the plane directly by C and I gives $V_{2}^{2}/V_{4} = 2$ on the full transverse disc, $1$ on a half-disc and $\tfrac{1}{2}$ on a quadrant, and S is undefined there, $P^{-1}\omega$ across two directions being a bivector. So C with I holds for adjacent nestings only, which is where the rule uses it, and O is not tested there; R3, which composes, is the statement, and the mechanism is a reading of one step. Second, C restricts the homogeneous integral of Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} to one cell radius, and under it the five-dimensional tower's matter further from this world sources the one metric not at all. With P1 that matter is either absent, so that the extra direction is about one cell deep (the co-expanding direction of Remark \claimref{note.dark-matter.extra-direction-must-co-expand}, forced rather than allowed) and C adds nothing to the integral over it, which is R3; or present and inert to gravity, against Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.sourcing-rule-forced-not-chosen}(i), which takes the integral over the whole direction as the only covariant choice. On the first branch C is R3; on the second it contradicts a proposition the paper calls forced. On neither is it a foundation.
