## formal
\textbf{Consequences for the owning papers.} For the singularity paper, what is defensible is this. Its Premise 7.30 says the completion ``provides no generator that is off-diagonal in that basis'', the basis being the joint number basis of one ladder pair per point of $E$; nothing here touches that, since the pre-ascent connection acts on a different space and, along the $t$- and $r$-lines, lies in the stabiliser, so Theorem C.8 is untouched, and the cross-point clause is untouched because no connection produces a cross-point generator. What the note adds is a clause for Remark 13.5: the framework compares two Witt decompositions and does not derive them, and it does derive one relation between two of them, the rotation of a vacuum transported along a path, measured against the vacuum field at the endpoint, which is the path-ordered transport whose generator's pairing part is the class of Proposition \claimref{pairing.pairing-class-transverse-derivative-null-direction}; what it does not derive is which two decompositions the Hawking pair uses, and the remark's clause on the pairing angle stands. For the laboratory paper: Proposition 4.1(3)'s null-direction datum has an exact transport law, Proposition \claimref{pairing.pairing-class-transverse-derivative-null-direction}, and the paragraph that says the connection returns the bivector tilted can say by how much: by $\kappa, \tau, \sigma, \rho$, with the discrete bits unmoved. Successor problems: the tractor connection on the singular collar (Remark \claimref{note.pairing.what-does-does-not-say}); a cross-point generator, which is the kinetic-term question of the singularity paper's Remark C.9 under another name; and vacuum selection, which this note does not address.
