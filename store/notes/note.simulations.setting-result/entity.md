## formal
So with the consistent yield the Galaxy test would be decided by either of two measurements not yet made: a $2\sigma$ limit below $5\ \Msun\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$ on extra disk-like mass at the Sun with scale height $100$ to $500$ pc (the room is $4.6 \pm 5$, the dynamical stellar surface density less the counted one, Fact \claimref{simulations.imported-milky-ways-mass-budget}), or a limit below $1.7 \times 10^{9}\ \Msun$ on disk-like dark mass in the bulge volume, which would bound the mirror's mass there directly, whatever reading of the room is taken. Either excludes the helium branch under every choice tried with yield $yF$; with the yield held at ours the limits would need to reach $4.2\ \Msun\,\mathrm{pc^{-2}}$ and $1.3 \times 10^{9}\ \Msun$. The thin-gas bound is not used to decide, because the mirror's gas at high $F$ sits at the star-formation threshold of the model, which is its least certain part.

Roux and Cline find $\Omega_{b'} \lesssim 0.8\,\Omega_{b}$ at low mirror temperature \cite{RouxCline2020}. Their analysis includes a helium mirror with $\eta' = \eta\,\beta/x^{3}$, down to $x = T'/T = 10^{-3}$, which covers T14's hot end; what it does not include is the floor of T14's interval and the helium-star supernova factor.
