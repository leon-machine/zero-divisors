## formal
Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-parity-anomaly} is Proposition \claimref{rule2.rule-2-plane}(i) with a coupling switched on: the ideal a two-component fermion lives in is the sign of its induced Chern-Simons level, the parity that exchanges the ideals is the reflection that flips the level, and a single species cannot be parity-invariant because no inner change of variables moves it to the other ideal. The four-component fermion of planar electrodynamics is Proposition \claimref{rule2.rule-2-plane}(ii): a module over $\Mat_4(\R)$, the fused algebra, on which the two species are the $\varepsilon_\pm$ halves and the matrix $\nu = \gamma^3$, which anticommutes with the three planar gamma matrices, is the inner automorphism that exchanges them; the parity-invariant mass is the one that respects the fusion. Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-graphenes-two-valleys-doubling} is the same proposition realised in a crystal: the two valleys are the two ideals, related by the sign of one spatial gamma matrix, which by (i) is exactly the reflection that exchanges them, and the doubling theorem is the statement that a lattice, having a third direction available to it in the form of a lattice regulator, cannot host the unfused algebra. In the series' language: nature has done the fusion, and the two-component fermion is what one sees when one looks at a single ideal of it.
