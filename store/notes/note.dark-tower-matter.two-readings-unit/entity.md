## formal
Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.third-pairs-unit-five-dimensions} is where the two five-dimensional towers part. At $r = 3$ the unit that builds the pairs is fixed by the algebra, and the quaternionic unit group of a single constituent, which preserves its momentum (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.ladder}), does not preserve the pair structure. At $r = 5$ every choice of unit is equivalent, and each choice cuts the quaternionic group to the circle it generates. Under U3 both towers therefore build their pairs with one complex unit; they differ in whether the algebra names it. With an external unit, which commutes with the whole quaternionic group, the group survives in both towers alike (Remark \claimref{note.dark-tower-matter.branches-break-u-what-they-give}).
