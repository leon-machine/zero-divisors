## formal
The result on the colour angle term (\claimref{synthesis.colour-angle-term-under-the-stored-maps-and-no-colour-anomalous-phase-of-the-rung-coupling}) rests at two places on a reading of a stored premise that the premise itself does not fix. Both are recorded here as open; neither is decided, and neither premise is edited. The registration of the physical colour angle (Premise \claimref{synthesis.colour-angle-registered-free-input}) does not depend on how they are answered: it registers one number for this tower.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Q1)] \emph{How the stored CPT conjugation acts on the gauge sector.} Premise \claimref{dark-matter.premise-cpt-covariance} states its convention for vector fields and for connections with values in the algebra, and carries the invariance of the imported gauge sector as premised; it does not say how the conjugation $C = \mathrm{Ad}(I_{6}) \circ \kappa$ acts on the colour index of a gauge field. Clause (ii) of the result therefore takes as a hypothesis that it acts there by an automorphism or an anti-automorphism of $\su(3)$; either preserves the trace form, and under the hypothesis the conjugation fixes the angle term in both stored conventions. A sentence in the premise stating the action on the internal index of an imported gauge field would remove the hypothesis. A related observation, also not decided: the rung results read the phases of family matrices in $\mathrm{span}\{1, I_{4}\}$, the conjugation fixes $I_{4}$ (it is antilinear with respect to the unit of the complexified ambient, which is a different unit), so it is linear with respect to the unit in which the mass phase is read; whether that is intended is a question about the stored conventions.
\item[(Q2)] \emph{Which identification of orientations the common-ceiling coupling premise uses for the angle.} Premise \claimref{synthesis.common-ceiling-coupling-premise} equates, at the ceiling, the gauge couplings and ``every other coupling of the copied theory'' of this tower and the mirror. The angle multiplies a pseudoscalar, so equating it across the two towers needs an identification of their orientations. By clause (iii) of the result the even algebras of the two signatures are identified by the stored reversal $\sigma$, which sends $I_{4}$ to $I'_{4}$, and equally by $\sigma \circ \mathrm{Ad}(\gamma_{0})$, which sends it to $-I'_{4}$; the copy theorem names neither. Read with the stored reversal, the premise makes the two towers' angles equal; read with its composite with a reflection, opposite. The premise does not say which, and the same question arises for every parity-odd coupling of the copied theory, the phases of the family matrices among them.
\end{enumerate}
Neither question changes the record's bound on the angle, which is a bound on its magnitude.

## technical
Two readings of stored premises are left open by the result on the colour angle term. First, the CPT-covariance premise does not say how the series' conjugation acts on the internal index of an imported gauge field; the result assumes an automorphism or anti-automorphism of the colour algebra, under which the angle term is fixed. Second, the common-ceiling coupling premise equates all couplings of the copied theory between this tower and the mirror without naming the identification of the two orientations; for the angle, and for any parity-odd coupling, the two natural identifications differ by a sign, so the mirror's angle is the same or the opposite one according to a choice the store has not made. Both are recorded as open items; the registration of the angle as a free input of this tower does not depend on them.

## lay
Two assumptions of the framework turn out to be silent on points that matter for the angle of the strong force. One is the framework's version of the combined reversal of charge, space and time: it is not spelled out how it acts on the colour charges of the strong force. The other is the assumption that the mirror world has the same force strengths as ours: for a quantity that changes sign in a mirror, what counts as the same depends on how the two worlds' handedness is matched, and that matching has not been chosen. This note records both points as open.

## one-liner
Two open items, not decided: (Q1) the CPT-covariance premise does not say how the stored conjugation acts on the colour index of a gauge field (the colour-angle result assumes an automorphism or anti-automorphism); (Q2) the common-ceiling coupling premise equates the two towers' angles only up to the sign of an identification of orientations that it does not name ($+$ for the stored reversal).
