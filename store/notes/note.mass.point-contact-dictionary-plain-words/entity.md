## formal
The universe, in this framework, has exactly one built-in scale: the length of the point at infinity, which is the cosmological constant under another name. The theorem says that mass is how strongly a field is coupled to that one scale: each field carries a number, its weight, and its mass is that number read against that single scale. A massless field is one whose weight gives zero mass against that scale. Against the data the natural conjecture fails: the scale is the Hubble scale, whose length is cosmological, so a field whose weight is an ordinary real number is lighter than anything ever observed, and everyday particles must carry weights whose imaginary part is extremely large. The mechanism does not explain why matter is so heavy compared to dark energy; it converts that question into a new one, which is the old hierarchy problem restated. The next section adds the other half of the account from the conformal spinor side, and Section \ref{sec:join} joins the two halves.
