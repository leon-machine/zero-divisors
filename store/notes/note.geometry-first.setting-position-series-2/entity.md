## formal
\textbf{Where the series uses this.} The switch of Theorem \claimref{synthesis.set-and-clear-of-a-bit-generate-the-hyperbolic-plane} is the hyperbolic pair that T1 \cite{ButlerT1} adjoins to repair a failed division. In T1's Example 3.4 the product $wv$, here the test for off, is the idempotent $e$ with $ev = 0$ and $v(1 - e) = 0$; that example and this note point to each other. The failure of division at $v$ and $w$, and its repair, remain T1's subject and are not treated here. T3 \cite{ButlerT3} iterates the adjunction, one switch per step. The pinching of Proposition \ref{prop:pinching} is the descent of T5 \cite{ButlerT5}. Theorem \claimref{synthesis.integer-clifford-algebras-contain-no-switch} is arithmetic of the integer algebra, and nothing in this note uses a rule of transport: none of it is evidence for the transport principle.
