## formal
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Premise (R) is not derived, and cannot be, from the singular side.} It is the standard premise, stated in the framework's own algebra (Proposition \claimref{state-selection.euclidean-partner-curvature-selected}), but the transport principle does not entail it, and the reason is structural rather than a shortcoming of the present proof. The only finite rate the geometry supplies to the boost flow is the surface gravity, a horizon quantity: on the singular side the connection rate diverges as $\sqrt{M/2r^{3}}$ and offers no finite period to identify with $2\pi$. The locus at which (R) has purchase is the fixed locus of the boost flow, the bifurcation surface at $r = 2M$, and the Euclidean section on which the periodicity argument runs is the region $r \geq 2M$, which does not contain the singular boundary at all. There, by the chain's own Step 1, the gauge field is invertible and unimodular and division does not fail. A theory of the loci where division fails is therefore silent, by its own diagnosis, at the locus where the temperature is calibrated. In particular, strengthening the rain-finiteness of the chain's null dyad \cite[Prop.~8.23, Rem.~8.24]{ButlerT4} from a statement about the dyad to a statement about admissible states at $E$, which is the question \cite{ButlerT4} itself poses and a reasonable item for the geometry paper (T6), would discharge faithfulness and exclude the endpoints $\beta \to 0$ and $\beta \to \infty$, but would not fix the value: it is a bound, not a calibration.

The live route to a better-placed premise runs through Step 3's landing zone instead. Bisognano-Wichmann does not assume the factor $2\pi$: it derives it, from the spectrum condition together with covariance and locality \cite{BW1976, Sewell1982}. The ascent lands in $\Cl(2,4)$, whose parabolic stabiliser of the adjoined null direction contains exactly the translations such a spectrum condition needs, so the premise could be restated as positivity of the ledger's energy with respect to a generator the ascent itself installed. That would still be an import, positivity of energy being no Clifford fact, but it would be stated in the algebra the chain produces rather than in the metric geometry. Transcribing the theorem needs an action of the relevant subgroup on the ledger, a wedge and locality structure, and analyticity of the state, each of which is real work and any of which could fail; the natural home is the twistor paper, not T6.
\item \emph{Premise (L$_E$) is a statement about available generators.} See Remark \claimref{note.state-selection.status-l-e}. A no-go excluding inter-point hopping generators on $E$, in the manner of the series' other no-go results, would settle it.
\item \emph{The infinite ledger.} Theorems \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average} to \claimref{singularity.ascended-marginals-are-diagonal} and \claimref{singularity.exact-equilibrium-over-determines-boundary-modes} are stated for the CAR algebra and their proofs do not use finiteness. Theorems \claimref{singularity.kms-uniqueness-factor}, \claimref{singularity.rate-rigidity-temperature-not-algebra-intrinsic} and \claimref{state-selection.partner-purification-are-canonical} are proved here in the matrix factor, which is the setting Step 4d works in; the general versions are the imported Facts \claimref{state-selection.imported-general-statement}, and in the genuine field-theoretic setting the horizon algebra is a type III$_1$ factor, where the Gibbs form is unavailable and the KMS condition must be used directly. Transcribing Sections \ref{sec:rigidity} and \ref{sec:rate} into that setting is the natural next technical step, and \cite{KayWald1991} is the template.
\item \emph{Universality across the sphere.} Corollary \claimref{singularity.universality-temperature-under-g} gives one $\beta$ for the whole ledger given the GMC, but the ledger is indexed by $E \cong \R \times S^{2}$ and the argument treats the sphere directions as spectators. That is correct for Schwarzschild by symmetry; for Kerr the boost weights vary over the horizon and the corresponding statement is the rigidity of the temperature over a rotating horizon, which the chain has not touched.
\item \emph{The greybody interface.} Nothing here bears on the partial-wave reconstruction flagged in \cite[Rem.~8.3]{ButlerT5}; it remains assembly level.
\end{enumerate}
