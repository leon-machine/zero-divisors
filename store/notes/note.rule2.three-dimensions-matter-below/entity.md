## formal
$n = 5$: $\Cl(1,4)$ is split, $\Cl(4,1)$ complex type. $n = 3$: $\Cl(2,1)$ is split, $\Cl(1,2)$ complex type. $n = 11$: $\Cl(10,1)$ is split, $\Cl(1,10)$ complex type. In the series' own convention the five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein extension of this world is the split one; the planar and eleven-dimensional cases are split in the mirror's convention. Physics done with complex gamma matrices sees the splitting in every odd dimension, as the corollary says it must; what the real form governs is the reality of the spinors, Remark \claimref{note.rule2.eleven-dimensions-reality-spinors}.
