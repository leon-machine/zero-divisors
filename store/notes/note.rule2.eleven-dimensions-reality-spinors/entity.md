## formal
Corollary \claimref{rule2.odd-lorentzian-dimension-exactly-one-real} at $n = 11$ says $\Cl(10,1) \cong \Mat_{32}(\R) \oplus \Mat_{32}(\R)$ is split while $\Cl(1,10) \cong \Mat_{32}(\C)$ has complex type. The irreducible real module of the first is $\R^{32}$ and of the second is $\C^{32}$, which is the classical statement that Majorana spinors exist in eleven dimensions with time of square $-1$ and not with time of square $+1$, the reason eleven-dimensional supergravity is written in the former convention. \emph{Provenance:} \cite{KugoTownsend1983, VanProeyen1999}. The note records this as a third physical face of the same theorem, without a section of its own: the Rule 2 repair of $\Cl(10,1)$ is $\Cl(11,1)$ or $\Cl(10,2)$, both $\Mat_{64}(\R)$; the former is the Rule 1 ascent of $\Cl(10,0)$, and the latter is the algebra of the two-time proposals, which the note mentions and does not endorse.
