## formal
Three consequences, at three declared levels. First, at proposition level, the exotic representations of \eqref{eq:fock} are accounted for without projection: $(1,6)$, $(2,8)$, $(4,1)$ and their partners are multi-quantum states (the $(1,6)$ is a two-pair composite, the $(4,1)$ a full row) and are no more in need of removal from the elementary content than two-particle states are anywhere in quantum theory; Remark \claimref{note.standard-model.eighth-refutation-split-conclusion-decisive-test}'s miss list was a list of composites. What the tensor state space does not account for is the absence of the lepton, which is Hypothesis (S)'s to supply. Second, at open-question level, the colour content of the sector of a single conformal spinor is $3 \oplus \bar{3}$ against the observed $\bar{3} \oplus \bar{3}$ of the two quark singlets, a single conjugation, which Proposition \claimref{standard-model.chirality-two-halves-spinor-module-conjugation} resolves by the handedness of the listing; the particle-antiparticle orientation behind it is the descent's one undetermined bit, shown underivable in the dark-matter paper T14 \cite[Prop.~5.2]{ButlerT14}. Third, now at proposition level rather than observation level: the abelian weights are integers and $Y = q/6$, so hypercharge comes in units of $1/6$, the observed quantum, and under (S) the lepton-to-quark ratio $Y_{L}/Y_{Q} = -3$ is the pair count with the sign reversed, the full pair system having weight $-6$ against the empty one's $0$, added to the slot's $+3$. Theorem \claimref{standard-model.hypercharge-global-form-pair-counting-consistency} completes the spectrum.
