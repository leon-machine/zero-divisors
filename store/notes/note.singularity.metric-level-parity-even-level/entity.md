## formal
Theorem \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average} sharpens the chain's slogan that descent keeps the records and loses the coherences. What descent keeps is the even subalgebra, and the sector the completion installs is exactly the odd one (its ladder pair mixes the physical and adjoined planes: $k^{*} = \nO/2\nu$ against the dying direction $k$ itself, Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-one-sided-completion-singularity}; this $\nu$ is T04's coupling constant, not Step 6a's cell density): the two-level split is the Clifford algebra's own $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading, not an extra structure imposed on it. This is a single-mode statement: on the full boundary mode space the metric-level algebra the chain uses is the joint-diagonal one, the fixed points of one parity per point of $E$, strictly smaller than the total-parity-even algebra, and its licence is Proposition \claimref{singularity.p-reduced-metric-level-reading-many}, not the grading alone. The one-way character recorded next as Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-descent-one-way-conditional-expectation} is then the statement that a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ average cannot be undone, and the uniqueness clause there is strengthened rather than merely imported: Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-descent-one-way-conditional-expectation}'s Takesaki clause is uniqueness among trace-preserving conditional expectations, while Theorem \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average} makes $E$ the unique conditional expectation onto $\Aev$ outright, trace-preserving or not; and the grading is not a choice. Step 4d turns this identification into the state-selection theorem of Section \ref{subsec:selection}.
