## formal
This is the paper's most falsifiable claim: it may be settled within a data release or two, it cannot be adjusted after the fact, and it ties the dark-energy stance to the neutrino sector so that they survive or are refuted together. As of this writing the record is unusually favourable to the prediction's \emph{structure} while unusually tight on its \emph{number}. Three points, each refreshed at submission. (i) The sub-floor bound is flagged by its own authors: the analysis reporting $\mnu < 0.052$ eV states that the value lies below the normal-ordering minimum and attributes the excursion to the zero-extending prior rather than to physics, which is exactly the reading Observation \claimref{neutrino.double-prediction-sum-predicted-floor} registers; with the physical floor imposed the bound is $0.092$ eV and the prediction is well inside it \cite{HouWangZhaoZhang2026}. (ii) Inverted ordering requires $\mnu \gtrsim 0.098$ eV (script-computed from the splittings) and is accordingly the first to be excluded by the tightening: the cosmological bound that threatens the framework's number is simultaneously excluding the ordering the framework excludes by rank (Proposition \claimref{neutrino.rank-one-seesaw-ordering-sum-double}(1)). (iii) The prediction can fail in two directions, and this should not be understated. Below: a robust, prior-independent determination of $\mnu$ under $0.057$ eV within $w = -1$, across likelihoods and free of the lensing anomaly, refutes the joint prediction outright, and with it normal ordering itself, since the threshold sits below the kinematic floor; no part of the framework can absorb it. Above: the prediction is that the sum is \emph{at} the floor, so a determination meaningfully above it, near $0.075$ eV say, or a measured $m_{1}$ above a few meV, refutes the framework's number while leaving normal ordering intact, and that is the failure mode specific to this framework.
