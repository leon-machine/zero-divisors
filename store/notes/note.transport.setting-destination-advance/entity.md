## formal
Section \claimref{transport.transport-algorithm} turns the theorems into a six-step procedure. It asks, in order:

   
\begin{enumerate}
  
\item Where does division fail?
\item Which of the three obstructions is it?
\item What is the minimal repair, and what does it adjoin?
\item How is the value at the obstruction computed?
\item What does descent back to the original algebra cost?
\item Does repeating the repair terminate?
\end{enumerate}
 \noindent The answers take the form of an algorithm: six steps, each with a theorem licensing it. It is set out as Table \ref{tab:algorithm} in Section \claimref{transport.transport-algorithm}, which names every step's instrument and its warrant, and which T5 follows in order.

Everything between here and there builds that algorithm. Each section proves the results that one or more of the steps invoke, one obstruction type at a time, and no step is stated before the theorem that licenses it, which is why the procedure itself comes last rather than first. A reader who would rather see the destination before the route can read \ref{tab:algorithm} now and return here.

\emph{A reading route.} Read straight through for the mathematics in the order it was found. To enter directly: the no-go and its cohomological form are Sections \ref{sec:nogo} and \ref{subsec:cohomology}; the completion, its totality and its two minimality theorems are Sections \ref{sec:model} to \ref{subsec:chartmin}; the fusion theorem is Section \ref{subsec:fusion}; the clock is Section \ref{sec:clock}; the algorithm is Section \claimref{transport.transport-algorithm}. Section \ref{sec:prelim} is notation and imported classical facts, and is meant to be consulted rather than read.
