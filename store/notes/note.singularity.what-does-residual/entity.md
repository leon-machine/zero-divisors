## formal
Remark \claimref{note.singularity.declared-residual-size}'s question, whether the physical dynamics on the singular boundary is generated within the supplied class or carries a differential or pairing term, splits into two halves of different kinds. A differential term would hop occupation between points of $E$ and would break the joint diagonality that Step 6b uses, and off the vacuum it entangles, as any hop does (the script exhibits one on $|10\rangle$); but it cannot move the vacuum, and so cannot create entanglement from it. A pairing term moves the vacuum, and only a pairing term does. The premised content of (L$_E$) is thereby separated into its two parts, both class-relative in the sense of Theorem \claimref{singularity.class-relative-no-go-no-supplied}: the exclusion of pairing terms, which is the statement that the closure supplies no generator in the $\nu(\nu - 1)/2$-dimensional tangent of the vacuum ray, and the exclusion of the unsupplied hops, the differential dynamics, which lie in the stabiliser and are excluded by the premise for the diagonality Step 6b uses rather than for what they do to the vacuum. Its necessity, Corollary \claimref{singularity.envelope-endpoint-why-boundary-mode-locality}, is the statement that one generator in the tangent, acting once on one pair, is what the return of the boundary modes needs. The framework compares two Witt decompositions and does not derive them; nothing here changes that, and the derivation of the one pairing angle the chain uses, the angle of Premise \claimref{singularity.r-regularity-fixed-locus}, remains outside its reach (Proposition \claimref{singularity.why-calibration-cannot-be-internal}).
