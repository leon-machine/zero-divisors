## formal
What this paper adds is not the classification and not the conformal model, but the reading they jointly permit: the organisation of the zero divisors of $\Cl(p,q)$ into three obstruction types with sharp existence loci; the no-go theorem that closes the element-level repair of the isotropic type, with its cohomological form; the minimality theorems that make each rule forced, in a representation-independent sense; and the change of reading these mandate, namely that the conformal construction is not an available convenience but the unique minimal completion demanded by the failure of division itself.

At the standard of complete proofs, with every theorem carrying its own hypotheses and every computational identity machine-verified, the paper proves: that the zero divisors of $\Cl(p,q)$ fall into exactly three types with sharp existence loci, and that the trichotomy is exhaustive because every zero divisor is annihilated by a nontrivial idempotent (Section \ref{sec:taxonomy}); that isotropic vectors admit no equivariant element-level repair in the Clifford algebra of any extension once $n \geq 3$, the obstruction being the tautological class of the unipotent radical, whose vanishing locus is exactly the hyperbolic atom (Sections \ref{sec:nogo} and \ref{subsec:cohomology}); that the reciprocal nevertheless extends totally, continuously and uniquely to the null quadric of the completion (Sections \ref{sec:model} and \ref{sec:totality}); that the increment $(+1,+1)$ is forced twice over, by a sign argument on lifts and by a rank computation on ambient quadrics that no exotic embedding evades (Sections \ref{sec:minimality} and \ref{subsec:chartmin}); that central idempotents fuse under a one-generator ascent into $\Mat_2(\Cl^0(p,q))$, minimal among all simple real algebras, with the sign of the adjoined direction a bit the algebra forgets, and that on every one-dimensional extension of an even-dimensional algebra exactly one sign obstructs, its ideals are copies of the base, and the opposite-sign repair is the Rule 1 ascent, so that the two rules coincide there (Section \ref{sec:rules}); that non-central idempotents demand nothing, because $eee = e$ where $vvv = 0$, so there is no fourth rule (Section \ref{sec:typeP-rule}); and that the bookkeeping closes, Rule 1 fixing the mod-8 clock, fusion occurring at most once, and no unital homomorphism returning to the base (Section \ref{sec:clock}).
