## formal
Theorems \claimref{singularity.finite-truncation-meet-big-fibres-equals} and \claimref{singularity.net-assembly-net-it-everything} quantify the two readings of ``the metric level over a region'' and do not decide between them. Both are determined by readings near the region's points; they differ in how the pointwise levels are assembled. The contravariant assembly (the sheaf, a limit: an element must survive every point's descent) gives $\operatorname{range}(\EN)$, the joint-diagonal algebra, and with it the nonzero two-level discrepancy of Steps 5 and 6b. The covariant assembly (the net, a colimit: the algebra generated by what each point's descent retains of the whole mode algebra) gives the whole mode algebra and a discrepancy of zero; it is the assembly an algebraic quantum field theory would adopt for its algebras of regions. The chain adopts the former, and that adoption is Proposition \claimref{singularity.p-reduced-metric-level-reading-many}'s antecedent: the sheaf assembly with the small fibres as stalks is the metric level if and only if the metric level over a region is the meet, so the antecedent is restated by this appendix, not reduced. What the appendix adds is the structural content of the choice: on the sheaf side the reading is a genuine sheaf whose finite truncations are $\operatorname{range}(E_F)$, on the net side it is a net whose finite truncations are the full mode algebras, and the discrepancy of Theorem \claimref{singularity.deficit-theorem-cost-descent-exactly} is precisely the cost of reading a region as a limit rather than a colimit. The choice is made in Definition \claimref{obj.singularity.two-fibres-two-assemblies}, before any sheaf theory runs, and it is a choice about which algebra ``the pointwise metric level'' names: the even part of the mode factor, or the even-in-that-mode part of the boundary mode space.
