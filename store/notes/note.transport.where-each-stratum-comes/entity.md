## formal
The three strata are distinguished by $\iota$ itself, which supplies a check on the bookkeeping. Stratum (i) is exactly the image of the null cone of $\mathbb{V}$ under $\iota \circ X$, and (iii) is the image of the origin. Stratum (ii) is the image of neither: it is fixed \emph{pointwise} by $\iota$, since $(0,x,0) \mapsto (0,x,0)$. Indeed the whole fixed locus of $\iota$ is $\{Q(x) = 1\} \cup \text{(ii)}$, as one sees by solving $(2t, x, \tfrac12 s) = \lambda(s,x,t)$ against \eqref{eq:incidence}: $\lambda = 1$ forces $s = 2t$ and $Q(x) = 4t^2$, which after normalisation is the unit quadric when $t \neq 0$ and stratum (ii) when $t = 0$. So the null directions at infinity are the ideal fixed points of the inversion, as they must be: they are the directions along which the null cone through the origin goes to infinity, and inversion carries that cone to itself.
