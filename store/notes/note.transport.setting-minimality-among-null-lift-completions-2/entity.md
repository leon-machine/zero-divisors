## formal
Two hypotheses in this definition carry weight, and both are deliberate.

The shape $X(x) = f(x) + x$ is the normalisation that makes the minimality question well posed. Without some such condition ``minimal completion'' is not yet a definite notion: any quadratic space whatever admits \emph{some} map into \emph{some} larger space, and one can always use a dimension of the ambient space to re-encode the base rather than extend it. Requiring the $\mathbb{V}$-coordinates of a represented point to be the point itself says exactly that the representation leaves the base alone and puts new structure only into the adjoined directions; the question then asked, and answered, is how much new structure that is. (Allowing an arbitrary re-embedding of $\mathbb{V}$ alongside would merely relabel the base without changing which spaces $\mathbb{W}$ can host nullity, so nothing is lost by the normalisation, and the counting becomes meaningful.) The conformal lift \eqref{eq:lift} is a null-lift completion with $\mathbb{W} = H$ and $f(x) = \nO + \tfrac12 Q(x)\nInf$, $f(0) = \nO \neq 0$.

Nondegeneracy of $\mathbb{W}$ is the second, and Remark \claimref{note.transport.degenerate-adjunctions-why-they-are-excluded} records precisely where it is needed and what happens without it.
