## formal
Every other fermion of the derived content has at least one pair mode occupied and is charged under something; the neutrino's right-handed partner is the one state with no mode occupied and nothing to be charged under: the empty pair system, the total singlet. The physics the seesaw needs (a fermion whose mass term requires no condensate) is exactly what a total singlet has in any gauge theory (a neutral bilinear with itself), and Section \ref{sec:seesaw} turns that match into the mechanism as far as gauge invariance can carry it, which is not as far as the framework's own bracket goes: that bracket is computed only on slot-occupying states, and T12 declares it undefined for an excited composite and says nothing about slot-free states (Fact \claimref{neutrino.imported-mass-bracket-quantum-numbers}), so the framework has not yet said which of its objects, if any, is the neutral bilinear of two grade-zero states. The reading explains the Standard Model's ambivalence, and Proposition \claimref{neutrino.empty-twistor}(2) makes the explanation exact: a bottom-up construction has only anomaly cancellation to fix hypercharges, and anomaly cancellation is insensitive to a fourth singlet, so it can neither require nor forbid $\nu_{R}$ nor fix its charge; a top-down content derivation computes the state and its weight, and carries it necessarily.
