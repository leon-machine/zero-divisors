## formal
Archimedes' hat-box identity \cite{Archimedes},
\[
|\Sph^{n+1}| \;=\; 2\pi\,V_{n}
\]
(the uniform measure on $\Sph^{n+1}$, projected onto $\R^{n}$ by forgetting two coordinates, is $2\pi$ times the uniform measure on $\B^{n}$; script-checked for $n = 0$ to $6$), gives $|\Sph^{3}| = 2\pi^{2}$, $|\Sph^{4}| = \tfrac{8\pi^{2}}{3}$ and $|\Sph^{5}| = \pi^{3}$. Hence R2 is equivalent to one quantum per unit sphere $\Sph^{n}$, $n = D + 1$, of each tower's spacetime, up to a universal factor $2\pi$ that cancels, and in that form
\[
f_{3\mathrm{D}} = \frac{|\Sph^{4}|}{|\Sph^{3}|} = \frac{4}{3}, \qquad f_{5\mathrm{D}} = \frac{|\Sph^{4}|}{|\Sph^{5}|} = \frac{8}{3\pi}, \qquad
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{dark}}}{\Omega_{b}} = |\Sph^{4}|\sum_{\text{dark }r}\frac{1}{|\Sph^{n(r)}|},
\]
with $n(r)$ the tower dimension of residue $r$ and each world's sphere the equator of the next world's (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.nesting}(iii), Figure \ref{fig:ballsphere}). A third assignment, our world as the ball $\B^{4}$ towards a tower we contain and as its boundary $\Sph^{3}$ towards a tower that contains us, $f_{3\mathrm{D}} = \tfrac{V_{3}}{V_{4}}$ and $f_{5\mathrm{D}} = \tfrac{|\Sph^{4}|}{|\Sph^{3}|}$, gives the same two numbers on the opposite towers, and so the same total. It is not physically consistent. Our own density stands in the denominator of every share, and by Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} it is one quantity, our mass per unit of our three-volume; this assignment reads our world as a volume in one comparison and as a surface in the other, and so gives our own density two values at once, which one metric cannot see. It is not a mechanism, since no rule can count our own density two ways (script: no fixed divisor with unit regions reproduces that pair in that order), and Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.total-function-thickness} shows it is the family at thickness $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, the arc of Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.two-roots-are-chord-arc-quarter} in place of the flat chord that R2's cells have. So the ball and sphere forms are one rule, the factor $2\pi$ cancelling, while the exchanged assignment agrees with them in the total only. The two differ in composition: under the rule dark matter is $18.6\%$ mirror, $49.7\%$ three-dimensional and $31.6\%$ five-dimensional, and under the exchanged assignment the last two are exchanged. The total cannot tell them apart, and that difference is what the galaxy-cluster tests of Section \ref{subsec:simulated} probe. Read at composite strength (Proposition \claimref{dark-matter.radiation-audit-what-theorem-closes-what}), the colliding share of the dark matter, the mirror's included, is $68\%$ under the rule and $50\%$ under the exchanged assignment, and both fail the Bullet Cluster and the shapes of galaxy clusters. With the odd towers' matter collisionless both collide only the mirror's $18.6\%$, and those tests cannot separate them. Either way the choice between the two rests on the consistency argument above, not on the clusters.
