## formal
The Principle is a summary, not an axiom: each of its clauses is one of the theorems proved above, and it adds nothing to them. What it does add is the reading. The three costs are not a graded scale of difficulty but three different answers to one equivariance question, and the question is answerable at all only because the taxonomy is exhaustive: it is the completeness of Theorem \claimref{transport.exhaustiveness-every-zero-divisor-annihilated-idempotent}, not the ingenuity of any rule, that turns a family of repairs into a procedure.

Two closing observations. First, the shape of the type-N answer is the surprising one, and it is worth stating plainly that it is a change of level rather than a technical fix: what is repaired is not the element, which remains non-invertible in every enlargement however large, but the space in which the element's division was asked. Second, no transport removes all obstructions. Every completion carries obstructions of its own, and in the type-N case the adjoined pair is itself null (Proposition \claimref{transport.no-transport-terminates}): the repair of the old null cone is effected by adjoining new members of it. What the bookkeeping of Section \ref{sec:clock} shows is therefore not that transport terminates but that it stabilises, the clock moving at most once and then remaining fixed. Obstruction is conserved; what the rules achieve is that it no longer changes the Wedderburn type.

The paper is intended to be independently checkable throughout: every algebraic statement can be verified from the axiom $v^2 = Q(v)$ with pencil and paper, the topological statements of Section \ref{sec:totality} from the elementary point-set facts recorded in Section \ref{sec:model}, and every computational identity by the deposited scripts.
