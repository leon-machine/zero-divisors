## formal
The answer to the question the note was asked is no: inside the two-level hierarchy as T12 defines it, the tensor ledger, there is no lepton doublet at the level of states, by Fact \claimref{lepton.imported-tensor-ledger-contains-no-lepton}; at the level of operators the question is unresolved rather than closed (Remark \claimref{note.lepton.operator-route-why-it-not-one}); and no theorem of the series extends the ladder's action from bare twistors to excited ones. What the note adds is the exact shape of a hypothesis that does supply the lepton, and the exact point at which it departs from the ledger. Hypothesis (C), with its bit fixed by Proposition \claimref{lepton.relative-handedness-slotted-free-sectors}, is (S) restricted to one constituent: if T12 were to adopt (C) in place of (S), the existence and hypercharge of the lepton (Proposition 5.3(2), equation (4)), the common chirality of the doublets (Theorem 5.5(1)) and the two twistor-level doublets as a doublet and its antiparticle (Proposition 5.6, last clause) would rest on (C), a weaker premise, and the second cost T12 lists for (S), a composite state space that does not record which pairs belong to which slot, would not be paid, because (C) has no composite state space. The relative handedness of the two sectors would remain, under (C), a consistency condition fixed by the anomalies (Proposition \claimref{lepton.relative-handedness-slotted-free-sectors}), which is what it already is in T12 Theorem 5.5(3).

Two results of this note hold under (C) and (S) alike. Proposition \claimref{lepton.self-conjugacy-forces-determinant-one-group} and Observation \claimref{lepton.what-self-conjugacy-says-about-thirteenth} address the first cost T12 listed for (S), the thirteenth generator: under either hypothesis, in the untwisted action, the constituent spectrum is self-conjugate only for the determinant-one group, so the automorphism rule, read with conjugation, gives twelve generators, not thirteen, at the level of the CPT premise the Observation prints. T12 now settles the point differently at proposition level \cite[Prop.~5.8(3), Prop.~10.7]{ButlerT12}: in the untwisted action the anomaly conditions exclude the generator, but the state space carries the twisted action, in which it is anomaly-free, the $U(1)_{X}$ of $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$, broken at the intermediate scale with the rest of the Pati-Salam algebra; so it is not a cost of (S) either way. That proposition's untwisted clause assumes (S) and computes over the five-mode charges; under (C) the singlets are the conjugated odd free states, so the charge list differs and the three linear sums are $2\alpha + 3\beta$, $4\alpha + 6\beta$ and $8\alpha + 12\beta$, with the same vanishing direction and the same conclusion. And Proposition \claimref{lepton.unit-action-cell-action} locates the departure from the ledger exactly: on twistors with two or more pairs excited, and nowhere else.

What (S) is still needed for, and (C) does not give: the composite's state space as $\Lambda^{\bullet}(\C^{2} \oplus W)$, hence $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ as the Bogoliubov group of five modes and the derived $3/8$ (T12 Proposition 5.8(2), Remark 5.9). Under (C), $Q \oplus L = \Con_{1} \oplus \Con_{3}$ is the $j = 1$ part of the even half of that Fock space and $\Con_{0} \oplus \Con_{2}$ the $j = 1$ part of the odd half, and the free sector is the row $j = 0$; the row $j = 2$ is (S)'s alone. Successor problem (6) of T12 stands. It is sharpened, not narrowed: of its five outcomes this note closes the one that would derive the lepton inside the two-level hierarchy, and prices two of the others, the lepton needing at least (C) and $\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ needing all of (S).
