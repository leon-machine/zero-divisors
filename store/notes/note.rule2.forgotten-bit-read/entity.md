## formal
Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-rule-2-matrix-units-forgotten} says the fused algebra forgets the sign of $\nu^2$. Here the bit has a name on each side. With $\nu^2 = +1$ the repair is $\Cl(2,4)$: the sixth direction is a second time, the pair $(\nu, e_4)$ is the hyperbolic plane of the conformal completion, the parity of the fifth direction is a boost in that plane, and the fused algebra is the one whose spinors are the conformal spinors $\CS$ (twistors) (Fact \claimref{rule2.imported-completion-spacetime-algebra-spinors}); the Kaluza-Klein direction and the conformal completion's null pair are the same two dimensions, differently sliced. With $\nu^2 = -1$ the repair is $\Cl(1,5)$: the sixth direction is a sixth space, the parity is a rotation into it, and the algebra sits at residue $4$. The two are isomorphic as algebras and the algebra cannot tell them apart; the geometry can, by whether the adjoined plane carries a null vector. The series' physical lineage has always chosen $\Cl(2,4)$, by the vector-level minimality of the root paper; the note observes that the same choice is, from the Kaluza-Klein side, the choice that returns the residue to $6$ and reads the fifth direction as half of a null pair.
