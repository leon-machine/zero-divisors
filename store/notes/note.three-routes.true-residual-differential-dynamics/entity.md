## formal
What Theorem \claimref{three-routes.class-relative-no-go} does not and cannot exclude is a generator outside the $C^\infty$-linear class: a first-order differential operator $D$ on sections over $\Sig$, of Dirac type, has $[D, f] = c(df) \neq 0$ and is precisely not a bundle map; its quantisation would hop, and nothing above forbids nature such a dynamics on $E$, the closure merely constructing none. The question T5's Remark 7.33(b) asks therefore has a sharp remaining form: \emph{is the physical dynamics on the singular boundary $C^\infty$-linear over $E$, or does it carry a differential term?} A differential term would be a new geometric structure on $E$ beyond the completed module, and none of the series' constructions produces one; deciding whether that is a theorem (no such structure is forced) or a physical assumption is the genuine successor question, and it belongs beside the closure-completeness programme rather than inside this note.
