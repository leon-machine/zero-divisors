## formal
Two transpositions reproduce this tower and break a clause of Premise \claimref{dark-tower-matter.u-transposition-rule}. (a) Counting the $r = 7$ constituent in the full Clifford module, where the central $\omega$ joins two real constituents into one complex element that is already massive (Theorem \claimref{dark-tower-matter.where-mass-begins}(i) for the two real parts), gives the hierarchy $\C^{1} \otimes \C^{2}$ and $\uu(2)$, with content a doublet of $Y = \tfrac{1}{2}$, a singlet of $Y = -1$ and a neutral singlet; it breaks U1, since $\omega$ is odd and does not act on the spin-irreducible ideal (Proposition \claimref{dark-tower-matter.residue-seven-unit}(ii)). (b) Building the five-dimensional third pair with an external $i$, which commutes with the quaternionic unit group, keeps that group as the slot group of the one constituent: the hierarchy is a quaternionic line tensor $\C^{3}$, with automorphisms $\su(2) \oplus \su(3) \oplus \uu(1)$ and global form $S(U(2) \times U(3))$, and vector-like content $(2,3)_{1/6}$, $(2,1)_{-1/2}$. It keeps U1 and U2 and breaks U3; it is T12's own practice of working in the complexification, carried to a tower whose field is $\HH$; and it is open at $r = 3$ and $r = 5$ on the same terms, so it does not separate them. Its $\su(2)$ acts on one constituent's quaternionic multiplicity, not on two constituents. Their arithmetic is in the supplementary scripts (\file{D3C}, \file{D5Q}; Section \ref{sec:verification}). Neither is a result of this paper.
