## formal
\textbf{Verdict on Claim R2.} K2.1 does not trigger: the classes of Propositions \claimref{pairing.static-frame-only-expansion-survives} and \claimref{pairing.regular-frame-class-vanishes-horizon-changes} are nonzero off the horizon. K2.2 does not trigger: the class is $\ip{\nabla_{X}\ell}{m}$, its zeros are invariant under rescaling, and the two frames agree covariantly. K2.3 does not trigger: the classes along $\partial_{t}$ and $\partial_{r}$ vanish in both frames. K2.4 triggers: every generator found acts on the two modes of one fibre, and the object its wording asks for, a generator in $\so(V_{x} \oplus V_{y})$ pairing the completion's independent ladders at two points, is not something a connection produces. So Claim R2 is closed for the pre-ascent Dirac fibre and open for the boundary modes across points. Of the predictions, P2.2 and P2.4 held, P2.1 held with its two clauses in two frames, and P2.3 was refuted as worded (Remark \claimref{note.pairing.departures-registered-wording}).

How much was at risk should be said plainly: nothing. K2.2 is an identity, since $\Omega$ is defined by $\nabla_{X}v = \tfrac12[\Omega(X), v]$ and $\pv$ by $\ip{B\cdot\ell}{m}$, so $\pv(\Omega(X)) = \ip{\nabla_{X}\ell}{m}$ could not have failed. K2.1 asks whether the outgoing radial congruence of Schwarzschild expands. K2.3 proposes to contradict an ascended statement about the completed pair by a pre-ascent exterior computation that cannot reach it. K2.4 was registered as expected to trigger, and did. The apparatus adjudicated nothing on this claim; what the registration did was fix the computation and its sign conventions before the numbers existed. The content of the note is the dictionary and the exact numbers.
