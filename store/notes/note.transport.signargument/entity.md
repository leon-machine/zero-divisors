## formal
Two small arguments carry the theorem, and they deserve emphasis for how little they assume. The sign argument: the adjoined directions must be capable of cancelling squared lengths of every sign, and only a mixed addition can: new null structure appears only where positive meets negative. The origin argument: merely representing the origin as a ray already requires a null vector of $\mathbb{W}$, which forces indefiniteness even over a Euclidean base. The obstruction thereby determines its own repair, including where the repair lies. Note also the consonance with Remark \claimref{note.transport.hyperbolic-atom}: the completion adjoins precisely the hyperbolic plane, the one quadratic space that is self-complete at the level of elements, the unit of transport.
