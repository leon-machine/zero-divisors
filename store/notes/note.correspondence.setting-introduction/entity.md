## formal
The transport principle \cite{ButlerT1} resolves division by zero in the spacetime algebra $\Cl(1,3)$ by a forced minimal completion into $\Cl(2,4)$, and the series has so far exploited the completion's vector side: the fibre quadric whose points resolve the division \cite{ButlerT1}, the sheaf of completions over a degenerating metric \cite{ButlerT4}, the tower of iterated completions and its CAR limit \cite{ButlerT3}, the vacuum equations as a parallel ideal point of the quadric \cite{ButlerT6}, and T5's chain from the Schwarzschild gauge field to the Page curve \cite{ButlerT5}. This paper develops the completion's \emph{spinor} side, and finds there a formalism that has existed since 1967: Penrose's twistor theory.

The correspondence is not an analogy, and the paper's task is to prove it as a set of theorems and then apply it in both directions. In one direction, the series gains: fifty years of twistor technology (the Klein correspondence, the incidence geometry, local twistor transport, the twistor description of massless fields) becomes available to the transport programme with an explicit dictionary. In the other direction, the series contributes: the completion explains \emph{why} the twistorial objects are what they are. The two distinguished skew lines of projective twistor space are the null pair adjoined by the Witt completion; the incidence relation is the completion's chart, re-derived rather than postulated; and the palatial programme's noncommutative twistor algebra is exactly one quantisation above the completion's ladder pairs, with the conformal algebra as the shared core.

Three questions organise the paper. \emph{What is twistor space, from the algebra alone?} Section \ref{sec:spinors} answers with the Chevalley pure-spinor construction, proved outright at this dimension: the complexified ascended algebra acts irreducibly on the eight-dimensional fermionic Fock space of three null pairs, twistor space and its conjugate dual are the two chirality halves, and the ambient vector space is recovered as $\Lambda^2\T$ with the Klein quadric as its projectivised null cone (Theorem \claimref{correspondence.spinor-identification}). \emph{Where is the completion, inside twistor geometry?} Section \ref{sec:incidence} computes the Pl\"ucker image of Penrose's incidence relation and finds, term by term, the completion's point embedding: the $(\omega,\pi)$ splitting is the Witt completion at spinor level, and the stabiliser of the infinity line is the Poincar\'e parabolic (Theorem \claimref{correspondence.incidence-relation-chart}); local twistor transport is then identified as the spinor square root of the tractor connection of \cite{ButlerT6}, up to one declared sign convention (Proposition \claimref{correspondence.local-twistor-transport-ascended-spinor-connection}, Remark \claimref{note.correspondence.one-imported-convention-local-twistor-identification}). \emph{What is the relation to quantised twistors?} Section \ref{sec:palatial} first refutes: the palatial algebra is bosonic and admits no finite-dimensional representation, so it cannot be the completion's fermionic pair algebra (Proposition \claimref{correspondence.statistics-no-go}). The corrected picture is a two-storey construction: the completion's fermionic quantisation turns ambient directions into twistor \emph{states}; the palatial bosonic quantisation turns twistor states into operators; $\mathfrak{so}(2,4)$ is the invariant core of both storeys, and the ordering shift of a half per dimension recurs at each (Theorem \claimref{correspondence.shared-core-ordering-dictionary}). Section \ref{sec:googly} states the Lorentzian boundary (no real Lorentzian vacuum is half-flat) and poses the googly-at-quartic-order conjecture precisely.
