## formal
Comparing \eqref{eq:fock} with one Standard Model generation (the grid of Figure \ref{fig:hierarchy} is the object being decomposed) returns neither confirmation nor total failure but the framework's eighth refutation, a split conclusion. The exact agreement: the first excitation of the minimal massive hierarchy is $(2,3)$, the quark doublet, the Standard Model's most distinctive representation, appearing as the one-pair state. The near agreement: the massless sector's colour content $3 \oplus \bar{3} \oplus 1 \oplus 1$ against the observed $SU(2)$-singlet fields' $\bar{3} \oplus \bar{3} \oplus 1 \oplus 1$, a single conjugation of discrepancy, definite enough to be decided rather than debated. The definite disagreements: the lepton doublet $(2,1)$ appears nowhere in the raw spectrum; exotic representations appear that nature does not show (a colour sextet, an octet-doublet, an $SU(2)$ quadruplet); and the abelian charges are pair numbers, not hypercharges. The refutation relocates the test rather than ending it: the raw Fock space was never the physical spectrum, because elementary fermions are \emph{generators}, not states, and the corrected comparison is Proposition \claimref{standard-model.quarks-leptons-two-level-fermion-content}'s. The list of disagreements is a specification of what the corrected comparison must supply.
