## formal
Premise (R) is not derived from the transport principle, and this note does not claim otherwise. It is the same premise that every derivation of the Hawking temperature uses: regularity of the Euclidean section at the bifurcation point in \cite{GibbonsHawking1977, HartleHawking1976}, regularity on the horizon in \cite{Unruh1976}, nonsingularity of the quasi-free state in \cite{KayWald1991}, the short-distance condition in \cite{FredenhagenHaag1990}. Dropping it gives the Boulware alternative \cite{Boulware1975}, which in this framework is the $\beta = \infty$ endpoint of the line: an unoccupied ledger, a descended state that is pure, a deficit of zero by \cite[Thm.~9.3]{ButlerT5}, and no flux. What the framework contributes is not the premise but its habitat. In a metric formulation the Euclidean section is a separate construction; here, by Proposition \claimref{state-selection.euclidean-partner-curvature-selected}, the object whose periodicity is being demanded is the second member of the very eigenplane pair that Step 4b's curvature equation selects, and its period is fixed by the double cover of the rotor group, not by a coordinate choice. The premise is thereby stated in the same algebra as the rest of the chain, and its content is exactly one number.
