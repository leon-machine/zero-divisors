## formal
Under continuation to imaginary time the ungraded identity is the antiperiodic boundary condition for the mode's Euclidean two-point function on a circle of circumference $\beta$, and the graded identity the periodic one: this is the Matsubara dichotomy, and it is quoted, not proved. On the smooth Euclidean section a spinor field expressed in the Killing frame, which is the polar frame of the $(\varrho, \tau)$ plane, is multiplied by $R_{i\theta}$ of Proposition \claimref{singularity.euclidean-rotor-where-it-lies}(2) relative to a frame regular at the origin, and so reverses sign under one full turn: single-valuedness on the section, in the frame in which the modes are defined, is the antiperiodic condition, and hence the ungraded identity of (R). This is the standard spin-structure argument for the thermal, rather than parity-twisted, character of the Hartle-Hawking and Unruh states of fermions, and it is where $R_{2\pi i} = -1$ enters. The chain records it as the reason (R) is stated with the ungraded gluing; it does not derive (R) from it, and the dependence of the boundary condition on the choice of frame is exactly why the statement has to be made in the Killing frame.
