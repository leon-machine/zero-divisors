## formal
Proposition \claimref{lepton.relative-handedness-slotted-free-sectors} is a consistency condition, not a kinematic derivation: gauging $S(U(2) \times U(3))$ on the constituents together with the free sector is possible only for the listing in (1). In T12's Hypothesis (S) the same bit was encoded structurally, as the slot mode's contribution to the parity of the total mode number, which is why (S) delivered it without an anomaly argument. In the constituent picture the bit is $\epsilon$: a slot reverses which chirality half counts as the particle handedness relative to a free conformal spinor. (C) leaves $\epsilon$ open, and this proposition is what fixes it: with $\epsilon = -1$ the chirality clause of (C) is exactly (S)'s rule restricted to the row $j = 1$, while $\epsilon = +1$, the reading on which the slot index carries no chirality, is inconsistent with the anomalies. Whether that reversal has a kinematic reason (a slot of a massive composite is not a free massless state, and the tie between chirality halves and helicity is T12's observation-level reading of \cite[Fact~2.3]{ButlerT12}, made for free states) is open, and this note does not claim one. What the note claims is narrower: the choice is one bit, it is the particle-antiparticle orientation the series already lists as underivable \cite[Prop.~5.2]{ButlerT14}, and the anomaly conditions fix it.
