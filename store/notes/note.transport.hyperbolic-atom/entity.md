## formal
The assumption $n \geq 3$ is necessary, and the bound is sharp. For $n = 2$ the space is itself a hyperbolic plane, $U = 0$, there are no null rotations, and the assignment $\sigma(\lambda v) = \lambda^{-1} w_0$, $\sigma(\lambda w_0) = \lambda^{-1} v$ defines a genuinely $\Pin(1,1)$-equivariant pseudo-inverse section in the sense of Definition \claimref{obj.transport.equivariance}, and no ascent is needed: the hyperbolic plane is self-complete \emph{at the level of elements}. (Its reciprocal on \emph{points} is of course still partial; Theorem \claimref{transport.totality} applies to it like any other base, and its own transport is $\Cl(2,2)$. The two senses of completion are exactly the two halves of the transport principle, and the atom separates them.)

The section has a coordinate-free description, which is the real reason for its equivariance and simultaneously explains the failure for $n \geq 3$. For null $x$, call $y$ a \emph{partner} of $x$ if $y$ is null with $x \cdot y = \tfrac12$. Writing $y = av + bw_0 + u$ with $u \in U$, the two conditions read $b = 1$ and $a = -Q(u)$, so the partners of $v$ are exactly
\begin{equation}
y_u \;=\; w_0 - u - Q(u)\,v \qquad (u \in U),
\end{equation}
an affine space of dimension $n - 2$ on which, by Step 2 of Theorem \claimref{transport.no-go-level-elements}, the null rotations act simply transitively. For $n = 2$ it is a single point, so ``the partner of $x$'' is a well-formed metric description and $\sigma$ is equivariant for that reason alone, with no computation; for $n \geq 3$ a torsor has no distinguished point, and Theorem \claimref{transport.no-go-level-elements} says no enlargement of the space produces one. This is precisely why the hyperbolic plane is the unit of transport: the ascent of Section \ref{sec:model} adjoins a copy of the one quadratic space that needs no ascent of the first kind.


The same dimension count is Zeeman's. By Zeeman's theorem \cite{Zeeman1964}, for $n \geq 3$ every bijection of Minkowski space preserving the causal order is generated by Lorentz transformations, translations and dilations, while in $n = 2$ the two null coordinates can be reparametrised independently and the causal automorphism group is infinite-dimensional. The two-dimensional counterexamples to Zeeman are exactly the freedom that the null rotations remove for $n \geq 3$ by mixing each null direction with the transverse ones; the little group of a null direction has a null-rotation part of dimension $n - 2$, and both theorems hold precisely when it is present. Zeeman's theorem is imported here, not proved, and nothing below depends on it; this paragraph records that the third hypothesis of Theorem \claimref{transport.no-go-level-elements} and Zeeman's are the same hypothesis, the existence of a null rotation, so that the hyperbolic atom is the exception to both for one reason.
