## formal
This paper is T1 of the series.

\emph{This paper relies on:} the companion primer T0 \cite{ButlerT0}, and on nothing else in the series. The division of labour is strict: the language belongs to the primer (the structural lemmas of Section \ref{sec:prelim} are restated with their toolkit numbers, and proved in the primer's toolkit \cite[Section 9]{ButlerT0}, save the classification theorem, cited there to its classical sources), while every fact about the failure of division, zero divisors, pseudo-inverses and idempotents included, is stated and proved in this paper. Beyond the toolkit the primer is a convenience for readers new to geometric algebra. The paper's other input is the founding idea of the programme itself, whose informal taxonomy it rigorises.

\emph{Papers that rely on this one:} every other research paper of the series. In brief: T2 \cite{ButlerT2} takes the element-level lemma that a square-zero element is invertible in no unital extension (Lemma \claimref{transport.noinverse}), the type-N reading of a radical direction with the taxonomy frame, and the scope of the sharpenings of Section \ref{subsec:chartmin}, and proves its own completion theorem for degenerate algebras against that frame; T3 \cite{ButlerT3} iterates Rule 1 into the tower, using the Morita theorem and the pinching; T4 \cite{ButlerT4} localises the taxonomy and Rule 1 over degenerate loci, using the splitting lemma and the null-generator clause of Theorem \claimref{transport.one-generator-minimal-minimal-completions-classified}; T5 \cite{ButlerT5} runs the whole algorithm on the black-hole singularity, importing the no-go, totality, minimality, the type-C exclusion for $\Cl(1,3)$, the pinching, and the one-way door; and the papers after T5 \cite{ButlerT5} import these results through T3 \cite{ButlerT3}, T4 \cite{ButlerT4} and T5 \cite{ButlerT5} rather than directly, with each paper's Fact environments carrying the exact provenance. The capstone T17 \cite{ButlerT17} maps the series and records the status of every claim.

One subsection points forward without relying on anything: the sentence after Corollary \claimref{transport.which-residues-obstruct-which-way} names the dark-matter paper T14 \cite{ButlerT14} for the residues $\{2, 6\}$. It is a pointer, and the theorems of Section \ref{subsec:extension} use only this paper.
