## formal
What remains of the former premise is the proposition's antecedent, and it is thin. The metric level was defined pointwise: at each mode it is the image of that mode's descent (Proposition \claimref{singularity.descent-what-it-erases}, Theorem \claimref{singularity.descent-parity-average}), and the localisation attaches exactly one descent to each point of $E$; reading a region of the boundary mode space at the metric level means surviving every one of its points' readings, which is the composite. The total-parity average is not an alternative candidate: it is the composite of nothing the closure constructs, there being no ``global pair'' for it to descend. Nor does the reduction touch Fact \claimref{singularity.imported-univalence-superselection-rule}: the superselection rule continues to constrain observability by total parity, and cross-mode even elements such as $X_{R_1}X_{L_1}Z_{L_2}$ remain perfectly good observables of the ascended description; they are simply not metric-level records, being annihilated by the composite, which is exactly the recoverability of Proposition \claimref{singularity.purity-correlation-transfer-recovery}(3): readable in the completion, invisible in the original space. Had the antecedent been dropped and the total-parity average used in $\EN$'s place, the two-level discrepancy of Steps 5 and 6b would be identically zero, the model's radiation state being even; the script verifies this too. The antecedent enters Table \ref{tab:chain} as the residue of (P$_{\mathrm{loc}}$), definitional rather than physical. Appendix \ref{app:threeroutes} states the residue in structural terms without reducing it: ``the pointwise metric level'' names two algebras, the even part of the mode factor and the even-in-that-mode part of the boundary mode space, and a region can be read by assembling either contravariantly (a sheaf, a limit: survive every point's descent) or covariantly (a net, a colimit: the algebra the points' retained parts generate). On a finite truncation the sheaf assembly gives $\operatorname{range}(\EN)$, at once the meet of the big fibres and the join of the small ones, and the net assembly gives the whole mode algebra with a discrepancy of zero (Theorems \claimref{singularity.finite-truncation-meet-big-fibres-equals}, \claimref{singularity.net-assembly-net-it-everything}). The chain adopts the sheaf assembly; that adoption is the antecedent, restated, and Remark \claimref{note.singularity.what-antecedent-stated-plainly} records that the appendix quantifies both and decides neither. The sheaf exists whenever the completed mode bundle does, that is under the standing hypothesis $w_1(\shL) = 0$, and needs nothing further.
