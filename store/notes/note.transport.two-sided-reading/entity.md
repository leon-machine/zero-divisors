## formal
Proposition \claimref{transport.pinching-kernel} and Theorem \claimref{transport.door-one-way} split the loose statement that descent is lossy into its two true halves. On transported elements the round trip is not lossy at all: it is the identity in the natural corner identification (the grade involution in Peirce coordinates; either way invertible), and the base algebra passes through the door unchanged in both directions. What cannot descend is the completion's \emph{new} content, the ladder pair and everything it generates off the diagonal, and this impossibility is a theorem about unital homomorphisms, not an artefact of choosing a particular descent map. The statement the computations support is sharper than the original: \emph{descent is invertible on the transported elements and impossible on the elements the ascent adjoined}. Compare the point-level statement of Lemma \claimref{transport.descent-read-out-map}: there the descent is exact on the whole invertible locus and fails only on the ideal points, which are precisely the values the ascent added. Element-level and point-level descent lose different things, and each loses exactly what its ascent supplied.
