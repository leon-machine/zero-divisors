## formal
Three levels, stated separately. \emph{Proved here:} within the quasi-free class the state is unique, with gauge invariance and the Fermi-Dirac symbol both forced by the KMS identity (Theorem \claimref{hawking.i-state}); in this sense the I-state is the framework's equilibrium vacuum, and the quasi-free restriction is a convenience of proof, removed at the next level. \emph{Imported:} given the boost dynamics and $\beta$, full uniqueness among all states (Corollary \claimref{hawking.full-uniqueness-given-dynamics}, from Fact \claimref{hawking.imported-ideal-fermi-gas-uniqueness}). \emph{Open, and named:} deriving the dynamics itself, i.e.\ an axiom selecting the boost flow and its temperature from the geometry. T5 has already treated this question at the level of a single factor: its geometric modular condition (G) is formulated and rejected there as over-determining the boundary modes, its premise (R) is proved irreducible, its rate-rigidity theorem shows the temperature is not algebra-intrinsic, and the resulting state class is recorded \cite[Def.~7.12, Thms.~7.16 and 7.28, Cor.~7.32]{ButlerT5}. The question this paper poses is different and remains open: a \emph{net-level} selection in the style of geometric modular action \cite{BDFS2000}, demanding that the modular groups of a net of boundary-mode algebras act geometrically and deriving the boost identification for the whole net rather than assuming it. Within the single atom the algebra already forces the orbits of any rotor-implemented flow, though not its speed (Proposition \claimref{hawking.modular-structure-completed-pair}(5)); the execution for the net is a successor problem (Section \ref{sec:scope}). \emph{Point of contact:} the I-state on a horizon pair is Israel's thermofield double \cite{Israel1976}, and its GNS representation is the Araki-Wyss doubling; the non-equilibrium state of the descent, the cosmological state problem, is outside this paper's scope (successor problem 5).
