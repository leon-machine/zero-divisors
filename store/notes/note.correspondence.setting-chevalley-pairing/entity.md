## formal
The passage from the module $\Lambda^\bullet W$ back to the vector space $V_\C$ is a bilinear pairing on spinors that Chevalley's construction carries with it \cite{Chevalley54}. We set it out with every sign computed, since the signs are what the incidence theorem depends on. Fix a generator $w_1 \wedge w_2 \wedge w_3$ of the line $\Lambda^3 W$ and write $\operatorname{top}(\xi)$ for the coefficient of $w_1\wedge w_2\wedge w_3$ in the degree-three component of $\xi \in \Lambda^\bullet W$. Let $\alpha$ be the linear map acting on $\Lambda^k W$ as $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$, so $\alpha = +1$ on degrees $0, 1$ and $-1$ on degrees $2, 3$. Throughout, $\mathrm{Spin}(V_\C)$ denotes the group generated by the exponentials of all bivectors $\beta \in \Lambda^2 V_\C$, realised in the Clifford algebra as $\tfrac12 c(u)c(v)$ for orthogonal $u, v$ and their sums, acting on vectors by conjugation ($e^{\beta} c(v) e^{-\beta} = c(e^{\operatorname{ad}\beta} v)$, the vector action being computed in Proposition \claimref{correspondence.spin-representation}) and on the module by the given action; $\rho$ denotes the action of bivectors on $\Lambda^\bullet W$ and on its halves, $\rho(u\wedge v) := \tfrac12 c(u)c(v) - \tfrac12\ip{u}{v}$ (Propositions \claimref{correspondence.spin-representation} and \claimref{correspondence.clifford-side-ordering-shift}).
