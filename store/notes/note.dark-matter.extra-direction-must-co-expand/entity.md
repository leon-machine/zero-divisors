## formal
R2 defines one spacing in every direction of each tower's own space, and the spacing grows with the scale factor. If the extra direction were static, a case Lemma \claimref{dark-matter.projection-every-towers-content-dilutes-ones} allows, the spacing along it would stay fixed while the spacing along the shared directions grew, so R2 could hold at one epoch only; under the lemma the projected density would still dilute as $a^{-3}$ and the ratio would stay constant, but at a value that is the rule's at one epoch alone. So R2, held at all times, selects the co-expanding case, and today the extra direction is about one baryon spacing, of order a metre comoving, growing with the universe. This is a cost of R2, not of the value R3 gives.
