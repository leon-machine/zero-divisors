## formal
\textbf{Scope of hypotheses.} Throughout, $(\mathbb{V}, Q)$ is nondegenerate; nothing else is assumed globally, and each theorem carries its own hypotheses. The mixed-signature and dimension conditions $p, q \geq 1$ and $n \geq 3$ are local to the no-go theorem (Theorem \claimref{transport.no-go-level-elements}), which needs them: they are exactly what guarantees that null vectors exist and that the null rotations shearing their candidate partners exist. Remarks \claimref{note.transport.obstruction-parabolic} and \claimref{note.transport.hyperbolic-atom} delimit the boundary: definite signatures pose no element-level question at all, and $n = 2$ is the sharp exception where the question is posed and answered affirmatively. The completion theorems, namely totality (Theorem \claimref{transport.totality}), minimality (Theorem \claimref{transport.minimality}) and the normal form (Proposition \claimref{transport.swap-rescale-normal-form}), hold for \emph{every} nondegenerate signature, definite included; Section \ref{subsec:sphere} exercises them over the base of signature $(2,0)$.

The two canonical involutions are written as in the primer \cite[Section 5]{ButlerT0}: the \emph{grade involution} $x \mapsto \hat{x}$ (the automorphism with $\hat{v} = -v$ on vectors) and the \emph{reversion} $x \mapsto \tilde{x}$ (the anti-automorphism fixing vectors). One consequence is used repeatedly enough to display, the commutation rule \cite[Lemma 9.1]{ButlerT0}: if $x$ lies in the subalgebra generated by vectors orthogonal to a vector $v$, then moving $v$ past $x$ introduces exactly a grade involution,
\begin{equation}
v\,x = \hat{x}\,v, \qquad x\,v = v\,\hat{x}.
\end{equation}
