## formal
Proposition \claimref{interior.filtration-cauchy-horizon-read-literature}(2) is a filtration statement, and it is read as exactly that: survival at the perturbed Cauchy horizon is ordered by derivative level, with the divergence in the nilpotent slots, which is the structure the framework predicts. What the literature does not supply is a strict separation of the conformal from the metric description at fixed derivative level: the framework's central claim, that the ascended description persists where the metric one fails, therefore rests at the Cauchy horizon on the open transport question of successor problem (1), not on a finished theorem. The Kerr test is passed at the level the imports reach, and no further. Two cautions make the conclusion more precise, both recorded as scope. First, generic perturbations destroy type-D rigidity: the exact shape factorisations of Propositions \claimref{interior.exact-scale-decoupling} and \claimref{interior.complex-factorisation-location-ring} are properties of the exact family, and robust statements off it must be made at the level of the boost-weight filtration rather than the fixed operator $S$. Second, what the framework most needs, a quantitative transport theorem (convergence of tractor parallel transport along causal curves crossing the Cauchy horizon), needs integrability of the connection along such curves: a property strictly between the established $C^0$ metric extension and the $C^1$ statement whose generic failure is proved in spherical symmetry and expected for Kerr, and not currently available in either direction. It is the sharpest open problem the test leaves, and it is registered in Section \ref{sec:scope}.
