## formal
Write $\Aa$ for the algebra generated by the completed pair, $\Aa \cong \Mat_2(\C)$ as a factor. It carries the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ grading it inherits from the Clifford algebra: $\Aev := \operatorname{span}\{1, \sigma\}$ is the even part and $\Aod := \operatorname{span}\{v, v^{*}\}$ the odd part, where $\sigma := v^{*}v - vv^{*}$ is the bivector of Theorem \claimref{singularity.modular-structure-completed-pair}. The \emph{parity automorphism} is $\alpha(x) := \sigma x \sigma$, well defined because $\sigma^{2} = 1$; it fixes $\Aev$ pointwise and acts by $-1$ on $\Aod$. On the full boundary mode space the parity automorphism is $\operatorname{Ad}(\Gamma)$ with $\Gamma := \prod_k \sigma_k$, the total fermion parity; the mode-wise refinement, one parity automorphism $\operatorname{Ad}(\sigma_k)$ per point of $E$, is Proposition \claimref{singularity.p-reduced-metric-level-reading-many}'s task, and on two or more modes the two generate different averages.

## technical
The algebra $\Aa \cong \Mat_2(\C)$ generated by a completed pair $(v, v^{*})$ inherits a $\Z_2$ grading from the Clifford algebra: the even part is $\Aev = \operatorname{span}\{1, \sigma\}$ and the odd part is $\Aod = \operatorname{span}\{v, v^{*}\}$, where $\sigma = v^{*}v - vv^{*}$. Because $\sigma^{2} = 1$, conjugation $\alpha(x) = \sigma x \sigma$ is an automorphism, the parity automorphism; it fixes $\Aev$ pointwise and multiplies $\Aod$ by $-1$.

On the full boundary mode space the parity automorphism is $\operatorname{Ad}(\Gamma)$ with $\Gamma = \prod_k \sigma_k$, the total fermion parity. The finer mode-wise version, one automorphism $\operatorname{Ad}(\sigma_k)$ for each point of $E$, is treated separately, and on two or more modes the two generate different averages.

## lay
A single fermionic mode, described by two-by-two matrices, splits naturally into an even part (the identity and the element that tells empty from occupied) and an odd part (the operators that create or remove the fermion). The parity automorphism leaves the even part alone and flips the sign of the odd part. For many modes the natural version flips the sign according to the total number of fermions; flipping mode by mode is a different, finer operation.

## one-liner
The algebra of a completed pair is graded into an even part spanned by $1$ and $\sigma$ and an odd part spanned by the ladder pair, and the parity automorphism $\alpha(x) = \sigma x \sigma$ fixes the first and negates the second.
