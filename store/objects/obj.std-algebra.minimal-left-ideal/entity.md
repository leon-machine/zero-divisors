## formal
A \emph{left ideal} of $\Cl(V, Q)$ is a subspace $L$ with $\Cl(V, Q)\, L \subseteq L$; it is \emph{minimal} when $L \ne 0$ and its only left ideals inside it are $0$ and $L$. A \emph{spinor space} of $\Cl(V, Q)$ is a minimal left ideal $S$, carrying the \emph{spinor representation} $x \mapsto (s \mapsto x s)$; its elements are \emph{spinors}. For an idempotent $e$, $\Cl(V, Q)\, e$ is a left ideal; it is the spinor space generated by $e$ when it is minimal.

## technical
Spinors are defined inside the algebra: a spinor space is a minimal left ideal, a subspace closed under multiplication from the left that contains no smaller such subspace. The algebra acts on it by left multiplication. Ideals of the form $\Cl\, e$ with $e$ an idempotent are the usual source, as in the Fock space of \claimref{std-algebra.fock-representation}.

## lay
Spinors are usually introduced as columns of numbers acted on by matrices. Here they are elements of the algebra itself: a smallest subspace that the algebra maps into itself by multiplying from the left. No matrices need to be chosen.

## one-liner
A spinor space is a minimal left ideal $S$ of $\Cl(V, Q)$, acted on by left multiplication; $\Cl\, e$ for an idempotent $e$ is the standard source.
