## formal
The \emph{projective} (plane-based) algebra of Euclidean three-space is $\Cl(3,0,1)$, generated by $e_1, e_2, e_3$ with $e_i^{2} = 1$ and one null generator $e_0$ spanning the radical, $e_0^{2} = 0$. A plane $a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + a_3 x_3 = \delta$ is the vector $a_1 e_1 + a_2 e_2 + a_3 e_3 - \delta e_0$; lines are bivectors and points are trivectors. The algebra is degenerate, and its even subalgebra contains the rotors and the translators $1 + \tfrac12 e_0 t$ of Euclidean motions.

## technical
The projective algebra is a degenerate Clifford algebra: three Euclidean generators and one null generator that is orthogonal to everything. Vectors represent planes, and points are represented by trivectors. Rigid motions, translations included, are products in its even part. It is a standard example of a degenerate algebra used in practice.

## lay
The projective algebra adds to ordinary space one direction of zero length that is perpendicular to everything. That extra direction lets translations be written as products, just as rotations are.

## one-liner
$\Cl(3,0,1)$, three Euclidean generators and one null radical generator $e_0$: vectors are planes, trivectors points, and $1 + \tfrac12 e_0 t$ translates.
