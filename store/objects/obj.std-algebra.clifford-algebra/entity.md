## formal
A \emph{Clifford algebra} of the real quadratic space $(V, Q)$ is a pair $(\Cl(V, Q), \iota)$ of an associative unital real algebra and a linear map $\iota : V \to \Cl(V, Q)$ with
\begin{equation}
\iota(v)^{2} = Q(v)\, 1 \qquad \text{for all } v \in V,
\end{equation}
which is universal: for every associative unital real algebra $A$ and linear map $f : V \to A$ with $f(v)^{2} = Q(v)\,1$, there is exactly one unital algebra homomorphism $\bar f : \Cl(V, Q) \to A$ with $\bar f \circ \iota = f$. We identify $v$ with $\iota(v)$ (justified by \claimref{std-algebra.blade-basis}) and call its product the \emph{geometric product}. $\Cl(p,q,r) = \Cl(\R^{p,q,r})$ and $\Cl(p,q) = \Cl(p,q,0)$.

## technical
The Clifford algebra of $(V, Q)$ is the associative unital algebra generated by the vectors of $V$ subject only to the rule that each vector squares to its quadratic form, $v^{2} = Q(v)$. "Subject only to" is made precise by the universal property: any linear map from $V$ into an associative algebra whose values square to $Q$ extends to exactly one algebra homomorphism. The product is the geometric product. $\Cl(p, q, r)$ is the algebra of the standard space of signature $(p, q, r)$.

## lay
The Clifford algebra lets you multiply arrows. The only rule imposed is that an arrow times itself is its square length, a plain number. Everything else, from complex numbers to rotations to spinors, follows from that single rule.

## one-liner
$\Cl(V, Q)$ is the associative unital algebra generated by $V$ with $v^{2} = Q(v)$, defined by its universal property; $\Cl(p,q,r) = \Cl(\R^{p,q,r})$.
