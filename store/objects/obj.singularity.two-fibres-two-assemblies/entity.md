## formal
For $x \in E$ the \emph{small fibre} is the even part of the mode factor, $\Aa_x^{+} := \{a \in \Aa_x : \alpha_x(a) = a\} = \operatorname{span}\{1, \sigma(x)\}$, and the \emph{even subbundle} $\Aa^{+} \subseteq \Aa$ has these fibres. On a finite truncation $F = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \subseteq E$ with its $F$-mode algebra, the \emph{big fibre} at $x_j$ is $B_j := \operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Ad}\sigma(x_j))$, the elements of the whole $F$-mode algebra even in mode $j$, of dimension $4^N/2$; the small fibre lies inside it as the elements of mode $j$'s own factor. The \emph{sheaf assembly} is $\mathfrak{M}(U) := \Gamma(U, \Aa^{+})$ for open $U \subseteq E$, families $s$ with $s(x) \in \Aa_x^{+}$ at every $x \in U$, with the restriction maps of sections. The \emph{net assembly} is $J(F) :=$ the algebra generated in the $F$-mode algebra by $\bigcup_j B_j$, for finite $F$, with the inclusion maps $J(F) \hookrightarrow J(F')$ for $F \subseteq F'$.

## technical
At each point $x \in E$ the small fibre is the even part $\Aa_x^{+} = \operatorname{span}\{1, \sigma(x)\}$ of the mode factor, the elements fixed by the parity automorphism $\alpha_x$; together these form the even subbundle $\Aa^{+}$. On a finite truncation $F = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\}$ the big fibre at $x_j$ is $B_j = \operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Ad}\sigma(x_j))$, the elements of the whole $F$-mode algebra that are even in mode $j$, of dimension $4^N/2$; the small fibre sits inside it as the even part of mode $j$'s own factor.

Two assemblies of these pointwise levels are defined. The sheaf assembly is $\mathfrak{M}(U) = \Gamma(U, \Aa^{+})$, the sections of the even subbundle over open $U \subseteq E$ with the usual restriction maps. The net assembly $J(F)$ is the algebra generated by all the big fibres $B_j$ over finite $F$, with the inclusions $J(F) \hookrightarrow J(F')$ for $F \subseteq F'$.

## lay
At each point of the singular locus there is one fermionic mode, and its parity-even part is a small two-dimensional algebra, the small fibre. When finitely many points are taken together, one can instead take everything in the combined system that is even in one chosen mode, the big fibre. The definition sets up two ways of gluing such pointwise pieces: as a sheaf of local sections of the small fibres, and as a net of algebras generated by the big fibres that grows with the set of points.

## one-liner
The small fibre is a mode's even part, the big fibre is everything even in that mode on a finite truncation, and the sheaf and net assemblies glue the small and the big fibres respectively.
