\newlabel{fig:wedge}{{1}{5}{The outer product. $a \wedge b$ is the oriented parallelogram the two vectors span: a plane, an area $|a|\,|b|\sin \theta $, and a sense of circulation, drawn here as the arrow inside. Taking the factors in the other order reverses the sense, which is what the sign in $b \wedge a = -\,a \wedge b$ records. The shape carries no information: replacing $b$ by $b + \lambda a$ slides the upper edge along the lower one and leaves the product unchanged. Nothing in the definition refers to a direction perpendicular to the plane, so it makes sense in any dimension}{figure.1}{}}

\newlabel{fig:cone}{{2}{8}{The hyperbolic plane $\Cl (1,1)$, drawn in the coordinates of Example \ref {ex:hyperbolic}. The two null lines (light blue) make up the null cone, the set where $Q$ vanishes; the dashed hyperbolas are the level sets $Q = \pm 1$, where every vector is invertible. The hyperbolic pair $v, w$ lies one on each null line, and $vw + wv = 1$. In dimension two the partner on the other line is unique, which is why T1 singles this plane out as the one self-complete mixed signature}{figure.2}{}}
